Le troisième conditionnel

Le troisième conditionnel (If I had known, I would have come) imagine un passé différent : ce qui serait arrivé si les choses s'étaient passées autrement. Il sert à exprimer des regrets, des reproches, du soulagement et à analyser des erreurs.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Rédigé par JG◎ Niveau B1

Envie d’immersion ? This lesson in English →

À la fin de cette leçon, tu sauras :
  • Construire le troisième conditionnel avec if + past perfect et would have + participe passé.
  • Reconnaître et produire les formes contractées ('d had / 'd would, would've) à l'oral.
  • Exprimer regrets, reproches et soulagement à propos du passé.
  • Nuancer avec could have et might have, et distinguer les trois conditionnels.

La structure

Condition : if + past perfect (had + participe). Résultat : would have + participe passé. Les deux parties parlent du passé et de quelque chose qui n'est pas arrivé.

C'est l'équivalent du français « si + plus-que-parfait, conditionnel passé » : « Si j'avais su, je serais venu » = If I had known, I would have come. Le piège : mettre would dans la partie en if (« If I would have known » est faux, même si on l'entend parfois en anglais américain familier).

Condition (if + past perfect)Résultat (would have + participe)Réalité
If I had known,I would have come.Je ne savais pas, je ne suis pas venu.
If she had studied,she would have passed.Elle n'a pas étudié, elle a échoué.
If we hadn't missed the bus,we wouldn't have been late.Nous avons raté le bus, nous étions en retard.
If it hadn't rained,the match would have taken place.Il a plu, le match a été annulé.

Les contractions : le casse-tête du 'd

À l'oral, on contracte presque tout : « If I'd known, I'd have come. » Le premier 'd = had (suivi d'un participe), le second 'd = would (suivi de have).

Would have se prononce /ˈwʊdəv/ et s'écrit parfois would've. Attention : on n'écrit jamais « would of », erreur fréquente même chez les natifs, parce que 've et of se prononcent pareil /əv/.

  • If you'd told me, I'd have helped you.
    If you had told me, I would have helped you.
  • She would've loved this place.
    Would've = would have (écrit informel).
  • If they'd left earlier, they wouldn't have missed the flight.
    Négation : wouldn't have.

Regrets, reproches, soulagement

Regret (sur soi) : « If I had studied harder, I would have got a better job. » Reproche (sur quelqu'un d'autre) : « If you had listened to me, this wouldn't have happened! » Soulagement (heureusement que…) : « If the doctor hadn't come so quickly, he would have died. »

Le troisième conditionnel permet aussi d'analyser un enchaînement de causes, au travail par exemple : « If we had tested the software, the client wouldn't have complained. »

  • If I'd taken the job in Toronto, I would have earned more.
    Regret.
  • If you had checked the address, we wouldn't have got lost.
    Reproche.
  • If she hadn't worn a helmet, she would have been badly hurt.
    Soulagement.

Could have, might have

Dans le résultat : could have + participe = aurait pu (possibilité ou capacité) ; might have + participe = aurait peut-être. « If you had asked me, I could have helped you » / « If we had left earlier, we might have caught the train. »

Dans la condition, could have n'est pas possible : on utilise had been able to. « If I had been able to come, I would have come. »

RésultatExempleSens
would haveIf he'd trained, he would have won.certain
could haveIf he'd trained, he could have won.il en aurait été capable
might haveIf he'd trained, he might have won.peut-être
wouldn't haveIf he'd trained, he wouldn't have lost.certain (négatif)
  • If I had been able to come, I would have come.
    Dans la condition : had been able to (pas could have).
  • You could have told me earlier!
    Reproche avec could have, sans if.

Les trois conditionnels côte à côte

Premier : futur possible (if + présent, will). Deuxième : présent ou futur imaginaire (if + prétérit, would). Troisième : passé imaginaire (if + past perfect, would have). On recule d'un temps à chaque fois.

On peut mélanger le deuxième et le troisième quand une cause passée a un effet présent : « If I had taken the medicine, I would feel better now. » Ce conditionnel mixte sera vu en détail au niveau B2 (leçon 158).

TypeExempleSens
1erIf I see Ali, I'll tell him.possible : je le verrai peut-être
2eIf I saw Ali, I'd tell him.improbable : il est à l'étranger
3eIf I had seen Ali, I'd have told him.passé : je ne l'ai pas vu
  • If I had taken the medicine, I would feel better now.
    Cause passée, effet présent (conditionnel mixte).
  • If I had seen Ali, I'd have told him the news.
    Troisième conditionnel : je ne l'ai pas vu.

La formule

If + had + participe, would / could / might have + participe (If I had known, I would have come.)

Prononciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • would have se réduit à /ˈwʊdəv/ ou même /ˈwʊdə/ à l'oral rapide : « I'd have come » /aɪd əv ˈkʌm/.
  • wouldn't have : /ˈwʊdntəv/, trois syllabes très liées.
  • had est faible et souvent contracté : « If I'd known » /ɪf aɪd ˈnəʊn/. Le 'd est léger mais indispensable.
  • Pour un reproche, l'accent tombe fortement sur le mot clé : « If you had LISTENED to me… »
  • could have /ˈkʊdəv/ et should have /ˈʃʊdəv/ suivent le même modèle (jamais « could of » à l'écrit).

Les erreurs fréquentes

À éviterÀ direPourquoi
✗ If I would have known, I would have come.✓ If I had known, I would have come.Past perfect dans la partie en if, jamais would have.
✗ If I had known, I would come.✓ If I had known, I would have come.Pour un résultat passé : would have + participe.
✗ If she had studied, she would have pass.✓ If she had studied, she would have passed.Après would have, le participe passé.
✗ I would of helped you.✓ I would have helped you. / I'd have helped you.On écrit have (ou 've), jamais of.
✗ If I knew, I would have told you.✓ If I had known, I would have told you.Situation passée : had known, pas knew.
✗ If we had left earlier, we could caught the train.✓ If we had left earlier, we could have caught the train.Could have + participe pour une possibilité passée.

En situation

Listening Studio

Bruxelles, après un entretien d'embauche raté. Inès en parle au téléphone avec sa sœur Mariam, à Dakar.

Mariam

So, how did the interview go?
Alors, comment s'est passé l'entretien ?

Inès

Badly. If I'd known they wanted a presentation, I would have prepared one.
Mal. Si j'avais su qu'ils voulaient une présentation, j'en aurais préparé une.

Mariam

Didn't they tell you in the email?
Ils ne te l'ont pas dit dans le mail ?

Inès

They did, but I didn't read it carefully. If I had read it, I wouldn't have been so surprised.
Si, mais je ne l'ai pas lu attentivement. Si je l'avais lu, je n'aurais pas été aussi surprise.

Mariam

Well, if you'd called me, I could have helped you prepare.
Bon, si tu m'avais appelée, j'aurais pu t'aider à te préparer.

Inès

I know. And if the train hadn't been late, I might have had time to calm down.
Je sais. Et si le train n'avait pas été en retard, j'aurais peut-être eu le temps de me calmer.

Mariam

Don't worry. You've learnt something. Next time you'll be ready.
Ne t'en fais pas. Tu as appris quelque chose. La prochaine fois, tu seras prête.

Inès

You're right. If I get another interview, I'll read everything twice!
Tu as raison. Si j'ai un autre entretien, je lirai tout deux fois !

Exercices

Writing Workshop

Du plus simple au plus difficile. Réponds sans regarder la leçon, puis vérifie chaque exercice.

1. Quelle est la réalité ?

Dis ce qui s'est vraiment passé.

  1. If I had known about the party, I would have gone.
  2. If she hadn't missed the bus, she wouldn't have been late.
  3. If they had saved money, they could have bought a house.
  4. If it hadn't snowed, the flight wouldn't have been cancelled.
Voir le corrigé
  1. I didn't know about the party, so I didn't go.
  2. She missed the bus, so she was late.
  3. They didn't save money, so they couldn't buy a house.
  4. It snowed, so the flight was cancelled.

2. Conjuguer

Mets les verbes au troisième conditionnel.

  1. If you ___ (ask) me, I ___ (help) you.
  2. We ___ (not / get) lost if we ___ (take) a map.
  3. If he ___ (not / eat) that fish, he ___ (not / be) ill.
  4. If I ___ (have) your number, I ___ (call) you.
  5. She ___ (might / win) if she ___ (train) more.
Voir le corrigé
  1. had asked … would have helped
  2. wouldn't have got … had taken
  3. hadn't eaten … wouldn't have been
  4. had had … would have called
  5. might have won … had trained

3. Transformer

Réécris la phrase au troisième conditionnel.

  1. I didn't set my alarm, so I overslept.
  2. You didn't tell me, so I didn't come.
  3. The shop was closed, so we didn't buy any bread.
  4. He forgot his passport, so he missed the flight.
Voir le corrigé
  1. If I had set my alarm, I wouldn't have overslept.
  2. If you had told me, I would have come.
  3. If the shop hadn't been closed, we would have bought some bread.
  4. If he hadn't forgotten his passport, he wouldn't have missed the flight.

4. Corriger les erreurs

Chaque phrase contient une erreur. Corrige-la.

  1. If I would have seen you, I would have said hello.
  2. If they had come, they would enjoy the concert.
  3. I would of called you, but my phone was dead.
  4. If she had went to the doctor, she would have felt better.
Voir le corrigé
  1. If I had seen you, I would have said hello.
  2. If they had come, they would have enjoyed the concert.
  3. I would have called you, but my phone was dead.
  4. If she had gone to the doctor, she would have felt better.

5. Traduire

Traduis en anglais.

  1. Si j'avais su, je serais venu.
  2. Si tu m'avais écouté, ça ne serait pas arrivé.
  3. Si nous étions partis plus tôt, nous aurions pu voir le début du film.
  4. Elle aurait peut-être accepté si tu lui avais demandé.
Voir le corrigé
  1. If I had known, I would have come.
  2. If you had listened to me, this wouldn't have happened.
  3. If we had left earlier, we could have seen the beginning of the film.
  4. She might have accepted if you had asked her.

6. À toi

Pense à une décision importante de ta vie. Écris 4 phrases au troisième conditionnel pour imaginer ce qui se serait passé autrement (would have, could have, might have).

  1. Exemple de réponse
Voir le corrigé
  1. If I hadn't moved to Montreal, I wouldn't have met my wife. If I had stayed in Douala, I might have opened a business with my brother. I could have become an engineer if I had chosen maths at university. But if I hadn't made those choices, I wouldn't have this life, and I love it.
À retenir
  • If + past perfect, would have + participe : passé imaginaire, qui n'a pas eu lieu.
  • Jamais would have dans la partie en if.
  • 'd = had devant un participe ; 'd = would devant have.
  • could have = aurait pu ; might have = aurait peut-être.
  • On écrit would have / would've, jamais « would of ».
