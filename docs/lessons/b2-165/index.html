Les temps du futur dans le passé (was going to, would)

« J'allais t'appeler, mais j'ai oublié », « Je savais qu'il viendrait », « Nous étions sur le point de partir » : pour parler d'un futur vu depuis un moment du passé, l'anglais utilise was going to, would, was about to, was + -ing et was to. Ces formes sont indispensables pour raconter une histoire, s'excuser d'un projet abandonné ou rapporter des paroles.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Rédigé par JG◎ Niveau B2

Envie d’immersion ? This lesson in English →

À la fin de cette leçon, tu sauras :
  • Exprimer une intention ou une prévision passée avec was / were going to, souvent non réalisée.
  • Employer would pour un futur vu du passé dans un récit ou au discours indirect.
  • Utiliser was about to, was + -ing, was due to et was to selon le sens.
  • Éviter « would » après when / as soon as et les calques du conditionnel français.

Le principe : déplacer le point de vue dans le passé

Chaque façon de parler du futur a son équivalent « dans le passé » : on recule simplement l'auxiliaire. will → would ; am going to → was going to ; am meeting → was meeting ; am about to → was about to.

Le point de référence est un moment passé ; l'action est future par rapport à ce moment. Elle a pu se réaliser ou non : c'est le contexte qui le dit.

Futur (depuis maintenant)Futur dans le passéExemple
willwouldI knew she would win.
be going towas / were going toI was going to call you, but I forgot.
présent en be + -ing (arrangement)was / were + -ingWe were leaving the next day, so we packed.
be about towas / were about toThe train was about to leave.
be due towas / were due toThe meeting was due to start at nine.
be to (formel)was / were toHe was to become president ten years later.

Was / were going to : l'intention ou la prévision passée

« Was going to » exprime une intention que l'on avait dans le passé. Très souvent, elle ne s'est pas réalisée, et on ajoute « but… » : « I was going to call you, but I forgot. » C'est la traduction naturelle du français « j'allais… », « je comptais… ».

Il exprime aussi une prévision fondée sur des indices dans le passé : « The sky was black. It was obviously going to rain. »

À l'oral, « was going to » se dit souvent « was gonna » ; on ne l'écrit ainsi que dans un message très familier.

  • I was going to buy a new phone, but the price went up.
    J'allais acheter un téléphone, mais…
  • We were going to visit Kribi, but it rained all week.
    Nous comptions visiter Kribi.
  • I could see she was going to cry.
    Je voyais qu'elle allait pleurer (prévision).
  • Sorry, I wasn't going to say anything, but…
    Désolé, je ne comptais rien dire, mais…

Would : le futur vu du passé

« Would » est le futur de « will » transposé dans le passé. On le rencontre dans le discours indirect (« She said she would call ») et dans les récits, pour annoncer ce qui allait arriver : « In 1995, she met the man she would marry two years later. »

C'est l'équivalent du conditionnel français dans « Je savais qu'il viendrait ». Mais attention : on ne peut pas employer « would » pour une intention personnelle non réalisée. « I would call you yesterday » est faux ; il faut « I was going to call you yesterday ».

  • I knew she would win.
    Je savais qu'elle gagnerait.
  • He promised he would help us the next day.
    Il a promis qu'il nous aiderait le lendemain.
  • Nobody thought the project would succeed.
    Personne ne pensait que le projet réussirait.
  • Little by little, the village would become a town.
    Récit : ce qui allait se produire.

Was about to, was + -ing, was due to

« Was about to + base verbale » : une action imminente dans le passé (être sur le point de). On ajoute souvent « when » pour l'interruption : « We were about to leave when the phone rang. » On peut renforcer avec « just » : « I was just about to call you! »

« Was + -ing » : un arrangement prévu dans le passé, comme le présent progressif pour le futur : « I was flying to Montréal the next morning, so I went to bed early. »

« Was due to + base verbale » : un horaire ou un événement prévu officiellement : « The flight was due to land at 6, but it was delayed. »

  • The film was about to start when she arrived.
    Le film allait commencer.
  • I was just about to call you!
    J'allais justement t'appeler !
  • She was meeting her parents that evening, so she couldn't stay.
    Arrangement prévu.
  • The new bridge was due to open in May.
    Ouverture prévue en mai.

Was to : le destin et l'arrangement formel

« Was / were to + base verbale » est plus littéraire. Il annonce un événement réellement arrivé plus tard (le destin) : « He was to become one of the greatest writers of his generation. »

Au passé composé de cette forme, « was to have + participe » signifie que l'arrangement prévu n'a pas eu lieu : « The wedding was to have taken place in June » (le mariage devait avoir lieu en juin, mais…). Cette forme est formelle et rare à l'oral.

« Was supposed to » est l'équivalent courant pour un projet ou une obligation non respectés : « We were supposed to meet at eight, but he never came. »

  • Nelson Mandela was to spend 27 years in prison.
    Il allait passer 27 ans en prison (fait historique).
  • The concert was to have taken place in Liège.
    Le concert devait avoir lieu à Liège (mais ne l'a pas eu).
  • We were supposed to meet at eight.
    Nous étions censés nous retrouver à huit heures.

Les subordonnées de temps et les calques

Comme avec « will », on n'emploie pas « would » après when, as soon as, before, after, until. On met le prétérit : « He said he would phone when he arrived » (et non « when he would arrive »).

Le français utilise le conditionnel (« je savais qu'il viendrait ») et l'imparfait d'« aller » (« j'allais partir »). Retenez : « viendrait » → would come ; « allait partir » → was going to leave / was about to leave.

Dans un récit au passé, ne mélangez pas avec le futur : « I thought it is going to rain » est faux ; « I thought it was going to rain ».

  • She told me she would call as soon as she got home.
    « got », pas « would get ».
  • I thought it was going to rain, so I took an umbrella.
    Concordance des temps.

La formule

was / were going to · would · was / were + -ing · was / were about to · was / were due to · was / were to + base verbale

Prononciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • « was going to » se réduit souvent à /wəz ˈɡʌnə/ (« was gonna ») à l'oral rapide.
  • « would » se contracte en 'd : « I knew she'd win » /aɪ njuː ʃiːd ˈwɪn/.
  • « were about to » : /wər əˈbaʊt tə/, avec un r de liaison en anglais britannique.
  • Dans « I was going to call you, but… », la voix monte sur « call » puis descend : l'intonation annonce que le projet n'a pas abouti.
  • « due » se prononce /djuː/ en britannique, /duː/ en américain.

✗ He said he would phone when he would arrive.✓ He said he would phone when he arrived.Pas de « would » après when.
✗ We were about leaving when it started to rain.✓ We were about to leave when it started to rain.be about to + base verbale.
✗ She was to became famous.✓ She was to become famous.was to + base verbale.
✗ I thought it is going to rain.✓ I thought it was going to rain.Récit au passé : was going to.

En situation

Listening Studio

À Nairobi, Wanjiru et son collègue belge Thomas, de retour au bureau après un long week-end.

Wanjiru

So, did you go to the Maasai Mara in the end?
Alors, finalement, tu es allé au Maasai Mara ?

Thomas

No! We were going to leave on Friday morning, but the car broke down.
Non ! On devait partir vendredi matin, mais la voiture est tombée en panne.

Wanjiru

Oh no. I knew that old car would cause problems one day.
Oh non. Je savais que cette vieille voiture finirait par poser des problèmes.

Thomas

We were just about to get on the road when the engine started smoking.
On était sur le point de prendre la route quand le moteur s'est mis à fumer.

Wanjiru

And the mechanic?
Et le garagiste ?

Thomas

He promised he would fix it by Saturday, but he didn't have the part.
Il a promis qu'il la réparerait pour samedi, mais il n'avait pas la pièce.

Wanjiru

Too bad. Our flight to Mombasa was due to leave on Sunday, so we had the same stress!
Dommage. Notre vol pour Mombasa devait partir dimanche, donc on a eu le même stress !

Thomas

Well, I was going to call you to ask for a lift, but I didn't want to ruin your weekend.
Bon, j'allais t'appeler pour te demander de m'emmener, mais je ne voulais pas gâcher ton week-end.

Exercices

Writing Workshop

Du plus simple au plus difficile. Réponds sans regarder la leçon, puis vérifie chaque exercice.

1. Quel sens ?

Explique le sens de chaque phrase.

  1. I was going to call you, but I forgot.
  2. She knew she would never see him again.
  3. We were leaving the next morning, so we packed our bags.
  4. The train was about to leave when I arrived.
Voir le corrigé
  1. Intention passée non réalisée.
  2. Futur vu du passé (et cela s'est réalisé).
  3. Arrangement prévu dans le passé.
  4. Futur immédiat dans le passé (sur le point de).

2. Compléter

Complète avec la forme qui convient (was going to, would, was about to, was due to).

  1. I ___ (go) to the gym yesterday, but it was closed.
  2. When I met Paul in 2015, I didn't know he ___ (become) my husband.
  3. We ___ (just / leave) when the phone rang. (sur le point de)
  4. He promised he ___ (help) us the next day.
  5. The meeting ___ (start) at nine, but it began at ten. (horaire prévu)
  6. Look at those clouds! I knew it ___ (rain).
Voir le corrigé
  1. was going to go
  2. would become
  3. were just about to leave
  4. would help
  5. was due to start
  6. was going to rain (ou would rain)

3. Réécrire

Réécris chaque phrase avec la forme indiquée.

  1. I planned to visit my aunt, but I didn't have time. (was going to)
  2. She said: “I will send the report tomorrow.” (discours indirect)
  3. We had arranged to meet at eight. (was / were + -ing)
  4. The plane was going to take off in a few seconds. (about to)
  5. Later he became a famous writer. (was to)
Voir le corrigé
  1. I was going to visit my aunt, but I didn't have time.
  2. She said (that) she would send the report the next day.
  3. We were meeting at eight.
  4. The plane was about to take off.
  5. He was (later) to become a famous writer.

4. Corriger les erreurs

Chaque phrase contient une erreur. Réécris-la correctement.

  1. I knew she will be late.
  2. I would call you yesterday, but I forgot.
  3. He said he would phone when he would arrive.
  4. We were about leaving when it started to rain.
Voir le corrigé
  1. I knew she would be late.
  2. I was going to call you yesterday, but I forgot.
  3. He said he would phone when he arrived.
  4. We were about to leave when it started to rain.

5. Traduire

Traduis en anglais.

  1. J'allais t'appeler, mais j'ai oublié.
  2. Je savais qu'il viendrait.
  3. Nous étions sur le point de partir quand elle est arrivée.
  4. Il ne savait pas encore qu'il deviendrait président.
Voir le corrigé
  1. I was going to call you, but I forgot.
  2. I knew he would come.
  3. We were (just) about to leave when she arrived.
  4. He didn't know yet that he would become (was to become) president.

6. À toi

Raconte en 4 ou 5 phrases un projet qui ne s'est pas passé comme prévu, avec au moins quatre formes de futur dans le passé.

  1. Exemple de réponse
Voir le corrigé
  1. Last summer, I was going to spend two weeks in Canada with my cousin. My flight was due to leave Brussels on 2 July. I was just about to go to the airport when I realised my passport had expired. I had promised my cousin I would arrive on time, so I felt terrible. In the end, I flew a week later, and it turned out to be the best holiday of my life.
À retenir
  • Futur dans le passé = on recule l'auxiliaire : will → would, am going to → was going to, am about to → was about to.
  • was going to = intention passée, souvent non réalisée (« j'allais… mais »). Jamais « I would call you yesterday ».
  • would = futur vu du passé dans un récit ou au discours indirect (« je savais qu'il viendrait »).
  • was about to (imminent), was + -ing (arrangement), was due to (horaire), was to (destin, formel).
  • Pas de « would » après when / as soon as : « when he arrived ».
