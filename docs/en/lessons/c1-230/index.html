C1 · Advanced · lesson 230

Advanced cleft sentences

At B2 you met “What I need is…” and “It was … that”. At C1 we go further: negative and contrastive clefts, cleft questions, “All I want is…”, “What happened was…”, reversed clefts (“That's what I mean”), and above all knowing when English does not need a cleft at all.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level C1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Build it-clefts with a noun, an adverbial or a clause (It was only when…, It's because… that…).
  • Use extended wh-clefts: What happened was…, What we did was (to)…, All I…, The reason why… is that…
  • Handle contrastive and negative clefts (It's not that…, it's that…; It wasn't X but Y who…) and cleft questions (What is it that…?).
  • Know when not to use a cleft: English often uses sentence stress instead.

Review: what a cleft sentence does

A cleft sentence splits a simple sentence into two parts to highlight one element. “Amina found the error” can become “It was Amina who found the error” (Amina, not someone else) or “What Amina found was an error” (the focus is on what she found).

An it-cleft highlights a specific element, often in contrast. A wh-cleft first presents the known topic (What I need…) and puts the new information at the end, after “is / was”.

  • It was the finance team that spotted the problem, not the auditors.
    A contrastive it-cleft correcting an assumption.
  • What surprised me most was the silence.
    A wh-cleft: the new information (the silence) comes last.

Advanced it-clefts: adverbials, clauses, negation

You can highlight much more than a noun: a time or place phrase, a because-clause, or an expression with only or not until.

“It's not until… that” and “It was only when… that” are very idiomatic ways to say “only at that moment”.

The contrastive structure “It's not (that)…, it's (that)…” corrects an interpretation: “It's not that I don't like him; it's that I don't trust him.”

Element highlightedExample
TimeIt was in 2019 that the company moved to Douala.
PlaceIt's in the details that the problems appear.
CauseIt's because of the strike that the flights were cancelled.
Only / not untilIt wasn't until I moved abroad that I understood my parents.
ContrastIt wasn't the price but the service that annoyed customers.
CorrectionIt's not that she's lazy; it's that she's overwhelmed.
  • It was only when the lights went out that we realised how late it was.
    “Only when… that”: very natural in English.
  • It's not that I don't want to help. It's that I genuinely can't this week.
    Correcting a misunderstanding.

Extended wh-clefts

Wh-clefts go beyond “What I need is…”. You can highlight a whole action with “What + subject + did was (to) + base verb”: “What we did was (to) call the police”. The “to” is optional.

“What happened was (that)…” introduces a story; “All (that) I want is…” means “the only thing I want is…”; “The reason (why)… is that…”, “The thing (that)… is…”, “The place where…”, “The person who…”, “The last thing…” work in the same way.

Agreement: “be” is usually singular (What we need is more nurses). With a plural complement, the plural is possible in careful writing (What they sell are spare parts), but the singular is common.

StructureExample
What + subject + do / did + is / was + (to) verbWhat she did was (to) rewrite the whole report.
What happened was (that)…What happened was that the server crashed during the update.
All (that) + subject + verb + is…All I want is a quiet weekend.
The reason (why)… is that…The reason why I'm calling is that your parcel has arrived.
The last thing… is…The last thing we need is another delay.
The person who / The place where…The person who helped me most was my aunt.
  • What I'd suggest is that we postpone the launch.
    A wh-cleft for a cautious proposal in a meeting.
  • All we can do now is wait.
    “All … is” + base verb: only wait.

Reversed clefts and cleft questions

A reversed wh-cleft puts the reference word first: “That's what I mean”, “This is where we disagree”, “That's why she left”, “Here's what we'll do”. Very frequent in speech, they summarise or announce.

Cleft questions add insistence to a question: “Was it you who called?”, “Who was it that called?”, “What is it that you want?”, “Why is it that prices keep rising?”.

  • That's exactly what I was trying to say.
    Reversed cleft: it refers back to the previous idea.
  • What is it that you find so difficult?
    Cleft question: what exactly do you find difficult?
  • This is where the plan starts to fail.
    Reversed cleft with where.

Register, pronouns and agreement

In “It is I who am responsible”, the subject pronoun and verb agreement are very formal. Everyday English says “It's me who's responsible” (or “It was me who…”).

In it-clefts, “who” is preferred for people and “that” for everything else; “that” is possible for people in speech. Do not use “which” when the highlighted element is an adverbial: “It was in May that…”, never “in May which”.

When not to cleft: English and sentence stress

Some languages, such as French, use “it is… who / that” structures constantly because their sentence stress is fixed. English can move the stress instead: “I called” (with stress on I) is often enough. Too many clefts make English heavy and translated.

Keep clefts for writing (where there is no intonation) and for real contrasts. In speech, try stress first: “JOHN broke it, not me”.

MeaningHeavy or wrongNatural English
I am the one who called.It's me who has called.I called. (stress on I) / It was me who called.
I have lived here since 2019.It's since 2019 that I live here.I've lived here since 2019.
The thing I need is a break.What I need, it's a break.What I need is a break.
This is the reason I left.It's for that that I left.That's why I left.

The formula

It + be + element (noun, adverbial, clause) + that / who… · What / All / The reason why + clause + be + new information · That's what / where / why…

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • In an it-cleft, the main stress falls on the highlighted element: “It was AMINA who found it.”
  • In a wh-cleft, the first part has a fall-rise (topic) and the main stress comes at the end: “What I need | is a BREAK.”
  • “It's not that…”: stress “not”, then a strong contrast on the second part: “it's that I'm TIRED.”
  • “That's why” /ðæts ˈwaɪ/ is said as one block; in “What happened was” /wɒt ˈhæpənd wəz/, “was” is reduced.
  • “It wasn't until”: /ɪt ˈwɒzənt ənˈtɪl/, with “until” stressed on the second syllable.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ What I need, it's a holiday.✓ What I need is a holiday.No extra “it”: the verb be follows the wh-clause directly.
✗ It's since 2019 that I live in Montreal.✓ I've lived in Montreal since 2019.Use the present perfect, with no cleft.
✗ It's for that that she resigned.✓ That's why she resigned.The English structure is “That's why”.
✗ What we did is to called the bank.✓ What we did was (to) call the bank.Consistent tense (was) and the base verb after (to).
✗ It was in May which the shop opened.✓ It was in May that the shop opened.With a highlighted adverbial, only “that”.
✗ This is the reason why that I came.✓ This is the reason (why) I came.No “that” after why.
✗ It's not that I don't like it, but that it's expensive.✓ It's not that I don't like it; it's that it's expensive.The structure is “It's not that…; it's that…”.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Dakar, two partners in a small travel agency discuss a fall in bookings.

Ousmane

Bookings are down 20 %. Is it the new website that's putting people off?
A cleft question.

Claire

I don't think so. It's not that the site is bad; it's that nobody can find it on Google.
A corrective cleft.

Ousmane

So what happened was that we lost our old links when we moved?
“What happened was” introduces the explanation.

Claire

Exactly. It was only when I checked the statistics that I saw the problem.
“It was only when… that”.

Ousmane

Then the last thing we need is to spend more on advertising.
“The last thing… is”.

Claire

Right. What I'd suggest is that we hire someone to fix the search settings.
A cautious proposal with a wh-cleft.

Ousmane

That's exactly what I was going to say. How much would it cost?
A reversed cleft.

Claire

All we'd need is about a week of work. That's why it's worth doing now.
“All… is” and “That's why”.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. It-cleft or wh-cleft?

Say what type of cleft sentence it is.

  1. It was my brother who told me.
  2. What annoys me is the noise.
  3. That's where we met.
  4. Who was it that left the door open?
Show the answers
  1. it-cleft
  2. wh-cleft
  3. reversed wh-cleft
  4. it-cleft question

2. Highlight it

Rewrite the sentence, highlighting the words in brackets with “It … that / who”.

  1. The company moved to Kigali [in 2021].
  2. [The manager] cancelled the meeting.
  3. We understood the risk [only when we read the report].
  4. I realised how tired I was [not until I sat down].
Show the answers
  1. It was in 2021 that the company moved to Kigali.
  2. It was the manager who cancelled the meeting.
  3. It was only when we read the report that we understood the risk.
  4. It wasn't until I sat down that I realised how tired I was.

3. Wh-clefts

Rewrite with the beginning given.

  1. We called a plumber. (What we did…)
  2. I only want some peace. (All…)
  3. The server crashed during the update. (What happened…)
  4. I'm calling because I've lost my card. (The reason why…)
  5. We really don't need another meeting. (The last thing…)
Show the answers
  1. What we did was (to) call a plumber.
  2. All I want is some peace.
  3. What happened was that the server crashed during the update.
  4. The reason why I'm calling is that I've lost my card.
  5. The last thing we need is another meeting.

4. Correct the mistake

Correct each sentence.

  1. What I want, it's a new job.
  2. It's since March that she works here.
  3. It was in Paris which they met.
  4. This is the reason why that I left.
Show the answers
  1. What I want is a new job.
  2. She has worked / has been working here since March.
  3. It was in Paris that they met.
  4. This is the reason (why) I left.

5. Natural or heavy?

Rewrite in the most natural way (use a cleft only if it helps).

  1. It's for that reason that he left.
  2. I'm not refusing. The problem is I don't have time. (It's not that…)
Show the answers
  1. That's why he left.
  2. It's not that I'm refusing; it's that I don't have time.

6. Your turn

Write a short paragraph (4 sentences) explaining a misunderstanding at work. Use three clefts of different types.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. It wasn't the client who changed the deadline; it was our own sales team. What happened was that they promised a date without checking with us. It was only when the client called on Monday that we found out. That's why I'd like us to agree on a clear procedure.
Key points
  • It-clefts: a noun, an adverbial or a clause (It was only when… that…, It's because… that…, It wasn't until… that…).
  • Wh-clefts: What happened was…, What we did was (to) + verb, All I want is…, The reason why… is that…, The last thing… is…
  • Reversed clefts (That's why / what / where…) and cleft questions (What is it that…?) are very common in speech.
  • Don't overuse clefts: no “What I need, it's…”, no “It's since… that”; in speech, stress is often enough.
