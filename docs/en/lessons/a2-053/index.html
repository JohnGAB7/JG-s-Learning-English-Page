Just, already, yet

“Just”, “already” and “yet” are mostly used with the present perfect to show how an action relates to now. They are essential when you check what is done and what is not done yet.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Say that something happened a very short time ago with “have just + past participle”.
  • Use already in positive sentences and yet in negatives and questions.
  • Put each word in the right place in the sentence.
  • Know the American use with the past simple and the meaning of “still haven't”.

Just: a very short time ago

“Just” shows that an action happened a very short time ago. It goes between have / has and the past participle.

The news is fresh, and the result is often important now.

  • I've just finished my report.
    A few minutes ago.
  • She's just left the office.
    She isn't here now — you missed her.
  • The bus has just arrived. Let's run!
    Result: we must hurry.

Already: sooner than expected

“Already” shows that an action is finished, often sooner than expected. In positive sentences it goes between have / has and the past participle; it can also go at the end for emphasis.

In a question, “already” shows surprise: “Have you already finished?” (That was quick!).

  • I've already paid the bill.
    It's done; you don't need to pay.
  • We've already seen this film.
    We don't want to see it again.
  • Have you already eaten? It's only eleven!
    Surprise: it's early.

Yet: in questions and negatives

“Yet” goes at the end of the sentence. In a question, it asks if something expected has happened. In a negative, it means the action has not happened, but we expect it to happen.

We do not use it in simple positive sentences with this meaning. A typical short answer is “Not yet.”

Type of sentenceExampleMeaning
questionHave you done your homework yet?Is it done? I expect it.
negativeI haven't done it yet.Not done, but I will do it.
short answerNot yet.No, but soon.
questionHas the parcel arrived yet?We are waiting for it.

Positions: a summary

Just and already: between the helper verb and the past participle (middle of the sentence). Yet: at the end of the sentence.

“Still” can be added to show impatience: “I still haven't received my visa” (I expected it before now). It goes before haven't / hasn't.

WordSentencePosition
justHe has just called.have + just + participle
alreadyThey've already left.have + already + participle
yet (question)Have they left yet?end of sentence
yet (negative)They haven't left yet.end of sentence
stillThey still haven't left.before haven't

American English: the past simple

In American English, people very often use the past simple with just, already and yet: “I just ate”, “Did you eat yet?”, “I already told him”. This is correct in the USA and in English-speaking Canada.

In British English, the present perfect is still the norm: “I've just eaten”, “Have you eaten yet?”. This site uses the British form, but you should understand both.

  • I just saw your message. (US)
    Past simple with just.
  • I've just seen your message. (UK)
    Present perfect with just — same meaning.

The formula

have / has + just / already + past participle · haven't + past participle … yet · Have … + past participle … yet?

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “just” is /dʒʌst/: j as in “jean”, u as in “cup”. Before a consonant, the final t often disappears: I've just seen /aɪv dʒʌs ˈsiːn/.
  • “already” is /ɔːlˈredi/: stress on the second syllable.
  • “yet” is /jet/, with the y sound of “yes”. Do not say “jet” /dʒet/.
  • “Not yet” has a falling tone, often with the stress on yet: not YET.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

The day before moving house in Lyon, Awa and her partner Hugo check their to-do list.

Awa

Have you called the moving company yet?
yet in a question.

Hugo

Yes, I've just called them. They're coming at eight tomorrow.
just = a moment ago.

Awa

Great. I've already packed the kitchen.
already = done, early.

Hugo

Already? That was fast! Have you packed the books yet?
Surprise, then a yet question.

Awa

No, not yet. I haven't found enough boxes.
Not yet.

Hugo

I've just bought ten more. They're in the car.
Recent action with a result now.

Awa

Perfect. Oh, and we still haven't told the bank our new address.
still haven't = impatience.

Hugo

Don't worry, I'll do it online tonight.
will for an instant decision.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Just, already or yet?

Complete with just, already or yet.

  1. Have you finished ___?
  2. I've ___ eaten, thanks. I'm not hungry.
  3. She's ___ gone out. You missed her by one minute.
  4. We haven't decided ___.
  5. The film has ___ started — sit down quickly!
Show the answers
  1. yet
  2. already
  3. just
  4. yet
  5. just

2. Put the word in

Rewrite the sentence with the word in brackets in the right place.

  1. I've sent the email. (just)
  2. Has the taxi arrived? (yet)
  3. They've booked the hotel. (already)
  4. He hasn't called. (yet)
  5. We haven't received the results. (still)
Show the answers
  1. I've just sent the email.
  2. Has the taxi arrived yet?
  3. They've already booked the hotel.
  4. He hasn't called yet.
  5. We still haven't received the results.

3. Check the list

Write a present perfect sentence with the word given (✓ = done, ✗ = not done).

  1. buy the tickets ✓ (already)
  2. clean the flat ✗ (yet)
  3. call the doctor ✓ two minutes ago (just)
  4. you / pay the rent? (yet)
Show the answers
  1. I've already bought the tickets.
  2. I haven't cleaned the flat yet.
  3. I've just called the doctor.
  4. Have you paid the rent yet?

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. I've done yet my homework.
  2. She hasn't yet arrived.
  3. We have just arrive.
  4. Have you already met him last week?
Show the answers
  1. I've already done my homework.
  2. She hasn't arrived yet.
  3. We have just arrived.
  4. Did you meet him last week?

5. American to British

Rewrite these American English sentences in British English (present perfect).

  1. I just got home.
  2. Did you book the tickets yet?
  3. She didn't reply yet.
Show the answers
  1. I've just got home.
  2. Have you booked the tickets yet?
  3. She hasn't replied yet.

6. Your turn

Check your day or week in five sentences with just, already, yet and still.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. It's Thursday evening. I've already finished my big project at work. I've just cooked dinner for my family. I haven't called my mother yet — I'll do it after dinner. And I still haven't started the book my friend gave me!
Key points
  • have just + past participle = a very short time ago (I've just arrived).
  • already (sooner than expected): between have and the participle, in positive sentences.
  • yet at the end: questions and negatives; Not yet.
  • still + haven't = not yet, and I'm impatient.
  • American English often uses the past simple (I just ate, Did you eat yet?).
