B2 · Upper intermediate · lesson 154

Reported speech

Telling people what someone said, asked or ordered is essential in daily life and at work: meeting notes, passing on a message, telling a story. At B2 you need the whole system: tense changes, time and place words, questions and orders, and knowing when nothing needs to change.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Apply backshift (moving tenses back) to all tenses and modal verbs.
  • Know when backshift is not necessary (things that are still true, a reporting verb in the present).
  • Change pronouns and time and place words (the day before, the following week, there).
  • Report questions, requests and orders with the correct word order.

Say or tell?

“Say” and “tell” have similar meanings, but different patterns. “Tell” is always followed by the person you speak to: tell somebody (that)… “Say” is used without the person, or with “to”: say (to somebody) (that)…

“That” is optional after say and tell, especially in speech: “She said (that) she was tired.”

Fixed expressions with tell: tell the truth, tell a lie, tell a story, tell a joke, tell the time, tell the difference. With say: say hello, say goodbye, say sorry, say a few words.

  • She said (that) she was tired.
    say + clause.
  • She told me (that) she was tired.
    tell + person + clause.
  • He said to me that he was sorry.
    say to + person: correct but less common.
  • He told us a funny story.
    tell a story: fixed expression.

Backshift: moving the tense back

When the reporting verb is in the past (said, told, asked), we usually move the tense one step back into the past, because the words were spoken before the moment we report them.

The past perfect and would, could, should, might and ought to do not change: they cannot go further back.

“Must” can stay “must” or become “had to” when it expresses obligation.

Direct speechReported speechReported example
present simple: “I live in Lille.”past simpleHe said he lived in Lille.
present continuous: “I'm working.”past continuousShe said she was working.
present perfect: “I've finished.”past perfectHe said he had finished.
past simple: “I saw her.”past perfect (or past simple)She said she had seen her.
will: “I'll call you.”wouldHe said he would call me.
can: “I can swim.”couldShe said she could swim.
may: “It may rain.”mightHe said it might rain.
must: “I must go.”had to / mustShe said she had to go.
would, could, should, might, past perfectno changeHe said he would love to come.

When not to change the tense

If the reporting verb is in the present, the present perfect or the future, nothing changes: “She says she's tired”, “He's told me he's leaving”.

If what was said is still true when you report it (a general truth, a situation that continues), backshift is optional: “He said he lives in Lille” (he still does). Backshift if the situation has changed, or if you want to keep a distance.

When you repeat words that were just spoken, you often keep the original tense: “Sorry, what did you say?” – “I said I'm hungry!”

The past simple can stay in the past simple when the order of events is clear: “She said she arrived on Monday” is common in speech.

  • Ama says she can't come tonight.
    Reporting verb in the present: no change.
  • Our teacher said the Earth goes round the Sun.
    General truth: “goes” is possible.
  • Tom told me he lives near the station, so we can walk to his place.
    Still true and relevant now.
  • Tom told me he lived near the station, but he's moved since then.
    No longer true: backshift.

Pronouns, time and place

Pronouns and possessives change according to who is speaking: “I'll help you,” said Marie → Marie said she would help me.

Time and place words change only if the time or the place has changed. If you report something on the same day, “today” stays “today”.

  • “I'll see you tomorrow,” Paul said. → Paul said he would see me the next day.
    Reported several days later.
  • “We arrived here two days ago,” they said. → They said they had arrived there two days before.
    Tense, place and time words all change.

Reporting questions

A reported question is not a question any more: use statement word order (subject + verb), remove do / does / did and the question mark.

Yes / no questions: if or whether. Wh- questions: keep the question word (where, why, how much…).

Reporting verbs: ask, want to know, wonder. You saw this at B1 (lesson 105); at B2 you must apply it automatically, together with backshift.

  • “Where do you work?” → She asked me where I worked.
    No “did”; subject + verb.
  • “Have you seen my keys?” → He asked if I had seen his keys.
    Yes / no question: if.
  • “Will you be there?” → They wanted to know whether I would be there.
    whether: a little more formal.

Reporting orders and requests

For an order: tell + person + to-infinitive. For a polite request: ask + person + to-infinitive.

In the negative, “not” goes before “to”: “He told me not to worry.” Avoid “He told me to not worry” in careful writing, and never say “He told me don't worry”.

Other verbs show the speaker's intention more precisely (advise, warn, remind, invite, suggest…): see lesson 155.

  • “Sit down, please.” → She asked us to sit down.
    Polite request: ask.
  • “Don't touch the screen!” → He told the children not to touch the screen.
    Negative order: not to.
  • “Could you send me the file?” → My manager asked me to send her the file.
    A polite question often becomes ask + to.

The formula

said (that) / told + person (that) + tense moved back; asked + if / wh- + subject + verb; told / asked + person + (not) to + infinitive

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “said” is /sed/ and “says” is /sez/, not /seɪz/.
  • “that” is weak, /ðət/, and often left out in speech: “She said she'd come” /ʃi sed ʃiːd ˈkʌm/.
  • The contraction 'd (would / had) is very common: “He said he'd finished” (= had), “He said he'd call” (= would). The next verb tells you which: a past participle → had; a base form → would.
  • A reported question has the falling intonation of a statement: “She asked where I lived ↘”, with no rise at the end.
  • “whether” /ˈweðə/ sounds exactly like “weather”.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Lyon, Inès tells her flatmate Chloé about her phone interview with a Canadian company.

Chloé

So? What did they say?
A direct question.

Inès

The recruiter said she had read my CV and that she was very impressed.
Backshift: “I've read” → had read; “I'm” → was.

Chloé

That's great! Did she ask you anything difficult?
A direct question.

Inès

She asked me why I wanted to leave France and whether I had ever worked in English.
Two reported questions: wh- and whether.

Chloé

And what did you tell her?
tell + person.

Inès

I told her I'd spent a year in Ottawa and that I loved the job market there.
'd = had; here → there.

Chloé

So when will you know?
A direct question.

Inès

She said they would call me back the following week, and she told me not to worry about the visa.
will → would; next week → the following week; negative order.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Move the tense back

Give the verb form in reported speech (the reporting verb is “said”).

  1. “I work in a bank.” → He said he ___ in a bank.
  2. “I'm leaving.” → She said she ___.
  3. “We've finished.” → They said they ___.
  4. “I'll call you.” → He said he ___ me.
Show the answers
  1. worked
  2. was leaving
  3. had finished
  4. would call

2. Report statements

Put into reported speech. You are reporting several days later, in a different place.

  1. “I can't come to the party,” said Marc.
  2. “We're moving to Montreal next month,” they told me.
  3. “I saw your sister yesterday,” Awa said.
  4. “You must be on time tomorrow,” the manager told us.
  5. “I've never been to Brussels,” she said.
  6. “The meeting may be cancelled,” he said.
Show the answers
  1. Marc said (that) he couldn't come to the party.
  2. They told me (that) they were moving to Montreal the following month.
  3. Awa said (that) she had seen my sister the day before (the previous day).
  4. The manager told us (that) we had to (must) be on time the next day.
  5. She said (that) she had never been to Brussels.
  6. He said (that) the meeting might be cancelled.

3. Questions, requests and orders

Put into reported speech.

  1. “Where do you live?” she asked me.
  2. “Are you coming tonight?” he asked me.
  3. “Why did you leave early?” my boss asked me.
  4. “Please close the window,” she said to me.
  5. “Don't be late!” Mum told us.
Show the answers
  1. She asked me where I lived.
  2. He asked me if (whether) I was coming that night.
  3. My boss asked me why I had left early.
  4. She asked me to close the window.
  5. Mum told us not to be late.

4. Correct the mistakes

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. She said me that she was busy.
  2. He asked me where did I work.
  3. They told to us to wait outside.
  4. She asked if was I hungry.
Show the answers
  1. She told me (that) she was busy. (or: She said (that) she was busy.)
  2. He asked me where I worked.
  3. They told us to wait outside.
  4. She asked if I was hungry.

5. Change or no change?

Report each sentence and say whether the tense must change.

  1. Your friend says right now: “I'm tired.” Report it now, with “says”.
  2. Last year the teacher said: “The Earth goes round the Sun.”
  3. Two years ago Tom said: “I live in Lille.” He lives in Liège now.
  4. Yesterday Ana said: “I would love to visit Canada.”
Show the answers
  1. She says (that) she's tired. No change: the reporting verb is in the present.
  2. The teacher said (that) the Earth goes (went) round the Sun. Change optional: it is still true.
  3. Tom said (that) he lived in Lille. Change needed: it is no longer true.
  4. Ana said (that) she would love to visit Canada. No change: “would” does not move back.

6. Your turn

Report a recent conversation (with a colleague, a friend, a shop assistant…) in 4–5 sentences: a statement, a question, a request and a promise.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. Yesterday my colleague Samuel told me he was exhausted because he had worked all weekend. He asked me whether I could help him with a report. I said I was busy that afternoon but that I would help him the next morning. He asked me not to tell our manager, and I promised I wouldn't.
Key points
  • say (that)… / tell + person (that)…: “She told me”, never “She said me”.
  • After a past reporting verb, move the tense one step back; would, could, should, might and the past perfect don't change.
  • No change if the reporting verb is in the present; optional change if the information is still true.
  • Reported questions: if / whether or a question word + subject + verb, with no do / did and no question mark.
  • Orders and requests: tell / ask + person + (not) to + infinitive.
