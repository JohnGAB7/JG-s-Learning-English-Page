I wish / if only

We use I wish and if only to say that we would like reality to be different, now or in the past. They work like the conditionals: we move one tense back to show distance from reality.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Express a wish about the present with wish / if only + past simple (or could).
  • Express regret about the past with wish / if only + past perfect.
  • Tell the difference between wish (unreal situation), hope (possible situation) and want.
  • Use if only for a stronger, more emotional wish.

Wishes about the present: wish + past simple

For a present situation that we would like to be different, the verb after wish goes into the past simple. The past tense doesn't mean past time: it shows that this is not reality, as in the second conditional.

The pattern is: subject + wish + subject + past simple. That is possible but often left out (I wish that I had…).

With be, we use were with every subject in careful English (I wish I were, I wish it were). Was is common in speech.

RealityWishMeaning
I don't have a car.I wish I had a car.I would like to have one.
I live far from my family.I wish I lived closer to my family.Distance makes me sad.
It's Monday.I wish it were (was) Friday.I want the weekend.
She's always late.I wish she weren't always late.Her lateness annoys me.
It's raining.I wish it wasn't raining.Right now.

Wish + could: an ability you don't have

To talk about an ability or possibility you don't have, use wish + could + base verb (could = past of can).

This is very common for languages, skills and blocked situations: “I wish I could stay longer.”

  • I wish I could speak Spanish.
    I can't speak it.
  • I wish I could come to your wedding, but I'll be in Canada.
    It isn't possible for me.
  • My son wishes he could drive.
    He isn't able to drive yet.

Regrets about the past: wish + past perfect

To regret something that happened (or didn't happen), put the verb in the past perfect (had + participle). It's the same step back as in the third conditional.

“I wish I hadn't said that” = I said it, and I'm sorry. We can also use regret + -ing (I regret saying that) or should have + participle (I should have listened).

  • I wish I had studied harder at school.
    I didn't study hard; I regret it.
  • She wishes she hadn't sold her car.
    She sold it; now she's sorry.
  • I wish I'd taken more photos in Kribi.
    I'd taken = I had taken.

If only: the strong version

If only means the same as I wish and uses the same tenses, but it is more emotional, often an exclamation: “If only I had more time!”

If only is often used alone, without a main clause, with an exclamation mark. It can also start a conditional sentence: “If only you had told me, I could have helped.”

  • If only I knew the answer!
    Present: I don't know it.
  • If only we hadn't missed the train!
    Past: we missed it.

Wish, hope or want?

Hope = a possible, realistic wish; it takes a normal tense (present or will): “I hope you pass your exam.” Wish = an unreal situation or one that can't be changed: “I wish I were younger.”

Want + to or want + someone + to: a simple desire (I want to leave, I want you to stay). We don't normally say “I wish you to…” in everyday English.

Wish + noun appears in fixed greetings: “I wish you a happy birthday”, “We wish you good luck”, “Best wishes”. To go further (wish + would to complain about someone's behaviour: “I wish you would stop”), see lesson 159.

VerbMeaningExample
hope + present / willpossible wishI hope it doesn't rain tomorrow.
wish + past simpleunreal presentI wish it weren't raining.
wish + past perfectpast regretI wish I had brought an umbrella.
wish + noun (greetings)good wishesI wish you a merry Christmas.

The formula

wish / if only + past simple (unreal present) · wish / if only + past perfect (past regret)

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • wish /wɪʃ/: short i as in “it”, not /wiːʃ/. wishes /ˈwɪʃɪz/ has two syllables.
  • If only /ɪf ˈəʊnli/: stress on only, and often on the verb too: “If ONLY I'd LISTENED!”
  • In “I wish I'd known”, the 'd is very light but changes everything: without it, the sentence is about the present.
  • In “I wish I were”, were is weak /wə/: /aɪ ˈwɪʃ aɪ wə/.
  • A sigh or falling intonation often goes with regret: “I wish I could ↘.”

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ I wish I have more time.✓ I wish I had more time.A wish about the present: past simple after wish.
✗ I wish I can swim.✓ I wish I could swim.Can becomes could after wish.
✗ I wish I didn't say that yesterday.✓ I wish I hadn't said that yesterday.A regret about the past: past perfect.
✗ I wish you pass your exam.✓ I hope you pass your exam.A possible wish: hope, not wish.
✗ I wish I would be taller.✓ I wish I were taller.No would when talking about yourself: were / past simple.
✗ I wish you to come to my party.✓ I'd like you to come to my party. / I hope you can come to my party.Wish + someone + to is very formal and rare; use would like or hope.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Montreal, in February. Kevin, who came from Libreville a year ago, talks to his colleague Julie outside the office.

Kevin

It's minus twenty again. I wish it were summer!
Unreal present: were.

Julie

You'll get used to it. Do you miss Gabon?
Get used to = become familiar with.

Kevin

A lot. I wish my parents lived closer. I can't visit them often.
Wish + past simple.

Julie

I hope you can go back this summer.
Hope: a real possibility.

Kevin

Me too. And I wish I'd bought a warmer coat before the winter!
Past regret: past perfect.

Julie

If only you'd asked me! I know a great shop.
If only + past perfect.

Kevin

Really? I wish I could go there now.
Wish + could.

Julie

Come on, it's open until nine. Let's go!
Let's = a suggestion.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Present or past?

Say if the sentence is a wish about the present (P) or a regret about the past (R).

  1. I wish I had a bigger flat.
  2. I wish I had listened to my teacher.
  3. If only she were here!
  4. He wishes he hadn't moved to the city.
Show the answers
  1. P
  2. R
  3. P
  4. R

2. Complete (present)

Complete with the correct form of the verb for a wish about the present.

  1. I don't have a car. I wish I ___ a car.
  2. I can't swim. I wish I ___ swim.
  3. My flat is very small. I wish it ___ bigger.
  4. We live in a noisy street. I wish we ___ in a quiet street.
  5. He doesn't speak English. He wishes he ___ English.
Show the answers
  1. had
  2. could
  3. were (was)
  4. lived
  5. spoke (could speak)

3. Complete (past)

Complete to express a regret about the past.

  1. I didn't go to the concert. I wish I ___ to the concert.
  2. She said something rude. She wishes she ___ it.
  3. We didn't take any photos. If only we ___ some photos!
  4. I ate too much cake. I wish I ___ so much.
Show the answers
  1. had gone
  2. hadn't said
  3. had taken
  4. hadn't eaten

4. Wish or hope?

Choose wish or hope, in the correct form.

  1. I ___ you have a good trip to Dakar.
  2. I ___ I were on holiday right now.
  3. We ___ the weather will be nice tomorrow.
  4. She ___ she had studied medicine.
Show the answers
  1. hope
  2. wish
  3. hope
  4. wishes

5. Rewrite with wish or if only

Rewrite each sentence with the words in brackets.

  1. I can't play the guitar. (I wish)
  2. I didn't call her, and I'm sorry. (I wish)
  3. It's raining, and I hate it. (If only)
  4. She left her job, and now she regrets it. (She wishes)
Show the answers
  1. I wish I could play the guitar.
  2. I wish I had called her.
  3. If only it wasn't (weren't) raining!
  4. She wishes she hadn't left her job.

6. Your turn

Write 4 true sentences: two wishes about your life now (wish + past simple / could) and two regrets about your past (wish / if only + past perfect).

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. I wish I had more time to read. I wish I could speak Dutch, because many of my colleagues in Brussels speak it. I wish I had started learning English earlier. If only I hadn't sold my old guitar!
Key points
  • Unreal present: wish / if only + past simple (I wish I had…, I wish it were…).
  • An ability you don't have: wish + could (I wish I could swim).
  • Past regret: wish / if only + past perfect (I wish I had listened).
  • A possible, realistic wish: hope + present / will, not wish.
  • If only = wish, but stronger and more emotional.
