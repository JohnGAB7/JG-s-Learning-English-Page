Can / can’t (ability, permission)

“Can” is a very common modal verb. We use it to say what we are able to do, to ask for or give permission, and to ask for help. Its form is very simple, but learners often add “to”, “do” or an s that are not there.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Make sentences with can and can't for every subject, with no “to” and no “-s”.
  • Ask questions with Can…? and answer them (Yes, I can / No, I can't).
  • Talk about your abilities (I can swim) and say how well (well, a little, not at all).
  • Ask for, give or refuse permission (Can I…? You can't…).

The form: can + base verb

“Can” is a modal verb: it never changes. No s with he, she, it (“she can”, never “she cans”), and the next verb is in the base form, with no “to”: “I can swim”, never “I can to swim”.

Negative: “cannot” (one word in careful writing) or “can't” (short form, the most common). Question: put “can” before the subject, with no “do”: “Can you swim?”

Short answers: “Yes, I can.” / “No, I can't.” Don't repeat the main verb.

Negativesubject + can't / cannot + verbShe can't drive.

Ability: what you are able to do

“Can” is for skills you learn (swim, drive, read) and for things you are physically able to do. Say “I can swim”, not “I know swim” or “I know to swim”.

To say how well: “very well”, “well”, “a little”, “not very well”, “can't… at all” (= not in any way). Careful: say “well” (adverb), not “good”.

For languages, say “I can speak English” or simply “I speak English”.

  • I can swim, but not very well.
    How well: not very well.
  • My grandmother can't use a computer at all.
    not at all = zero.
  • Can you play the piano? — Yes, a little.
    a little = not much.
In the past, “can” becomes “could” (I could swim when I was five). For other tenses, use “be able to” (lesson 108).

Permission: Can I…? You can / can't

To ask for permission: “Can I…?”. To be more polite, say “Could I…?” (lesson 57), and in formal English “May I…?”.

To give or refuse permission, or talk about a rule: “You can…” (it's allowed), “You can't…” (it's not allowed).

Natural answers: “Yes, of course.”, “Sure.”, “Go ahead.”; to say no politely: “Sorry, you can't.” or “I'm afraid not.”

  • Can I open the window? — Yes, of course.
    Asking for permission.
  • You can't park here. It's for buses.
    A rule.
  • You can use my phone.
    Giving permission.

Requests, offers and possibility

“Can you…?” asks someone to do something for you: “Can you help me?”. “Can I…?” can also offer help: “Can I help you?”.

“Can” also says what is possible: “You can buy tickets online.” Here “you” means “people in general”.

  • Can you close the door, please?
    A request.
  • Can I help you? — Yes, I'm looking for the station.
    An offer (in a shop, in the street).
  • You can see the sea from my window.
    Possibility.

Common traps

Never add “do”: “Do you can…?” and “I don't can…” are wrong. “Can” makes questions and negatives on its own.

In speech, “can” and “can't” sound similar. The difference is mostly the vowel and the stress (see pronunciation). If you are not sure, listen to the short answer, which is always clear: “Yes, I can” / “No, I can't”.

WrongRightWhy
I can to swim.I can swim.no “to” after can
He cans swim.He can swim.no s
Do you can swim?Can you swim?no “do”
I don't can swim.I can't swim.no “do”

The formula

subject + can / can't + verb (no to) · Can + subject + verb…? · Yes, I can. / No, I can't.

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • In a positive sentence, “can” is weak and quick: “I can swim” = /aɪ kən ˈswɪm/. Stress the main verb.
  • “can't” is never weak: /kɑːnt/ in British English (like “aunt” with a k), /kænt/ in American English. Stress can't AND the verb: “I CAN'T SWIM”.
  • In a short answer, “can” is strong: “Yes, I CAN” /kæn/.
  • In American English, you hardly hear the t in “can't”; listen to the length and strength of the vowel.
  • “Can I…?” is linked: /kənaɪ/. The question goes up at the end.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Quebec City, Moussa has an interview for a summer job in a hotel. Mrs Tremblay asks him questions.

Mrs Tremblay

So, Moussa, can you speak English and French?
A question about ability.

Moussa

Yes, I can. And I can speak a little Spanish too.
Short answer; “a little”.

Mrs Tremblay

Very good. Can you drive?
Can + subject + verb.

Moussa

No, I can't. But I can ride a bike!
can't is strong; ride a bike.

Mrs Tremblay

That's OK. Can you work at the weekend?
Possibility.

Moussa

Yes, I can work on Saturdays and Sundays. Can I ask a question?
Asking for permission.

Mrs Tremblay

Of course. Go ahead.
Giving permission.

Moussa

Can staff eat at the hotel restaurant?
staff = the people who work there.

Mrs Tremblay

Yes, you can have lunch there for free. But you can't use your phone at reception.
A permission and a rule.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Can or can't?

Complete with can or can't.

  1. Fish ___ swim.
  2. A baby ___ drive a car.
  3. Birds ___ fly.
  4. I'm sorry, I ___ come to your party. I'm at work.
  5. You ___ smoke in the hospital. It's not allowed.
  6. You ___ pay by card here. We accept all cards.
Show the answers
  1. can
  2. can't
  3. can
  4. can't
  5. can't
  6. can

2. Questions and short answers

Write the question with can and the short answer.

  1. you / swim? (yes)
  2. she / cook? (no)
  3. they / speak French? (yes)
  4. I / sit here? (yes)
  5. he / play the piano? (no)
Show the answers
  1. Can you swim? — Yes, I can.
  2. Can she cook? — No, she can't.
  3. Can they speak French? — Yes, they can.
  4. Can I sit here? — Yes, you can.
  5. Can he play the piano? — No, he can't.

3. Ability or permission?

Does “can” show ability or permission?

  1. Can I sit here?
  2. She can speak four languages.
  3. You can't take photos in the museum.
  4. Can you play the guitar?
Show the answers
  1. permission
  2. ability
  3. permission (a rule: it's not allowed)
  4. ability

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. I know to swim.
  2. Do you can help me?
  3. He don't can cook at all.
  4. Can I to borrow your pen?
  5. She cans park here.
Show the answers
  1. I can swim.
  2. Can you help me?
  3. He can't cook at all.
  4. Can I borrow your pen?
  5. She can park here.

5. Your turn

Write four sentences: two things you can do (with well or a little) and two things you can't do.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. I can speak French very well and I can speak a little English. I can cook a good ndolé. I can't drive, and I can't play any musical instrument at all.
Key points
  • can + base verb, no “to”, no s, no “do”: She can swim. Can she swim? She can't swim.
  • Short answers: Yes, I can. / No, I can't.
  • Ability: I can swim; say how well with well, a little, not at all.
  • Permission and rules: Can I…? You can / You can't…; more polite: Could I…?
  • Pronunciation: weak can /kən/, strong can't /kɑːnt/.
