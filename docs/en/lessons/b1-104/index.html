Reported statements

Reported speech lets you tell someone what another person said without quoting their exact words: “She said she was tired.” It involves three changes: tenses move back, pronouns change, and time and place words change. You need it to tell others about a conversation, a phone call or a meeting.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Use say and tell correctly (tell + person; say with no person, or say to + person).
  • Move tenses back after a past reporting verb (am → was, will → would, have done → had done…).
  • Change pronouns, possessives and time and place expressions (tomorrow → the next day, here → there).
  • Know when you don't need to move tenses back (something still true, a present reporting verb).

Say or tell?

Say and tell have similar meanings, but different patterns. Tell is always followed by the person you speak to (tell me, tell her, tell the manager). Say is not followed directly by a person; if you want to mention the person, use say to.

That after say or tell is optional, especially in speech: “She told me (that) she was busy.”

Some fixed phrases use tell without a person: tell the truth, tell a lie, tell a story, tell a joke, tell the time. See also lesson 74 (say or tell).

VerbPatternExample
saysay (that) + clauseHe said (that) he was hungry.
say tosay to + person (rare in reported speech)He said to me that he was hungry.
telltell + person + (that) + clauseHe told me (that) he was hungry.
tell (phrases)tell the truth / a lie / a storyShe told a funny story.
  • Amadou told his boss that he was ill.
    Tell + person.
  • Amadou said that he was ill.
    Say with no person.

Moving tenses back (backshift)

When the reporting verb is in the past (said, told), the reported verb usually moves one tense back: present becomes past, past becomes past perfect, will becomes would, can becomes could.

The past perfect, would, could, should, might and must (for obligation, often had to) don't change, because they can't move further back.

Direct speechReported speech
“I am tired.” (present simple)She said she was tired.
“I'm working.” (present continuous)She said she was working.
“I've finished.” (present perfect)She said she had finished.
“I lost my keys.” (past simple)She said she had lost her keys. (in speech, also: she lost)
“I will call you.” (will)She said she would call me.
“I can help.” (can)She said she could help.
“I must go.” (must)She said she had to go. / She said she must go.
“I'm going to move.” (going to)She said she was going to move.

Pronouns and possessives

Pronouns and possessives change depending on who is reporting and to whom. If Moussa tells me “I love my job”, I report: “Moussa said he loved his job.” If Aminata tells me “I'll help you”, I report: “Aminata said she would help me.”

It's logical: put yourself in the place of the person who is reporting.

  • “I'm proud of you.” → My father told me he was proud of me.
    I → he; you → me.
  • “We've sold our house.” → They said they had sold their house.
    we → they; our → their.
  • “Your idea is great.” → She told me my idea was great.
    your → my.

Time and place words

If we report the words later or somewhere else, time and place expressions change: now → then / at that moment, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day / the following day, yesterday → the day before / the previous day, here → there, this → that.

If we report on the same day and in the same place, we keep the original words: “She said she'd call me tomorrow” (if tomorrow is still tomorrow).

  • “I'll finish it tomorrow.” → He said he would finish it the next day.
    Reported a week later.
  • “I arrived two days ago.” → She said she had arrived two days before.
    ago → before.

When we don't move tenses back

If the reporting verb is in the present (says, tells, has said), tenses don't change: “She says she's tired” (she is saying it now). This is common when reporting a message, an email or a text you have just read.

If the information is still true when we speak, backshift is optional: “He said he lives in Lyon” (he still does) or “He said he lived in Lyon”. For a general truth, we usually don't move back: “The teacher said that water boils at 100 °C.”

  • Mum says she's coming at six.
    Present reporting verb: no backshift.
  • She told me she's from Garoua.
    Still true: backshift optional.

The formula

said / told + person + (that) + tense moved back (am → was, will → would, have done → had done)

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • said is /sed/ (like “bed”), never /seɪd/. says is /sez/.
  • told /təʊld/: a dark l and the vowel in “no”.
  • The conjunction that is weak /ðət/ and often left out: “She told me (that) she was ill” /ʃi ˈtəʊld mi ʃi wəz ˈɪl/.
  • Contracted 'd (would or had) is common in reported speech: “He said he'd call” (= would), “He said he'd left” (= had). The next word tells you which.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

An office in Lausanne, Monday morning. Nadia tells her colleague Claire about a call she received from a Canadian client on Friday.

Claire

Did Mr Tremblay call back on Friday?
Call back = phone again.

Nadia

Yes. He said he was very happy with our offer.
“I am happy” → he was happy.

Claire

Great! Did he say anything about the price?
Say anything about = mention.

Nadia

He told me the price was fine, but he said they needed the products earlier.
Told me / said: both patterns.

Claire

How much earlier?
A short question.

Nadia

He said they had opened a new shop in Quebec City the week before and they would need everything by March.
Past perfect, the week before, would.

Claire

That's possible. Did he say he would sign?
Will → would.

Nadia

He said he could sign the contract the next day if we agreed. And his assistant's email says she's sending it today!
Can → could; present reporting verb: no backshift.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Say or tell?

Complete with the correct form of say or tell (past simple unless another form is needed).

  1. She ___ me she was from Dakar.
  2. He ___ that the shop was closed.
  3. They ___ us the truth.
  4. I ___ to him that I was sorry.
  5. Can you ___ me the time, please?
Show the answers
  1. told
  2. said
  3. told
  4. said
  5. tell

2. Move the tenses back

Report these words, starting with “She said (that)…”.

  1. “I'm hungry.”
  2. “I've lost my phone.”
  3. “I'll be late.”
  4. “I can't swim.”
  5. “I'm working from home today.” (reported the following week)
Show the answers
  1. She said (that) she was hungry.
  2. She said (that) she had lost her phone.
  3. She said (that) she would be late.
  4. She said (that) she couldn't swim.
  5. She said (that) she was working from home that day.

3. Pronouns and time words

Report the words (later, somewhere else) with told me.

  1. Paul: “I bought this car yesterday.”
  2. Ana: “We are moving to Toronto next month.”
  3. My boss: “You can leave early tomorrow.”
  4. Karim: “My sister lives here.”
Show the answers
  1. Paul told me (that) he had bought that car the day before.
  2. Ana told me (that) they were moving to Toronto the following month.
  3. My boss told me (that) I could leave early the next day.
  4. Karim told me (that) his sister lived there.

4. Correct the mistakes

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. He said me that he was tired.
  2. She told that she would come.
  3. They said they will help us.
  4. He told to me that he had finished.
Show the answers
  1. He told me that he was tired. / He said that he was tired.
  2. She told me that she would come. / She said that she would come.
  3. They said they would help us.
  4. He told me that he had finished.

5. Report the words

Report each sentence with the words in brackets.

  1. Amira: “I'm ill.” (She told me…)
  2. Leo: “I'll come tomorrow.” (He said… — reported a week later)
  3. The children: “We've already eaten.” (They said…)
  4. Your brother's text now: “I'm arriving at eight.” (My brother says…)
Show the answers
  1. She told me (that) she was ill (sick).
  2. He said (that) he would come the next day.
  3. They said (that) they had already eaten.
  4. My brother says (that) he's arriving at eight.

6. Your turn

Think of a recent conversation (a phone call, a meeting, a family chat). In 4–5 sentences, report what people said, using said and told.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. Yesterday my cousin called me from Douala. She told me she had passed her exams. She said she was going to study medicine in Yaoundé. She also said that our grandmother was feeling better. I told her I would visit them in December.
Key points
  • tell + person (told me); say with no person (said that) or say to + person.
  • After a past reporting verb, tenses move back (is → was, will → would, has done → had done, can → could).
  • Pronouns, possessives and time words change depending on who reports (tomorrow → the next day, here → there).
  • Present reporting verb (says): no backshift.
  • Information still true: backshift is optional.
