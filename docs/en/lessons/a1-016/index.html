There is / there are

Use “there is” and “there are” to say that something exists or is in a place. You need them to describe a town, a house, a fridge or a situation.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Choose between there is (singular, uncountable) and there are (plural).
  • Make negatives (there isn't, there aren't, there's no) and questions (Is there…? Are there any…? How many… are there?).
  • Know the difference between there is and it is, and between there / their / they're.
  • Avoid word-for-word translations: for past time use ago; for distance use it's.

Positive sentences

Use “there is” (short form: there's) before a singular or uncountable noun, and “there are” before a plural noun. In writing, “there are” has no short form.

The verb agrees with the noun that follows. In a list, it usually agrees with the first thing: There's a table and four chairs. There are four chairs and a table.

FormExample
Singularthere is / there's + a / an + nounThere's a bank near the station.
Uncountablethere is / there's + (some) + nounThere's some milk in the fridge.
Pluralthere are + plural nounThere are two bakeries in my street.
Plural + quantitythere are + a lot of / many / some + nounThere are a lot of tourists in summer.
  • There's a cat in the garden.
    Singular.
  • There are thirty students in my class.
    Plural.
  • There's a lot of traffic today.
    Uncountable → is.

Negatives

Put “not” after is / are: there isn't (there is not), there aren't (there are not). Before a plural or uncountable noun, we often add “any”: There aren't any shops. There isn't any bread. (Some and any: lesson 31.)

Another very common way: “there's no” / “there are no” + noun, with no article: There's no bus today. There are no taxis. With “no”, the verb stays positive: never “there isn't no”.

  • There isn't a hospital in my village.
    isn't + a.
  • There aren't any chairs in this room.
    aren't + any.
  • There's no sugar in my coffee.
    there's no + noun.

Questions and short answers

For questions, change the order: Is there…? Are there…? With plural and uncountable nouns, we often add “any”. Short answers: Yes, there is. / No, there isn't. Yes, there are. / No, there aren't. Like with “to be”, no short form after Yes (not “Yes, there's”).

To ask about quantity: How many + plural noun + are there? / How much + uncountable noun + is there? (lesson 30).

QuestionYesNo
Is there a pharmacy near here?Yes, there is.No, there isn't.
Are there any good restaurants?Yes, there are.No, there aren't.
Is there any coffee left?Yes, there is.No, there isn't.
How many rooms are there?There are three.There's only one.
  • Excuse me, is there a cash machine near here?
    A useful question in town.
  • How many people are there in your family?
    How many + plural.

There is or it is? There, their or they're?

“There is” introduces something for the first time (it exists). “It is” describes or identifies something we already know about. We often use them one after the other: There's a new café on my street. It's very nice.

Don't confuse “there are” and “they are” either: There are two parks in my town. They are big and green. And in writing: there (in that place), their (possessive), they're (= they are) sound the same (lesson 14).

  • There's a parcel for you. It's from Canada.
    Introduce, then describe.
  • There are some new students. They're from Belgium.
    there are, then they are.

When not to use there is

For a time in the past, English uses “ago” after the period: two years ago (you will see it at A2 with the past simple). For distance, use “it's”: It's 5 km from here.

To describe a place, don't put the place as a subject with “has” in the wrong order: say “In my town, there's a cinema” or “My town has a cinema”, never “In my town has a cinema”.

To ask about a problem, say “What's wrong?” or “What's the matter?”, not “What is there?”.

IdeaEnglishNote
a park exists near hereThere's a park near here.existence → there is
I arrived, and now it is two days laterI arrived two days ago.past time → ago
the distance to the beach is 10 kmIt's 10 km to the beach.distance → it's
you want to know the problemWhat's wrong? / What's the matter?a problem

The formula

There's + singular / uncountable · There are + plural · Is there…? / Are there any…? · There isn't / aren't (any) · There's no…

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “There's” /ðeəz/ is one syllable. “there are” often joins: /ðeər ə/ (in British English, the r is pronounced before a vowel).
  • In the sentence, “there” is weak: /ðə/ (There's a cat /ðəz ə ˈkæt/). The noun has the stress.
  • “there” /ðeə/, “their” /ðeə/ and “they're” /ðeə/ sound the same.
  • Question (Is there a bank?): your voice goes up. “How many… are there?”: your voice goes down.
  • “isn't” /ˈɪznt/ and “aren't” /ɑːnt/: stress them so people hear the negative.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ There is two banks.✓ There are two banks.Plural → there are.
✗ There are a lot of traffic.✓ There's a lot of traffic.Uncountable → there is.
✗ In my town has a cinema.✓ In my town, there's a cinema. / My town has a cinema.Use there is to say something exists.
✗ There isn't no milk.✓ There's no milk. / There isn't any milk.No double negative.
✗ Is there a bank? — Yes, there's.✓ Yes, there is.No short form in a positive short answer.
✗ There is two years, I was in Paris.✓ Two years ago, I was in Paris.Past time = period + ago.
✗ It's a cat in the garden.✓ There's a cat in the garden.To say something exists, use there is.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

A Belgian student who is new in Québec City visits a flat with the landlord.

Landlord

So, this is the flat. There are two bedrooms and a small living room.
there are + plural.

Élodie

It's nice. Is there a washing machine?
Is there…? question.

Landlord

No, there isn't, but there's a laundry room in the basement.
Short answer, then there's.

Élodie

OK. And are there any shops near here?
Are there any + plural.

Landlord

Yes, there are. There's a supermarket on the corner and there are two cafés on the next street.
Singular and plural.

Élodie

Great. How many people are there in the building?
How many… are there?

Landlord

There are six flats, so about ten people. It's very quiet.
there are, then it's (description).

Élodie

Perfect. There's just one problem: there's no desk!
there's no + noun.

Landlord

No problem. There's an old desk in my garage. You can have it.
an before a vowel sound.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. There is or there are?

Complete with there's or there are.

  1. ___ a bank next to the post office.
  2. ___ five people in my family.
  3. ___ some water on the floor.
  4. ___ a lot of trees in the park.
  5. ___ a sofa and two armchairs in the room.
  6. ___ many tourists in Bruges in summer.
Show the answers
  1. There's
  2. There are
  3. There's
  4. There are
  5. There's
  6. There are

2. Negatives

Write the sentence in the negative (two possible forms: isn't / aren't any, or no).

  1. There's a cinema in my village.
  2. There are some eggs in the fridge.
  3. There's some bread on the table.
Show the answers
  1. There isn't a cinema in my village. / There's no cinema in my village.
  2. There aren't any eggs in the fridge. / There are no eggs in the fridge.
  3. There isn't any bread on the table. / There's no bread on the table.

3. Questions and short answers

Write the question, then the short answer shown.

  1. a lift / in the building? (yes)
  2. any good restaurants / here? (no)
  3. How many / students / in your class? (22)
  4. any milk / in the fridge? (no)
Show the answers
  1. Is there a lift in the building? — Yes, there is.
  2. Are there any good restaurants here? — No, there aren't.
  3. How many students are there in your class? — There are twenty-two.
  4. Is there any milk in the fridge? — No, there isn't.

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Correct it.

  1. There is three bedrooms.
  2. In my street has a bakery.
  3. There aren't no taxis.
  4. There's a new restaurant. There's very good.
  5. Are there a park near here?
Show the answers
  1. There are three bedrooms.
  2. In my street, there's a bakery. / There's a bakery in my street.
  3. There aren't any taxis. / There are no taxis.
  4. There's a new restaurant. It's very good.
  5. Is there a park near here?

5. What do you say?

Write one sentence with there is / there are for each situation.

  1. You want to say that something is wrong: a problem exists.
  2. The room has no chairs.
  3. You ask a stranger about shops in the area.
  4. The metro is full of people.
Show the answers
  1. There's a problem.
  2. There aren't any chairs. / There are no chairs.
  3. Are there any shops near here?
  4. There are a lot of people on the metro.

6. Your turn

Describe your neighbourhood or village in five sentences with there is / there are, positive and negative, and ask a visitor one question.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. In my neighbourhood there's a big market and there are three schools. There's a pharmacy near my house. There isn't a cinema, but there are two cafés. Is there a cinema in your town?
Key points
  • There's + singular or uncountable; there are + plural.
  • Negative: there isn't / aren't (any) or there's no / there are no + noun.
  • Question: Is there…? Are there any…? → Yes, there is. / No, there aren't. How many… are there?
  • There is introduces, it is describes: There's a café. It's nice.
  • Past time = period + ago; distance = it's.
