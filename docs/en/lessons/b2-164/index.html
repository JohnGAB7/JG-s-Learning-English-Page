The passive with modals and perfect tenses

“The form must be signed”, “The parcel should have been delivered yesterday”, “By June, the school will have been finished”: long passive forms are essential at work (instructions, reports, complaints, project updates). This lesson builds on lesson 151 and looks in detail at modals, perfect tenses and passive infinitives.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Form the passive with modals (can / must / should / might be done).
  • Form the passive in the perfect tenses: has been done, had been done, will have been done.
  • Use past modal passives: should / must / could / might have been done.
  • Use the passive infinitive and gerund (to be told, being interrupted) and “need doing”.

Modal + be + past participle

After a modal (can, could, must, should, may, might, will, would) or after “have to / ought to / be going to”, the passive is “be” (base form) + past participle.

These forms are typical of instructions, rules and user guides: “Bags must not be left unattended”, “This medicine should be taken with food”.

Negative: modal + not + be + past participle: “It can't be done today.” Question: modal + subject + be + past participle: “Can it be fixed?”

  • All forms must be completed in black ink.
    A rule.
  • This medicine should be taken with food.
    An instruction.
  • The meeting might be postponed.
    A possibility.
  • Can the order be changed?
    A question.

Perfect tenses in the passive

Present perfect: has / have been + past participle. It presents a result now: “The flight has been cancelled.”

Past perfect: had been + past participle. It shows an action before another past moment: “When we arrived, the food had already been eaten.”

Future perfect: will have been + past participle. It shows that an action will be finished before a future moment, often with “by”: “By June, the new school will have been built.”

Remember: with a specific past time or date, use the past simple passive, not the present perfect: “The bridge was built in 1932”, not “has been built in 1932”.

TensePassive formExample
Present perfecthas / have been + participleYour application has been received.
Past perfecthad been + participleThe room had been cleaned before we arrived.
Future perfectwill have been + participleThe results will have been published by Monday.
Present perfect (negative)hasn't / haven't been + participleThe invoice hasn't been paid yet.
QuestionHas / Have + subject + been + participle?Have the tickets been sent?

Modal + have been + past participle: the past

For criticism, deduction or possibility about the past in the passive: modal + have been + past participle.

“should have been done”: criticism or regret (it was necessary but it didn't happen). “must have been done”: deduction. “could / might have been done”: an unreal possibility or a guess. “can't have been done”: impossibility.

These forms are very useful for complaining politely without blaming anyone: “This problem could have been avoided.”

  • The parcel should have been delivered yesterday.
    Criticism: it wasn't delivered.
  • The window must have been left open.
    Deduction.
  • This accident could have been avoided.
    It was possible to avoid it, but it wasn't avoided.
  • The email can't have been sent: it's still in the drafts.
    Impossibility.

Passive infinitives and gerunds

After a verb followed by “to” (want, expect, hope, need, would like…), the passive infinitive is “to be + past participle”: “I want to be told the truth.”

After a verb followed by -ing (hate, like, enjoy, avoid, remember…) or after a preposition, the passive gerund is “being + past participle”: “I hate being interrupted.”

Past forms: “to have been + past participle” and “having been + past participle”: “She was lucky to have been chosen”, “He denied having been paid”.

  • Everyone wants to be listened to.
    The preposition stays at the end.
  • She hates being called by her surname.
    Passive gerund.
  • He's afraid of being fired.
    After a preposition: being + participle.
  • I'm glad to have been invited.
    Past passive infinitive.

“Need doing” and form traps

In British English, “need + -ing” has a passive meaning: “The car needs washing” = “The car needs to be washed”.

Don't say “The car needs to wash”: it means the car will wash something itself.

Very long forms like “has been being done” hardly exist: avoid them. Prefer “has been under repair” or an active sentence.

  • The grass needs cutting.
    Someone needs to cut it.
  • Your shoes need to be cleaned.
    A more neutral equivalent.
  • The road has been under repair for months.
    Instead of “has been being repaired”.

The formula

modal + be + participle · has / had / will have + been + participle · modal + have been + participle · to be / being + participle

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “been” is weak in connected speech: /bɪn/: “It's been done” /ɪts bɪn ˈdʌn/.
  • “should have been” reduces to /ˈʃʊdəv bɪn/ in fast speech, or even /ˈʃʊdə bɪn/. The stress falls on the participle: “It should've been DONE.”
  • “must be”: the t of “must” often disappears before “be”: /ˈmʌs bi/.
  • When criticising, you can stress the modal: “It SHOULD have been sent yesterday!”
  • “being” /ˈbiːɪŋ/ (two syllables) is not “been” /biːn/ or /bɪn/.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Dakar, a site meeting about the construction of a school. Mrs Sow, the project manager, checks progress with the engineer, Mr Ndiaye.

Mrs Sow

So, where are we? The roof should have been finished last week.
should have been + participle: criticism.

Mr Ndiaye

I know. The materials had been ordered in April, but the delivery was delayed at the port.
Past perfect passive.

Mrs Sow

Has the problem been solved now?
Present perfect passive question.

Mr Ndiaye

Yes. Everything has been delivered, and the work can be completed in ten days.
Present perfect passive; modal passive.

Mrs Sow

Good. The classrooms must be inspected before the opening in October.
Modal passive: an obligation.

Mr Ndiaye

Don't worry. By the end of September, everything will have been checked and approved.
Future perfect passive.

Mrs Sow

I hope so. I don't want to be told about any more delays.
Passive infinitive.

Mr Ndiaye

Understood. This delay could have been avoided, and it won't happen again.
could have been + participle.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Recognise the form

Name the passive form.

  1. The tickets have been sold.
  2. The room had been cleaned before we arrived.
  3. The results will have been published by Monday.
  4. The form should have been signed.
Show the answers
  1. Present perfect passive (have been + participle).
  2. Past perfect passive (had been + participle).
  3. Future perfect passive (will have been + participle).
  4. Past modal passive (should have been + participle).

2. Complete

Complete with the correct passive form of the verb in brackets.

  1. All forms must ___ (complete) in black ink.
  2. Look at the board: our flight ___ (cancel).
  3. When we arrived, all the food ___ already ___ (eat).
  4. By the end of the year, the new metro line ___ (build).
  5. This letter ___ (should / send) last week!
  6. I hate ___ (tell) what to do.
Show the answers
  1. be completed
  2. has been cancelled
  3. had already been eaten
  4. will have been built
  5. should have been sent
  6. being told

3. Transform

Put these sentences into the passive (no agent).

  1. They have repaired the bridge.
  2. Someone had already booked the room.
  3. You can't use this lift during a fire.
  4. They should have warned us.
  5. The workers will have finished the school by June.
Show the answers
  1. The bridge has been repaired.
  2. The room had already been booked.
  3. This lift can't be used during a fire.
  4. We should have been warned.
  5. The school will have been finished by June.

4. Correct the mistakes

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. The report must be finish by Friday.
  2. The work should have done yesterday.
  3. The house has been built in 1990.
  4. The car needs to wash.
Show the answers
  1. The report must be finished by Friday.
  2. The work should have been done yesterday.
  3. The house was built in 1990.
  4. The car needs washing. (or: The car needs to be washed.)

5. Complain without blaming

Rewrite each complaint in the passive so that no person is named.

  1. “You should have told us about the delay.”
  2. “Somebody must have damaged the box during delivery.”
  3. “Nobody has answered my emails.”
  4. “You could have avoided this problem.”
Show the answers
  1. We should have been told about the delay.
  2. The box must have been damaged during delivery.
  3. My emails haven't been answered.
  4. This problem could have been avoided.

6. Your turn

Write a short complaint email (4 sentences) to a supplier whose delivery is late. Use at least four different passives (modal, present perfect, past perfect, should / could have been).

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. Our order was placed on 3 May and it should have been delivered last Friday. We had been promised delivery within five days. So far, none of our emails have been answered. This situation could have been avoided, and we expect the goods to be sent by tomorrow at the latest.
Key points
  • Modal + be + past participle: must be signed, can be done, should be taken.
  • has / have been, had been, will have been + past participle for the perfect tenses.
  • Modal + have been + past participle: should have been done (criticism), must have been done (deduction), could have been avoided.
  • Passive infinitive: to be told; passive gerund: being interrupted.
  • Specific past date → past simple passive (was built in 1990); “needs washing” = needs to be washed.
