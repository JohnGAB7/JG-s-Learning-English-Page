Articles: a, an, the

“A / an” means one thing that is not specific; “the” means a specific thing. But English often uses no article at all: “I like coffee”, not “I like the coffee”. This lesson explains when to use a, an, the… or nothing.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Choose between a and an by the sound (not the letter) that follows.
  • Use “a / an” for a thing that is not specific, a job or a first mention.
  • Use “the” for a specific thing, a thing you already know about, or a unique thing.
  • Know when to use no article: general ideas, meals, languages, sports, most countries, at home, by bus.

A or an? The sound decides

“A” and “an” have the same meaning. Use “an” before a word that starts with a vowel SOUND, and “a” before a consonant sound. The pronunciation is important, not the spelling.

So “hour” starts with a silent h (a vowel sound) → an hour. “University” starts with the sound /j/ (like “you”) → a university. For abbreviations, listen to the name of the first letter: an MBA (/em/), a UFO (/juː/).

ArticleBeforeExamples
aa consonant sounda book, a car, a house, a big apple
au and eu sounding /juː/; o sounding /w/a university, a European city, a one-way ticket
ana vowel soundan apple, an egg, an idea, an old man, an umbrella
ana silent han hour, an honest person, an heir
anan abbreviation starting with a vowel soundan MP, an X-ray, an FBI agent
Only the word right after the article matters: an apple but a red apple; a house but an old house.

When to use a / an

Use “a / an” only before a singular countable noun. It has no plural: in the plural, use nothing or “some” (lesson 31): a book → books / some books.

Uses: one thing or person among others, not specific; the first time you talk about it; a job or a type of person (necessary in English); the meaning “one”; frequency and price (twice a week, 3 euros a kilo); exclamations (What a beautiful day!).

  • I need a pen.
    Any pen.
  • She's an engineer.
    A job: the article is necessary.
  • We go to the gym three times a week.
    a = in each.
  • What a nice surprise!
    Exclamation.

When to use the

“The” is the same for singular and plural, countable and uncountable nouns. It shows that we know which thing we mean.

Uses: something you mentioned before (first a, then the); something the rest of the sentence makes clear (the man in the blue shirt); something clear in the situation (Close the door, the kitchen); something unique (the sun, the moon, the world, the internet); some places and media (the cinema, the theatre, the radio); musical instruments (play the piano); family names in the plural (the Martins); seas, rivers and oceans (the Atlantic, the Seine); and countries with a plural or long name (the UK, the USA, the Netherlands).

  • I've got a cat and a dog. The cat is black.
    Second mention: the.
  • The woman in the red coat is my aunt.
    The sentence makes it specific.
  • Can you open the window?
    The window in this room.
  • The sun is very hot today.
    Unique.
  • She plays the guitar.
    Instrument.

No article: the big trap

To talk about something in general, English uses NO article with plural and uncountable nouns: I like cats (cats in general), Coffee is expensive, Life is beautiful. If you say “the cats”, you mean specific cats.

Also no article before: meals (have breakfast, at lunch), languages and school subjects (English is easy, I study history), sports (play football), most countries, cities and continents (France, Paris, Africa), days, months and holidays (on Monday, in June, at Christmas), and in fixed expressions: at home, at work, at school, in bed, go to school, go to bed, by bus, by car.

WrongRightWhy
I like the music.I like music.general idea
The children love the chocolate.Children love chocolate.general idea: plural + uncountable
The English is useful.English is useful.language
I have the lunch at noon.I have lunch at noon.meal
He plays the tennis.He plays tennis.sport
She's at the home / at the work.She's at home / at work.fixed expression
I go to the work by the bus.I go to work by bus.fixed expressions
Compare: “I like music” (music in general) / “I like the music in this film” (the specific music of this film).

Summary: a, the or nothing?

To choose, ask two questions. 1) Is it specific, known to both people, or unique? → the. 2) If not, is it a singular countable noun? → a / an. If not (plural or uncountable, in general) → no article.

“The” sounds like /ðə/ before a consonant sound (the book) and often /ði/ before a vowel sound (the apple), but many speakers today say /ðə/ everywhere.

  • There's a bank near here. The bank opens at nine.
    First a, then the.
  • Dogs are friendly, but the dog next door is noisy.
    General vs specific.

The formula

a + consonant sound · an + vowel sound · the = specific / known / unique · Ø = general idea (plural, uncountable), meals, languages, sports, at home, by bus

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “a” is almost always weak: /ə/ (a book /ə bʊk/). The strong form /eɪ/ is only for emphasis.
  • “an” joins the next word: an apple /ə ˈnæpl/, an hour /ə ˈnaʊə/.
  • “the”: /ðə/ before a consonant sound (the car), /ði/ before a vowel sound (the end). Strong form /ðiː/ for emphasis: “THE best”.
  • The /ð/ sound in “the”: tongue between the teeth, voice on. Don't say “ze” or “de”.
  • Don't drop the small /ə/ of “a”: “She's a nurse” has three syllables: /ʃiːz ə ˈnɜːs/.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Two friends look for a restaurant in the evening in Brussels.

Yanis

I'm hungry. Is there a good restaurant near here?
a: any good restaurant.

Laura

Yes, there's an Italian restaurant and a Cameroonian restaurant on the next street.
an before the vowel sound of Italian.

Yanis

I love Cameroonian food! Is the Cameroonian restaurant expensive?
food in general: no article; then the (second mention).

Laura

No, it isn't. And the owner is very friendly. She's a chef from Douala.
a + job.

Yanis

Great. What time does it close?
A timetable question.

Laura

At eleven. We've got an hour and a half.
an hour: silent h.

Yanis

Perfect. I usually have dinner at home, so this is a nice change!
No article: dinner, at home.

Laura

Let's go by tram. The stop is just there.
by tram: no article; the stop: clear in the situation.

Key points
  • a + consonant sound, an + vowel sound: a university, an hour.
  • a / an: one of many, first mention, job (She's a nurse), frequency (twice a week).
  • the: already known, made specific, unique, instruments (play the piano).
  • No article for general ideas: I like cats, Life is short, Coffee is good.
  • No article: meals, languages, sports, at home, at work, go to bed, by bus.
