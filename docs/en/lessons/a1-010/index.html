Subject and object pronouns

Pronouns replace a noun so you don't repeat it: she, him, us, them… English has one form for the subject (I) and one for the object (me), and the object pronoun always comes after the verb (I love him).

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Know the seven subject pronouns and the seven object pronouns.
  • Put the object pronoun after the verb or after a preposition.
  • Choose between he, she, it and they for a person, an animal or a thing.
  • Avoid the traps: It's me, Me too, my friend and I, the order “give it to me”.

Subject and object: the table

The subject pronoun does the action; it goes before the verb. The object pronoun receives the action; it goes after the verb or after a preposition (with, for, to, at…).

Two pronouns don't change: “you” (subject and object) and “it”. The others have two forms to learn.

SubjectObjectExample 1Example 2
ImeI know him.He knows me.
youyouYou're kind.I love you.
hehimHe's nice.I like him.
sheherShe's here.Call her.
ititIt's new.I like it.
weusWe're ready.Wait for us!
theythemThey're late.Ask them.
“I” always has a capital letter, even in the middle of a sentence: Yesterday I… But “me” has a small letter.

Where the object pronoun goes

In some languages the object pronoun goes before the verb. In English, it ALWAYS goes after the verb: I see her, He helps us. Never say “I her see”.

After a preposition, use the object form too: with me, for her, to them, about us, between you and me. Don't say “with I” or “for she”.

  • I don't understand him.
    Object after the verb.
  • Can you help me?
    Object after help.
  • This present is for you.
    After the preposition for.
  • Come with us!
    After with.
  • She often talks about them.
    After about.

He, she, it or they?

In English, things have no gender: a table, a car, a city are all “it”. For a beautiful house, say “It's beautiful”.

For an animal you don't know, say “it”. For a pet you know, people often say “he” or “she”. For a person, don't say “it”: if you don't know if a baby is a boy or a girl, say “they” or “he or she”.

“They” is also used for one person when you don't know their gender, or when they prefer this pronoun: Someone is at the door. What do they want? The verb stays plural: they are.

  • I love this city. It's so lively!
    A city = it.
  • Where's my phone? — It's on the table.
    A thing = it.
  • We've got a cat. She's ten years old.
    A pet you know: she.
  • A customer called. They want a refund.
    Gender not known: they.

Traps: It's me, Me too, my friend and I

After “to be”, in normal English, use the object form: “Who's there? — It's me.” “It is I” exists but is very formal and old-fashioned.

To react, say “Me too” and “Me neither”, never “I too”. Don't repeat the subject with “me”: not “Me, I like tea”, but “I like tea” (stress I) or “Personally, I like tea”.

When you talk about yourself and another person, put yourself last to be polite: “my friend and I” as a subject, “my friend and me” as an object. Tip: remove the other person to check (I went → my friend and I went; for me → for my friend and me).

  • Who's speaking? — It's me, Didier.
    It's me.
  • I'm hungry. — Me too!
    Me too.
  • My sister and I live in Montréal.
    Subject: … and I.
  • She invited my husband and me.
    Object: … and me.

Two objects: “give it to me”

With verbs like give, send, show, tell and bring, there can be two objects: the thing and the person. With two nouns, you can say “Give Ana the book” or “Give the book to Ana”.

When the thing is a pronoun (it, them), normally put the thing first, then “to” + person: Give it to me. “Give me it” is heard in informal British English, but “Give it to me” is always safe.

  • Can you send me the photos? — Sure, I can send them to you tonight.
    them (thing) + to you (person).
  • Show it to him.
    it + to him.

The formula

Subject + verb + object: I · you · he · she · it · we · they → me · you · him · her · it · us · them

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • Object pronouns are often weak and unstressed: I like him /aɪ ˈlaɪk ɪm/ (the h often disappears), Call her /ˈkɔːl ə/, Tell them /ˈtel ðəm/.
  • Stress them only for emphasis or contrast: I love HER, not HIM.
  • “them” /ðem/ starts with /ð/ (tongue between the teeth, voice on). Don't say “dem” or “zem”.
  • “us” /ʌs/ has an unvoiced s. “he” /hiː/ has a breathed h: hear the difference between “he's” /hiːz/ and “is” /ɪz/.
  • “her” is /hɜː/ strong, /ə/ weak; “him” is /hɪm/ strong, /ɪm/ weak.

✗ Where's the car? — She's in the garage.✓ It's in the garage.A thing = it, not he / she.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Two friends prepare a birthday party for their flatmate in their flat in Lyon.

Clara

Is the cake ready? Paul loves chocolate. I'm sure he'll love it.
it = the cake.

Mariam

Yes, it's in the fridge. Where are the candles? I can't find them.
them = the candles.

Clara

They're in my bag. Here, take them.
Subject they, object them.

Mariam

Thanks. Are his parents coming?
Present continuous for a plan.

Clara

Yes, they are. I'm meeting them at the station. And his sister is coming with us to the restaurant.
them, us after a verb or preposition.

Mariam

Great! I like her a lot.
her = his sister.

Clara

Me too. Shh! Someone's at the door. Is it him?
Me too; Is it him?

Paul

Hi, it's me! Why is it so dark in here?
It's me.

Clara and Mariam

Surprise! Happy birthday, Paul!
A birthday greeting.

Key points
  • Subject / object: I-me, you-you, he-him, she-her, it-it, we-us, they-them.
  • The object goes after the verb (I see her) or after a preposition (with us).
  • A thing = it. A person whose gender you don't know = they (+ are).
  • It's me · Me too · my friend and I (subject) / my friend and me (object).
  • Give it to me: the thing (pronoun) first, then to + person.
