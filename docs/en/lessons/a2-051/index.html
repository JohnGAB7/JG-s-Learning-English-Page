The present perfect: a first look

The present perfect (I have worked, she has seen) connects the past and the present. It is one of the most difficult tenses for learners, so this lesson takes it step by step.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Form the present perfect with have / has + past participle, in all forms.
  • Know the most common irregular past participles.
  • Use the present perfect for life experience and for a past action with a result now.
  • Choose between the present perfect and the past simple: is there a finished past time?

The form

The present perfect uses the helper verb have (has for he / she / it) + the past participle. Short forms: I've, you've, he's / she's / it's, we've, they've; negative haven't / hasn't.

Be careful: “he's” can mean “he is” or “he has”. The next word tells you: “he's tired” (is) / “he's finished” (has + past participle).

FormPatternExample
positivesubject + have / has + past participleI've finished. She's visited Quebec.
negativesubject + haven't / hasn't + past participleWe haven't eaten. He hasn't called.
questionHave / Has + subject + past participle?Have you seen my keys?
short answerYes, I have. / No, she hasn't.Has he arrived? — No, he hasn't.

The past participle

For regular verbs, the past participle is the same as the past simple: base + -ed (worked, played, studied, stopped).

For irregular verbs, it is the third column of the list (go – went – gone). Some verbs have the same past simple and past participle; others don't, so learn both.

BasePast simplePast participle
bewas / werebeen
gowentgone
dodiddone

Use 1: life experience

Use the present perfect to say what you have done (or not) in your life until now, without saying when. The experience matters, not the time.

The words “ever” and “never” are very common with this use: you will study them in detail in the next lesson (52).

  • I've visited Canada twice.
    In my life, until now.
  • She has worked in three countries.
    Her experience.
  • We've never eaten ndolé.
    never = not at any time.

Use 2: a past action with a result now

We also use it for a recent action when we can see the result now. The present situation is what matters.

Often a present sentence could say the same thing: “I've lost my keys” = I don't have my keys now.

  • I've lost my keys. I can't open the door.
    Result: I can't get in.
  • Look! It has stopped raining.
    Result: it isn't raining now.
  • He's broken his leg, so he can't play.
    Result: he can't play now.

Present perfect or past simple?

The golden rule: when you say (or think of) a finished past time — yesterday, last week, in 2019, two days ago, when I was a child — use the past simple, never the present perfect.

In conversation, we often start with the present perfect (the experience), then change to the past simple for the details (when, where, how).

Present perfect (no finished time)Past simple (finished time)
I've been to Senegal.I went to Senegal in 2022.
She's lost her phone.She lost her phone yesterday.
Have you seen the film?When did you see it?
  • I've met the new manager. I met her this morning.
    Experience, then the detail in the past simple.
  • Have you ever been to Brussels? — Yes, I went there last summer.
    Question in the present perfect, answer with a time in the past simple.

Been or gone?

The verb go has two possible participles in the present perfect, with different meanings. “Has been to” = went there and came back (experience). “Has gone to” = went there and is still there (not here now).

  • Paul has been to Dakar.
    He went and came back.
  • Paul has gone to Dakar.
    He is in Dakar now.

The formula

have / has + past participle (worked, seen, gone, been…)

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • In speech, have and has are contracted and very short: I've /aɪv/, we've /wiːv/, she's /ʃiːz/. Listen for the small /v/ or /z/ sound.
  • In a question, “have” is weak: Have you seen…? /həv juː ˈsiːn/. In a short answer, it is strong: Yes, I HAVE /hæv/.
  • “haven't” /ˈhævnt/ and “hasn't” /ˈhæznt/ are never weak.
  • “been” is /biːn/ (UK) or /bɪn/ (US); “gone” /ɡɒn/ (UK) / /ɡɔːn/ (US); “done” /dʌn/ rhymes with “sun”.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ I have seen him yesterday.✓ I saw him yesterday.Finished past time (yesterday): past simple.
✗ She have finished.✓ She has finished.he / she / it: has.
✗ I have went to London.✓ I have been to London.After have: past participle (gone / been), not the past simple.
✗ Have you eat?✓ Have you eaten?eat → ate → eaten: you need the participle.
✗ When have you arrived?✓ When did you arrive?When asks about the time: past simple.
✗ He has gone to Paris twice.✓ He has been to Paris twice.For experience (there and back): been.
✗ She is arrived.✓ She has arrived.The helper is always have, never be, even with verbs of movement.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Brussels, Clémence is interviewing Olivier for a job in a travel agency.

Clémence

So, Olivier, have you worked in tourism before?
Question about experience.

Olivier

Yes, I have. I worked in a hotel in Liège for two years.
Short answer, then a finished period: past simple.

Clémence

Great. Which countries have you visited?
Experience, no time.

Olivier

I've been to Morocco, Cameroon and Canada. I went to Canada last year.
been to (experience); last year → past simple.

Clémence

Have you ever organised a group trip?
ever = at any time in your life.

Olivier

No, I haven't, but I've helped my manager with several trips.
Negative short answer.

Clémence

OK. And have you read our job description?
Result now: do you know it?

Olivier

Yes, I have. I've prepared some ideas for your website, too.
Result: the ideas are ready.

Key points
  • Present perfect = have / has + past participle (worked, seen, gone, been, done).
  • Life experience with no time: I've been to Canada.
  • Past action with a result now: I've lost my keys.
  • Finished past time (yesterday, in 2020, ago, When…?) = past simple, never present perfect.
  • been to = went and came back; gone to = went and is still there.
