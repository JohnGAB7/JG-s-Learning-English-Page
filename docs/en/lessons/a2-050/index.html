Present continuous for future arrangements

The present continuous (I'm meeting, she's flying) is also used for the future when something is already organised: a meeting, a booking, an event with a fixed date. It is the most natural way to talk about your diary.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Use the present continuous with a future time word for a fixed arrangement.
  • Tell the difference between an arrangement (present continuous), an intention (going to) and an instant decision (will).
  • Use the present simple for timetables and official schedules.
  • Ask and answer “What are you doing tonight / this weekend?”.

The form: nothing new

The form is the present continuous (lesson 8): am / is / are + verb-ing. What changes is the meaning: with a future time word, the sentence is about the future.

Without a time word, “I'm meeting Paul” can mean “now”. With “tomorrow”, “at six”, “on Friday”, “next week”, the future meaning is clear.

FormExampleNote
positiveI'm seeing the dentist on Tuesday.an appointment
negativeWe aren't working next Monday.a day off
questionAre you coming to the party on Saturday?asking about plans
Wh- questionWhat time is she arriving?asking for details
short answerYes, I am. / No, I'm not.use be

When to use it: the diary test

Use the present continuous for a future event that is arranged with other people or already booked: you could write it in your diary. There is often a time, a date, a place, a ticket or a booking.

Ask yourself: “Is it organised?” If yes, the present continuous is natural.

  • I'm having lunch with my boss tomorrow.
    It's arranged.
  • They're getting married in June.
    The date is fixed.
  • We're flying to Montreal on the 12th.
    The tickets are bought.
  • What are you doing this weekend?
    A very common question about plans.

Present continuous, going to or will?

All three talk about the future, but in different ways. For a plan, the present continuous and going to are often both possible: the present continuous focuses on the arrangement, going to on the intention.

Do not use the present continuous for predictions or for events nobody controls: not “It's raining tomorrow” but “It's going to rain / It will rain tomorrow”.

FormMeaningExample
present continuousfixed arrangement (diary)I'm meeting Awa at the station at 5.
be going tointention, decision already madeI'm going to ask her about the job.
willinstant decision, offer, opinionI'll call her now. / I think she'll say yes.
present simpletimetable, official scheduleHer train arrives at 4:55.

Present simple for timetables

For a public timetable or a fixed programme (transport, classes, films, shops, official events), use the present simple, even for the future. An organisation fixes these times, not you.

Compare: “The film starts at 8” (the cinema's timetable) and “We're seeing a film tonight” (our arrangement).

  • The train to Antwerp leaves at 7:15 tomorrow.
    Timetable.
  • My English class starts next Monday.
    Official programme.
  • What time does the museum open on Sunday?
    Question about a timetable.

Useful expressions and special cases

With go and come, we prefer “I'm going to Dakar next week” to “I'm going to go to Dakar”. “I'm coming!” also means “I'll be there in a second” when someone calls you.

State verbs (be, have for possession, know, like…) do not go in the present continuous, even for the future: say “I'll be at home tomorrow” or “I'm going to be at home”, not “I'm being at home”.

  • I'm going to the dentist after work.
    go + present continuous.
  • Are you coming with us? — Yes, I'm coming!
    I'm coming = I'll be there soon.

The formula

am / is / are + verb-ing + future time word (tomorrow, on Friday, at 6, next week)

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • Contractions are natural: I'm /aɪm/, she's /ʃiːz/, we're /wɪə/ (UK), they're /ðeə/ (UK).
  • In “What are you doing tonight?”, “are you” is often reduced to /ə jə/: /ˈwɒt ə jə ˈduːɪŋ təˈnaɪt/.
  • Stress the verb and the time word: I'm MEETing her on FRIday.
  • “tomorrow” is /təˈmɒrəʊ/: stress on the second syllable; the first o is weak.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ I meet my friend tonight.✓ I'm meeting my friend tonight.For a personal arrangement, use the present continuous.
✗ What do you do this weekend?✓ What are you doing this weekend?“What do you do?” asks about your job; for plans: What are you doing…?
✗ It's raining tomorrow.✓ It's going to rain tomorrow. / It will rain tomorrow.No present continuous for predictions.
✗ The train is leaving at 7 tomorrow.✓ The train leaves at 7 tomorrow.Official timetable: present simple (the continuous is possible but less common).
✗ I'm being in Paris next week.✓ I'll be in Paris next week.“be” does not go in the present continuous here.
✗ I'm going to go to Brussels tomorrow.✓ I'm going to Brussels tomorrow.With go, the present continuous is more natural.
✗ She's come to dinner on Friday.✓ She's coming to dinner on Friday.You need the -ing form (she's = she is).

In a conversation

Listening Studio

On the phone, Moussa tries to arrange a meeting with his friend Pierre in Ottawa.

Moussa

Hi Pierre! What are you doing on Friday evening?
Asking about plans.

Pierre

I'm playing basketball with my colleagues at seven. Why?
An arrangement.

Moussa

My sister is arriving from Dakar and we're having a small dinner.
Two arrangements.

Pierre

Oh, nice! What time does her plane land?
Timetable: present simple.

Moussa

It lands at 5:40. I'm picking her up at the airport.
Timetable + arrangement.

Pierre

And Saturday? Are you doing anything?
Yes / no question.

Moussa

No, we aren't doing anything yet.
Negative.

Pierre

Then come to my place. I'm cooking jollof rice!
Invitation + arrangement.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Arrangement or now?

Is the sentence about the future (an arrangement) or about now?

  1. I'm meeting Sarah tomorrow.
  2. Shh! I'm watching the news.
  3. We're visiting my grandparents next weekend.
  4. She's cooking dinner — it smells good.
Show the answers
  1. future (arrangement)
  2. now
  3. future (arrangement)
  4. now

2. Complete the diary

Put the verb in the present continuous to talk about these arrangements.

  1. On Monday I ___ (see) the doctor.
  2. On Tuesday we ___ (have) a meeting with the clients.
  3. ___ you ___ (come) to the concert on Friday?
  4. My parents ___ (not / travel) this summer.
  5. What time ___ Paul ___ (arrive) tomorrow?
Show the answers
  1. 'm seeing
  2. 're having
  3. Are … coming
  4. aren't travelling (US: aren't traveling)
  5. is … arriving

3. Present continuous or present simple?

Choose the correct form.

  1. The bus (leaves / is leaving) at 6:30 every morning.
  2. I (have / am having) dinner with Léa tonight.
  3. The film (starts / is starting) at 9 p.m.
  4. We (fly / are flying) to Lomé on Saturday — we bought the tickets.
Show the answers
  1. leaves
  2. am having
  3. starts
  4. are flying

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. What do you do tonight?
  2. It's snowing tomorrow.
  3. I play tennis with Max on Sunday at ten.
  4. We're meet the manager at 4.
Show the answers
  1. What are you doing tonight?
  2. It's going to snow tomorrow. / It will snow tomorrow.
  3. I'm playing tennis with Max on Sunday at ten.
  4. We're meeting the manager at 4.

5. Write the sentence

Write a natural future sentence from the words.

  1. I / have dinner / at my parents' / tonight (arranged)
  2. the shop / open / at 9 a.m. tomorrow (timetable)
  3. you / do / anything / on Saturday? (question)
Show the answers
  1. I'm having dinner at my parents' tonight.
  2. The shop opens at 9 a.m. tomorrow.
  3. Are you doing anything on Saturday?

6. Your turn

Describe your diary for next week in five sentences (at least four arrangements in the present continuous and one timetable in the present simple).

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. Next week is busy. On Monday I'm meeting my new manager at 9. On Wednesday evening I'm playing football with my friends. On Thursday my cousin is arriving from Lyon; her train gets in at 6:20. On Saturday we're having a barbecue in the garden.
Key points
  • Present continuous + future time word = fixed arrangement (it could go in your diary).
  • What are you doing tonight? asks about plans; What do you do? asks about your job.
  • Official timetables (trains, films, classes): present simple (The train leaves at 7).
  • No present continuous for predictions (It's going to rain, not It's raining tomorrow).
  • Going to = intention; will = instant decision or opinion.
