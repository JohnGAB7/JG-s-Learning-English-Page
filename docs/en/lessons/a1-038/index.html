Shopping

In a shop, you need to answer the shop assistant, ask for a size or a colour, try clothes on, pay and sometimes take something back. This lesson gives you the names of shops and the key phrases for customers and shop assistants.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Name the main shops (baker's, chemist's, supermarket…).
  • Answer the shop assistant and ask for a thing, a size, a colour.
  • Try things on, pay (by card, in cash) and understand the shop assistant at the till.
  • Return or change something with a receipt.

Shops

In British English, we often name a shop after the person who works there + ’s: “the baker's”, “the butcher's”, “the chemist's”. You can also say “the bakery”, “the pharmacy”.

Careful: a “magazine” is something you read. A place where you buy things is “a shop” (UK) or “a store” (US).

A “library” is where you borrow books for free; a place where you buy books is “a bookshop”.

Talking to the shop assistant

The shop assistant (US “salesclerk”) often says: “Can I help you?” or “Are you looking for anything in particular?”

If you only want to look: “No, thanks. I'm just looking.” If you want something: “Yes, I'm looking for a jacket.” or “Have you got any umbrellas?” / “Do you have any umbrellas?”

For a size or a colour: “Have you got this in a medium?”, “Do you have it in blue?”, “What size are you? — I'm a medium / a size 40.”

  • Excuse me, have you got this shirt in white?
    Asking for a colour.
  • Do you have these shoes in a size 42?
    Asking for a size.

Trying on and choosing

To try clothes: “Can I try it on?” / “Can I try them on?” (for trousers, shoes). The rooms for this: “the fitting rooms” (US “dressing rooms”).

To give your opinion: “It's too big / too small / too long.”; “It fits.” (it's the right size); “It suits you.” or “It looks great on you.” (it looks good on you). Lesson 48 looks at “too” in more detail.

To decide: “I'll take it.” (I want to buy it) or “I'll leave it, thanks.” (I don't want it). These are fixed phrases.

  • Can I try these trousers on? — Of course. The fitting rooms are on the left.
    try on + them / these trousers.
  • This jacket is too small. Have you got a large?
    too + adjective.
  • That dress really suits you!
    It looks good on you.

Paying

Ask the price with “How much is it?” / “How much are they?” (lesson 30). The assistant says: “That's 25 euros, please.” or “That's 40 dollars altogether.”

Pay “by card” or “in cash”. Useful questions: “Can I pay by card?”, “Do you take cards?”, “Can I pay contactless?”. You pay at “the till” / “the checkout” (UK) or “the register” (US).

The assistant gives you money back: “Here's your change.” and a paper: “Here's your receipt.” They may ask: “Would you like a bag?”. Low prices for a short time: “the sales”; a cheaper item is “in the sale” (UK) or “on sale” (US).

  • Can I pay by card? — Yes, of course. Just tap here.
    tap = touch the machine with your card.
  • That's 12 euros 50. — Here you are. — Thank you. Here's your change and your receipt.
    At the till.
  • These shoes are in the sale: 30% off!
    30% off = 30% cheaper.

Returning things and useful verbs

To take something back: “Can I return this, please?” / “Can I change this for a bigger size?”. Explain: “It doesn't work.”, “It's broken.”, “It's the wrong size.”. Show the receipt: “I've got the receipt.” To get your money back: “Can I have a refund?”

Verbs: “buy”, “sell”, “pay for something” (with “for”), “spend” (use money), “cost” (have a price).

“Do the shopping” = buy food and things for the home; “go shopping” = go to shops for pleasure, often for clothes.

  • Excuse me, I'd like to return this phone. It doesn't work.
    Returning something.
  • I pay for my groceries by card.
    pay for + thing.
  • I do the shopping on Saturday mornings, and sometimes I go shopping with friends in the afternoon.
    do the shopping vs go shopping.

The formula

Can I help you? — I'm just looking / I'm looking for… · Have you got it in…? · Can I try it on? · Can I pay by card?

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “receipt” is /rɪˈsiːt/: don't say the p (“ri-seet”).
  • “Can I try it on?” is linked in one chain: /kən aɪ ˈtraɪ ɪt ˈɒn/ ≈ “kuh-nai-trai-yi-ton”.
  • “chemist's” /ˈkemɪsts/: “ch” sounds like /k/. The final -sts needs practice.
  • “size” /saɪz/ ends with /z/; “fit” /fɪt/ has a short i: don't confuse it with “feet” /fiːt/.
  • “I'm just looking”: stress LOOKing; “just” is quick.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Montreal, in November, Fatima is looking for a winter coat in a clothes shop.

Assistant

Hi there! Can I help you?
The usual first question.

Fatima

Yes, please. I'm looking for a warm winter coat.
Saying what you want.

Assistant

These coats are very warm. What size are you?
Asking the size.

Fatima

I'm a medium. Have you got this one in dark green?
Asking for a colour.

Assistant

Yes, here you are. The fitting rooms are over there.
Where to try it on.

Fatima

Hmm, it's a bit too big. Can I try a small?
too big = bigger than she needs.

Assistant

Sure. Oh, that looks great on you!
A compliment.

Fatima

Thanks! How much is it?
Asking the price.

Assistant

It's 180 dollars, but it's on sale today: 20% off.
on sale (US/Canada) = cheaper.

Fatima

Perfect, I'll take it. Can I pay by card?
Deciding and paying.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Which shop?

Where do you buy these things? Write the name of the shop (British English).

  1. bread and croissants
  2. medicine
  3. a novel
  4. meat
  5. a newspaper
  6. a T-shirt
Show the answers
  1. the baker's / the bakery
  2. the chemist's / the pharmacy
  3. the bookshop
  4. the butcher's
  5. the newsagent's
  6. a clothes shop

2. Customer or shop assistant?

Who says this: the customer or the shop assistant?

  1. Can I help you?
  2. I'm just looking.
  3. What size are you?
  4. Can I pay by card?
Show the answers
  1. shop assistant
  2. customer
  3. shop assistant
  4. customer

3. Complete

Complete with the missing word.

  1. Can I try it ___?
  2. Can I pay ___ card?
  3. Have you got it ___ a medium?
  4. I'd like to pay ___ cash.
  5. Here's your ___. (the money you get back)
Show the answers
  1. on
  2. by
  3. in
  4. in
  5. change

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. No, thanks. I'm only watching.
  2. Have you it in blue?
  3. Can I try it? (a jacket)
  4. It's too much expensive.
  5. I'd like to return this jumper. Here's the ticket of the shop.
Show the answers
  1. No, thanks. I'm just looking.
  2. Have you got it in blue? / Do you have it in blue?
  3. Can I try it on?
  4. It's too expensive.
  5. I'd like to return this jumper. Here's the receipt.

5. Your turn

Write a six-line dialogue in a shoe shop: the assistant offers help, you ask for a size and a colour, you try them on, you ask the price and you pay.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. Assistant: Can I help you? Me: Yes, I'm looking for trainers. Have you got these in a size 41? Assistant: Yes. What colour would you like? Me: White, please. Can I try them on? … They fit! How much are they? Assistant: They're 65 euros. Me: OK, I'll take them. Can I pay by card?
Key points
  • Shops: the baker's, the butcher's, the chemist's, the bookshop, the supermarket; a shop / store, not a “magazine”.
  • Can I help you? — No, thanks. I'm just looking. / Yes, I'm looking for…
  • Have you got this in a medium / in blue? Can I try it on? It's too big. It suits you.
  • Paying: by card, in cash; Here's your change and your receipt; pay FOR something.
  • do the shopping (food, home) ≠ go shopping (for pleasure).
