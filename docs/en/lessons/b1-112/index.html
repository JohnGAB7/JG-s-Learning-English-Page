Phrasal verbs for work

In the office, English speakers constantly use phrasal verbs: they “set up a meeting”, “follow up” with a client, “hand in” a report. Knowing them helps you understand colleagues and write natural emails that sound less stiff than those using only formal verbs.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Know about thirty common work phrasal verbs, grouped by situation.
  • Know whether each one is separable and place the object correctly.
  • Choose between the phrasal verb (more common) and its formal equivalent depending on register.
  • Use them in real situations: meetings, projects, the phone, human resources.

Organising meetings

Meetings are planned, moved and cancelled with phrasal verbs. Be careful with “bring forward” (move to an earlier time) and “put off / push back” (move to a later time): they are opposites.

VerbMeaningTypeExample
set uporganise, arrangeseparableCan you set up a meeting with the client?
call offcancelseparableThey called the meeting off.
put off / push backmove to a later timeseparableLet's push it back to Thursday.
bring forwardmove to an earlier timeseparableWe've brought the deadline forward to Monday.
go over / run throughcheck or reviewinseparableLet's go over the figures before the meeting.
bring upstart talking about a topicseparableShe brought up the budget problem.
sum upgive a short summaryseparableTo sum up, sales are rising.
  • The director has called off tomorrow's meeting.
    call off = cancel.
  • Could we bring the call forward to 9 a.m.?
    Earlier than planned.

Doing tasks and projects

These verbs describe the work itself: taking responsibility, doing, examining, submitting. Several are inseparable (deal with, look into): the object always goes after the particle, even a pronoun (deal with it).

VerbMeaningTypeExample
carry outdo, performseparableWe carried out a survey of 500 customers.
deal withtake action on, handleinseparableI'll deal with the complaint.
look intoinvestigate, examineinseparableWe're looking into the problem.
take onaccept (work); employ (people)separableShe took on a new project. / We're taking on two engineers.
hand ingive a document to someone in chargeseparablePlease hand in your report by Friday.
work out / figure outfind the answer, understandseparableWe need to work out the cost.
follow up (on)take further action, check laterseparable / inseparableI'll follow up with the supplier. / I'll follow up on your request.
fall behindnot do something as fast as plannedno objectWe're falling behind schedule.
  • We need to look into why the sales fell in May.
    Inseparable: look into it.
  • I'm afraid we're falling behind with the Kinshasa project.
    fall behind (with): no direct object.

On the phone and by email

These expressions are essential for calls and correspondence. “Get back to someone” (reply or contact someone later) is one of the most useful phrases in a work email.

VerbMeaningExample
get back to (someone)reply or contact laterI'll get back to you by Monday.
get through (to)manage to contact by phoneI couldn't get through to the bank.
put (someone) throughconnect a phone callI'll put you through to Mr Mbarga.
hold on / hang onwait (on the phone)Hold on a moment, please.
call back / ring back (UK)phone againCan she call you back later?
catch up (on / with)do what you didn't do; exchange newsLet's catch up after lunch. / I need to catch up on my emails.
send outsend to many peopleWe sent out the invitations last week.
  • Thanks for your email. I'll get back to you as soon as possible.
    A standard email phrase.
  • Hold on, I'll put you through to the accounts department.
    A receptionist connecting a call.

Careers and human resources

For jobs, recruitment and leaving. “Lay off” means to end someone's job for economic reasons (not their fault); “fire” or “sack” (UK, informal) means to end someone's job because of their behaviour or performance.

VerbMeaningExample
apply forformally ask for (a job, a visa)She applied for a job in Toronto.
take overtake control or responsibility from someonePaul will take over from me next month.
hand overgive responsibility to someone elseI'll hand over my files before I leave.
step downleave an important jobThe CEO stepped down in May.
lay offend jobs for economic reasonsThe factory laid off 200 workers.
get on (well) withhave a good relationship withI get on well with my colleagues.
turn downrefuseHe turned down the promotion.
  • Who will take over when Mrs Ateba retires?
    take over = take responsibility.
  • I applied for three jobs in Montreal last month.
    apply for + job.

Register: phrasal verb or formal verb?

In an internal email, a meeting or a conversation, the phrasal verb is natural and friendly. In an official document, a contract or a very formal letter, the formal equivalent is often preferred.

Phrasal verbs are not “slang”: “carry out a survey” and “deal with a complaint” appear in very serious reports. But learners often choose the formal word (“postpone”, “investigate”) where a native speaker would say “put off”, “look into”.

Phrasal verb (everyday)Formal equivalent
call offcancel
put offpostpone
look intoinvestigate
carry outconduct, perform
hand insubmit
take on (staff)recruit, hire
get back toreply to, contact again
  • We're looking into what went wrong.
    Speech, email.
  • The company is investigating the incident.
    Official statement.

The formula

verb + particle (+ object): separable → set it up; inseparable → deal with it

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • The main stress is on the particle: call OFF, set UP, hand IN, look INto (first syllable of into).
  • Phrasal verbs link to the next word: carry out a survey /ˌkæri ˈaʊt ə ˈsɜːveɪ/, set it up /ˌse tɪ ˈtʌp/.
  • The noun “follow-up” is stressed on the first part: a FOLlow-up; the verb “follow up” on the particle: I'll follow UP.
  • The same rule for “set-up”, “handover”, “lay-offs”: nouns are stressed at the start, verbs on the particle.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ We need to advance the meeting.✓ We need to bring the meeting forward.To move a meeting earlier = bring forward.
✗ I'll deal it with.✓ I'll deal with it.deal with is inseparable.
✗ I'll come back to you tomorrow.✓ I'll get back to you tomorrow.To reply later = get back to you.
✗ The meeting was called out.✓ The meeting was called off.Cancel = call off.
✗ Can you set it the meeting up?✓ Can you set the meeting up? / Can you set it up?Only one object: the noun or the pronoun.
✗ She postulated for the job.✓ She applied for the job.Ask for a job = apply for (postulate = suggest a theory).
✗ We hand on our reports every Friday.✓ We hand in our reports every Friday.Submit a document = hand in.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

A team video call between Montreal and Douala. Sandra, the project manager, checks progress with Eric and Nadine.

Sandra

OK, let's start. First, let's go over the action points from last week.
go over = review.

Eric

I've carried out the customer survey. I'll send out the results this afternoon.
carry out; send out.

Sandra

Great. Nadine, did you deal with the supplier problem?
deal with (inseparable).

Nadine

Not completely. I couldn't get through to them yesterday. I'll follow up this morning.
get through; follow up.

Sandra

Thanks. Now, the client wants to bring the launch forward to 15 March.
Earlier date.

Eric

That's tight. We're already falling behind. Could we push it back a week instead?
fall behind; push back = later.

Sandra

I'll bring it up with them and get back to you by tomorrow.
bring it up; get back to you.

Nadine

And if they say no, we may need to take on a freelancer.
take on = employ.

Sandra

Good idea. To sum up: Eric sends the survey, Nadine calls the supplier, I talk to the client.
sum up = summarise.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Match

Give the phrasal verb for each formal verb.

  1. cancel
  2. postpone
  3. investigate
  4. submit (a report)
  5. recruit
  6. conduct (a survey)
Show the answers
  1. call off
  2. put off / push back
  3. look into
  4. hand in
  5. take on
  6. carry out

2. Complete

Complete with the right particle (up, off, with, into, forward, back, through, behind).

  1. The manager called ___ the meeting because half the team was ill.
  2. Can you look ___ why the invoice hasn't been paid?
  3. I tried to call Mr Ngono, but I couldn't get ___.
  4. We're falling ___ schedule. We need more people.
  5. The client asked us to bring the deadline ___ by two days.
  6. I'll get ___ to you after I speak to my boss.
Show the answers
  1. off
  2. into
  3. through
  4. behind
  5. forward
  6. back

3. Pronouns and word order

Replace the object in brackets with a pronoun and put it in the right place.

  1. Could you set up [the video call]?
  2. I'll deal with [the complaints] tomorrow.
  3. Please hand in [your timesheet] on Friday.
  4. The board turned down [our proposal].
Show the answers
  1. Could you set it up?
  2. I'll deal with them tomorrow.
  3. Please hand it in on Friday.
  4. The board turned it down.

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. We must advance the meeting to 10 a.m.
  2. I'll come back to you with an answer.
  3. She postulated for a job in Brussels.
  4. Can you look it into?
Show the answers
  1. We must bring the meeting forward to 10 a.m.
  2. I'll get back to you with an answer.
  3. She applied for a job in Brussels.
  4. Can you look into it?

5. Make it less formal

Rewrite with a phrasal verb.

  1. The meeting has been cancelled.
  2. I will connect your call to customer service.
  3. She has a good relationship with her new boss.
Show the answers
  1. The meeting has been called off. / They've called off the meeting.
  2. I'll put you through to customer service.
  3. She gets on well with her new boss.

6. Your turn

Write a short email (5–7 sentences) to your team giving an update on a project, with at least six phrasal verbs from this lesson.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. Hi all, Here's a quick update on the website project. We've carried out the user tests and Marie will send out the report tomorrow. Unfortunately, we're falling behind schedule, so I've asked the client to push the launch back to 30 June. I'm still looking into the hosting problem and I'll get back to you on Friday. Could someone set up a call with the designer next week? Thanks, Joël
Key points
  • Meetings: set up, call off, put off / push back, bring forward, go over, sum up.
  • Tasks: carry out, deal with, look into, take on, hand in, work out, follow up.
  • Phone / email: get back to, get through, put through, hold on, catch up.
  • Careers: apply for, take over, hand over, step down, lay off, get on with.
  • Inseparable: deal with it, look into it; separable: set it up, call it off.
