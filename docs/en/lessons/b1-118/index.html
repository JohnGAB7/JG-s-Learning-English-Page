Although, however, despite

To contrast two ideas, “but” is not always enough. “Although”, “however” and “despite” let you express contrast in a more precise way, in speech and in writing. But each one has its own grammar: a clause, a noun or a separate sentence.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Use although / though / even though + subject + verb.
  • Use despite / in spite of + noun, + -ing or + the fact that.
  • Place however and nevertheless correctly, with the right punctuation.
  • Change a sentence from one structure to another without changing the meaning.

Overview

All these words express contrast: the second idea is surprising or unexpected compared with the first. The difference is grammatical: what comes after the word.

A very common mistake is “despite of”: it does not exist. Say “despite” or “in spite of”.

WordFollowed byExampleRegister
buta clause (in the middle)It was raining, but we went out.neutral
although / thoughsubject + verbAlthough it was raining, we went out.neutral
even thoughsubject + verb (stronger)Even though it was raining, we went out.neutral
despite / in spite ofnoun or verb-ingDespite the rain, we went out.neutral / a bit formal
howevera new sentenceIt was raining. However, we went out.neutral / written
neverthelessa new sentenceIt was raining. Nevertheless, we went out.formal

Although, though, even though

These conjunctions introduce a full clause (subject + verb). They can go at the start of the sentence (followed by a comma) or in the middle.

“Though” is more common in speech. “Even though” makes the contrast stronger and is used for real facts. In speech, “though” can also go at the end of a sentence, meaning “however”: “It was expensive. I bought it, though.”

Be careful: don't use “but” in the same sentence as although (“Although it was late, but…” is wrong).

  • Although she was tired, she finished the report.
    although + clause, at the start.
  • I enjoyed the trip to Kinshasa, though it was very hot.
    though in the middle.
  • Even though he earns a lot, he never has any money.
    Stronger contrast.
  • The hotel was nice. The food wasn't great, though.
    though at the end = however (spoken).

Despite and in spite of

“Despite” and “in spite of” have exactly the same meaning. They are followed by a noun, a pronoun or a verb-ing, never directly by a clause.

To add a full clause, use “despite the fact that” / “in spite of the fact that” + subject + verb. Never write “despite of”.

PatternExample
despite + nounDespite the traffic, we arrived on time.
in spite of + nounIn spite of his age, he still plays football.
despite + verb-ingDespite feeling ill, she went to work.
despite the fact that + clauseDespite the fact that it was late, the shop was open.
  • We enjoyed the evening despite the rain.
    despite + noun, in the middle of the sentence.
  • In spite of working hard, he didn't get a promotion.
    in spite of + -ing.

However and nevertheless: linking two sentences

“However” is a linking adverb: it connects two separate sentences, not two clauses inside one sentence. It usually starts the second sentence, followed by a comma; it can also go in the middle, between commas.

Inside one sentence, you can use a semicolon: “It was raining; however, we went out.” But “It was raining, however we went out” (with only a comma) is considered incorrect in writing.

“Nevertheless” and “nonetheless” mean the same but are more formal: they suit reports and professional emails.

  • The project was difficult. However, the team finished it on time.
    however + comma at the start.
  • The new phone is expensive. It is, however, very fast.
    however in the middle, between commas.
  • Sales fell in March; nevertheless, the company made a profit.
    Semicolon + nevertheless.

Changing from one structure to another

It is very useful (and common in exams) to change from one structure to another. With despite, find a noun or an -ing form that matches the verb: “it rained” → “the rain”; “he was ill” → “his illness” / “being ill”.

althoughdespitehowever
Although it rained, we played.Despite the rain, we played.It rained. However, we played.
Although he was ill, he came.Despite being ill, he came.He was ill. However, he came.
Although she has a lot of experience, she didn't get the job.Despite her experience, she didn't get the job.She has a lot of experience. However, she didn't get the job.

The formula

although / even though + subject + verb · despite / in spite of + noun / -ing · Sentence 1. However, sentence 2.

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “although” /ɔːlˈðəʊ/: stress on the second syllable, voiced th /ð/, silent gh.
  • “though” /ðəʊ/ rhymes with “go”; don't confuse it with “through” /θruː/ or “thought” /θɔːt/.
  • “however” /haʊˈevə/: stress on the second syllable; in speech, make a short pause after it: However, | we…
  • “despite” /dɪˈspaɪt/: stress on the second syllable.
  • At the end of a sentence, “though” has a fall-rise tone: It was good, though.

Don’t saySayWhy

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Two friends, Didier and Sandrine, talk in Liège about Didier's new job.

Sandrine

So, how's the new job?
A friendly question.

Didier

I like it, although the hours are long.
although + clause.

Sandrine

Do you have to commute every day?
commute = travel to work.

Didier

Yes. Despite the distance, it only takes forty minutes by train.
despite + noun.

Sandrine

And your colleagues?
A short question.

Didier

Most are great. My manager is quite strict. However, she's always fair.
however starting a new sentence.

Sandrine

And the salary? You didn't seem sure about it.
Asking for more details.

Didier

It's lower than at my old job. I'm much happier, though.
though at the end.

Sandrine

That's what matters. Even though money is important, it's not everything.
even though + clause.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Choose the right word

Choose although, despite or however.

  1. ___ the bad weather, the match went ahead.
  2. ___ he studied hard, he failed the exam.
  3. The hotel was cheap. ___, it was very clean.
  4. She went to work ___ having a headache.
  5. ___ I don't like football, I watched the final.
  6. The plan is good. It is, ___, very expensive.
Show the answers
  1. Despite
  2. Although
  3. However
  4. despite
  5. Although
  6. however

2. Complete

Complete with the correct form after despite / in spite of (noun or -ing).

  1. Despite ___ (be) very young, she runs her own company.
  2. In spite of ___ (the / delay), we caught our connection.
  3. Despite ___ (not / speak) French, he found a job in Quebec.
  4. Despite ___ fact that it was Sunday, the shop was open.
Show the answers
  1. being
  2. the delay
  3. not speaking
  4. the

3. Transform

Rewrite with the word in brackets. Keep the same meaning.

  1. Although it was raining, they had a picnic. (despite)
  2. Despite being tired, he went out. (although)
  3. The car is old, but it works well. (however)
  4. She didn't get the job although she had good qualifications. (in spite of)
Show the answers
  1. Despite the rain, they had a picnic.
  2. Although he was tired, he went out.
  3. The car is old. However, it works well.
  4. She didn't get the job in spite of her good qualifications / in spite of having good qualifications.

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. Despite of the cost, we bought the house.
  2. Although he was rich, but he wasn't happy.
  3. Despite she was ill, she came to the meeting.
  4. Even I was late, they waited for me.
Show the answers
  1. Despite the cost, we bought the house.
  2. Although he was rich, he wasn't happy.
  3. Although she was ill, she came to the meeting. / Despite being ill, she came to the meeting.
  4. Even though I was late, they waited for me.

5. Join the ideas

Join the two ideas with the word in brackets.

  1. There was a strike. I was able to get to work. (despite)
  2. He is shy. He gave an excellent speech. (although)
  3. The restaurant was expensive. The meal was delicious. (however)
Show the answers
  1. Despite the strike, I was able to get to work.
  2. Although he is shy, he gave an excellent speech.
  3. The restaurant was expensive. However, the meal was delicious.

6. Your turn

Write a short paragraph (5 sentences) about the good and bad points of your town or job, with although, even though, despite and however.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. I love living in Douala, although the traffic is terrible. Despite the heat, there's always a nice breeze in the evening near the river. Rent is quite high. However, food is still affordable at the local markets. Even though I sometimes miss the calm of my village, I wouldn't move.
Key points
  • although / though / even though + subject + verb (Although it rained, …).
  • despite / in spite of + noun or -ing (Despite the rain / Despite being tired); never “despite of”.
  • despite the fact that + clause.
  • however / nevertheless: at the start of the second sentence + comma, or after a semicolon.
  • No but with although; “though” at the end of a sentence = however (spoken).
