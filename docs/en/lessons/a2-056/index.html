Should (giving advice)

We use “should” to give advice or a recommendation, and to say what is right or a good idea to do. It is an essential tool to help someone, give your opinion or ask for advice.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Form should / shouldn't + base verb and the question Should I…?
  • Give advice naturally with I think you should / I don't think you should.
  • Ask for advice (What should I do?).
  • Tell the difference between should (advice) and must / have to (obligation).

The form

“Should” is a modal verb, like must and will: it never changes (no -s) and it is followed by the base verb without to.

Negative: should not, short form “shouldn't”. Question: put should before the subject. Short answers: Yes, you should. / No, you shouldn't.

FormPatternExample
positivesubject + should + baseYou should rest.
negativesubject + shouldn't + baseYou shouldn't work so much.
questionShould + subject + base?Should I call the doctor?
Wh- questionWh- + should + subject + base?What should we do?
short answerYes, you should. / No, you shouldn't.Should I go? — Yes, you should.

Giving advice

“You should + base” is the simplest way to give advice. To be more polite or less direct, add “I think” or “maybe”: “I think you should…”, “Maybe you should…”.

For negative advice, English prefers “I don't think you should…” to “I think you shouldn't…”, as with will (lesson 49).

  • You look tired. You should go to bed early.
    Simple advice.
  • I think you should talk to your manager.
    Softer advice.
  • I don't think you should buy that car. It's too old.
    Negative advice: I don't think + should.
  • Maybe we should take a taxi.
    A gentle suggestion.

Asking for advice

To ask for someone's opinion, use “Should I…?” or “What / Where / When should I…?”. You can also ask “What do you think I should do?”.

Be careful: in “What do you think I should do?”, the word order after “do you think” is like a statement: I should do, not “should I do”.

  • Should I bring something to the party?
    Yes / no question.
  • Which course should I choose?
    Wh- question.
  • What do you think I should do?
    Statement order after do you think.

Should, must or have to?

“Should” is advice: you can choose. “Must” and “have to” are obligation (lesson 54). Compare: “You should wear a coat” (it's a good idea, it's cold) and “You must wear a helmet” (it's the rule).

“Ought to” means almost the same as should, but it is less common and a bit more formal: “You ought to apologise”. “You'd better + base” is stronger: if you don't do it, there will be a problem.

FormStrengthExample
shouldadviceYou should see a doctor.
ought toadvice (more formal, less common)You ought to see a doctor.
had better ('d better)strong advice, or there's a problemYou'd better see a doctor before it gets worse.
must / have toobligationYou must / have to see a doctor to get a certificate.

Should for what is right or expected

“Should” also expresses what is right or correct: a moral opinion or a criticism. It can also show a logical expectation: what will probably happen if everything is normal.

The fixed phrase “If I were you, I'd…” is another very common way to give advice; you will study its grammar at B1.

  • People shouldn't throw rubbish in the street.
    Opinion: it's wrong.
  • The bus should arrive in five minutes.
    Expectation: it will probably arrive.
  • If I were you, I'd accept the offer.
    Advice with a fixed phrase.

The formula

should / shouldn't + base verb · Should I + base …? · I (don't) think you should + base

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “should” is /ʃʊd/: the l is silent, and the vowel is short as in “book”. Weak form: /ʃəd/.
  • “shouldn't” is /ˈʃʊdnt/: two syllables, a light t.
  • “could”, “would” and “should” all rhyme: /kʊd/, /wʊd/, /ʃʊd/ (the l is never pronounced).
  • To make advice softer, the voice goes down and then up a little: “Maybe you should ↘ call her ↗”.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Inès, a student in Brussels, can't sleep before her exams. She talks to her friend Mariam.

Inès

I can't sleep at all. My exams start next week. What should I do?
Asking for advice.

Mariam

First, you shouldn't drink coffee after four o'clock.
Negative advice.

Inès

Oh. I drink three cups in the evening!
Surprise.

Mariam

That's too much. And I think you should turn off your phone an hour before bed.
Softer advice with I think.

Inès

Should I study at night when it's quiet?
Yes / no question with should.

Mariam

No, I don't think you should. Your brain needs rest. Study in the morning.
I don't think you should.

Inès

OK. Maybe I should go for a walk every day too.
Maybe I should = a gentle idea.

Mariam

Great idea! And if you still can't sleep, you should see a doctor.
Advice for the future.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Should or shouldn't?

Complete with should or shouldn't.

  1. You have a fever. You ___ go to work today.
  2. It's very cold. You ___ wear a warm jacket.
  3. You ___ eat so much sugar. It's bad for your teeth.
  4. We ___ leave now, or we'll miss the train.
  5. Children ___ watch TV all day.
Show the answers
  1. shouldn't
  2. should
  3. shouldn't
  4. should
  5. shouldn't

2. Give advice

Give advice with You should / You shouldn't.

  1. I've got a terrible headache.
  2. I always arrive late at work.
  3. My English isn't improving.
  4. I spend too much money.
Show the answers
  1. You should take an aspirin and rest. / You shouldn't look at your screen.
  2. You should get up earlier.
  3. You should practise every day and watch films in English.
  4. You shouldn't buy things you don't need.

3. Ask for advice

Write the question with should.

  1. I / take / an umbrella?
  2. what / we / buy / for Ali's birthday?
  3. where / I / stay / in Montreal?
  4. she / accept / the job?
Show the answers
  1. Should I take an umbrella?
  2. What should we buy for Ali's birthday?
  3. Where should I stay in Montreal?
  4. Should she accept the job?

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. You should to drink more water.
  2. Do we should book a table?
  3. I think you shouldn't worry.
  4. She shoulds see a dentist.
Show the answers
  1. You should drink more water.
  2. Should we book a table?
  3. I don't think you should worry.
  4. She should see a dentist.

5. Say it with should

Rewrite the sentence with should, shouldn't or What … should …?

  1. It's a good idea for you to call your sister.
  2. It isn't a good idea for us to come home too late.
  3. Tell me your opinion: what is the right thing for me to do?
Show the answers
  1. You should call your sister.
  2. We shouldn't come home too late.
  3. What do you think I should do?

6. Your turn

A friend is visiting your town for the first time. Give five pieces of advice (at least one negative and one with I don't think you should).

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. Welcome to Douala! You should try the grilled fish at Youpwe. You should always agree on the price before you take a moto-taxi. You shouldn't walk alone late at night in quiet areas. I don't think you should rent a car — the traffic is crazy! And you should visit the Doual'art gallery.
Key points
  • should / shouldn't + base verb = advice.
  • Questions: Should I…? / What should I do? (no do).
  • More polite: I think you should… / Maybe you should…; negative: I don't think you should…
  • should = advice (a choice); must / have to = obligation.
  • “should” is pronounced /ʃʊd/: the l is silent.
