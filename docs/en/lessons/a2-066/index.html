As … as: comparing equal things

After comparatives (lesson 46) and superlatives (lesson 47), here is how to say that two things are equal: as … as. In the negative, not as … as means “less … than”. This lesson also shows the same as and as much / as many as, and the common traps (“as … than”, “same than”).

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Make as + adjective / adverb + as to say two things are equal.
  • Use not as … as (or not so … as) as the natural way to say “less … than”.
  • Use the same (as), as much / as many … as, and similar to / different from.
  • Choose the right pronoun after as (as tall as me / as tall as I am).

The form: as + adjective + as

To say that two things are equal, put the adjective in its simple form between two as: Tom is as tall as his father.

The adjective never changes: no -er, no more. Say “as big as”, never “as bigger as” or “as more interesting as”.

The second word is as, not than. “As … than” is a very common mistake.

MeaningStructureExample
equalas + adjective + asLyon is as busy as Marseille.
equal (adverb)as + adverb + asShe runs as fast as her brother.
lessnot as + adjective + asMy flat isn't as big as yours.
almost equalalmost / nearly as … asIt's nearly as hot as yesterday.
two times moretwice as … asThis bag is twice as heavy as mine.
  • The film is as good as the book.
    Equal.
  • Kribi is as beautiful as I imagined.
    as + a full clause.
  • Can you speak as slowly as possible?
    as … as possible = the most … you can.

The negative: not as … as

Not as … as means “less … than”. It is the most natural way to say “less” in English: My car isn't as fast as yours is more common than “My car is less fast than yours”.

You can also hear not so … as, with the same meaning, but not as … as is more frequent.

Think about the meaning: The test wasn't as difficult as last year = it was EASIER than last year.

  • Today isn't as cold as yesterday.
    = Today is warmer than yesterday.
  • I'm not as good at maths as my sister.
    She is better than me.
  • The bus isn't as expensive as the taxi.
    The bus is cheaper.

After as: me or I am?

After the second as, you can put a noun, an object pronoun or a clause: as tall as Paul / as tall as me / as tall as I am.

In speech, the object pronoun (me, him, her, us, them) is the most common: She's as old as me. “As old as I” alone is very formal and sounds strange; if you want the subject pronoun, add the verb: as old as I am.

You can also compare two times: The town is as quiet as it was ten years ago.

  • My brother is as tall as me.
    Object pronoun (common).
  • My brother is as tall as I am.
    Subject pronoun + verb (careful).
  • He doesn't work as hard as he did before.
    Comparing two times.

As much / as many … as

To compare quantities: as much + uncountable noun + as, as many + plural countable noun + as (see lesson 58). I don't earn as much money as you. She has as many books as a library!

Without a noun, after a verb: He eats as much as his father.

Be careful: there is no of here: as many friends as, not “as many of friends as”.

  • There aren't as many tourists as in August.
    many + countable.
  • I don't drink as much coffee as before.
    much + uncountable.
  • You can take as many photos as you want.
    Any number you want.

The same as, similar to, different from

The same: My phone is the same as yours. With a noun: We're in the same class. Always use the before same, and as after it (never “same than” or “same that”).

The same + noun + as: She has the same shoes as me.

For things that are almost the same: similar to. For things that are not the same: different from (in British English you also hear different to).

  • Your bag is the same as mine!
    Identical.
  • We were born in the same year.
    the same + noun.
  • Belgian French is a little different from French in France.
    different from.

The formula

as + adjective / adverb + as · not as … as = less … than · as much / as many + noun + as · the same (+ noun) as · similar to · different from

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • as is weak in the sentence: /əz/. Say “əz TALL əz”, with the stress on the adjective.
  • In as … as, the two as are weak and linked: “as good as” ≈ /əz ˈɡʊd əz/.
  • After not as, stress the adjective: It isn't as EXpensive as the taxi.
  • the same /ðə ˈseɪm/: the “a” is /eɪ/, as in “name”.
  • twice /twaɪs/ ends with a voiceless /s/, not /z/.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ She is as tall than her mother.✓ She is as tall as her mother.as … as, never as … than.
✗ This film is as more interesting as the first one.✓ This film is as interesting as the first one.The adjective stays in its simple form.
✗ My phone is the same than yours.✓ My phone is the same as yours.the same as, never same than.
✗ We are in same class.✓ We are in the same class.Always the before same.
✗ I have as many of friends as you.✓ I have as many friends as you.No of after as many.
✗ He doesn't earn as many money as me.✓ He doesn't earn as much money as me.Money is uncountable: much.
✗ She is different of her sister.✓ She is different from her sister.different from (or to), not of.
✗ My town is less big than Douala.✓ My town isn't as big as Douala.Less big is possible but not natural.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Two flatmates in Ottawa compare two flats before they move.

Inès

So, what do you think? The flat on King Street or the one near the park?
A choice between two flats.

Samuel

The King Street flat isn't as big as the other one, but it's nearer the university.
not as … as = smaller.

Inès

True. And the rent is the same as our rent now.
the same as.

Samuel

The park flat is nearly twice as expensive! We can't pay that much.
nearly twice as … as.

Inès

But it's as quiet as a village. I can sleep as long as I want!
as … as (adjective and adverb).

Samuel

You don't sleep as much as you think, Inès.
as much as + clause.

Inès

Ha! OK. The bedrooms in King Street are similar to ours, right?
similar to.

Samuel

Yes, and the kitchen is as modern as the one in the park flat.
as modern as.

Inès

Then let's take King Street. It's not as nice, but it's cheaper.
not as … (the second part is understood).

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. As … as

Complete with as … as and the adjective or adverb in brackets.

  1. My sister is ___ my mother. (tall)
  2. This exercise is ___ the first one. (easy)
  3. He drives ___ a racing driver! (fast)
  4. Please answer ___ possible. (quickly)
  5. Is Yaoundé ___ Douala? (hot)
Show the answers
  1. as tall as
  2. as easy as
  3. as fast as
  4. as quickly as
  5. as hot as

2. Not as … as

Rewrite each sentence with not as … as. Keep the same meaning.

  1. Paris is bigger than Brussels.
  2. The train is faster than the bus.
  3. My old job was more interesting than this one.
  4. Anna sings better than me.
Show the answers
  1. Brussels isn't as big as Paris.
  2. The bus isn't as fast as the train.
  3. This job isn't as interesting as my old one.
  4. I don't sing as well as Anna.

3. Much, many or same?

Complete with much, many, the same or from.

  1. I don't have as ___ free time as last year.
  2. There weren't as ___ people as at the last concert.
  3. Her dress is ___ as mine!
  4. Life in Montréal is different ___ life in Dakar.
  5. You can eat as ___ cakes as you like.
Show the answers
  1. much
  2. many
  3. the same
  4. from
  5. many

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Correct it.

  1. He is as old than me.
  2. Our car is the same that yours.
  3. This book is as better as the film.
  4. We live in same street.
  5. I don't have as many money as you.
Show the answers
  1. He is as old as me.
  2. Our car is the same as yours.
  3. This book is as good as the film.
  4. We live in the same street.
  5. I don't have as much money as you.

5. What do you say?

Write one sentence for each situation.

  1. You and your brother are both 1.80 m.
  2. Yesterday it was 2°C. Today it's 8°C. (not as … as)
  3. You and your friend have one teacher: Mr Diallo.
  4. You want your friend to come very soon.
Show the answers
  1. My brother is as tall as me. / My brother is as tall as I am.
  2. Today isn't as cold as yesterday.
  3. We have the same teacher.
  4. Come as soon as possible.

6. Your turn

Compare yourself with someone in your family or a friend. Write four sentences: one with as … as, one with not as … as, one with the same (as) and one with as much / as many … as.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. My cousin Paul is as old as me: we were both born in 2001. He isn't as tall as me, but he's much stronger. We have the same favourite team. He doesn't read as many books as I do.
Key points
  • as + adjective / adverb (simple form) + as = equal.
  • not as … as = less … than (more natural than less … than).
  • as much + uncountable + as · as many + plural + as (no of).
  • the same (+ noun) as · similar to · different from: never “same than”.
  • After as: me / him (common) or I am / he is (careful).
