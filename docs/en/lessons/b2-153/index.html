The causative: “have / get something done”

When someone else does a job for you (a haircut, a car repair, a new kitchen), or when something bad happens to you (a stolen phone), English uses “have / get + object + past participle”. It is an everyday structure that learners often avoid or get wrong.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Make “have something done” in all tenses, with questions and negatives.
  • Understand the difference between “I cut my hair” (I did it) and “I had my hair cut” (someone did it for me).
  • Use “get something done”, which is more informal, and understand its idea of effort (“get the job done”).
  • Talk about a bad experience: “I had my phone stolen”.

Form: have + object + past participle

The word order is fixed: have + the thing + the past participle. “I had my car repaired.” The object always comes between “have” and the participle.

Here “have” is a normal main verb: it changes for every tense, uses “do / does / did” in questions and negatives, and does not contract. You cannot say “I'd my car repaired”.

To say who did the work, add “by”: “We had the house painted by a local company.”

TenseExampleMeaning
Present simpleI have my hair cut every month.a regular service
Present continuousWe're having the kitchen painted.work in progress now
Past simpleShe had her phone repaired.finished in the past
Present perfectHave you had your eyes tested?at any time up to now
Be going toI'm going to have my suit cleaned.a plan

NegativeI didn't have the car washed.did + not + have

Doing it yourself or having it done?

This is the key difference. “I cut my hair” means I used the scissors myself. “I had my hair cut” means I went to the hairdresser's.

Some languages say “make + verb” for this idea (for example French “faire réparer”). In English, “I made repair my car” is impossible.

We use this structure all the time for services: hairdressers, mechanics, tailors, dry cleaners, plumbers, doctors, opticians, photographers.

  • I repaired my bike. / I had my bike repaired.
    I did it myself. / Someone did it for me.
  • She's having a dress made for the wedding.
    A tailor is making it.
  • We had solar panels installed last year.
    Technicians installed them.
  • Where do you have your suits dry-cleaned?
    A question with do + have.

“Get something done”: more informal

“Get + object + past participle” has the same meaning as “have”, but it is more common in speech and messages: “I need to get my phone fixed.”

“Get” often adds an idea of effort, urgency or a result to achieve: “I must get this report finished by Friday.” Here, the speaker may do the work personally.

In the imperative, we prefer “get”: “Get your eyes tested!”

American English: in the present perfect, Americans say “I've gotten my car fixed”; in British English, “I've got my car fixed” or, more often, “I've had my car fixed”.

  • I'll get the tickets printed this afternoon.
    Someone will print them, or I will make sure it happens.
  • Let's get this job done and go home.
    Effort and result: let's finish it.
  • You should get that cough checked by a doctor.
    Advice.

A bad experience: “I had my wallet stolen”

The same structure can describe something unpleasant that happened to you, which you did not ask for.

You can also use a simple passive: “My wallet was stolen.” The “have” structure puts the focus on the person who suffered.

With “get”, you also hear “He got his nose broken in a fight” in informal speech.

  • I had my wallet stolen at the market.
    Someone stole it; I was the victim.
  • They had their house broken into while they were on holiday.
    Burglars entered their house.
  • She had her flight cancelled at the last minute.
    The airline cancelled it.

Don't confuse it with similar structures

When you name the person who does the action, the structure changes: “have + person + base form” or “get + person + to-infinitive”. “I had the mechanic check the brakes.” “I got my brother to help me.” These patterns are studied in lesson 186.

Watch the word order: “I had cut my hair” is a past perfect (I had cut it myself). “I had my hair cut” is the causative.

StructureExampleMeaning
have + thing + past participleI had my laptop repaired.Someone repaired it for me.
get + thing + past participleI got my laptop repaired.Same meaning, more informal.
have + person + base formI had a technician repair it.I arranged for a technician to do it.
get + person + to-infinitiveI got my cousin to repair it.I persuaded my cousin to do it.
had + past participle (past perfect)I had repaired it myself.I did it myself, before another past moment.

The formula

have / get + object + past participle (+ by + person)

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • Here “have” is a full verb: it is not reduced to 'd or 've. “I had my car washed”: /aɪ hæd maɪ ˈkɑː wɒʃt/.
  • The main stress usually falls on the last word that carries new information: “I had my car SERviced”, “I'm having my HAIR cut” when “cut” is obvious.
  • Past participles in -ed follow the usual rule: washed /wɒʃt/, cleaned /kliːnd/, painted /ˈpeɪntɪd/.
  • “cut”, “done” and “made” are irregular: cut /kʌt/ (same as the base form), done /dʌn/, made /meɪd/.
  • “hairdresser's” is /ˈheədresəz/: it means the hairdresser's shop.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ I made repair my car.✓ I had (got) my car repaired.English does not use “make + verb” for services.
✗ I cut my hair at the hairdresser's.✓ I had my hair cut at the hairdresser's.If someone else does it, use the causative.
✗ She had cut her hair yesterday at the salon.✓ She had her hair cut yesterday at the salon.The object goes between “have” and the participle.
✗ Did you had your passport renewed?✓ Did you have your passport renewed?After “did”, use the base form “have”.
✗ I'd my nails done this morning.✓ I had my nails done this morning.Causative “have” does not contract.
✗ I had stolen my wallet.✓ I had my wallet stolen.“I had stolen my wallet” means I stole my own wallet!
✗ We're going to have cleaned the carpets.✓ We're going to have the carpets cleaned.Always: have + object + participle.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Yaoundé, two friends, Nadège and Clarisse, are getting ready for the wedding of Nadège's brother on Saturday.

Clarisse

Are you ready for Saturday? Have you had your dress made yet?
Present perfect question with the causative.

Nadège

Almost. The tailor is finishing it today, but I need to have the sleeves shortened.
need to have + object + participle.

Clarisse

And your hair? Are you doing it yourself?
She asks if Nadège will do it herself.

Nadège

No way! I'm having it braided on Friday at a salon in Bastos.
Present continuous for an arrangement.

Clarisse

Good idea. My brother wants to get his car washed and decorated for the couple.
get + object + two participles.

Nadège

That's kind. Oh, and we're having the photos taken by a professional this time.
“by” names the person who does the work.

Clarisse

Last time you had your camera stolen, didn't you?
A bad experience.

Nadège

Exactly. So this year, I'm just going to dance!
A happy ending.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Who does the action?

Say if the person did the action themselves or if someone else did it for them.

  1. I cut my hair last night.
  2. I had my hair cut last night.
  3. She's having her kitchen painted.
  4. He had repaired his bike before the race.
Show the answers
  1. Myself.
  2. Someone else (a hairdresser).
  3. Someone else (painters).
  4. Himself (past perfect, not a causative).

2. Complete

Complete with “have” in the right tense + object + past participle.

  1. My car is making a strange noise. I need to ___ (it / check).
  2. Last week we ___ (our house / paint).
  3. She ___ (her nails / do) every two weeks.
  4. I'm ___ (a suit / make) by a tailor in Douala at the moment.
  5. ___ you ever ___ (your fortune / tell)?
  6. We're going to ___ (solar panels / install) next month.
Show the answers
  1. have it checked (or get it checked)
  2. had our house painted
  3. has her nails done
  4. having a suit made
  5. Have you ever had your fortune told?
  6. have solar panels installed

3. Transform

Rewrite with “have something done”, starting with the word given.

  1. A mechanic services my car every year. (I…)
  2. The optician tested my eyes yesterday. (I…)
  3. Someone stole Paul's phone on the bus. (Paul…)
  4. A photographer is going to take our wedding photos. (We…)
  5. A plumber has repaired our shower. (We…)
Show the answers
  1. I have my car serviced every year.
  2. I had my eyes tested yesterday.
  3. Paul had his phone stolen on the bus.
  4. We're going to have our wedding photos taken.
  5. We've had our shower repaired.

4. Correct the mistakes

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. I made repair my laptop.
  2. She had cut her hair yesterday at the salon.
  3. Did you had your passport renewed?
  4. I'd my car washed this morning.
Show the answers
  1. I had (got) my laptop repaired.
  2. She had her hair cut yesterday at the salon.
  3. Did you have your passport renewed?
  4. I had my car washed this morning.

5. Give advice

Give advice with “You should have / get + it / them + past participle”.

  1. My jacket is too big.
  2. My shower is leaking.
  3. I can't read the board in class.
  4. My suit has a coffee stain.
Show the answers
  1. You should have (get) it altered.
  2. You should have (get) it repaired (fixed).
  3. You should have (get) your eyes tested.
  4. You should have (get) it dry-cleaned.

6. Your turn

In 4 sentences, describe a service you pay for regularly, something you had done for you recently, and something you are going to have done soon.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. I have my hair cut every three weeks at a small salon near my office. Last month I had my car serviced and the tyres changed. I'm going to have my glasses repaired because one arm is broken. I'd also like to get my flat painted before the summer.
Key points
  • have / get + object + past participle = someone else does the job for you.
  • I cut my hair (myself) is not I had my hair cut (at the hairdresser's). Never “make repair”.
  • “have” changes normally, uses do / did and does not contract.
  • “get” is more informal and can add an idea of effort: get the job done.
  • “I had my phone stolen” = someone stole my phone.
