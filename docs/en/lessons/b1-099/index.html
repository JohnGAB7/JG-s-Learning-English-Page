The zero conditional (general truths)

The zero conditional (If you heat ice, it melts) describes what always happens when a condition is true: laws of nature, habits, rules and instructions. It uses the present simple in both parts.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Build the zero conditional with if / when + present simple, present simple.
  • Use it for scientific facts, habits, rules and instructions.
  • Tell it apart from the first conditional (one specific future situation).
  • Use an imperative or a modal in the result for instructions.

The structure

Condition: if (or when, whenever) + present simple. Result: present simple. Both parts are in the present because this is not a single event: it is true every time.

If can almost always be replaced by when or whenever without changing the meaning: “If / When you mix red and blue, you get purple.” This is the easiest test for a zero conditional.

Condition (if / when + present)Result (present)
If you heat water to 100 °C,it boils.
When it rains,the roads get slippery.
If I drink coffee after 6 p.m.,I can't sleep.
If you don't water plants,they die.

General and scientific truths

This is the best-known use: laws of nature and facts that are always true. It is very common in science, geography and economics lessons.

The structure is simple; the usual trap is adding will by mistake, or forgetting the -s of the third person.

  • If you heat ice, it melts.
    Always true.
  • Plants die if they don't get enough light.
    Result first, no comma.
  • When demand rises, prices go up.
    An economic rule.

Habits and personal reactions

We also use it for what always happens to us, our habits or reactions: “If I'm stressed, I eat chocolate.” It isn't a universal law, but it's true for me every time.

For a past habit, put both verbs in the past simple: “When I was a child, if it rained, we stayed inside and played cards.”

  • If I don't have breakfast, I feel tired all morning.
    A habit: every time.
  • My grandmother gets angry if we arrive late.
    A usual reaction.
  • When we lived in Garoua, if it was too hot, we slept on the terrace.
    Past habit: past simple in both parts.

Rules and instructions: imperatives and modals

To give a general instruction, the result is often an imperative: “If the alarm rings, leave the building.” You see this on signs, in manuals and in rules.

We can also use a modal (can, must, should): “If you are under 18, you can't vote.” / “If you feel dizzy, you should sit down.”

ContextExample
SafetyIf there is a fire, don't use the lift.
InstructionsIf the light turns red, press the reset button.
RulesIf you lose your card, you must pay a fee.
HealthIf you have a fever, drink plenty of water.

Zero or first conditional?

Zero: true every time, in general (present + present). First: one specific situation in the future (present + will).

“If you press this button, the machine stops” (that's how it always works). “If you press this button now, the machine will stop” (now, this one time).

A useful signal: if you can say “whenever” or “every time”, it's a zero conditional. With tomorrow, tonight or next week, it's a first conditional.

  • If you mix oil and water, they don't combine.
    Always true: zero.
  • If you mix these colours, your painting will look dark.
    This time, a predicted result: first.

The formula

If / When + present simple, present simple (If you heat ice, it melts.)

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • The third-person -s is necessary and must be heard: melts /melts/, boils /bɔɪlz/, rises /ˈraɪzɪz/. Three sounds: /s/, /z/, /ɪz/.
  • when /wen/ and if /ɪf/ are often weak at the start; the stress falls on the important verbs and nouns: “If you HEAT ICE, it MELTS.”
  • Intonation: rising at the end of the condition, falling at the end of the result.
  • whenever /wenˈevə/: stress on the second syllable.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ If you heat the ice, it melts.✓ If you heat ice, it melts.General truth: no article before an uncountable noun used in a general sense.
✗ If water freeze, it become ice.✓ If water freezes, it becomes ice.Don't forget the third-person -s.
✗ When I will be tired, I go to bed.✓ When I'm tired, I go to bed.Never will after when / if.
✗ If you will press the button, the door opens.✓ If you press the button, the door opens.Present simple in the condition.
✗ If it rains, people are taking umbrellas.✓ If it rains, people take umbrellas.A general habit: present simple, not present continuous.
✗ If there will be a fire, use the stairs.✓ If there is a fire, use the stairs.Instruction: if + present, imperative.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

A science class in a secondary school in Liège. Ms Dubois explains an experiment to her students, Théo and Aïcha.

Ms Dubois

What happens if you put salt on ice?
A question about a general truth.

Théo

It melts faster.
Present simple with -s.

Ms Dubois

Exactly. That's why the city puts salt on the roads when it snows.
When = every time.

Aïcha

And if the temperature is very low, does salt still work?
Zero conditional question.

Ms Dubois

Good question. If it's colder than about minus ten, salt doesn't work very well.
Negative result in the present.

Théo

In Cameroon, my cousins never see snow. If it rains a lot, the roads just get muddy.
Get muddy = become covered in mud.

Ms Dubois

That's right. Now, safety first: if you spill anything, tell me immediately.
Rule: if + present, imperative.

Aïcha

And if we finish early, can we try with sugar?
Can in the result: permission.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Match

Match the beginnings and endings: a) it turns into steam. b) I get a headache. c) you get green. d) leave the building.

  1. If you mix blue and yellow, …
  2. If water boils, …
  3. If I don't drink enough water, …
  4. If the fire alarm rings, …
Show the answers
  1. c) you get green.
  2. a) it turns into steam.
  3. b) I get a headache.
  4. d) leave the building.

2. Put the verbs in the right form

Put the verbs in the present simple (zero conditional).

  1. If you ___ (drop) a glass, it ___ (break).
  2. When my phone battery ___ (be) low, the screen ___ (get) darker.
  3. If people ___ (not / sleep) enough, they ___ (feel) irritable.
  4. My dog ___ (bark) if someone ___ (ring) the bell.
  5. If you ___ (not / pay) the bill, they ___ (cut) the electricity.
Show the answers
  1. drop … breaks
  2. is … gets
  3. don't sleep … feel
  4. barks … rings
  5. don't pay … cut

3. Zero or first?

Choose the correct form.

  1. If you heat metal, it (expands / will expand). (always)
  2. If you call me tonight, I (tell / will tell) you everything.
  3. When I'm nervous, I (talk / will talk) too much.
  4. If it rains tomorrow, the match (is / will be) cancelled.
Show the answers
  1. expands
  2. will tell
  3. talk
  4. will be

4. Correct the mistakes

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. If you press this button, the machine stop.
  2. When the sun will set, it gets cold in the desert.
  3. If there will be an emergency, call 112.
  4. If my brother is hungry, he is getting angry.
Show the answers
  1. If you press this button, the machine stops.
  2. When the sun sets, it gets cold in the desert.
  3. If there is an emergency, call 112.
  4. If my brother is hungry, he gets angry.

5. Make zero conditional sentences

Write a zero conditional sentence from the prompts.

  1. heat ice / melt
  2. when / I / drink coffee in the evening / not sleep
  3. lose your ticket / must / pay a fine
  4. have a fever / drink a lot of water (imperative)
Show the answers
  1. If you heat ice, it melts.
  2. When I drink coffee in the evening, I don't sleep. / I can't sleep.
  3. If you lose your ticket, you must pay a fine.
  4. If you have a fever, drink a lot of water.

6. Your turn

Write 4 zero conditional sentences: a scientific fact, one of your habits, a rule at your school or workplace, and a safety instruction (imperative).

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. If you freeze water, it turns into ice. If I go to bed late, I feel tired the next day. At my company, if you work on Saturday, you get a day off. If you smell gas, open the windows and leave the house.
Key points
  • If / When + present simple, present simple: what always happens.
  • Uses: laws of nature, habits, rules, instructions.
  • If can be replaced by when / whenever without changing the meaning.
  • Use an imperative or a modal in the result for instructions.
  • One specific future situation → first conditional (will).
