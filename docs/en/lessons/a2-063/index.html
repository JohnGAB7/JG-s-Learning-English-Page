Possessive pronouns (mine, yours…)

Mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs replace a possessive + noun so you don't repeat the noun (This is my bag → This bag is mine). They are simple: no article, no gender, no plural.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Know the possessive pronouns and tell them apart from possessive adjectives (my / mine).
  • Use them with no article and no apostrophe: mine, yours, hers (never “the mine”, “your's”).
  • Answer “Whose…?” with a possessive pronoun or 's (It's mine / It's Paul's).
  • Use the structure “a friend of mine”.

Possessive adjective or possessive pronoun?

A possessive adjective (lesson 14) goes BEFORE a noun: my car, your keys. A possessive pronoun stands ALONE, in place of possessive + noun: This car is mine (= my car).

In English, the possessive pronoun never changes: “mine” can mean my car, my keys, my shoes… The choice depends only on the owner, like his / her.

“His” is the same as an adjective and as a pronoun: It's his car. The car is his. “Its” is almost never used as a pronoun.

No article, no apostrophe

Don't put “the” before a possessive pronoun: say “mine”, never “the mine” or “the yours”.

Pronouns ending in -s (yours, hers, ours, theirs) NEVER have an apostrophe. “Your's” and “her's” are common mistakes, even for native speakers.

The verb agrees with the thing: My shoes are black. Mine are black / Yours are brown. Her car is red. Hers is red.

  • Is this your pen or mine?
    mine = my pen.
  • Your flat is bigger than ours.
    ours = our flat.
  • My parents live in Lille. Where do yours live?
    yours = your parents (plural verb).
  • These seats aren't theirs.
    No apostrophe.

Whose…? — It's mine / It's Anna's

To ask who something belongs to: “Whose is this?”, “Whose bag is this?”, “Whose are these?”. The natural answer uses a possessive pronoun or 's with no noun (lesson 15): It's mine. It's Anna's. They're my brother's.

The 's form works like a possessive pronoun: “It's Paul's” = it belongs to Paul.

  • Whose phone is ringing? — It's mine. Sorry!
    Answer with mine.
  • Whose are these gloves? — They're Karim's.
    Answer with 's.
  • Is this coffee yours or hers?
    Two pronouns.

A friend of mine

To talk about one of your friends, say “a friend of mine” (or “one of my friends”). You can't say “a my friend”. The structure is: a / an / this / that + noun + of + possessive pronoun (or 's).

It works with many nouns: a colleague of hers, a cousin of ours, that car of yours (often informal or a little critical), a friend of Paul's.

  • A friend of mine lives in Kigali.
    = one of my friends.
  • She's a colleague of my sister's.
    = one of my sister's colleagues.
  • Is that old bike of yours still working?
    that + noun + of yours.

Comparing and contrasting

Possessive pronouns are very useful to compare or contrast two owners, because you don't repeat the noun: My job is boring, but yours is exciting.

When we speak, we often stress them: It's not YOURS, it's MINE! With “too”: My phone is old. — Mine too!

  • My English is OK, but yours is excellent.
    Contrast.
  • Their garden is small; ours is huge.
    Contrast.
  • My train is late. — Mine too!
    Mine too = my train is also late.

The formula

my → mine · your → yours · his → his · her → hers · our → ours · their → theirs (no article, no apostrophe) · a friend of mine

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “mine” /maɪn/ rhymes with “fine”; “yours” /jɔːz/ sounds like “your” + z.
  • “hers” /hɜːz/, “ours” /ˈaʊəz/ (like “hours”), “theirs” /ðeəz/ (like “there's”).
  • Possessive pronouns are usually STRESSED, but possessive adjectives are often weak: It's MINE /maɪn/, but it's my bag.
  • The final -s sounds like /z/ in yours, hers, ours, theirs.
  • “Whose” /huːz/ sounds like “who's”.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

After a yoga class in Brussels, some people look for their things in the changing room.

Nora

Whose water bottle is this? It's blue.
Whose + noun.

Tom

It's not mine. Mine is green. Maybe it's Léa's.
mine; Léa's.

Léa

Yes, it's mine, thanks! And is this towel yours, Tom?
yours: no apostrophe.

Tom

No, mine is in my bag. I think it's Sami's. He always forgets his things.
mine / my bag: pronoun and adjective.

Nora

Ha ha! Oh, and these shoes? Are they yours?
Plural: they + yours.

Léa

No, they aren't. They're too big. They're probably the teacher's.
the teacher's = the teacher's shoes.

Tom

By the way, a friend of mine wants to join the class. Is there a place?
a friend of mine.

Nora

Yes, tell your friend to come next Monday. Our group is small, not like theirs on Saturday!
our group / theirs.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Adjective → pronoun

Write the possessive pronoun.

  1. my
  2. your
  3. her
  4. our
  5. their
  6. his
Show the answers
  1. mine
  2. yours
  3. hers
  4. ours
  5. theirs
  6. his

2. Replace

Replace the words in brackets with a possessive pronoun.

  1. This is [my coat].
  2. Is that [your car]?
  3. The blue house is [their house].
  4. These books are [her books].
  5. Our kitchen is bigger than [your kitchen].
Show the answers
  1. This is mine.
  2. Is that yours?
  3. The blue house is theirs.
  4. These books are hers.
  5. Our kitchen is bigger than yours.

3. Adjective or pronoun?

Choose the correct form.

  1. Is this (your / yours) phone?
  2. No, it isn't (my / mine).
  3. (Our / Ours) teacher is from Canada.
  4. That bike is (her / hers).
Show the answers
  1. your
  2. mine
  3. Our
  4. hers

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Correct it.

  1. Is this bag your's?
  2. Your phone is new, but the mine is old.
  3. She's a my colleague.
  4. Who is this pen? — It's my.
  5. These seats are their.
Show the answers
  1. Is this bag yours?
  2. Your phone is new, but mine is old.
  3. She's a colleague of mine. / She's one of my colleagues.
  4. Whose is this pen? — It's mine.
  5. These seats are theirs.

5. Rewrite the sentence

Rewrite with a possessive pronoun or “of mine / of hers…”.

  1. It isn't my bag, it's her bag.
  2. One of my friends works in Geneva.
  3. Whose keys are these? — They're our keys.
  4. Their house is bigger than our house.
Show the answers
  1. It isn't my bag, it's hers.
  2. A friend of mine works in Geneva.
  3. Whose keys are these? — They're ours.
  4. Their house is bigger than ours.

6. Your turn

Compare three things you own with the things of a friend or family member. Use at least three different possessive pronouns.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. My phone is quite old, but my sister's is brand new. Her car is red and mine is grey. Our flat is small, but my parents' house is big; theirs has a garden.
Key points
  • mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs replace possessive + noun.
  • They never change, take no article and no apostrophe: This is mine (not “the mine”, not “your's”).
  • Before a noun: my, your…; alone: mine, yours…
  • Whose is this? — It's mine / It's Anna's.
  • a friend of mine = one of my friends.
