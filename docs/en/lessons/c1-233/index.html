The subjunctive (I suggest he be…)

“The committee recommends that she be promoted”, “It is essential that every applicant sign the form”: English has a subjunctive. It is discreet but very present in formal, legal and American English, and much more limited than the subjunctive of languages like French or Spanish.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level C1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Form the mandative subjunctive: the base form for every person (he be, she sign, they not leave).
  • Use it after verbs, adjectives and nouns of demand or necessity (suggest, insist, essential, recommendation).
  • Know the alternatives: should + base form (British) and the indicative (informal British).
  • Recognise fixed subjunctive expressions (Be that as it may, If need be, Long live…) and avoid using a subjunctive where English has none (want, although, before).

The form: the base verb, with no -s

The English present subjunctive is simply the base form of the verb (the infinitive without to), for every person. So you only notice it in the third person singular (he sign, not he signs) and with be (I be, he be, they be).

The negative uses “not” before the base form, with no do: “that he not be informed”, “that they not leave”. The passive: “be + past participle” (that she be promoted).

The tense of the main clause does not matter: “They insist / insisted that he leave.”

IndicativeSubjunctiveExample
he signshe signWe ask that each member sign the register.
she isshe beThey recommend that she be appointed.
he doesn't attendhe not attendThe judge ordered that he not attend the hearing.
it is approvedit be approvedThe board requires that the budget be approved by March.
  • The doctor recommended that he take a week off.
    “take” with no -s: subjunctive.
  • It is vital that the data not be shared with third parties.
    Negative: not + be, no do.

After which words?

The mandative subjunctive appears in a that-clause expressing a demand, a recommendation or a necessity. “That” can be left out in speech, but it is almost always present in formal writing.

CategoryTrigger wordsExample
Verbssuggest, recommend, propose, insist, demand, request, require, ask, urge, adviseI propose that the meeting be postponed.
Adjectivesessential, vital, crucial, important, necessary, imperative, advisableIt is imperative that she arrive on time.
Nounssuggestion, recommendation, proposal, requirement, demand, insistenceHis suggestion that we hire more staff was rejected.
  • The union demands that the decision be reversed.
    Verb of demand + be + past participle.
  • It is essential that every passenger have a valid ticket.
    “have” with no -s after essential.

The alternatives: should and the indicative

In British English, the subjunctive is often replaced by “should + base form”: “They recommended that she should be promoted.” It is slightly less formal, but perfectly correct.

In everyday British English you also hear the indicative: “I suggest he calls them tomorrow.” This is accepted in speech and informal writing, less so in legal or academic texts.

In American English, the subjunctive is the norm, even in fairly neutral register: “I suggest he call them.” That is why you meet it so often in the American press.

FormRegister / varietyExample
Subjunctiveformal (everywhere), everyday (US)I suggest that he call them.
should + baseformal or neutral (mainly BrE)I suggest that he should call them.
Indicativeinformal (mainly BrE)I suggest he calls them.
-ingneutral, no specific subjectI suggest calling them.

Watch the double meaning: insist, suggest

“Insist” and “suggest” have two meanings. With a subjunctive (or should), they express a demand; with an ordinary indicative tense, they report a claim.

“She insisted that he leave” = she demanded that he go. “She insisted that he left” = she firmly claimed that he had gone. Similarly: “The report suggests that the policy be changed” (recommends) / “The report suggests that the policy has failed” (indicates).

  • The manager insisted that the invoice be paid immediately.
    A demand: subjunctive.
  • The manager insisted that the invoice had been paid.
    A claim: indicative (past perfect).

Fixed subjunctives and “were”

A few expressions keep an old subjunctive; they are fixed and formal: “Be that as it may” (nevertheless), “If need be” (if necessary), “Come what may” (whatever happens), “Suffice it to say” (it is enough to say), “So be it”, “God save the King”, “Long live…”, “Heaven forbid”.

The past subjunctive “were” (If I were you, I wish it were, as if he were) belongs to unreal meaning (lessons 159 and 160). In spoken British English “was” is common (If I was you), but “were” is still recommended in writing and expected in “If I were you” in careful English.

  • Be that as it may, we still need a decision by Friday.
    = Even so: a fixed expression.
  • We'll work through the weekend if need be.
    = if necessary; never “if need is”.

Where English has no subjunctive

Many languages use a subjunctive after “want that”, “it is necessary that”, “although”, “before”, “so that”… English does not: it uses other structures, usually an infinitive or the indicative.

The English subjunctive is therefore much rarer: keep it for formal demands and necessities (suggest, require, essential…).

MeaningWrong or heavyCorrect English
wish for someone to comeI want that you come.I want you to come.
obligationIt's necessary that you leave. (heavy)You have to leave. / You need to leave.
concessionAlthough he be rich…Although he is rich…
timeBefore he leave…Before he leaves…
doubtI doubt that he come.I doubt (that) he will come.

The formula

Verb / adjective / noun of demand or necessity + that + subject + BASE FORM (be, sign, not leave), whatever the tense of the main clause

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • The third person with no -s must be heard clearly: “that he take” /teɪk/, not “takes” /teɪks/; pronounce the end of the verb carefully so it does not sound like a mistake.
  • In “that she be promoted”, “be” is unstressed /bi/; the stress falls on “promoted”.
  • “insist” /ɪnˈsɪst/ and “require” /rɪˈkwaɪə/ are stressed on the second syllable.
  • “imperative” /ɪmˈperətɪv/ is stressed on the second syllable.
  • “If need be” is said in one breath: /ɪf ˈniːd bi/.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Ottawa, two board members draft the recommendations of an annual report.

Grace

For point one, I'd write: “The board recommends that the director's contract be renewed.”
Recommend + passive subjunctive.

Paul

Agreed. And for the audit, “It is essential that all accounts be checked by an external firm.”
Essential + subjunctive.

Grace

Should we say “should be checked”? It sounds less American.
The British alternative.

Paul

Both are fine, but let's be consistent. The rest of the report uses the subjunctive.
Consistency matters.

Grace

Fine. Then point three: “We request that each member not discuss the results before publication.”
Negative subjunctive: not + base.

Paul

Good. Some members insisted that the results had leaked last year, by the way.
“Insisted” = claimed: indicative.

Grace

Be that as it may, the rule is clear. If need be, we'll add a confidentiality clause.
Two fixed subjunctives.

Paul

Perfect. I'll propose that the clause be voted on in January.
Propose + passive subjunctive.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Spot the subjunctive

Say whether the verb in brackets is subjunctive or indicative.

  1. They demanded that he [resign].
  2. She insisted that he [was] innocent.
  3. It is vital that the patient [rest].
  4. The report suggests that sales [are] falling.
Show the answers
  1. subjunctive
  2. indicative
  3. subjunctive
  4. indicative

2. Complete with the subjunctive

Put the verb in brackets into the subjunctive.

  1. The committee recommends that she ___ (be) promoted.
  2. It is essential that each candidate ___ (bring) an ID card.
  3. We request that the payment ___ (not / be) delayed.
  4. The judge ordered that he ___ (pay) a fine.
  5. I propose that the meeting ___ (start) at nine.
Show the answers
  1. be
  2. bring
  3. not be
  4. pay
  5. start

3. Transform

Rewrite the sentence with the structure given.

  1. “You should take a holiday,” the doctor told him. (recommended that)
  2. Every visitor must wear a badge. (It is essential that)
  3. I suggest that he should call them. (subjunctive without should)
  4. They want the decision to be reversed. (demand that)
Show the answers
  1. The doctor recommended that he take a holiday.
  2. It is essential that every visitor wear a badge.
  3. I suggest that he call them.
  4. They demand that the decision be reversed.

4. Correct the mistake

Correct each sentence.

  1. I suggest her to apply for the job.
  2. I want that you call me tonight.
  3. Although she be young, she is very experienced.
  4. The rules require that every member pays on time. (legal register)
Show the answers
  1. I suggest (that) she apply / should apply for the job.
  2. I want you to call me tonight.
  3. Although she is young, she is very experienced.
  4. The rules require that every member pay on time.

5. Make it formal

Rewrite in formal English with the words in brackets.

  1. Every employee really must complete the training. (essential that)
  2. Even so, we must decide today. (Be that…)
Show the answers
  1. It is essential that every employee complete / attend the training.
  2. Be that as it may, we must decide today.

6. Your turn

Write three formal recommendations for an association or company, using three different triggers (verb, adjective, noun).

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. The committee recommends that the annual fee be reduced for students. It is essential that every new member receive a welcome pack within a week. Our main proposal is that the general meeting be held online as well as in person.
Key points
  • Subjunctive = base form for every person (he be, she sign, they not leave), after a demand or a necessity.
  • Triggers: suggest, recommend, insist, demand, require; essential, vital, imperative; suggestion, requirement…
  • Alternatives: should + base (formal or neutral BrE), indicative (informal BrE), -ing after suggest / recommend.
  • No subjunctive after want, although, before: I want you to come; Although he is…; fixed expressions: if need be, be that as it may.
