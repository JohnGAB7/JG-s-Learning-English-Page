Separable phrasal verbs

Many phrasal verbs (turn off, pick up, fill in…) allow their object to go in the middle: “turn the light off” or “turn off the light”. But with a pronoun there is no choice: “turn it off”. Mastering this word-order rule removes a very noticeable mistake.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B1

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Tell the difference between separable, inseparable and intransitive phrasal verbs.
  • Place a noun object (two possible positions) and a pronoun object (only one position) correctly.
  • Know about twenty very common separable phrasal verbs and their meanings.
  • Use a dictionary to check whether a phrasal verb is separable.

Three families of phrasal verbs

A phrasal verb combines a verb and a particle (up, off, on, out, down, back, away…). The meaning is often different from the verb alone: “give up” means “stop trying”, which has nothing to do with giving.

For word order, there are three families. Intransitive verbs have no object: get up, break down, grow up. Separable verbs allow the object between the verb and the particle. Inseparable verbs always have the object after the particle.

FamilyExamplesObject position
no object (intransitive)get up, break down, go out, grow up, set offno object: The car broke down.
separableturn off, pick up, fill in, put on, throw awayturn off the TV / turn the TV off / turn it off
inseparablelook after, get on (a bus), run into, come across, look forlook after the kids / look after them (never “look them after”)

The golden rule: pronouns go in the middle

With a separable verb and a NOUN object, both positions are correct: “Can you pick up the kids?” = “Can you pick the kids up?”.

With a PRONOUN object (it, them, him, her, me, us, you), the pronoun must go between the verb and the particle: “pick them up”, never “pick up them”. This is the most frequent mistake with phrasal verbs.

ObjectCorrectWrong
nounPut on your coat. / Put your coat on.—
pronounPut it on.Put on it.
nounI threw away the old shoes. / I threw the old shoes away.—
pronounI threw them away.I threw away them.
  • Turn the music down, please. — OK, I'll turn it down.
    Noun in the middle, then pronoun in the middle.
  • Could you fill in this form? — Sure, I'll fill it in now.
    Noun after the particle, pronoun in the middle.

Long objects

When the object is long (a noun with a lot of information, or a clause), put it after the particle. Putting it in the middle makes the sentence hard to follow, because the listener waits too long for the particle.

In practice: short object → both positions; long object → after the particle; pronoun → in the middle.

  • Please pick up the documents that I left on the reception desk.
    Long object → after up.
  • We need to sort out the problem with the delivery to our client in Kinshasa.
    Long object → after out.
  • She turned down the job offer from the bank in Abidjan.
    Long object → after down.

How do you know if a verb is separable?

There is no simple rule. The dictionary tells you: in the Cambridge Dictionary, “turn something off” (with something in the middle) shows a separable verb; “look after someone” (with someone at the end) shows an inseparable verb.

The same verb can change family with its meaning: “take off” (remove clothes) is separable (take it off), but “take off” (a plane leaving the ground) has no object (The plane took off at 9).

  • Take your shoes off, please.
    Separable.
  • Our plane took off two hours late.
    No object.
  • I ran into Fatima at the market.
    Inseparable: I ran into her.

The formula

verb + particle + noun · verb + noun + particle · verb + PRONOUN + particle (turn it off)

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • In a phrasal verb, the main stress usually falls on the particle: turn OFF, pick UP, give BACK.
  • When a pronoun is in the middle, it is weak and the stress stays on the particle: turn it OFF /ˌtɜːn ɪt ˈɒf/.
  • Consonant + vowel linking is natural: pick it up /ˌpɪk ɪ ˈtʌp/, turn it off /ˌtɜː nɪ ˈtɒf/.
  • When a noun follows the phrasal verb, the stress often moves to the noun: turn off the LIGHT.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Saturday morning in a flat in Brussels. Thomas and his flatmate Aminata are tidying up before their friends arrive.

Aminata

Can you turn the music down? I can't hear myself think.
Noun object in the middle.

Thomas

Sorry, I'll turn it down. What should I do with these old magazines?
Pronoun in the middle.

Aminata

Throw them away. And put your shoes away too, please.
throw them away; put your shoes away.

Thomas

OK. Oh, your mum called. She asked you to call her back.
call her back.

Aminata

I'll call her back later. Did you pick up the cake?
Noun after the particle.

Thomas

Not yet. I'll pick it up at eleven. The baker wanted to put it off until noon, but I said no.
pick it up; put it off = postpone.

Aminata

Good. And I've set up the speaker on the balcony.
set up = install.

Thomas

Great — but don't turn it up too much. The neighbours will complain!
turn it up = make it louder.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Separable or not?

Is the verb in CAPITALS separable (S), inseparable (I) or without an object (0)?

  1. Please TURN OFF the lights when you leave.
  2. Who will LOOK AFTER your cat?
  3. My car BROKE DOWN on the way to Kribi.
  4. I need to PICK UP my sister from school.
  5. I CAME ACROSS an old photo of my grandparents.
Show the answers
  1. S
  2. I
  3. 0
  4. S
  5. I

2. Use a pronoun

Rewrite the sentence, replacing the object in brackets with a pronoun.

  1. Can you put on [your jacket]?
  2. I threw away [the old letters].
  3. She gave back [the money] last week.
  4. Could you write down [the address]?
  5. We'll pick up [you and your brother] at the station.
  6. Please fill in [the form] in capital letters.
Show the answers
  1. Can you put it on?
  2. I threw them away.
  3. She gave it back last week.
  4. Could you write it down?
  5. We'll pick you up at the station.
  6. Please fill it in in capital letters.

3. Choose the right verb

Complete with: turn down, look up, put off, sort out, try on, set up. Use the right form and place the pronoun if needed.

  1. These shoes look nice. Can I ___ them ___?
  2. I don't know what “bargain” means. I'll ___ it ___.
  3. The meeting is cancelled. They've ___ it ___ until next week.
  4. She got a job offer from Google, but she ___ it ___.
  5. My brother ___ his own company in Douala in 2021.
  6. There's a problem with your bill, but I'll ___ it ___ today.
Show the answers
  1. try … on
  2. look … up
  3. put … off
  4. turned … down
  5. set up
  6. sort … out

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one word-order mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. My alarm went off but I turned off it.
  2. Can you look my plants after while I'm away?
  3. I'll call back you in ten minutes.
  4. Don't let down me!
Show the answers
  1. My alarm went off but I turned it off.
  2. Can you look after my plants while I'm away?
  3. I'll call you back in ten minutes.
  4. Don't let me down!

5. Replace the formal verb

Rewrite with a phrasal verb and a pronoun.

  1. Switch the TV off. (use “it”)
  2. I'll collect you at eight.
  3. They postponed the wedding.
Show the answers
  1. Turn it off, please.
  2. I'll pick you up at eight.
  3. They put the wedding off. / They put off the wedding. / They put it off.

6. Your turn

Show the answers
  1. Last Saturday we moved to a new flat in Lyon. In the morning I picked up the van and my friends helped me. We took the old furniture apart and threw most of it away. My sister found some old letters, but I didn't want to throw them away, so I put them away in a box. Key points
  • Separable verb + noun: two positions (turn off the TV / turn the TV off).
  • Separable verb + pronoun: always in the middle (turn it off, pick them up).
  • Long object: after the particle.
  • Inseparable verbs (look after, run into, come across): object always after the particle.
  • The dictionary shows the position: turn something off (separable) / look after someone (inseparable).
