The future continuous

The future continuous (will be + -ing) describes an action that will be in progress at a specific time in the future: “This time tomorrow, I'll be flying to Douala.” We also use it for actions that will happen as part of the normal course of events, and to ask politely about someone's plans.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Form the future continuous in the affirmative, negative and question forms.
  • Describe an action in progress at a specific future time (this time tomorrow, at 8 p.m. on Friday).
  • Talk about an action that will happen anyway, as part of the normal course of events.
  • Ask a polite question about someone's plans (Will you be using the car tonight?) and tell it apart from the future simple.

Form: will be + -ing

We make the future continuous with will be (the same for every subject) and the -ing form of the verb: I will be working, she will be working. In speech: I'll be, she'll be; negative: won't be + -ing.

Question: Will + subject + be + -ing? Short answer: Yes, I will. / No, I won't.

FormStructureExample
Affirmativesubject + will be + -ingAt ten tomorrow, I'll be sitting my exam.
Negativesubject + won't be + -ingWe won't be watching the match tonight.
QuestionWill + subject + be + -ing?Will you be working on Saturday?
Short answerYes, subject + will. / No, subject + won't.Will she be coming? – Yes, she will.

Use 1: an action in progress at a future time

We imagine a specific moment in the future and describe what will be happening then: the action will start before that moment and continue after it. It's the same idea as the present continuous (now) or the past continuous (at a moment in the past), but in the future.

Common signals: this time tomorrow / next week, at + time + day, when + present simple, all day / all evening tomorrow.

  • This time next week, I'll be lying on a beach in Kribi.
    In progress at that moment next week.
  • Don't call at eight. We'll be having dinner.
    Dinner will be in progress at eight.
  • When you arrive, I'll be waiting for you at the station.
    When + present simple.
  • Tomorrow afternoon, the team will be presenting the project to the client.
    An action in progress tomorrow afternoon.

Use 2: what will happen anyway

The future continuous can also present a future action as a matter of course, part of the normal plan, with no idea of decision or intention: “I'll be seeing Paul at work tomorrow, so I can give him the book.” (I'll see him anyway; he's my colleague.)

This meaning is very common in announcements: “We'll be landing in Montreal in twenty minutes.” / “The shop will be closing in ten minutes.”

  • I'll be passing the pharmacy on my way home. Do you need anything?
    I'm going past it anyway.
  • Ladies and gentlemen, we'll be arriving in Brussels in fifteen minutes.
    An announcement on a train.

Use 3: a polite question about plans

“Will you be + -ing…?” is a polite way to ask about someone's plans without sounding as if you are asking for a favour or putting pressure on them. It often prepares a request.

Compare: “Will you come to the meeting?” (a request or invitation) / “Will you be coming to the meeting?” (a simple question: is it planned?).

Future simple (will + verb)Future continuous (will be + -ing)
Will you use the car tonight? (a request, maybe a complaint)Will you be using the car tonight? (neutral: could I borrow it?)
Will you help me? (asking for help)Will you be staying long? (just information)
I'll call you at six. (a promise, a decision)I'll be driving at six. (in progress at that time)
  • Will you be needing the meeting room this afternoon?
    A polite question before asking for the room.

Limits and rules

As with all continuous tenses, state verbs (know, like, want, believe, own, belong) are not used in the future continuous: “I'll know the results tomorrow” (not “I'll be knowing”).

After when, if, as soon as and until, use a present tense (simple or continuous), never will be: “When I'm working in Toronto, I'll visit you” (not “When I'll be working”).

  • I'll know the answer tomorrow.
    State verb: future simple.

The formula

will be + verb-ing (This time tomorrow, I'll be flying to Douala.)

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • I'll be sounds like /aɪl bi/: the 'll is light but must be heard. Without it, you would say “I be”, which is wrong.
  • won't be /wəʊnt bi/: the t joins the b and is almost silent.
  • In a polite question, the voice rises gently at the end: “Will you be using the car tonight ↗?”
  • The stress falls on the -ing verb and the time expression: “This time TOMORROW, I'll be FLYING.”

In a conversation

Listening Studio

A Friday in Paris. Clémence is organising a video call with her colleague Joseph, who is from Douala and works in Montreal.

Clémence

Joseph, will you be working on Monday morning? I'd like to set up a call.
A polite question about plans.

Joseph

Yes, but at nine I'll be visiting a client in Laval.
In progress at nine.

Clémence

What about eight, before your visit?
What about…? = a suggestion.

Joseph

At eight I'll probably be driving to work. Could we say eleven, my time?
Probably goes after will.

Clémence

Eleven in Montreal is five o'clock in Paris. I'll be finishing a meeting then, but it's fine.
In progress at that time.

Joseph

Perfect. Will you be sending the documents before the call?
Polite question.

Clémence

Yes, I'll be writing the report all weekend, so you'll have it on Sunday night.
All weekend + future continuous.

Joseph

Great. I'll be waiting for it!
A friendly ending.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Recognise the use

Say if the sentence describes an action in progress at a future time (A), an action that will happen anyway (B) or a polite question (C).

  1. At midnight, we'll be dancing at the party.
  2. Will you be using your laptop this evening?
  3. I'll be seeing Fatou at the meeting anyway.
  4. This time next year, she'll be studying in Montreal.
Show the answers
  1. A
  2. C
  3. B
  4. A

2. Complete

Put the verb in the future continuous.

  1. At eight tomorrow, I ___ (travel) to Ghent.
  2. Don't phone at six; the children ___ (have) their bath.
  3. ___ you ___ (use) the car tomorrow?
  4. We ___ (not / work) on Monday; it's a holiday.
  5. This time next month, they ___ (live) in their new house.
Show the answers
  1. will be travelling ('ll be travelling)
  2. will be having
  3. Will … be using
  4. won't be working
  5. will be living

3. Future simple or future continuous?

Choose the most natural form.

  1. I promise I (will call / will be calling) you tonight.
  2. At 3 p.m. tomorrow, I (will take / will be taking) my driving test.
  3. I (will know / will be knowing) the results on Friday.
  4. It's heavy. – Don't worry, I (will carry / will be carrying) it.
Show the answers
  1. will call
  2. will be taking
  3. will know
  4. will carry

4. Correct the mistakes

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. This time tomorrow I will sleeping.
  2. When I will be travelling, I'll send you photos.
  3. Will you be come to the party?
  4. She'll be wanting a coffee when she arrives.
Show the answers
  1. This time tomorrow I will be sleeping.
  2. When I'm travelling, I'll send you photos.
  3. Will you be coming to the party?
  4. She'll want a coffee when she arrives.

5. What will be happening?

Write one sentence in the future continuous for each situation.

  1. Your flight to Montreal leaves tomorrow at this time.
  2. Tell a friend not to call at noon because of your work with a client.
  3. Ask your flatmate politely about his bike plans for the weekend.
  4. You are a pilot. Announce the landing in Brussels in 20 minutes.
Show the answers
  1. This time tomorrow, I'll be flying to Montreal. / I'll be sitting on the plane to Montreal.
  2. Don't call at noon: I'll be working with a client. / I'll be having a meeting.
  3. Will you be using your bike this weekend?
  4. We'll be landing in Brussels in twenty minutes.

6. Your turn

In 4–5 sentences, describe what you will be doing tomorrow at different times (8 a.m., noon, 3 p.m., 8 p.m.), then ask a friend a polite question with Will you be…?

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. At eight tomorrow, I'll be taking the bus to work. At noon, I'll be having lunch with my colleagues. At three, I'll be giving a presentation to our new clients. At eight in the evening, I'll be watching the match at home. Will you be watching it too?
Key points
  • Form: will be + -ing; negative won't be + -ing.
  • An action in progress at a specific future time: This time tomorrow, I'll be flying.
  • An action that will happen anyway: I'll be seeing him at work anyway.
  • A polite question about plans: Will you be using the car tonight?
  • No state verbs in the future continuous, and no will after when / if.
