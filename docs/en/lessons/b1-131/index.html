Explaining a problem and proposing a solution

At work, at school or in daily life, you often need to report a problem clearly, explain its cause and its consequences, and then suggest a solution without sounding aggressive. This lesson gives you the phrases and structures to do it naturally, in speech and in an email.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Present a problem clearly and politely (There's a problem with…, I'm afraid…).
  • Explain the cause and the consequences (because of, due to, as a result, so).
  • Suggest one or more solutions with could, why don't we, how about, I suggest, if we…
  • React to a suggestion and agree on the next step.

Announcing the problem

English speakers often “soften” bad news with I'm afraid, unfortunately, it seems that or I think we have a small problem. This shows that you are calm and want to cooperate, not to blame anyone.

Then describe the problem with precise facts: what, when, since when. The present perfect is very useful for a problem that continues (The printer hasn't worked since Monday) or that has just happened (The client has cancelled the order).

PhraseRegisterExample
There's a problem with…neutralThere's a problem with the delivery.
I'm afraid (that)…politeI'm afraid the report won't be ready on time.
Unfortunately,…polite, writtenUnfortunately, the system crashed this morning.
It seems that…carefulIt seems that some emails haven't arrived.
We've got a bit of a problem.informal, softenedWe've got a bit of a problem with the budget.
The thing is,…spokenThe thing is, the room is too small for 30 people.

Explaining the cause and the consequences

For the cause: because + clause (because the supplier is late), because of / due to + noun (because of the strike, due to bad weather). Due to is more formal and very common in announcements and emails.

For the consequences: so (spoken), as a result, this means that, which means that. “The train was cancelled, which means that I'll be two hours late.”

  • The flight was delayed due to a technical problem.
    Formal cause: due to + noun.
  • We can't start the meeting because the projector isn't working.
    Because + subject + verb.
  • The server was down all morning. As a result, we lost several orders.
    Consequence in a new sentence.
  • Two people are ill, which means that we'll need extra help on Saturday.
    Which means that links the consequence to the whole idea before.

Suggesting a solution

Suggest a solution with polite forms that leave room for discussion: We could…, Why don't we…?, How about / What about + -ing?, I suggest (that) we…, One option would be to…, If we…, we could…

Be careful with suggest: I suggest that we leave early / I suggest leaving early, but never “I suggest us to leave”. After how about / what about, use -ing: How about calling the supplier?

StructureExample
We could + base verbWe could ask another supplier.
Why don't we + base verb?Why don't we move the meeting to Thursday?
How about / What about + -ing?How about working from home tomorrow?
I suggest (that) we + base verbI suggest (that) we call the client today.
I suggest + -ingI suggest postponing the launch.
One solution would be to + base verbOne solution would be to hire a temporary assistant.
If we…, we could / we'd…If we started earlier, we'd finish on time.
  • The room is too small. Why don't we book the conference hall instead?
    A suggestion in question form.
  • If we send the documents by email, the client will get them today.
    First conditional for a realistic solution.

Reacting and agreeing

To accept: That's a good idea. / That sounds good. / That could work. / Let's do that. To express a doubt: That might be difficult because… / I'm not sure that would work. / The only problem is…

To finish, sum up the decision and the next step: So, to sum up, we'll… / OK, so I'll call them and you'll update the file. / Let's meet again on Friday to check.

  • That could work, but the only problem is the cost.
    Agreement with a reservation.
  • OK, so I'll contact the supplier and you'll inform the team.
    Sharing out the tasks.

In writing: the structure of an email

An email that reports a problem often follows four steps: 1) the context (I'm writing about / regarding…), 2) the problem (Unfortunately…), 3) the proposed solution (I would suggest… / Would it be possible to…?), 4) the next step (Please let me know if this works for you).

Stay factual, avoid direct blame (You didn't send…) and prefer neutral or passive sentences (The documents haven't been received yet).

  • I'm writing regarding order no. 4521. Unfortunately, two items were missing.
    Context, then the problem.
  • Would it be possible to send them by Friday? Please let me know if this works for you.
    Solution and next step.

The formula

I'm afraid there's a problem with… · because of / due to… · We could… / Why don't we…? / How about + -ing? / I suggest (that) we…

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • I'm afraid /aɪm əˈfreɪd/: stress on -fraid; I'm links smoothly to afraid.
  • In “Why don't we…?”, don't is often reduced: /waɪ dəʊn wi/. The voice goes down at the end, because it is a suggestion, not a real question.
  • suggest is /səˈdʒest/ in British English (often /səɡˈdʒest/ in American English); stress on the second syllable.
  • due to /ˈdjuː tə/ (UK) or /ˈduː tə/ (US).
  • To sound softer, speak a little more slowly and stress the positive words: “That's a GOOD idea, but…”

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ I suggest you to call the client.✓ I suggest (that) you call the client. / I suggest calling the client.Suggest + that + subject + verb, or suggest + -ing; never suggest + person + to.
✗ How about to move the meeting?✓ How about moving the meeting?How about / What about + -ing.
✗ The flight was cancelled due to the weather was bad.✓ The flight was cancelled due to bad weather. / … because the weather was bad.Due to / because of + noun; because + clause.
✗ Why we don't take a taxi?✓ Why don't we take a taxi?Question word order: Why don't we…?
✗ We have a problem of delivery.✓ We have a problem with the delivery.A problem with + noun.
✗ The printer doesn't work since Monday.✓ The printer hasn't worked since Monday.A problem that started in the past and continues: present perfect.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

A small travel agency in Dakar. Aïssatou, the manager, and her colleague Julien, from Lyon, discover a booking problem.

Julien

Aïssatou, I'm afraid there's a problem with the group booking for Saint-Louis.
Softened announcement: I'm afraid.

Aïssatou

What's happened?
Present perfect: a recent event with a result now.

Julien

The hotel has cancelled ten rooms due to renovation work. So twenty people have nowhere to stay.
Cause (due to) and consequence (so).

Aïssatou

Oh no. The group arrives on Friday, which means we only have three days.
Which means: the consequence.

Julien

We could book rooms in the guesthouse next door. It's small, but it's very nice.
First suggestion: we could.

Aïssatou

That could work. Why don't we also ask the hotel for a discount on the other rooms?
Reaction, then a second suggestion.

Julien

Good idea. I suggest we call them this morning.
I suggest + we + base verb.

Aïssatou

OK. So you'll call the guesthouse and I'll speak to the hotel manager. Let's check again at two.
Sharing the tasks and fixing the next step.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Problem, cause or solution?

Say if the sentence announces a problem (P), explains a cause (C) or suggests a solution (S).

  1. I'm afraid the delivery hasn't arrived.
  2. It's because of the strike at the port.
  3. How about asking another company?
  4. The meeting was cancelled due to illness.
  5. We could work on Saturday morning.
Show the answers
  1. P
  2. C
  3. S
  4. C
  5. S

2. Complete

Complete with because, because of, due to, so or which means that.

  1. The shop is closed ___ the public holiday.
  2. I can't come ___ my car has broken down.
  3. The bus was late, ___ I missed the start of the class.
  4. Two drivers are ill, ___ some deliveries will be late.
Show the answers
  1. because of (due to)
  2. because
  3. so
  4. which means that (so)

3. Suggest a solution

Rewrite the suggestion with the structure in brackets.

  1. Let's change the date. (Why don't we…?)
  2. Let's take the train. (How about…?)
  3. We should call the technician. (I suggest that…)
  4. Hire a temporary assistant. (One solution would be…)
Show the answers
  1. Why don't we change the date?
  2. How about taking the train?
  3. I suggest that we call the technician.
  4. One solution would be to hire a temporary assistant.

4. Correct the mistakes

Each sentence has one mistake. Correct it.

  1. I suggest you to send the file again.
  2. What about to meet on Thursday?
  3. The internet doesn't work since yesterday.
  4. There's a problem of the heating.
Show the answers
  1. I suggest (that) you send the file again. / I suggest sending the file again.
  2. What about meeting on Thursday?
  3. The internet hasn't worked since yesterday.
  4. There's a problem with the heating.

5. Make it more polite

Rewrite each direct sentence in a softer, more professional way.

  1. The report isn't ready for Friday. (I'm afraid…)
  2. The flight was cancelled. The weather was bad. (due to)
  3. Move the meeting to Monday! (Why don't we…?)
  4. Ask the client what they think! (I suggest…)
Show the answers
  1. I'm afraid the report won't be ready on Friday.
  2. The flight was cancelled due to bad weather.
  3. Why don't we postpone the meeting until Monday? / Why don't we move the meeting to Monday?
  4. I suggest (that) we ask the client's opinion. / I suggest asking the client.

6. Your turn

Write a short email (5–6 sentences) to a colleague: the projector in the meeting room isn't working and the client presentation is tomorrow. Announce the problem, explain the consequence, suggest two solutions and finish.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. Hi Marc, I'm afraid there's a problem with the projector in the meeting room: it hasn't worked since this morning. This means that we can't show our slides to the client tomorrow. We could book the small conference room, which has a screen. Another option would be to send the slides to the client in advance and present them on a laptop. Could you let me know which you prefer by 5 p.m.? Thanks, Awa
Key points
  • Announce: I'm afraid… / Unfortunately… / There's a problem with…
  • Cause: because + clause; because of / due to + noun.
  • Consequence: so, as a result, which means that…
  • Solution: We could… / Why don't we…? / How about + -ing? / I suggest (that) we… / I suggest + -ing.
  • Finish: So, I'll… and you'll… / Let's check again on…
