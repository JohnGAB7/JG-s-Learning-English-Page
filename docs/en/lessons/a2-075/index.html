Sequencing words: so, then, after that

To tell a story, explain a recipe or describe your day, you need to put the steps in order (first, then, after that, finally) and show results (so). These small words make a story clear and natural.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Put the steps of a story in order with first, then, next, after that, later, finally.
  • Show a result with so and know how it is different from because.
  • Use before and after correctly (+ noun, + -ing, + clause), and don't say “After, we…”.
  • Choose between finally, in the end, at the end and eventually.

Putting steps in order

Sequencing words usually go at the start of the sentence, with a comma after them in writing (the comma is optional after “then”).

Start with “First” or “First of all”, continue with “Then”, “Next”, “After that”, “Later”, and finish with “Finally”.

“Then” is the most common in speech, often as “and then”. Use different words: “then… then… then…” sounds heavy.

WordUseExample
First / First of allthe first stepFirst, I had a shower.
Then / And thenthe next stepThen I made breakfast.
Nextthe next step (instructions)Next, add the onions.
After that / Afterwardsafter the step beforeAfter that, we went to the beach.
Later / Later onsome time afterLater, my cousins arrived.
Finallythe last stepFinally, we had dinner.
  • First, we visited the old town. Then we had lunch by the river.
    Two steps.
  • After that, we took the train back to Namur.
    The next step.
  • Finally, we got home at midnight.
    The last step.

So: the result

“So” introduces the result of a situation. We often put a comma before it: “It was raining, so we stayed at home.”

“So” and “because” show the same link in opposite directions: “because” introduces the cause, “so” the result. “We stayed at home because it was raining.” = “It was raining, so we stayed at home.”

In careful writing, don't start a new sentence with “So” to link it to the sentence before. In speech, “So, …” at the start is also used to begin or sum up: “So, how was your trip?”

CauseResult with soSame idea with because
I was tired.I was tired, so I went to bed early.I went to bed early because I was tired.
The bus didn't come.The bus didn't come, so I took a taxi.I took a taxi because the bus didn't come.
She's ill.She's ill, so she isn't coming.She isn't coming because she's ill.
  • The shop was closed, so we went to the market.
    Cause, so + result.
  • I didn't have any cash, so I paid by card.
    Cause, so + result.

Before and after: a trap

“Before” and “after” are prepositions or conjunctions: they need something after them. Say “after lunch” (+ noun), “after eating” (+ -ing) or “after we ate” (+ clause).

“After, we went…” alone at the start of a sentence is a very common mistake. Say “After that, …” or “Afterwards, …”.

“Before” alone at the end of a sentence means “at an earlier time”: “I've never been there before.”

WrongRight
After, we watched a film.After that, we watched a film. / Afterwards, we watched a film.
After to eat, we went out.After eating, we went out. / After we ate, we went out.
Before to leave, close the window.Before leaving, close the window. / Before you leave, close the window.
  • After dinner, we played cards.
    after + noun.
  • Before going to bed, I always read a little.
    before + -ing.

Finally, in the end, at the end, eventually

“Finally” marks the last step in a list, or something that happens after a long wait: “Finally, the bus arrived.”

“In the end” gives the final result after changes or hesitation: “We wanted to go to the beach, but in the end we stayed at home.”

“At the end of” + noun = at the last part of a place or time: “at the end of the film”, “at the end of the street”. “Eventually” means “after a long time”; it does not mean “possibly”.

  • We waited for two hours. Finally, the plane took off.
    After a long wait.
  • I wanted the red phone, but in the end I bought the black one.
    The final result after a change.
  • Everybody cried at the end of the film.
    at the end of + noun.
For something that may happen, say “possibly” or “maybe”: “I can possibly come on Friday.”

A complete story

Here is a short story in the past that uses the linking words. Notice the variety and the commas.

  • First of all, we drove to Kribi early in the morning.
    First step.
  • Then we found a small hotel near the beach.
    Next step.
  • The sea was very calm, so we went swimming.
    Result.
  • After that, we had grilled fish for lunch.
    Next step.
  • In the end, we stayed three days instead of two!
    Final result after a change of plan.

The formula

First, … Then … After that, … Finally, … · cause, so + result · after + noun / -ing / clause

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “then” is /ðen/ with a voiced th (tongue between the teeth); don't confuse it with “than” /ðən/.
  • “afterwards” /ˈɑːftəwədz/: stress at the start; in British English you don't say the r (US: /ˈæftərwərdz/, often “afterward”).
  • In speech, “So” is often long when you are thinking: “Sooo… what do we do now?”
  • Make a small pause after a linking word at the start: “First, | we went…”, “After that, | we…”.
  • “eventually” /ɪˈventʃuəli/: stress on the second syllable (“i-VEN-choo-uh-lee”).

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ After, we went to the cinema.✓ After that, we went to the cinema. / Afterwards, we went to the cinema.Don't use “after” alone like this at the start.
✗ After to eat, we left.✓ After eating, we left. / After we ate, we left.After “after”: noun, -ing or clause, never “to”.
✗ I was tired, because I went to bed.✓ I was tired, so I went to bed.Result = so; because = cause.
✗ At the end, we decided to stay.✓ In the end, we decided to stay.Final result after hesitation = in the end.
✗ I can eventually come tomorrow. (meaning maybe)✓ I can possibly come tomorrow. / Maybe I can come tomorrow.eventually = after a long time.
✗ Firstly we went to the beach, then, then we ate, then we slept.✓ First, we went to the beach. Then we had lunch. After that, we had a rest.Use different words and clear sentences.
✗ In the end of the film, she dies.✓ At the end of the film, she dies.“At the end of” + noun for an exact moment.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Liège, Nora tells her colleague Samuel how she cooked a meal for her mother's birthday.

Samuel

So, how was your mum's birthday?
“So” to start a topic.

Nora

Great, but busy! First, I went to the market on Saturday morning.
First step.

Samuel

What did you cook?
Past simple question.

Nora

Chicken yassa. I cut the onions, then I put the chicken in a lemon sauce.
then = next step.

Samuel

Sounds delicious. And after that?
Asking for the next step.

Nora

After that, I made a cake, but the oven stopped working!
A problem.

Samuel

Oh no! What did you do?
Asking about a solution.

Nora

I called my neighbour, so I baked it in her kitchen.
so = result.

Samuel

Good idea! And in the end?
Asking for the final result.

Nora

In the end, everything was perfect. Finally, at ten o'clock, we sang “Happy Birthday”.
Final result + last step.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Choose the linking word

Choose the right word.

  1. (First / Finally), wash your hands. Then start cooking.
  2. It was cold, (so / because) I put on a jumper.
  3. (After / After that), we went home.
  4. We wanted to fly, but (in the end / at the end) we took the train.
  5. I read the menu, and (then / before) I ordered.
  6. Everybody clapped (in the end / at the end) of the concert.
Show the answers
  1. First
  2. so
  3. After that
  4. in the end
  5. then
  6. at the end

2. So or because?

Complete with so or because.

  1. I missed the bus, ___ I was late for work.
  2. I was late for work ___ I missed the bus.
  3. The restaurant was full, ___ we ate at home.
  4. She stayed in bed ___ she had a fever.
  5. It was sunny, ___ we went to the beach.
Show the answers
  1. so
  2. because
  3. so
  4. because
  5. so

3. Complete the story

Complete with First, Then, After that, in the end or Finally (use each one once).

  1. ___, we got up early and had breakfast.
  2. ___ we took the bus to the city centre.
  3. ___, we visited the museum for two hours.
  4. We wanted to eat in a restaurant, but ___ we bought sandwiches in the park.
  5. ___, we came home at seven, very tired but happy.
Show the answers
  1. First
  2. Then
  3. After that
  4. in the end
  5. Finally

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. After, we went to the cinema.
  2. After to eat, he left.
  3. It was raining, because we stayed at home.
  4. I wasn't sure, but at the end I chose the blue dress.
  5. I can eventually come on Monday, but I'm not sure.
Show the answers
  1. After that, we went to the cinema. / Afterwards, we went to the cinema.
  2. After eating, he left. / After he ate, he left.
  3. It was raining, so we stayed at home.
  4. I wasn't sure, but in the end I chose the blue dress.
  5. I can possibly come on Monday, but I'm not sure. / Maybe I can come on Monday.

5. Your turn

Tell the story of a recent day (a trip, a party, a day at work) in five or six sentences in the past. Use first, then, after that, so and finally or in the end.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. Last Saturday was my cousin's wedding. First, I went to the hairdresser's at eight. Then I put on my new dress and took a taxi to the church. The ceremony was very long, so the children were tired. After that, we all went to a big restaurant and danced for hours. In the end, I got home at three in the morning!
Key points
  • Order: first (of all) → then / next → after that / afterwards → later → finally.
  • so = result (It was late, so I left); because = cause (I left because it was late).
  • after + noun / -ing / clause; alone at the start: After that or Afterwards, never “After, …”.
  • finally = last step; in the end = final result; at the end of + noun; eventually = after a long time.
  • Put a comma after a linking word at the start: First, … After that, …
