Mixed conditionals

“If I had accepted that job, I would live in Montreal now”; “If he were more organised, he wouldn't have missed the deadline”. The condition and the result do not always belong to the same time: mixed conditionals combine the second and third conditionals to connect the past and the present. You need them to talk about choices, regrets and their consequences.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Connect a past condition to a present result: If + past perfect, would + base form.
  • Connect a present (permanent) situation to a past result: If + past simple, would have + past participle.
  • Use could, might and continuous forms in the result.
  • Analyse the time of the condition and of the result to choose the right structure.

Review: the two building blocks

The second conditional talks about an unreal situation in the present or future: If + past simple, would + base form. “If I had more time, I would learn Spanish.”

The third conditional talks about an unreal situation in the past: If + past perfect, would have + past participle. “If you had told me, I would have helped.”

A mixed conditional takes the if-clause from one and the main clause from the other, according to the real time of each part.

TypeCondition (if)ResultExample
Second conditionalunreal present: past simplepresent: would + baseIf I had a car, I would drive to work.
Third conditionalunreal past: past perfectpast: would have + participleIf I had left earlier, I would have caught the train.
Mixed Aunreal past: past perfectpresent: would + baseIf I had left earlier, I would be there now.
Mixed Bunreal present: past simplepast: would have + participleIf I were more organised, I wouldn't have missed the train.

Mixed A: past → present

This is the most common type. We imagine that a past event happened differently, and we look at the consequence today.

Words like “now”, “today”, “still” and “at the moment” in the result are useful clues.

A frequent mistake is to write “would have been” automatically, even when the result is in the present.

  • If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now.
    Past choice, present situation.
  • If she hadn't missed the plane, she would be in Dakar at the moment.
    Past event, present location.
  • If we had bought that flat in 2010, we would be rich today.
    Past decision, present result.
  • If I hadn't met you, my life would be very different.
    Past meeting, present life.

Mixed B: present (permanent) → past

The condition is about a lasting characteristic, true yesterday and today (personality, skills, situation), and the result is about a specific past event.

Use the past simple (often “were” for all persons in careful style) in the if-clause, and would have + past participle in the main clause.

A typical example: “If I spoke Dutch, I would have understood the announcement”: I don't speak Dutch (then or now), so I didn't understand the announcement yesterday.

  • If he were more patient, he wouldn't have shouted at the children.
    He isn't a patient person.
  • If she spoke French, she would have got the job in Lyon.
    She doesn't speak French.
  • If I weren't afraid of flying, I would have visited you in Toronto.
    I am afraid of flying.
  • If they didn't trust you, they wouldn't have given you the keys.
    They do trust you.

Variations: could, might, continuous forms

In the result, “would” can be replaced by “could” (ability) or “might” (possibility): “If I had learnt to drive, I could apply for this job now.”

Continuous forms are common: “If I hadn't stayed up so late, I wouldn't be feeling so tired”; “If she hadn't moved to Canada, she would still be living with her parents.”

A past condition can also have a future consequence: “If he had booked the tickets, we would be flying to Montreal tomorrow.”

  • If I had learnt English as a child, I might be working abroad now.
    might: possibility.
  • If you had listened to the instructions, you could do the exercise now.
    could: present ability.
  • If I hadn't drunk three coffees, I wouldn't be feeling so nervous.
    Continuous result.

Method: place each part in time

Before you write, ask two questions. 1) Is the condition past (a finished event) or present (a situation that is still true)? 2) Is the result about the present or the past?

Past condition → past perfect. Present condition → past simple. Present result → would + base form. Past result → would have + past participle.

As with all conditionals, never put “would” in the if-clause. The clauses can change order; use a comma only when “if” comes first.

Real situationAnalysisMixed conditional
I didn't take the job. I'm unemployed now.past → presentIf I had taken the job, I wouldn't be unemployed now.
I'm not tall. I wasn't chosen for the team.present → pastIf I were taller, I would have been chosen for the team.
She lost her passport. She can't travel now.past → present (could)If she hadn't lost her passport, she could travel now.

The formula

If + past perfect, would + base (past → present) · If + past simple, would have + past participle (present → past)

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “I'd” can mean “I had” or “I would”: “If I'd known” (had), “I'd be there” (would). The next verb tells you which.
  • “would have” is reduced to /wʊdəv/ or /ˈwʊdə/ in fast speech: “I'd have come” /aɪd əv ˈkʌm/. “wouldn't have”: /ˈwʊdntəv/.
  • “hadn't” /ˈhædnt/: the final t is often hard to hear.
  • Intonation: a slight rise at the end of the if-clause, then a fall on the result: “If I'd taken the job ↗, I'd be rich now ↘”.
  • Stress the words that carry the contrast: “If I'd STAYED, I'd be MANager now.”

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Toronto, two friends, Jérôme (from Lille) and Fatou (from Thiès, Senegal), talk about their life choices in a café.

Fatou

Do you ever think about what your life would be like if you hadn't moved here?
Past condition → present result.

Jérôme

All the time. If I had stayed in Lille, I would probably be working in my father's shop now.
Continuous present result.

Fatou

And you wouldn't speak English so well!
Present result.

Jérôme

True. What about you? If you hadn't got that scholarship, would you still be in Senegal?
Question form: would you still be…?

Fatou

I think so. But honestly, if I weren't so stubborn, I would have given up after the first winter.
Present character → past result.

Jérôme

Ha! If you weren't stubborn, you wouldn't have finished your PhD either.
Same structure, negative.

Fatou

Exactly. And if we hadn't met at that conference, we wouldn't be sitting here today.
Past event → present situation.

Jérôme

So no regrets, then. Another coffee?
A natural ending.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Analyse the time

For each sentence, say whether the condition and the result are about the past or the present.

  1. If I had saved more money, I would be on holiday now.
  2. If he were more patient, he wouldn't have shouted at the kids.
  3. If you had told me, I would have helped.
  4. If I had more time, I would learn Spanish.
Show the answers
  1. Past condition, present result (mixed A).
  2. Present condition, past result (mixed B).
  3. Past condition, past result (third conditional).
  4. Present condition, present result (second conditional).

2. Complete

Put the verb in brackets into the correct form.

  1. If I ___ (not / miss) the bus, I would be at work now.
  2. If she ___ (be) less shy, she would have asked for a pay rise.
  3. We ___ (not / be) lost now if we had brought a map.
  4. If they ___ (speak) English, they would have understood the guide.
  5. If you had gone to bed earlier, you ___ (not / feel) so tired now.
  6. If I didn't love my job, I ___ (leave) years ago.
Show the answers
  1. hadn't missed
  2. were (or was)
  3. wouldn't be
  4. spoke
  5. wouldn't feel (or wouldn't be feeling)
  6. would have left

3. Combine

Make one sentence with a mixed conditional.

  1. I didn't learn to drive. Now I can't get a job as a delivery driver.
  2. He's very lazy. He didn't finish the project.
  3. We didn't buy the house in 2015. We aren't rich now.
  4. I don't speak Dutch. I didn't understand the announcement.
  5. She accepted the job in Montreal. She lives in Canada now.
Show the answers
  1. If I had learnt (learned) to drive, I could get a job as a delivery driver now.
  2. If he weren't (wasn't) so lazy, he would have finished the project.
  3. If we had bought the house in 2015, we would be rich now.
  4. If I spoke Dutch, I would have understood the announcement.
  5. If she hadn't accepted the job in Montreal, she wouldn't live (wouldn't be living) in Canada now.

4. Correct the mistakes

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. If I would have known, I would be there now.
  2. If I had studied law, I would have been a lawyer now.
  3. If I didn't lose my phone yesterday, I could call her now.
  4. If she isn't so stubborn, she would have apologised.
Show the answers
  1. If I had known, I would be there now.
  2. If I had studied law, I would be a lawyer now.
  3. If I hadn't lost my phone yesterday, I could call her now.
  4. If she weren't (wasn't) so stubborn, she would have apologised.

5. Finish the sentences

Finish each sentence logically with the correct form.

  1. If I hadn't met my best friend, …
  2. If I were better at maths, …
  3. If my parents hadn't moved to this city, …
  4. If I weren't so busy, …
Show the answers
  1. Model answer: …my life would be much less fun now.
  2. Model answer: …I would have studied engineering.
  3. Model answer: …I wouldn't speak the local language so well now.
  4. Model answer: …I would have come to your party last night.

6. Your turn

Think of an important decision in your life and one of your personality traits. Write 4 sentences: two “past → present” and two “present → past”.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. If I hadn't started learning English, I wouldn't be able to work with clients in Canada now. If I had chosen to study engineering, I might be earning more today. If I weren't so impatient, I would have finished my first novel. If I were more organised, I wouldn't have missed so many deadlines last year.
Key points
  • Past condition → present result: If + past perfect, would / could / might + base (If I had left, I would be there now).
  • Present (permanent) condition → past result: If + past simple, would have + past participle (If I were braver, I would have asked).
  • Ask: when is the condition? when is the result?
  • Never “would” after if; “now / today” in the result → would + base form.
  • Continuous forms are possible: wouldn't be feeling, would be living.
