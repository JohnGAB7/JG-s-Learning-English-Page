A1 · Beginner · lesson 2

Introducing yourself

Say hello, give your name, country, city, job and age, then ask the other person the same questions. This is your first conversation in English, at work and when you travel.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Greet someone and answer a greeting, in a formal or relaxed way.
  • Give your name, nationality, country, city, job and age in simple sentences.
  • Ask the basic questions: What's your name? Where are you from? What do you do?
  • Answer politely when you meet someone (Nice to meet you) and introduce another person (This is…).

Saying hello and goodbye

Your greeting depends on the time of day and on the situation. “Hello” is fine everywhere. “Hi” and “Hey” are relaxed: for friends, close colleagues and messages. Say “Good morning” until about 12 o'clock, “Good afternoon” until about 5 or 6 pm, and “Good evening” after that.

Be careful: “Good night” is never a greeting when you arrive. Say it only when you leave in the evening or before you go to bed. To leave, say “Goodbye” (neutral), “Bye” or “See you” (relaxed), or “See you tomorrow”.

SituationFormalRelaxedNote
Arriving (morning)Good morning.Morning! / Hi!Until about 12 pm.
Arriving (afternoon)Good afternoon.Hi! / Hello!From 12 pm to about 6 pm.
Arriving (evening)Good evening.Hi! / Hey!After about 6 pm.
LeavingGoodbye.Bye! / See you!Any time of day.
Leaving at night, going to bedGood night.Night!Only to leave, never to arrive.
“How are you?” is mostly a polite phrase. Give a short answer: “Fine, thanks. And you?” or “I'm good, thanks.” Don't tell all your problems.

Giving your name

To give your name, say “I'm…” (the most natural) or “My name is…” (a little more formal). Your “first name” is your personal name. Your “surname” (British) or “last name” (American) is your family name.

To ask someone's name, say “What's your name?”. In official places (a hotel, an office), you also hear “Can I have your name, please?” or “What's your surname?”.

  • Hi, I'm Lucas.
    The most common way when you speak.
  • My name is Aïcha Mbarga.
    A little more formal.
  • My first name is Tom and my surname is Dupont.
    First name + surname.
  • Please call me Kate.
    To give a short name that you prefer.
Don't say “I call myself Lucas”. Say “I'm Lucas” or “My name is Lucas”.

Country, nationality and city

For where you come from, say “I'm from + country or city”. For your nationality, say “I'm + nationality adjective”. In English, nationalities and languages always start with a capital letter: French, Belgian, Canadian.

For the place where you live now, say “I live in + city or country”. This sentence is in the present simple (lesson 6). For now, learn it as a fixed phrase.

CountryNationalitySentence
FranceFrenchI'm from France. I'm French.
BelgiumBelgianShe's from Belgium. She's Belgian.
CanadaCanadianWe're from Canada. We're Canadian.
CameroonCameroonianHe's from Cameroon. He's Cameroonian.
SwitzerlandSwissI'm from Switzerland. I'm Swiss.
the United Kingdom (the UK)BritishThey're from the UK. They're British.
the United States (the USA)AmericanAre you from the USA? Are you American?
  • I'm from Lyon, but I live in Brussels.
    Where you come from + where you live now.
  • Where are you from? — I'm from Montréal.
    Question and answer about origin.
A few countries take “the”: the UK, the USA, the Netherlands, the Philippines. The others don't: say “I'm from France”, never “from the France”.

Job and age

For your job, say “I'm a / an + job”. You need the article: a before a consonant sound, an before a vowel sound. You can also say “I work in + area” (I work in IT, I work in a bank) or “I'm a student”.

The natural question about someone's job is “What do you do?”. The answer is your job, not what you are doing today.

For age, say “I'm 25” or “I'm 25 years old”, never with “have”. The question is “How old are you?”. Between adults who don't know each other, this question can seem too personal.

  • I'm an engineer.
    an before the vowel sound of “engineer”.
  • I'm a nurse. I work in a hospital.
    Job + place of work.
  • What do you do? — I'm an accountant.
    The usual question about a job.
  • How old are you? — I'm nineteen.
    Age with “to be”.

Answering and introducing someone

When you meet a new person, say “Nice to meet you”. The other person answers “Nice to meet you too” or “You too”. In very formal situations, you hear “Pleased to meet you” and, rarely, “How do you do?”. The answer to “How do you do?” is also “How do you do?”.

To introduce another person, say “This is + name”, not “He is”: This is my colleague, Ana. On the phone, you also say “This is Paul” or “It's Paul”.

  • Sophie, this is Marc. Marc, this is Sophie.
    Introducing two people.
  • Nice to meet you. — Nice to meet you too.
    The usual answer.
  • Let me introduce myself: I'm Dr Nkemba.
    Formal way to start.

The formula

Hello, I'm + name. I'm from + country. I'm a / an + job. I'm + age. Nice to meet you.

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • Say “Nice to meet you” in one breath: “to” is weak /tə/, and “meet you” often joins as /miːtʃuː/.
  • “What's your name?”: the voice goes down at the end (Wh- question). “Are you French?” (yes / no question): the voice goes up.
  • Nationalities have fixed stress: CaNAdian /kəˈneɪdiən/, BELgian /ˈbeldʒən/, JapaNESE /ˌdʒæpəˈniːz/, CameROOnian /ˌkæməˈruːniən/.
  • “Hello” is /həˈləʊ/: breathe out the h, like when you breathe on a window. Don't drop it.
  • In “I'm from France”, “from” is weak: /frəm/, not /frɒm/.

Listening Studio

At a conference in Montréal, two people meet during the coffee break.

Julie

Hi! I'm Julie. Nice to meet you.
A greeting + name + polite phrase.

Samuel

Nice to meet you too. I'm Samuel. Where are you from, Julie?
“too” in the answer.

Julie

I'm from Belgium, from Liège. And you?
Country, then city.

Samuel

I'm Cameroonian, but I live in Montréal now.
Nationality + where he lives now.

Julie

Oh, nice! What do you do?
The usual question about a job.

Samuel

I'm a software developer. And you?
a before the job.

Julie

I'm an architect. Oh, this is my colleague, Tom.
an before a vowel sound; “This is” to introduce.

Tom

Hello, Samuel. Pleased to meet you.
A more formal answer.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Choose the right greeting

Choose the best phrase.

  1. You arrive at the office at 9 am: Good morning / Good night?
  2. You arrive at a dinner at 8 pm: Good evening / Good night?
  3. You leave your friends at 11 pm to go to bed: Good evening / Good night?
  4. You meet someone for the first time: Nice to meet you / See you?
  5. Someone says “How are you?”: Fine, thanks. And you? / I'm 25.
Show the answers
  1. Good morning
  2. Good evening
  3. Good night
  4. Nice to meet you
  5. Fine, thanks. And you?

2. Complete the introduction

Complete with: I'm, from, a, an, live, name.

  1. My ___ is Inès.
  2. ___ 22 years old.
  3. I'm ___ Belgium.
  4. I ___ in Namur.
  5. I'm ___ student.
  6. My brother is ___ architect.
Show the answers
  1. name
  2. I'm
  3. from
  4. live
  5. a
  6. an

3. Find the question

Write the question for each answer.

  1. I'm Karim.
  2. I'm from Canada.
  3. I'm a teacher.
  4. I'm 31.
Show the answers
  1. What's your name?
  2. Where are you from?
  3. What do you do?
  4. How old are you?

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. I call myself Paul and I'm french.
  2. She's cameroonian; she lives in Yaoundé.
  3. He's engineer.
  4. I have 40 years.
  5. She is my colleague, Léa. (introducing her to a friend)
Show the answers
  1. My name is Paul and I'm French. / I'm Paul and I'm French.
  2. She's Cameroonian; she lives in Yaoundé.
  3. He's an engineer.
  4. I'm 40. / I'm 40 years old.
  5. This is my colleague, Léa.

5. Your turn

Introduce yourself in five sentences: a greeting, your name, your country or city, your job or studies, your age (if you want), and a polite phrase.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. Hello! My name is Fatou Diallo. I'm from Senegal, but I live in Brussels. I'm a nurse. I'm 29. Nice to meet you!
Key points
  • Introduce yourself: I'm Lucas / My name is Lucas, never “I call myself”.
  • Origin: I'm from + country. Nationality with a capital letter: I'm French, I'm Canadian.
  • Job: I'm a / an + job. Question: What do you do?
  • Age: I'm 25. Question: How old are you?
  • Nice to meet you → Nice to meet you too. To introduce someone: This is…
  • Good evening when you arrive in the evening; Good night only when you leave.
