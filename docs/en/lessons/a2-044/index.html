“Used to” (past habits)

“Used to” is for things that were true in the past but are not true now: a past habit or a past situation. It is very useful to compare your life before and now.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Form “used to” in positive, negative and question forms.
  • Talk about past habits and past situations that have changed.
  • Contrast the past (used to) with the present (usually / now).
  • Avoid confusion with “be used to” and with the past simple for one action.

The form

“Used to” is followed by the base verb. It is the same for every subject: I used to, she used to, they used to.

In negatives and questions, we use did, and the “d” of used disappears because did already shows the past. So we write “didn't use to” and “Did you use to…?”. (You may see “didn't used to”, but careful writing does not accept it.)

FormPatternExample
positivesubject + used to + baseI used to live in Garoua.
negativesubject + didn't use to + baseI didn't use to like coffee.
negative (another way)subject + never used to + baseHe never used to call me.
questionDid + subject + use to + base?Did you use to play the piano?
short answerYes, I did. / No, I didn't.Did she use to smoke? — Yes, she did.
“Used to” is only for the past. For a present habit, use the present simple with usually: “I usually get up at 6” (never “I use to get up at 6”).

Use 1: past habits

We talk about actions we repeated in the past and do not do now. We often show the contrast with the present: “but now…”, “not any more”, “any longer”.

  • I used to walk to school every day.
    A repeated past action; I don't do it now.
  • We used to spend our holidays in Kribi.
    A past habit.
  • She used to work late, but now she leaves at five.
    Contrast: before / now.

Use 2: past states and situations

“Used to” is also for states that have changed: where you lived, what you liked, what a person or a place looked like. It is great for comparing “before” and “now”.

  • There used to be a cinema in this street.
    The cinema is not there now.
  • He used to have long hair.
    His hair is short now.
  • I didn't use to like vegetables, but now I love them.
    A changed state (like).

Used to or past simple?

The past simple can also describe a past habit, especially with a length of time: “I lived in Lille for five years”. But “used to” stresses that the situation is finished and has changed.

“Used to” is not possible for one single action, or when you say how many times something happened.

SituationCorrectWrong
past habitI used to go / I went to the gym every week.—
one actionI went to the gym yesterday.I used to go to the gym yesterday.
number of timesI visited Ghent three times.I used to visit Ghent three times.
exact length of timeI lived in Lille for five years.I used to live in Lille for five years. (avoid)

Don't confuse it with “be used to”

“Be used to + noun / verb-ing” means “be familiar with something, find it normal”. It is a different structure and it exists in the present: “I'm used to the cold.” You will study it at B1.

For now, remember: used to + base verb = finished past habit; be used to + -ing = find something normal.

  • I used to live in Canada.
    I lived there before, not now.
  • I'm used to living in Canada.
    Living in Canada is normal for me now.

The formula

used to + base verb · didn't use to + base · Did … use to + base?

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “used to” is /ˈjuːs tə/, with an s sound, like “juice” + “tuh”. The two t sounds join into one.
  • Do not confuse it with the past of the verb “use”: “I used a knife” /juːzd/, with a z sound.
  • “to” is weak /tə/ before a consonant (used to live /ˈjuːs tə lɪv/) and often /tu/ before a vowel (used to eat).
  • In fast speech, “Did you use to…?” often sounds like /ˈdɪdʒə ˈjuːs tə/.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ I use to get up at 6.✓ I usually get up at 6.There is no present “use to”: use usually + present simple.
✗ I didn't used to like tea.✓ I didn't use to like tea.After did / didn't: use to (no d).
✗ Did you used to play football?✓ Did you use to play football?The same rule in questions.
✗ She used to living in Paris.✓ She used to live in Paris.Used to + base verb, not -ing.
✗ I used to go to the cinema last night.✓ I went to the cinema last night.One action: past simple, never used to.
✗ When I was young, I was used to play outside.✓ When I was young, I used to play outside.No “was”: “was used to” means “was familiar with”.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

A grandfather, Joseph, shows old photos of Bafoussam to his granddaughter Inès, who lives in Brussels.

Inès

Grandpa, is that you? You used to have so much hair!
used to + have: a past state.

Joseph

Ha! Yes, and I used to wear these big glasses.
A past habit.

Inès

Where did you use to live?
Wh- question: did + use to.

Joseph

In a small house near the market. There used to be a big tree in front of it.
There used to be = there was before.

Inès

Did you use to walk to school?
Yes / no question.

Joseph

Yes, every day — five kilometres! We didn't use to have a car.
Negative: didn't use to.

Inès

Wow. Now I take the metro for two stops!
Present simple for now.

Joseph

Times change, my dear.
A common expression.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. The right form

Choose the right form: used to, use to or usually.

  1. I ___ play tennis, but I stopped last year.
  2. Did you ___ live in the city centre?
  3. Now I ___ cycle to work.
  4. We didn't ___ have a TV.
  5. There ___ be a bakery here.
Show the answers
  1. used to
  2. use to
  3. usually
  4. use to
  5. used to

2. Complete

Complete with the correct form of used to and the verb in brackets.

  1. My father ___ (work) in a bank.
  2. I ___ (not / eat) fish when I was a child.
  3. ___ you ___ (have) a pet?
  4. They ___ (live) in Toulouse before they moved to Quebec.
  5. She ___ (not / speak) English, but now she's fluent.
Show the answers
  1. used to work
  2. didn't use to eat
  3. Did … use to have
  4. used to live
  5. didn't use to speak

3. Before and now

Use used to or didn't use to to describe the past.

  1. Now I drink tea. (before: coffee)
  2. Now he goes to the gym. (before: not)
  3. Now this street is busy. (before: quiet)
  4. Now they have a car. (before: not)
Show the answers
  1. I used to drink coffee.
  2. He didn't use to go to the gym.
  3. This street used to be quiet.
  4. They didn't use to have a car.

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. I use to go to bed late.
  2. Did she used to work here?
  3. We used to going to the beach.
  4. I used to see him yesterday.
Show the answers
  1. I usually go to bed late. (present habit) / I used to go to bed late. (past habit)
  2. Did she use to work here?
  3. We used to go to the beach.
  4. I saw him yesterday.

5. Make questions

Write the question with used to.

  1. you / live / in Lomé?
  2. he / like / sport?
  3. where / you / play / when you were children?
Show the answers
  1. Did you use to live in Lomé?
  2. Did he use to like sport?
  3. Where did you use to play when you were children?

6. Your turn

Compare your life ten years ago and today in five sentences (at least three with used to / didn't use to).

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. Ten years ago I used to live with my parents in Douala. I used to take a taxi to university every day. I didn't use to cook at all! Now I live alone in Brussels and I cook every evening. I usually go to work by tram.
Key points
  • Used to + base verb = a past habit or situation that has changed.
  • Negative: didn't use to; question: Did … use to …? (no d after did).
  • No present “use to”: say I usually…
  • No used to for one action or with a number of times (past simple).
  • Don't confuse it with be used to + -ing (find something normal).
