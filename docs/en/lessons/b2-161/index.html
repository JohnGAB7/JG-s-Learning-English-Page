Cleft sentences (What I need is…, It was … that)

“It was Awa who won”, “What I need is a holiday”: a cleft sentence splits one idea into two clauses to put one part in the spotlight. English has two main structures, “It is / was … that / who” and “What … is …”. They help you correct, contrast and highlight what matters, in speech and in writing.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B2

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Make an it-cleft to highlight a subject, an object or a circumstance.
  • Make a what-cleft (What I need is…, What happened was…), including with a verb (What I did was call…).
  • Use the variants: All I want is…, The reason why…, The thing that…, That's what…
  • Avoid common errors (“What I need, it's…”, “It's in 2010 that…”).

What is a cleft sentence for?

A cleft sentence divides one idea into two clauses so that one element stands out: you correct a mistake, contrast two things or build up to the important information.

Compare: “Marie sent the email” (neutral); “It was Marie who sent the email” (Marie, not someone else); “What Marie sent was an email” (an email, not a letter).

In speech, English often highlights with the voice alone: “MARIE sent the email.” Some languages use cleft structures much more often than English, so don't overuse them.

  • It was Marie who sent the email, not Paul.
    Highlighting the subject, contrast.
  • What surprised me was his reaction.
    Delaying the key information to make it stand out.
  • MARIE sent it.
    In speech, stress is often enough.

It-clefts: It is / was + element + that / who

Structure: It + be + highlighted element + that / who + rest of the sentence. Use “who” for a person (“that” is also possible) and “that” for everything else. Avoid “which” here.

You can highlight a subject, an object or a circumstance (place, time, reason), but not the verb itself.

The tense of “be” usually follows the tense of the sentence: past → “It was”. Say “It was in 2010 that I moved”, not “It's in 2010 that I moved”.

Negative and contrast: “It wasn't the price that worried me, it was the delay.” With a personal pronoun, everyday English says “It was me who…”; “It was I who…” is very formal.

Neutral sentenceHighlighted elementCleft sentence
Awa won the prize.the subjectIt was Awa who won the prize.
I lost my keys.the objectIt was my keys that I lost (not my wallet).
We met in Brussels.the placeIt was in Brussels that we met.
She left because of the noise.the reasonIt was because of the noise that she left.
The delay worries me.the subject (present)It's the delay that worries me.

What-clefts: What + clause + be + element

Structure: What + subject + verb + be + highlighted element. “What” means “the thing that”. The important information comes at the end, after “is / was”.

To highlight an action, use “do”: “What I did was (to) call the police.” After “was”, use the base form, with or without “to”.

To tell a story: “What happened was (that) the train broke down.”

You can reverse the order: “A holiday is what I need.” “That's what I mean.” “That's what I said!”

  • What I need is a long holiday.
    The key information comes last.
  • What worries me is the cost.
    What + verb (as subject).
  • What I did was (to) call the bank immediately.
    Highlighting an action.
  • What happened was that the train broke down near Namur.
    Introducing a story.
  • Patience is what you need.
    Reversed order.

Common variants

Other words can replace “What”: All (= the only thing), The thing (that), The reason (why), The place (where), The person (who), The day (when), The only thing.

“All I want is…” is very common and means “I only want…”: “All I want is some peace and quiet.”

“The reason why… is that…”: avoid “the reason is because” in careful writing, as it repeats the idea.

BeginningExampleMeaning
All…All I want is to sleep.I only want to sleep.
The thing (that)…The thing I love about Montreal is the festivals.What I love…
The reason (why)…The reason why I left is that I was bored.I left because…
The place where…The place where we met was a café in Liège.We met in…
The person who…The person who helped me most was my aunt.My aunt helped me most.
The only thing…The only thing I regret is not learning sooner.My one regret.

Agreement, punctuation and errors to avoid

After a what-cleft, “be” is usually singular, even when the element is plural: “What we need is more teachers.” (“are” is possible but less common.)

No comma and no extra “it”: “What I need is a coffee”, never “What I need, it's a coffee”.

Don't combine “what” with another noun: “The thing what I like” is wrong; say “The thing (that) I like” or “What I like”.

  • What we need is more volunteers.
    Singular “is” even with a plural.
  • What I love about Dakar is the ocean.
    No comma, no “it”.
  • It's not that I don't like you, it's just that I'm tired.
    Common expression to explain gently.

The formula

It is / was + element + that / who … · What + subject + verb + is / was + element

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • The main stress falls on the highlighted element: “It was AWA who won”, “What I need is a HOLiday”.
  • In an it-cleft, “It was” and “that” are weak: /ɪt wəz ˈɑːwə ðət ˈwʌn/.
  • In a what-cleft, the first part often has a rising or level tone, then the voice falls on the element: “What I need ↗ is a holiday ↘”.
  • “What I did was call…”: a short pause after “was” can build suspense.
  • In writing, cleft sentences replace spoken stress; that is why they are common in argumentative texts.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ What I need, it's a holiday.✓ What I need is a holiday.No comma and no extra “it”.
✗ It's in 2015 that I moved to Douala.✓ It was in 2015 that I moved to Douala.Past event: “It was”.
✗ It was my sister which told me.✓ It was my sister who (that) told me.For a person: who or that.
✗ What I did was I called the police.✓ What I did was (to) call the police.After “What I did was”: base form.
✗ The thing what annoys me is the noise.✓ The thing that annoys me (What annoys me) is the noise.Don't combine “the thing” and “what”.
✗ That I like is the food.✓ What I like is the food.The cleft starts with “What”, not “That”.
✗ The reason is because I was tired.✓ The reason (why I left) is that I was tired.Careful writing: the reason… is that.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Ottawa, Jonathan and Sabrina have a friendly argument after finding their restaurant closed on their wedding anniversary.

Sabrina

I can't believe it. You forgot to book the table!
An accusation.

Jonathan

Wait. It wasn't me who was supposed to book. It was you!
Negative it-cleft to correct.

Sabrina

No. What I said was that I would choose the restaurant, not book it.
What-cleft to clarify.

Jonathan

OK, maybe. But what really annoys me is that they close on Mondays and nobody told us.
What + verb as subject.

Sabrina

It's not that I'm angry. It's just that I was really looking forward to it.
It's not that…, it's just that…

Jonathan

I know. So what I suggest is this: we buy some poutine and eat it by the canal.
What I suggest is this: introduces a proposal.

Sabrina

Poutine on our anniversary? Well, that's what we ate on our first date…
Reversed cleft: that's what…

Jonathan

Exactly. All I want is to spend the evening with you.
All I want is…

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Find the highlighted element

Which element does the sentence highlight?

  1. What I need is a long holiday.
  2. It was in Brussels that we met.
  3. All I want is some peace and quiet.
  4. It's the price that worries me.
Show the answers
  1. a long holiday
  2. in Brussels (the place)
  3. some peace and quiet
  4. the price

2. Complete

Complete with one word: it, what, that, who or is.

  1. ___ was my brother who told me the news.
  2. ___ I like most about Montreal is the festivals.
  3. It was in 2019 ___ she started her company.
  4. What we need ___ more time.
  5. It was the manager ___ made the decision.
  6. ___ I did was call the bank immediately.
Show the answers
  1. It
  2. What
  3. that
  4. is
  5. who (or that)
  6. What

3. Transform

Rewrite starting with the word given, to highlight the element indicated.

  1. Paul broke the window. (It…; Paul)
  2. I need a good night's sleep. (What…)
  3. We met in Dakar. (It…; Dakar)
  4. His attitude annoys me. (What…)
  5. I only want to sleep. (All…)
Show the answers
  1. It was Paul who (that) broke the window.
  2. What I need is a good night's sleep.
  3. It was in Dakar that we met.
  4. What annoys me is his attitude.
  5. All I want is to sleep. (or: All I want to do is sleep.)

4. Correct the mistakes

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. What I need it's a coffee.
  2. It's in 2010 that they got married.
  3. What I did was I called a taxi.
  4. It was the neighbours which complained.
Show the answers
  1. What I need is a coffee.
  2. It was in 2010 that they got married.
  3. What I did was (to) call a taxi.
  4. It was the neighbours who (that) complained.

5. Correct the information

Answer “No” and correct the information with an it-cleft, using the word in brackets.

  1. “Tom sent the email, didn't he?” (Lisa)
  2. “You met in Paris, right?” (Brussels)
  3. “Does the noise bother you?” (the smell)
  4. “You started your job in May?” (June)
Show the answers
  1. No, it was Lisa who (that) sent the email.
  2. No, it was in Brussels that we met.
  3. No, it's the smell that bothers me.
  4. No, it was in June that I started.

6. Your turn

Describe your town or your job in 4 different cleft sentences: one with “It”, one with “What”, one with “All” and one with “The thing / The reason”.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. It was in 2018 that I moved to Liège for my studies. What I love about the city is its friendly atmosphere. All I miss is the sun of my home town. The reason why I stayed is that I found a great job here.
Key points
  • It is / was + element + that / who…: highlights a subject, object or circumstance.
  • Past tense → “It was”: It was in 2010 that…
  • What + subject + verb + is + element: What I need is…, What I did was (to) call…, What happened was that…
  • Variants: All I want is…, The thing (that)…, The reason why… is that…, That's what…
  • Never “What I need, it's…”; English uses fewer clefts than some languages and often uses stress instead.
