Past of “to be” (was / were)

“Was” and “were” are the past of “to be”. Use them to say where you were, how you felt, what the weather was like or how an event was. It is your first step to talk about the past, before other verbs (lessons 41 and 42).

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Use “to be” in the past: I was, you were, he was, we were, they were.
  • Make negatives (wasn't, weren't), questions and short answers.
  • Use past time expressions: yesterday, last week, two days ago.
  • Say when and where you were born (I was born…) and use there was / there were.

The forms

In the past, “to be” has only two forms: “was” with I, he, she, it, and “were” with you, we, they.

The past of “to be” describes a past state (I was tired) and a past fact (I was in Paris last week).

SubjectPresentPastNegative (short form)
Iamwaswasn't

  • I was at home yesterday.
    Place in the past.
  • We were very happy.
    Feelings in the past.
  • It was cold last night.
    Weather in the past.
Use “you were” for one person and for many people. “You was” is wrong in standard English.

Negatives, questions and short answers

Negative: add “not”: was not → wasn't, were not → weren't. Never use “did” with “to be”.

Question: put the verb before the subject: “Were you at work?”, “Was the film good?”. With a question word: “Where were you?”, “Why was she late?”

Short answers: “Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't.”, “Yes, they were. / No, they weren't.”

  • Was the exam difficult? — No, it wasn't.
    Question and short answer.
  • Why were you late? — Because the bus was late.
    Wh- question.

Uses

Like in the present, “was / were” is for place, state, feelings, age, weather and description: “I was 18 in 2010”, “The hotel was very nice”.

“How was…?” is the perfect question to ask someone's opinion about a past event: “How was the party?”, “How was your trip?”.

In the past, “there is / there are” becomes “there was / there were”: “There was a problem.”, “There were a lot of people.”

  • My grandfather was a teacher.
    Job in the past.
  • There were twenty people at the party.
    there were + plural.
  • How was your trip to Kribi? — It was wonderful, but the road was long.
    Asking for an opinion.

“I was born”

In English, we always talk about birth in the past: “I was born in 1995”, “She was born in Bafoussam”. “I am born” is wrong.

Prepositions: “in” + year, month or place (I was born in May / in Liège); “on” + exact date (I was born on the 3rd of March).

  • Where were you born? — I was born in Quebec City.
    Question and answer.
  • My son was born on the 12th of June 2020.
    on + exact date.

Past time expressions

“yesterday”, “yesterday morning / afternoon / evening”, “last night”, “last week / month / year / Monday”, “in 2015”, “when I was a child”.

“Ago” goes AFTER the period of time: “two days ago”, “ten years ago”. Use no preposition or article before “last” and “yesterday”: “last week”, not “the last week” or “in last week”.

    period + ago.
  • When I was a child, I was very shy.
    when + past.

The formula

I / he / she / it was · you / we / they were · wasn't / weren't · Was…? Were…? · I was born in…

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • In positive sentences, “was” and “were” are weak: “I was tired” = /aɪ wəz ˈtaɪəd/; “we were late” = /wi wə ˈleɪt/.
  • In short answers and when you stress them, they are strong: “Yes, I WAS” /wɒz/, “Yes, they WERE” /wɜː/.
  • “wasn't” /ˈwɒznt/ and “weren't” /wɜːnt/: the final t is light, but you hear the n clearly.
  • Don't confuse “were” /wɜː/ and “where” /weə/: “Where were you?” = /ˈweə wə ju/.
  • “born” /bɔːn/: a long vowel; in British English you don't say the r.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Monday morning in an office in Douala. Joël asks his colleague Rose about her weekend.

Joël

Good morning, Rose! How was your weekend?
Asking for an opinion.

Rose

It was great, thanks! I was in Limbe with my family.
was + place.

Joël

Lucky you! Was the weather good?
Question with was.

Rose

Yes, it was sunny and the beach was beautiful. But there were a lot of people.
there were + plural.

Joël

Were your children happy?
Question with were.

Rose

Oh yes, they were very happy. And you? Where were you on Saturday? You weren't at the football match.
Wh- question; weren't.

Joël

No, I wasn't. I was at home. I was ill.
Short answer.

Rose

Oh no! Are you OK now?
Back to the present: are.

Joël

Yes, I'm fine now, thanks.
Present: I'm.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Was or were?

Complete with was or were.

  1. I ___ tired yesterday.
  2. They ___ at the cinema.
  3. The weather ___ very hot.
  4. You ___ right!
  5. My parents ___ teachers.
  6. There ___ a lot of cars on the road.
Show the answers
  1. was
  2. were
  3. was
  4. were
  5. were
  6. were

2. Make it negative

Write the sentence in the negative (short form).

  1. She was at work.
  2. We were late.
  3. The film was interesting.
  4. They were in Canada.
Show the answers
  1. She wasn't at work.
  2. We weren't late.
  3. The film wasn't interesting.
  4. They weren't in Canada.

3. Questions and short answers

Write the question and the short answer.

  1. you / at home last night? (yes)
  2. the hotel / expensive? (no)
  3. your friends / at the party? (yes)
  4. Paul / in the office yesterday? (no)
  5. Where / you / born?
Show the answers
  1. Were you at home last night? — Yes, I was.
  2. Was the hotel expensive? — No, it wasn't.
  3. Were your friends at the party? — Yes, they were.
  4. Was Paul in the office yesterday? — No, he wasn't.
  5. Where were you born?

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. I am born in 1998.
  2. Where you were yesterday?
  3. Before two days, it was very cold.
  4. How did be your trip?
  5. There was a lot of people at the market.
Show the answers
  1. I was born in 1998.
  2. Where were you yesterday?
  3. Two days ago, it was very cold.
  4. How was your trip?
  5. There were a lot of people at the market.

5. Your turn

Write about your last weekend in four or five sentences with was, were, wasn't, weren't and there was / there were.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. Last weekend I was in Brussels with my sister. The weather wasn't very good: it was cloudy and cold. On Saturday we were at a concert. There were a lot of people and the music was fantastic. We were very tired on Sunday!
Key points
  • was with I, he, she, it; were with you, we, they.
  • Negative: wasn't / weren't; question: Was she…? Were you…?; never “did” with “be”.
  • How was your weekend? — It was great!; there was / there were.
  • Birth in the past: I was born in 1995 / on the 3rd of March.
  • yesterday, last week (no article), two days ago (ago after the period).
