How much / how many

English has two ways to ask about quantity: “how many” for things you can count (eggs, people) and “how much” for things you can't count (water, money) and for prices. The noun after it tells you which one to use.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Know the difference between common countable and uncountable nouns.
  • Ask questions with how many + plural and how much + uncountable noun.
  • Ask and give prices: How much is it? How much does it cost?
  • Answer with a lot, not many, not much, a few, a little, none.

Countable or uncountable?

A countable noun can be counted: one egg, two eggs, three people. It has a singular and a plural.

An uncountable noun can't be counted directly: water, rice, money, time, information. It has no plural and doesn't take “a / an”. We count it with a unit: a glass of water, a kilo of rice (lesson 26).

Careful: some nouns are uncountable in English but countable in many other languages: information, advice, news, furniture, luggage, homework, bread.

Countable (how many)Uncountable (how much)
eggs, apples, tomatoesrice, bread, sugar, milk, water
people, children, friendsmoney, time, work
euros, dollars, coinsinformation, advice, news
chairs, rooms, bagsfurniture, luggage, homework
times (occasions)time (minutes, hours)

How many + plural noun

Use “how many” with a plural countable noun. Then use normal question order (lesson 29): how many + noun + auxiliary + subject + verb.

Very common patterns: “How many … are there?”, “How many … have you got? / do you have?”.

  • How many students are there in your class? — Twenty-two.
    how many + are there.
  • How many brothers and sisters have you got?
    how many + have you got.
  • How many times a week do you go running? — Three times.
    times = occasions (countable).

How much + uncountable noun

Use “how much” with an uncountable noun, which stays singular. So the verb is singular: “How much water is there?”.

When the noun is clear, you can leave it out: “I need some sugar. — How much (sugar) do you need?”.

  • How much water do you drink a day? — About two litres.
    water = uncountable.
  • How much time have we got? — Ten minutes.
    time = uncountable.
  • How much money do you need?
    money = uncountable.

Asking about prices

For prices, always use “how much”, because we think of money: “How much is it?”. For a plural: “How much are they?” / “How much are these shoes?”.

With the verb “cost”: “How much does it cost?” / “How much do they cost?”. Careful with the order: don't say “How much costs it?”.

To answer: “It's five euros.” / “They're two dollars each.” “Cheap” = low price; “expensive” = high price.

How much is this bag?It's 30 euros.

  • Excuse me, how much is this jacket? — It's 45 euros.
    Asking in a shop.
  • Wow, that's expensive!
    A reaction to a high price.

Answering: a lot, not many, not much…

You can answer with a number or a quantity expression. “A lot” works with both kinds of noun. “Not many” goes with countable nouns, “not much” with uncountable nouns.

Lesson 58 looks at “a few” and “a little” in more detail. “None” means “not one, nothing”.

  • How many friends have you got in Lyon? — Not many. Just two.
    not many + countable.
  • How much homework have you got? — A lot!
    a lot: countable or uncountable.

The formula

How many + plural noun…? · How much + uncountable noun…? · How much is it? / How much does it cost?

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • In “How many”, stress “many”: /haʊ ˈmeni/. “Many” sounds like “menny”.
  • “How much is it?” is linked in one chain: /haʊ ˈmʌtʃ ɪz ɪt/ ≈ “how-mu-chi-zit”.
  • “a lot of” is /ə ˈlɒt əv/ and often sounds like “a lotta” in informal speech.
  • “cost” /kɒst/ and “costs” /kɒsts/: say the final -sts clearly.
  • For prices, say the currency after the number: £5 = “five pounds”, $20 = “twenty dollars”, €3.50 = “three euros fifty”.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ How much brothers have you got?✓ How many brothers have you got?“Brothers” is countable: how many.
✗ How many money do you need?✓ How much money do you need?“Money” is uncountable: how much.
✗ How much people are there?✓ How many people are there?“People” is a countable plural.
✗ How much costs it?✓ How much is it? / How much does it cost?With “cost”, you need “does” in the question.
✗ How many informations do you need?✓ How much information do you need?“Information” is uncountable, no s.
✗ How many time have we got?✓ How much time have we got?time (minutes) is uncountable; times (occasions) is countable.
✗ How much are the bread?✓ How much is the bread?An uncountable noun takes a singular verb.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

At Mokolo market in Yaoundé, Claire is shopping at Mama Ngo's stall.

Claire

Good morning! How much are the mangoes?
Price of a plural: are.

Mama Ngo

They're two hundred francs each.
each = for one.

Claire

OK. I'd like six, please. And how much is a kilo of tomatoes?
Price of one thing: is.

Mama Ngo

A kilo is one thousand francs. How many kilos do you want?
kilos = countable.

Claire

Just one. And some rice, please.
rice = uncountable.

Mama Ngo

How much rice do you need?
how much + uncountable.

Claire

Two kilos. So, how much is that altogether?
altogether = in total.

Mama Ngo

Four thousand two hundred francs.
A big number.

Claire

Here you are. Thank you!
When you give money or a thing.

Key points
  • how many + plural countable noun: How many children have you got?
  • how much + singular uncountable noun: How much water do you drink?
  • Prices: How much is it? / How much are they? / How much does it cost? (never “How much costs it?”).
  • Tricky uncountable nouns: money, time, information, advice, news, furniture, luggage, homework, bread.
  • Answers: a lot, not many / not much, a few / a little, none.
