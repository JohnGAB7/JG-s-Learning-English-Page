The alphabet and spelling your name

Many languages use the same 26 letters, but the English names of the letters are different: E sounds like “ee”, I sounds like “eye”. You need to spell your name, an email address or a word on the phone, at a hotel or in an office.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Say the 26 letters of the English alphabet correctly.
  • Avoid the common confusions: E / I, G / J, A / R, H, W, Y.
  • Spell your name, your address and your email (double, capital, hyphen, dot, at).
  • Ask someone to spell a word and check information: How do you spell that?

Letters grouped by sound

The easiest way is to learn the letters in groups with the same vowel. Each group rhymes: A, H, J, K rhyme with “day”; B, C, D, E… rhyme with “see”.

The letter Z is “zed” /zed/ in British English (and in Canada) and “zee” /ziː/ in American English.

Common traps

Some English letter names sound like a DIFFERENT letter in other languages. This causes most mistakes on the phone.

E is /iː/ (like “see”) and I is /aɪ/ (like “eye”). G is /dʒiː/ (like “gee”) and J is /dʒeɪ/ (like “jay”). A is /eɪ/ (like “day”) and R is /ɑː/ (like “car”). U is /juː/ (like “you”), and W is “double u”, not “double v”.

Spelling: useful words

For two same letters together, say “double”: double L, double S, double T (in American English, people often say the two letters: L-L).

Other words: capital (a big letter) or upper case; small letter or lower case; hyphen; apostrophe; space; accents: “e with an acute accent” (é), “e with a grave accent” (è). For an email: at (@), dot (.), underscore (_), dash or hyphen (-), slash (/).

Asking and checking

To ask someone to spell: “How do you spell that?” / “How do you spell your surname?” / “Could you spell that, please?” (more polite). To check: “Sorry, could you repeat that?”; “Was that B or P?”; “Is that M for mother or N for night?”.

On the phone, people often say a word for a letter (“M for Mike, N for November”). Professionals use the international alphabet: Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, Delta, Echo, Foxtrot, Golf, Hotel, India, Juliett, Kilo, Lima, Mike, November, Oscar, Papa, Quebec, Romeo, Sierra, Tango, Uniform, Victor, Whiskey, X-ray, Yankee, Zulu.

  • How do you spell your name? — K-A-M-G-A.
    Asking and spelling.
  • Is that B for Bravo or P for Papa?
    Checking a letter.
  • Could you spell that again, please?
    A polite request.

Abbreviations

Many abbreviations are said letter by letter, with the English names of the letters: the UK /ˌjuː ˈkeɪ/, the USA, the EU /ˌiː ˈjuː/, a CV /ˌsiː ˈviː/, the BBC, a PC, an ID card /ˌaɪ ˈdiː/. The stress is on the last letter.

Remember (lesson 11): choose a / an by the sound of the first letter: an MP /em/, an FBI agent /ef/, a UN report /juː/.

  • Please send me your CV.
    Say: /siː viː/.
  • Have you got an ID card?
    an before /aɪ/.

The formula

A /eɪ/ · E /iː/ · I /aɪ/ · O /əʊ/ · U /juː/ · G /dʒiː/ · J /dʒeɪ/ · R /ɑː/ · double L · capital · hyphen · at · dot

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • Make E /iː/ (one long sound, lips spread) different from I /aɪ/ (two sounds, “a” then “i”).
  • G /dʒiː/ and J /dʒeɪ/ both start with /dʒ/ (as in “jam”); only the vowel changes.
  • H is /eɪtʃ/: don't forget the “tch” at the end. Some speakers say /heɪtʃ/.
  • W /ˈdʌbəljuː/ has three syllables: DOU-ble-you, stress on the first.
  • When you spell, make small groups of 3 or 4 letters with a pause. Your voice goes up at the end of each group except the last.

Listening Studio

A customer calls a car rental office in Toronto to make a booking.

Agent

Hélène

Yes, it's Hélène Ngassa.

Sorry, how do you spell your first name?

H, E, L, E, N, E. With accents on the first two Es, but that's not important.

N, G, A, double S, A.

Hélène

Yes, G for Golf. And my email is helene underscore ngassa at mail dot com.
underscore, at, dot.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Which sound?

Write the sound of the letter: /eɪ/, /iː/, /e/, /aɪ/, /əʊ/, /uː/ or /ɑː/.

  1. E
  2. I
  3. J
  4. G
  5. R
  6. U
Show the answers
  1. /iː/
  2. /aɪ/
  3. /eɪ/ (dʒeɪ)
  4. /iː/ (dʒiː)
  5. /ɑː/
  6. /uː/ (juː)

2. The odd one out

Find the letter that doesn't have the same sound as the others.

  1. A – H – K – E
  2. B – D – F – T
  3. I – Y – R
  4. Q – U – W – O
Show the answers
  1. E (/iː/; the others have /eɪ/)
  2. F (/ef/; the others have /iː/)
  3. R (/ɑː/; the others have /aɪ/)
  4. O (/əʊ/; the others have /uː/)

3. Read it aloud

Write how you say these addresses and names in English.

  1. anna.b@mail.com
  2. Jean-Marc
  3. Bassey
  4. O'Neil
Show the answers
  1. anna dot b at mail dot com
  2. J, E, A, N, hyphen, M, A, R, C (capital J, capital M)
  3. B, A, double S, E, Y
  4. capital O, apostrophe, N, E, I, L

4. What do you say?

Write the right sentence for each situation.

  1. You want to know the letters of a word.
  2. You ask a customer, politely, for the letters of his name.
  3. You are not sure if the letter is M or N.
  4. Your name has two Ls together.
Show the answers
  1. How do you spell that?
  2. Could you spell your name, please?
  3. Is that M for Mike or N for November?
  4. My name is spelt with a double L. / My name has a double L.

5. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one vocabulary mistake. Correct it.

  1. How do you write your surname? (asking for the letters)
  2. My email is tom arobase mail point fr.
  3. Write your name in big letters.
Show the answers
  1. How do you spell your surname?
  2. My email is tom at mail dot fr.
  3. Write your name in capital letters.

6. Your turn

Write how you spell your first name, your surname and your email address (real or invented), as on the phone.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. My first name is Joël: J, O, E with two dots, L. My surname is Mbappé: M, B, A, double P, E acute. My email is joel dot m at mail dot com.
Key points
  • E = /iː/, I = /aɪ/, A = /eɪ/, R = /ɑː/, U = /juː/.
  • G = /dʒiː/ (gee) and J = /dʒeɪ/ (jay).
  • double L, capital M, hyphen, apostrophe; @ = at, . = dot, _ = underscore.
  • To ask: How do you spell that? Could you spell your name, please?
  • Z = zed (UK, Canada), zee (US).
