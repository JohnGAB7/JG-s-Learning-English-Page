A2 · Elementary · lesson 62

Verbs + -ing or + to

When two verbs come together, the second one is either an -ing form (I enjoy cooking) or to + base verb (I want to cook). The first verb decides, so you need to learn the most common ones.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Use -ing after enjoy, finish, mind, avoid, suggest, keep, practise, can't stand…
  • Use to + verb after want, would like, decide, hope, plan, need, learn, forget…
  • Know that like, love, hate, start, begin can take both forms.
  • Use -ing after a preposition (good at cooking, before leaving) and build want / ask / tell + someone + to.

Verb + -ing

After these verbs, the second verb takes the -ing form. Many of them talk about likes, habits, or an action that is already happening or that you think about.

The most common is “enjoy”: I enjoy swimming. Never say “I enjoy to swim”. Other very useful verbs: finish, mind (be unhappy about: Do you mind waiting?), avoid, suggest, keep (continue, not stop), practise, miss (feel sad because something is not there), imagine, can't stand (hate), give up (stop).

  • I enjoy reading in the evening.
    enjoy + -ing.
  • Have you finished cleaning the kitchen?
    finish + -ing.
  • Do you mind opening the window?
    A polite request.
  • He keeps calling me!
    keep = not stop.
  • I miss living near the sea.
    miss + -ing.
“Suggest” takes -ing (She suggested going to the cinema), never “to”: don't say “She suggested to go”.

Verb + to + base verb

After these verbs, the second verb is the infinitive with “to”. Many of them talk about wishes, decisions, plans or efforts for the future.

The most common: want, would like (lesson 34), need, hope, plan, decide, learn, agree, promise, refuse, offer, forget, manage (succeed after trying), try, afford (have enough money for), seem.

VerbExampleMeaning
wantI want to learn Spanish.a wish
would likeI'd like to book a table.a polite wish
decideWe decided to stay at home.a decision
hopeI hope to see you soon.a wish for the future
learnShe's learning to drive.getting a new skill
forgetDon't forget to call your mother.not remember to do it
manageDid you manage to find the hotel?succeed after trying
can't affordWe can't afford to buy a car.not have enough money
Put “not” before “to” for the negative: I decided not to go.

Verbs with both forms

After like, love, hate, prefer, start, begin, continue, you can use -ing or to with almost no change in meaning: I like swimming / I like to swim. It started raining / It started to rain.

British nuance: “like + -ing” is more about enjoyment (I like dancing); “like + to” is more about a habit you choose because it is useful (I like to arrive early). In American English, “like to” is very common for both.

Be careful: “would like”, “would love”, “would hate”, “would prefer” ALWAYS take to: I'd love to come (not “I'd love coming”).

  • I love cooking. / I love to cook.
    Both are correct.
  • She began working here in 2020.
    begin + -ing (or to work).
  • Would you like to dance?
    would like → to.

After a preposition: always -ing

After a preposition (in, at, of, about, for, without, before, after, by…), a verb is always in the -ing form.

Very common expressions: be good at, be interested in, be afraid of, be tired of, think about, look forward to (here “to” is a preposition!), thank you for, how about…? / what about…?

  • She's good at drawing.
    at + -ing.
  • I'm interested in learning Chinese.
    in + -ing.
  • Wash your hands before eating.
    before + -ing.
  • He left without saying goodbye.
    without + -ing.
  • I'm looking forward to seeing you.
    to (preposition) + -ing.
  • How about going to the beach?
    A suggestion.

Want, ask, tell + someone + to

With want, would like, ask, tell, help, expect, you can put a person between the two verbs: verb + object + to + base verb. Say “I want you to come”. Never say “I want that you come”.

Negative: tell someone not to do something.

  • I want you to listen.
    want + you + to.
  • My boss asked me to stay late.
    ask + me + to.
  • The doctor told him not to smoke.
    tell + him + not to.
Some verbs (stop, remember, forget, try) change meaning with the form: stop smoking (not smoke any more) / stop to smoke (stop doing something else so you can smoke). You will study this in lesson 113 (B1).

The formula

enjoy / finish / mind / avoid / suggest / keep + -ing · want / need / decide / hope / learn / forget + to + verb · preposition + -ing · want someone to…

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “to” before a verb is weak: /tə/ (I want to go /aɪ ˈwɒnt tə ˈɡəʊ/). In informal speech: “wanna” (want to), “gonna” (going to). Understand them, but don't write them.
  • “I'd like to” sounds like /aɪd ˈlaɪk tə/: the d of “I'd” is light but present.
  • -ing is /ɪŋ/, with no hard g at the end.
  • “Do you mind…?”: stress MIND; the voice goes up at the end. A polite answer: “No, not at all” (= it's no problem).
  • “look forward to”: stress FOR-: /lʊk ˈfɔːwəd tə/.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Two friends in Québec talk about their summer plans.

Maya

Have you decided to go anywhere this summer?
decide + to.

Zoé

Yes! I want to visit Vancouver. I enjoy travelling by train, so I'm planning to take the train across Canada.
want + to; enjoy + -ing; plan + to.

Maya

Wow! Isn't it expensive?
A negative question.

Zoé

A bit. I can't afford to stay in hotels, so I'm thinking about staying with friends.
afford + to; about + -ing.

Maya

Good idea. I'd love to come with you, but I need to work in July.
would love + to; need + to.

Zoé

What about meeting me there in August?
What about + -ing: a suggestion.

Maya

Maybe! I don't mind flying. I'll ask my boss to give me a week off.
mind + -ing; ask + someone + to.

Zoé

Great! Don't forget to tell me. I'm really looking forward to seeing the mountains!
forget + to; look forward to + -ing.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. -ing or to?

Choose the right form.

  1. I enjoy (to cook / cooking).
  2. She wants (to buy / buying) a new phone.
  3. We decided (to leave / leaving) early.
  4. Have you finished (to eat / eating)?
  5. He hopes (to find / finding) a job soon.
  6. Do you mind (to close / closing) the door?
Show the answers
  1. cooking
  2. to buy
  3. to leave
  4. eating
  5. to find
  6. closing

2. Complete

Put the verb in brackets in the right form (-ing or to + verb).

  1. Don't forget ___ (buy) some bread.
  2. I'm tired of ___ (wait).
  3. She's good at ___ (sing).
  4. They suggested ___ (go) to a museum.
  5. I'd like ___ (speak) to the manager.
Show the answers
  1. to buy
  2. waiting
  3. singing
  4. going
  5. to speak

3. Verb + person + to

Rewrite the sentence with want / ask / tell + person + to.

  1. My mother says: “Paul, clean your room.” (tell)
  2. I say to you: “Please help me.” (ask)
  3. The teacher says: “Don't talk!” (tell … not)
  4. I wish you would stay. (want)
Show the answers
  1. My mother tells Paul to clean his room.
  2. I'm asking you to help me.
  3. The teacher tells us not to talk.
  4. I want you to stay.

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Correct it.

  1. I want that you call me.
  2. He avoided to answer.
  3. Thank you for help me.
  4. I'm looking forward to meet you.
  5. We can't afford going on holiday.
Show the answers
  1. I want you to call me.
  2. He avoided answering.
  3. Thank you for helping me.
  4. I'm looking forward to meeting you.
  5. We can't afford to go on holiday.

5. Write the sentence

Use the words to write a correct sentence.

  1. I / enjoy / swim
  2. she / learn / drive / last year
  3. he / leave / without / pay
  4. I / would like / you / come / with me
Show the answers
  1. I enjoy swimming.
  2. She learnt to drive last year. / She learned to drive last year.
  3. He left without paying.
  4. I'd like you to come with me.

6. Your turn

Write five true sentences: something you enjoy doing, something you want to do, something you avoid, something you are good at, and a plan (plan / hope).

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. I enjoy cooking for my friends. I want to learn the guitar. I avoid driving in Brussels at rush hour. I'm quite good at drawing. I'm planning to visit my cousins in Douala next year.
Key points
  • + -ing: enjoy, finish, mind, avoid, suggest, keep, practise, miss, can't stand.
  • + to: want, would like, need, hope, plan, decide, learn, forget, manage, afford, promise.
  • Both: like, love, hate, start, begin (but would like / would love + to).
  • Preposition + -ing: good at cooking, before leaving, look forward to seeing.
  • want / ask / tell + person + to: I want you to come (never “want that”).
