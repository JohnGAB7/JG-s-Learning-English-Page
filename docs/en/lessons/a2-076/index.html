Talking about your weekend

“How was your weekend?” is the classic Monday morning question in English-speaking countries. This lesson helps you talk about your weekend in the past simple, ask questions back and react naturally to other people's stories.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Talk about a past weekend in the past simple with the most common irregular verbs.
  • Ask questions: How was your weekend? What did you do? Did you go out?
  • React to a story: That sounds fun! Lucky you! Oh no!
  • Talk about next weekend with the present continuous and be going to.

Monday questions

The most common questions are “How was your weekend?”, “Did you have a good weekend?” and “What did you do at the weekend?” (US: “on the weekend”).

You can ask for more details: “Did you do anything nice / special?”, “Did you go out?”, “Where did you go?”, “Who did you go with?”. Remember: past simple questions use “did” + base verb (lesson 67).

The usual answer is short and positive, with one detail: “It was great, thanks. I went to the beach.” Then ask the question back: “What about you?” / “How was yours?”

QuestionPossible answer
How was your weekend?It was great / lovely / quiet / busy / awful.
Did you have a good weekend?Yes, thanks. / Not really.
What did you do?I visited my grandparents in Bafoussam.
Did you go out?Yes, we went to a concert. / No, I stayed in.
Did you do anything special?Not really. Nothing special.
  • How was your weekend? — Really nice, thanks. I went hiking. How was yours?
    Answer + detail + question back.
  • Did you do anything special? — No, nothing special. I just relaxed.
    A quiet weekend.

Weekend verbs in the past simple

Weekend activities use a lot of irregular verbs (lesson 42). Learn them in full phrases: “went shopping”, “had a barbecue”, “saw a film”.

  • On Saturday I had a lie-in and then I did the shopping.
    had a lie-in = stayed in bed late.
  • We spent Sunday at my in-laws' house.
    spend time somewhere.
  • I met some friends and we ate out in a Lebanese restaurant.
    ate out = ate in a restaurant.
Careful: say “have a party” (not “make a party”) and “spend time” (not “pass time”).

Organising your story

Say when: “on Saturday morning”, “on Sunday afternoon”, “in the evening”, “all weekend” (no “the”).

Link the steps with the words from lesson 75: first, then, after that, in the end. For the background or an action that was interrupted, use the past continuous (lesson 43): “We were having lunch outside when it started to rain.”

For a quiet weekend: “I stayed in”, “I just relaxed”, “I didn't do much”, “I caught up on some sleep”.

  • On Saturday morning I went to the gym. In the afternoon I visited my sister.
    Times of the day.
  • We were walking in the park when we saw an old friend.
    Past continuous + past simple.
  • I was ill, so I stayed in bed all weekend.
    all weekend: no “the”.

Reacting to a story

In a conversation, you don't just listen: you react. English speakers use many short phrases to show interest.

“That sounds…” + adjective is very common: “That sounds fun / great / lovely / awful”. You can also ask a follow-up question: “Really? Where did you go?”

SituationReactionNote
good newsThat sounds great! / How nice!positive
good luckLucky you!a little jealous, in a friendly way
bad newsOh no! / That's a shame. / Poor you!showing sympathy
surpriseReally? / No way!“No way!” is informal
a quiet timeSounds relaxing.“That” is often dropped
  • I went to the beach in Grand-Bassam. — Lucky you! Was it hot?
    Reaction + follow-up question.
  • My car broke down on the motorway. — Oh no! That's awful.
    Sympathy.

And next weekend?

On Friday the question changes: “What are you doing this weekend?” or “Have you got any plans for the weekend?”. For an arrangement, use the present continuous (lesson 50); for an intention, “be going to” (lesson 45).

To wish someone a good weekend: “Have a nice / good weekend!” — answer: “Thanks, you too!”

  • What are you doing this weekend? — I'm meeting my cousins in Ghent on Saturday.
    An arrangement.
  • I'm going to paint my bedroom.
    An intention.
  • Have a good weekend! — Thanks, you too!
    Saying goodbye on Friday.

The formula

How was your weekend? — It was… I went / had / saw… What about you? · That sounds great! · What are you doing this weekend?

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • The -ed of regular verbs: /t/ (watched, relaxed), /d/ (stayed, played), /ɪd/ only after t or d (visited, rested).
  • “Did you” is linked in speech: /ˈdɪdʒə/ (“didja”). “What did you do?” ≈ /ˈwɒdɪdʒə ˈduː/.
  • Irregular verbs to say well: saw /sɔː/, bought /bɔːt/, caught /kɔːt/ (same long vowel), ate /et/ (UK) or /eɪt/, met /met/.
  • “That sounds great!”: the voice falls on GREAT and shows enthusiasm; a flat voice can sound ironic.
  • “weekend”: stress on the first syllable in British English (WEEKend), often on the second in American English (weekEND).

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ What did you do the weekend?✓ What did you do at the weekend? / What did you do last weekend?You need a preposition (at, US on) or “last”.
✗ I goed to the beach.✓ I went to the beach.“Go” is irregular: went.
✗ Did you went out?✓ Did you go out?After “did”, use the base verb.
✗ I have seen a film on Saturday.✓ I saw a film on Saturday.A finished time in the past: past simple, not present perfect.
✗ I stayed at home all the weekend.✓ I stayed at home all weekend.“All weekend”, with no “the”.
✗ We made a party.✓ We had a party.Say “have a party”.
✗ I rested myself.✓ I rested. / I relaxed.“Rest” and “relax” don't take “myself”.
✗ Nothing of special.✓ Nothing special.No “of” after nothing / something.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Monday morning at the coffee machine in a company in Lyon. Inès and Kwame talk about their weekend.

Kwame

Morning, Inès! How was your weekend?
The classic question.

Inès

Lovely, thanks. My sister came from Brussels, so we went to Annecy.
came, went: irregular verbs.

Kwame

Lucky you! What did you do there?
Reaction + follow-up question.

Inès

We walked around the lake and had lunch in the old town. Then we rented a boat.
A sequence of actions.

Kwame

That sounds amazing.
That sounds + adjective.

Inès

It was! And you? Did you do anything special?
Asking the question back.

Kwame

Not really. On Saturday I did the shopping and cleaned the flat.
A quiet weekend.

Kwame

On Sunday I watched the Africa Cup final with some friends.
watched: regular verb.

Inès

Oh, was it a good match?
Follow-up question.

Kwame

Fantastic! We were jumping and shouting all evening!
Past continuous for a long action.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Past simple forms

Write the past simple.

  1. go
  2. have
  3. spend
  4. meet
  5. stay
  6. buy
Show the answers
  1. went
  2. had
  3. spent
  4. met
  5. stayed
  6. bought

2. Ask the questions

Write the question in the past simple.

  1. how / be / your weekend?
  2. what / you / do / on Saturday?
  3. you / go out / on Friday night?
  4. where / you / have / lunch?
  5. who / you / go / with?
Show the answers
  1. How was your weekend?
  2. What did you do on Saturday?
  3. Did you go out on Friday night?
  4. Where did you have lunch?
  5. Who did you go with?

3. React

Choose a good reaction: Lucky you! / Oh no! / That sounds relaxing. / Really?

  1. I won 500 euros in a competition!
  2. I lost my phone on the train.
  3. I read a book in the garden all afternoon.
  4. I met the president of the football club in a café!
Show the answers
  1. Lucky you! (or: Really?)
  2. Oh no!
  3. That sounds relaxing.
  4. Really?

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. What did you do the last weekend?
  2. We made a party on Saturday night.
  3. I passed Sunday with my family.
  4. I stayed in all the weekend.
  5. Nothing of special. I rested myself.
Show the answers
  1. What did you do last weekend?
  2. We had a party on Saturday night.
  3. I spent Sunday with my family.
  4. I stayed in all weekend.
  5. Nothing special. I relaxed. / I just rested.

5. Your turn

A colleague asks you “How was your weekend?”. Answer in five or six sentences in the past simple, then ask the question back and say what you are doing next weekend.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. It was really nice, thanks. On Saturday morning I had a lie-in, and then I went to the market with my mum. In the afternoon some friends came over and we watched a film. On Sunday I visited my grandparents and we ate a big lunch together. What about you? Next weekend I'm going to a wedding in Douala.
Key points
  • How was your weekend? / Did you have a good weekend? / What did you do at (US on) the weekend?
  • Past simple: went, had, saw, met, did, spent, ate, slept; questions with did + base verb.
  • have a party, spend time, all weekend, stay in, go out, have a lie-in.
  • React: That sounds great! Lucky you! Oh no! Really? — and ask back: What about you?
  • Next weekend: What are you doing this weekend? — I'm meeting… / I'm going to…
