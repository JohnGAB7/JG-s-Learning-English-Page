Adverbs of frequency

Always, usually, often, sometimes, never… These small words say how often you do something. They go with the present simple very often, and their position in the sentence is important.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Know the adverbs of frequency and put them in order from 100% to 0%.
  • Put them in the right place: before the main verb, but after “to be”.
  • Use frequency expressions (every day, twice a week, once a month) at the end of the sentence.
  • Ask and answer questions with “How often…?” and “Do you ever…?”.

From “always” to “never”

Adverbs of frequency say how many times an action happens. The percentages in the table are not exact: they help you compare the words.

“Never” is already negative: don't use it with “don't”. “Hardly ever” (almost never) is more common in speaking than “rarely” or “seldom”, which are more formal.

AdverbHow oftenMeaningExample
always100%every timeI always have breakfast.
usually≈ 90%almost every timeShe usually walks to work.
often≈ 70%many timesWe often eat rice.
sometimes≈ 50%some of the timeThey sometimes go to the cinema.
rarely / seldom≈ 10%not oftenHe rarely drinks coffee.
hardly ever≈ 5%almost neverI hardly ever watch TV.
never0%not at any timeMy father never smokes.
“Normally” and “generally” mean the same as “usually” and go in the same place.

Position: before the main verb

In many languages the adverb goes after the verb. In English, it goes BEFORE the main verb: I often eat. This is the most common mistake.

With he / she / it, the verb keeps its -s: She often eats. The adverb doesn't change the verb.

In a negative sentence with don't / doesn't, the adverb goes after the auxiliary: I don't often go out.

  • I usually get up at seven.
    usually before get up.
  • She never eats meat.
    never before eats; no doesn't.
  • We sometimes play football on Sundays.
    sometimes before play.
  • He doesn't usually work late.
    doesn't + adverb + verb.

After “to be” and between two verbs

With the verb “to be”, it is the opposite: the adverb goes AFTER am / is / are. She is always late. They are never tired.

When there is an auxiliary or a modal (have got, can, will…), the adverb goes between it and the main verb: I can never remember his name. I've always got my phone with me.

Summary: subject + be + adverb; subject + adverb + verb; subject + auxiliary + adverb + verb.

PatternExampleNote
subject + be + adverbHe is often tired.after be
subject + adverb + verbHe often feels tired.before the main verb
subject + auxiliary + adverb + verbHe doesn't often go out.after doesn't
subject + modal + adverb + verbI can never find my keys.after can
  • The bus is usually on time.
    after is.
  • I'm never sure about the answer.
    after 'm (am).

Usually, sometimes, often at the start or end

To make them stronger, “sometimes”, “usually”, “normally” and “often” can also go at the start of the sentence, and “sometimes” can go at the end. “Always”, “never”, “rarely” and “hardly ever” don't go at the start in normal style.

So don't say “Always I go…” or “Never I eat…”.

  • Sometimes I work from home.
    sometimes at the start.
  • Usually, the shop is quiet in the morning.
    usually at the start.
  • I go to the gym sometimes.
    sometimes at the end.

Frequency expressions and questions

For an exact frequency, use an expression with several words: every day, every week, once a week, twice a month, three times a year. These go at the end of the sentence (sometimes at the start), never between the subject and the verb.

Say “twice a week”: “a” means “in each”. “Per week” is more formal or technical. For two times, say “twice”; from three, say “three times, four times”.

To ask about frequency: “How often do you…?”. To ask if something happens at all: “Do you ever…?” (ever = at any time, in questions).

  • How often do you go swimming? — Twice a week.
    Question and answer.
  • I visit my grandparents once a month.
    Expression at the end.
  • Do you ever cook? — Yes, sometimes. / No, never.
    ever in a question.
  • We go to Kribi every summer.
    every + season.

The formula

subject + always / usually / often / sometimes / never + verb · subject + be + adverb · How often…? — once / twice / three times a week

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “often” is /ˈɒfn/ (silent t) or /ˈɒftən/: both are correct. American: /ˈɔːfn/.
  • “usually” /ˈjuːʒuəli/: stress on the first syllable, with the /ʒ/ sound of “television”. In fast speech: /ˈjuːʒli/.
  • “always” /ˈɔːlweɪz/: stress on AL-, and say the l lightly.
  • “twice” /twaɪs/ rhymes with “nice”. “once” is /wʌns/, with a w sound at the start, like “one”.
  • In “How often…?”, your voice goes down at the end, because it is a Wh- question.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ I go always to work by bus.✓ I always go to work by bus.The adverb goes before the main verb.
✗ She always is late.✓ She is always late.With “to be”, the adverb goes after the verb.
✗ I don't never eat fish.✓ I never eat fish.“Never” is already negative: no don't.
✗ He never eat meat.✓ He never eats meat.Keep the -s for he / she / it after the adverb.
✗ I play tennis two times by week.✓ I play tennis twice a week.Two times = twice; in each week = a week.
✗ I eat every day rice.✓ I eat rice every day.A frequency expression doesn't go between the verb and its object: put it at the end.
✗ Always I drink tea.✓ I always drink tea.“Always” doesn't go at the start of the sentence.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

A fitness coach in Ottawa asks a new client about her habits.

Coach

So, Émilie, how often do you do sport?
A question about frequency.

Émilie

Not very often. I go swimming once a week, usually on Saturday.
once a week; usually.

Coach

OK. Do you ever walk to work?
ever in a question.

Émilie

Sometimes, in summer. But in winter I always take the bus. It's too cold!
always before take.

Coach

And what about food? Do you often eat fast food?
often between subject and verb.

Émilie

No, I hardly ever eat fast food. But I'm often hungry at night.
hardly ever; often after 'm.

Coach

Do you sleep well?
A simple question.

Émilie

Not really. I never go to bed before midnight.
never: no don't.

Coach

OK. Let's start with three short walks a week.
three … a week.

Key points
  • always > usually > often > sometimes > rarely > hardly ever > never.
  • Before the main verb (I often eat), but after “to be” (I'm often late).
  • Never is already negative: I never eat meat, not “I don't never”.
  • Exact frequency at the end: once a week, twice a month, three times a year, every day.
  • Questions: How often do you…? / Do you ever…?
