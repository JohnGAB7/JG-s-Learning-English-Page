A1 · Beginner · lesson 24

Describing a person

We describe people every day: to find someone at the airport, to talk about a colleague, to present a friend. English uses “be” for height and character, and “have got” for hair and eyes.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Know the difference between “What does she look like?” (appearance) and “What is she like?” (character).
  • Describe height, body, age, hair and eyes.
  • Describe character with common adjectives and avoid false friends.
  • Build a description with be, have got, wear and with.

Three questions: don't mix them up

“What does he look like?” asks about appearance: “He's tall with short hair.” “What is he like?” asks about character (or a general opinion): “He's very kind.” “How is he?” asks about health and feelings: “He's fine, thanks.”

In “What is he like?”, “like” is not the verb “like” (love). It is a preposition that means “similar to”. The question means “Describe him for me”.

QuestionIt asks about…Possible answer
What does she look like?appearanceShe's tall and slim. She's got long hair.
What is she like?characterShe's friendly and funny.
How is she?health, feelingsShe's fine. / She's a bit tired.
  • What does your new boss look like? — She's short with grey hair.
    Appearance.
  • What's your new boss like? — She's very patient.
    Character.

Height, body and age: be + adjective

For height, body and age, use “be” + adjective. Adjectives never take an s: “They're tall”, not “They're talls”.

For exact height, also use “be”: “He's one metre eighty” or “He's 1.80 m (tall)”. In the UK and the USA, people also use feet and inches: “He's six feet tall”.

Be polite: “fat” can hurt people. Say “a bit overweight” or “big”. For an old person, “elderly” is more polite than “old”.

TopicAdjectivesNote
Heighttall · short · medium height“short” is the opposite of “tall”
Bodyslim · thin · well-built · a bit overweight“slim” is positive; “thin” can be negative
Ageyoung · middle-aged · old / elderly“elderly” is polite
Age (more or less)in his twenties · in her forties20–29 years old · 40–49 years old
Looksgood-looking · pretty · handsomepretty: usually women; handsome: usually men
  • My brother is tall and slim.
    be + two adjectives.
  • She's in her thirties.
    She is between 30 and 39.
“Short” is the opposite of “tall” for people, and of “long” for hair. Don't say “He is little” about an adult: say “He's short”.

Hair, eyes and details: have got / wear / with

For hair, eyes and beards, use “have got” or “have”: “She's got brown eyes.” / “She has brown eyes.”

“Hair” (on your head) is uncountable: it stays singular, with no “a” and no s. “Her hair is long.” The plural “hairs” means a few single hairs.

Order of adjectives before “hair”: length, then shape, then colour: “long curly black hair”. Lesson 35 looks at adjective order in more detail.

For clothes and glasses, use “wear”: “He wears glasses.” You can also use “with”: “the woman with red hair”, “a man with a beard”.

LengthShapeColour
long · short · shoulder-lengthstraight · curly · wavyblack · brown · fair / blonde · red · grey · white
  • He's got short curly black hair.
    Order: length, shape, colour.
  • My grandfather is bald and he has a white beard.
    bald = with no hair.
  • She wears glasses.
    wear + glasses.
  • Who's the man with the moustache?
    with + a detail.
No article before “hair” in a description: “She has black hair”, never “She has the black hair”.

Character

For character, use “be” + adjective: “She's kind.” You can add “very”, “quite” (fairly) and “a bit” (a little). We use “a bit” mostly for negative qualities: “He's a bit lazy.”

False friends: “sympathetic” means you understand someone's problems; it does not mean “nice”. “Sensible” means practical and reasonable; a person who cries easily is “sensitive”.

PositiveOppositeExample
friendlyunfriendlyThe staff are very friendly.
kindunkindIt's kind of you to help.
hard-workinglazyAma is very hard-working.
patientimpatientTeachers need to be patient.
chatty / outgoingshy / quietMy son is shy with new people.
generousmean (UK) / stingyMy uncle is very generous.
funnyseriousHer husband is really funny.
  • My colleague is very friendly but a bit shy.
    very + quality; a bit + small problem.
  • Our teacher is quite strict but very funny.
    quite = fairly.

The formula

be + height / character (He's tall and kind) · have got + hair / eyes (He's got short black hair) · wear glasses

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “hair” /heə/ and “her” /hɜː/ are different: “hair” sounds like “air” with an h. Breathe out for the h.
  • “eyes” /aɪz/ sounds like “I's”; the final s is /z/.
  • “beard” /bɪəd/ sounds like “here'd”; “bald” /bɔːld/ sounds like “bold”.
  • “moustache” is /məˈstɑːʃ/ in British English, with the stress on the second syllable.
  • “What does she look like?”: stress LOOK and LIKE; “does” is weak /dəz/.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ He has blue eyes and blonds hairs.✓ He has blue eyes and blond hair.“Hair” is uncountable; adjectives never take an s.
✗ She is very sympathetic. (meaning nice)✓ She's very nice. / She's very friendly.“Sympathetic” means understanding someone's problems.
✗ How is your brother? — He's tall and slim.✓ What does your brother look like? — He's tall and slim.“How is he?” asks about health; for appearance: “What does he look like?”.
✗ She has the long hair.✓ She has long hair.No article before “hair” in a description.
✗ He has long hair black.✓ He has long black hair.Adjectives go before the noun: length + colour + hair.
✗ He measures 1.80 m.✓ He's 1.80 m tall. / He's one metre eighty.Use “be” for a person's height.
✗ My sister is very sensible, she cries at every film.✓ My sister is very sensitive.sensible = reasonable; sensitive = easily upset or touched.
✗ What is like your sister?✓ What is your sister like?“Like” goes at the end of the question.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Marc must meet his wife's cousin at Montreal airport. He has never seen her.

Marc

What does your cousin look like? I don't know her.
Question about appearance.

Awa

She's quite tall and slim, and she's got long curly black hair.
be + height; have got + hair.

Marc

Does she wear glasses?
wear + glasses.

Awa

Yes, big red glasses. And she's in her twenties.
Between 20 and 29.

Marc

OK. And what's she like? I'm a bit nervous.
Question about character.

Awa

Don't worry, she's really friendly and very funny. She's a bit chatty!
Character adjectives.

Marc

Great. So: a tall woman with red glasses and curly hair.
“with” + details.

Awa

Exactly. Her name is Fatou. Here's her number.
“Here's” = here is.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Look like or like?

Which question goes with the answer: “What does he/she look like?” or “What is he/she like?”?

  1. … — He's tall with short hair.
  2. … — He's very kind and funny.
  3. … — She's got long curly hair.
  4. … — She's quite shy.
Show the answers
  1. What does he look like?
  2. What is he like?
  3. What does she look like?
  4. What is she like?

2. Opposites

Give the opposite.

  1. tall (person)
  2. long (hair)
  3. curly (hair)
  4. shy
  5. lazy
  6. friendly
Show the answers
  1. short
  2. short
  3. straight
  4. chatty / outgoing
  5. hard-working
  6. unfriendly

3. Put the words in order

Put the words in the right order to make a sentence.

  1. hair / she / long / has / black
  2. got / beard / he's / a
  3. eyes / brown / has / he
  4. wears / glasses / my teacher
Show the answers
  1. She has long black hair.
  2. He's got a beard.
  3. He has brown eyes.
  4. My teacher wears glasses.

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. She has short blondes hairs.
  2. My neighbour is very sympathic.
  3. He has forty years more or less.
  4. What is like your teacher?
  5. The man of the beard is my uncle.
Show the answers
  1. She has short blonde hair.
  2. My neighbour is very nice. / My neighbour is very friendly.
  3. He's in his forties.
  4. What's your teacher like?
  5. The man with the beard is my uncle.

5. Your turn

Describe a person you know well: appearance (height, hair, eyes) and character, in four or five sentences.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. My best friend is called Samuel. He's in his thirties. He's tall and well-built, and he's got short black hair and brown eyes. He wears glasses. He's very kind and funny, but he's a bit lazy!
Key points
  • What does she look like? = appearance; What is she like? = character; How is she? = health.
  • be + height, age, character: She's tall, young and friendly.
  • have got + hair / eyes: He's got short curly black hair (length, shape, colour). “Hair” stays singular, with no article.
  • wear glasses; the man with a beard.
  • False friends: nice / friendly (not “sympathetic”); sensitive (not “sensible”) for a person who is easily upset.
