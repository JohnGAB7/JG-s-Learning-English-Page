“Be going to” (plans and predictions)

We use “be going to” to talk about plans and intentions (“I'm going to change jobs”) and to predict something when we can already see the signs (“Look at those clouds — it's going to rain”).

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Form “be going to” + base verb with every subject, in all forms.
  • Talk about a plan or intention decided before speaking.
  • Make a prediction based on something you can see now.
  • Understand “gonna” in speech and know when not to write it.

The form

Use the present of “to be” (am / is / are), then “going to” and the base verb. Only “be” changes with the subject; “going to” + base verb never changes.

Negative: add not after be (I'm not going to, she isn't going to). Question: put be before the subject (Are you going to…?). Short answers: Yes, I am. / No, she isn't.

Use 1: plans and intentions

Use “be going to” for a decision you made before the moment of speaking: a plan, an intention, a resolution. Nothing needs to be booked or organised, but the decision exists.

Common time words: tomorrow, next week, next year, this summer, soon, one day.

  • I'm going to learn Spanish next year.
    I have decided.
  • We're going to paint the kitchen this weekend.
    A plan.
  • She isn't going to accept the job in Ottawa.
    A negative intention.

Use 2: predictions with present evidence

We also use “be going to” to predict something when we can see now what makes it likely: a sign, some evidence, a situation that has already started.

Ask yourself: “Can I see it coming?” If yes, going to is natural.

  • Look at those dark clouds. It's going to rain.
    Evidence: the clouds.
  • Be careful! You're going to fall!
    I can see it happening.
  • The score is 3–0 with five minutes left. Cameroon are going to win.
    Evidence: the score. In British English, a team often takes a plural verb.

Special points

With the verb “go”, people often just say “I'm going to the market” (present continuous) instead of “I'm going to go to the market”. Both are correct; the second one is a bit heavy but common in speech.

“Gonna” is the fast pronunciation of “going to” + verb. You hear it everywhere in films and songs, but do not write it in an email or careful text. Note: “gonna” only replaces “going to” before a verb, never “going to” + a place (not “I'm gonna the shop”).

“Will” (lesson 49) is used, among other things, for decisions made at the moment of speaking, and the present continuous (lesson 50) for fixed arrangements. Here, remember: decision already made or visible evidence → going to.

SituationSentenceWhy

decided nowThe phone's ringing. — I'll answer it.instant decision → will (lesson 49)

The formula

am / is / are + going to + base verb

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • In everyday speech, “going to” + verb often becomes /ˈɡʌnə/ (gonna) or /ˈɡəʊɪŋ tə/ with a weak to: I'm going to call /aɪm ˈɡʌnə kɔːl/.
  • In very fast conversation, “I'm going to” can even sound like /ˈaɪmənə/. Learn to recognise it; you don't have to copy it.
  • Contractions are normal in speech: I'm, you're, he's, we're, they're; and isn't / aren't in negatives.
  • In “Are you going to…?”, the voice goes up at the end of a yes / no question and down in a Wh- question (What are you going to do?).

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Two friends, Ama and Lucas, talk about their summer plans in a café in Lille.

Lucas

So, what are you going to do this summer?
Wh- question about plans.

Ama

I'm going to visit my family in Accra. I'm going to stay for a month.
Two plans.

Lucas

Lucky you! Are you going to work there?
Yes / no question.

Ama

No, I'm not. I'm going to relax. And you?
Short answer: No, I'm not.

Lucas

I'm going to look for a new job. I'm not going to stay at the bank.
An intention and a negative intention.

Ama

Good idea. Oh, look at the sky — it's going to rain.
Prediction with evidence.

Lucas

Again? I'm going to buy a bigger umbrella!
A joke: a new plan.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Choose the form of be

Complete with am, is or are.

  1. I ___ going to cook tonight.
  2. My sister ___ going to study in Canada.
  3. They ___ not going to come.
  4. ___ you going to call him?
  5. It ___ going to be hot tomorrow.
Show the answers
  1. am
  2. is
  3. are
  4. Are
  5. is

2. Complete with going to

Complete with the right form of be going to and the verb in brackets.

  1. We ___ (buy) a new car.
  2. He ___ (not / take) the exam this year.
  3. What ___ you ___ (wear) to the wedding?
  4. I ___ (not / eat) meat this month.
  5. ___ they ___ (sell) their house?
Show the answers
  1. are going to buy
  2. isn't going to take
  3. are … going to wear
  4. 'm not going to eat
  5. Are … going to sell

3. Prediction: what is going to happen?

Write a prediction with going to from the evidence.

  1. The sky is black. (rain)
  2. He's running very fast to the bus stop and the bus is leaving. (miss the bus)
  3. She's very tired and her eyes are closing. (fall asleep)
  4. The glass is at the edge of the table. (fall)
Show the answers
  1. It's going to rain.
  2. He's going to miss the bus.
  3. She's going to fall asleep.
  4. It's going to fall.

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. I'm going to visiting Rome.
  2. Do you going to help me?
  3. She going to start a new job.
  4. They're going to buys a house.
Show the answers
  1. I'm going to visit Rome.
  2. Are you going to help me?
  3. She's going to start a new job.
  4. They're going to buy a house.

5. Plan or prediction?

Write the sentence with going to, then say if it is a plan or a prediction.

  1. I / call / my mother / tonight
  2. Be careful, you / fall!
  3. They / not / come / tomorrow
Show the answers
  1. I'm going to call my mother tonight. (plan)
  2. Be careful, you're going to fall! (prediction)
  3. They aren't going to come tomorrow. (plan / intention)

6. Your turn

Write five sentences about your plans for next year (at least one negative and one question).

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. Next year I'm going to take an English exam. I'm going to read one book in English every month. I'm not going to watch films with French subtitles anymore. My brother and I are going to visit our cousins in Montreal. What are you going to do?
Key points
  • am / is / are + going to + base verb (never -s or -ing after going to).
  • Plan or intention decided before speaking: I'm going to change jobs.
  • Prediction with visible evidence: Look! It's going to rain.
  • Negative: I'm not / isn't / aren't going to; question: Are you going to…?
  • Gonna = fast pronunciation of going to + verb: understand it, don't write it.
