This, that, these, those

This, that, these and those help you point at things: this book, that house, these shoes. English shows if the thing is near or far, and singular or plural: four words to learn.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Choose between this, that, these and those by distance and number.
  • Use them before a noun (this car) or alone, as pronouns (This is my car).
  • Use “this one / that one” so you don't repeat a noun.
  • Know the practical uses: introducing people, phoning, talking about time (this morning, that day), reacting (That's great!).

Near or far, singular or plural

“This” (singular) and “these” (plural) are for things near the speaker: things you hold, things here. “That” (singular) and “those” (plural) are for things further away: over there, on the other side of the room.

The word must agree with the noun: this book / these books; that house / those houses. The verb too: This is…, These are…

Near (here)Far (there)
Singularthis: this phonethat: that phone
Pluralthese: these shoesthose: those shoes
  • This bag is heavy.
    The bag I'm holding.
  • Look at that bird on the roof!
    Far away.
  • These apples are delicious.
    Plural, near.
  • Who are those people over there?
    Plural, far.

Before a noun or alone

These words can go before a noun (determiner): this car, those flowers. They can also stand alone, as pronouns: “This is my car”, “Are those yours?”.

Alone, “this / that” normally means a thing. You can also use it to introduce a person: “This is my sister” (lesson 2). But to continue talking about the person, use he / she: “This is Marc. He's my colleague.”

  • What's this? — It's a mango.
    Pronoun for a thing.
  • Is that your car? — Yes, it is.
    A thing further away.
  • These are my children, Léa and Tom.
    Introducing people.
In the answer, replace this / that with it, and these / those with they: What's that? — It's a church. What are those? — They're bats.

This one, that one

To avoid repeating a countable noun, say “this one” or “that one”. In the plural, just say “these” or “those” (“these ones” is heard in informal British English).

This is very useful in shops, when you choose.

  • Which T-shirt do you want? — This one, please.
    one = T-shirt.
  • I don't like these shoes. I prefer those.
    those = those shoes.

Practical uses: phone, time, reactions

On the phone: give your name with “This is…” or “It's…” (Hello, this is Karim). To ask who is calling, British people say “Who's that?” or, more politely, “Who's calling, please?”. Americans often say “Who is this?”. To check: “Is that Anna?” (UK) / “Is this Anna?” (US).

With time: “this” is for the present or very near period (this morning, this week, this year). “That” is for a past or more distant time that you already talked about (that day, that summer, at that time).

To react to what someone just said, use “that”: That's great! That's true. That's a good idea. “This” is more for what comes next: Listen to this!

  • Hello, this is Karim from the bank.
    Giving your name on the phone.
  • I'm very busy this week.
    The present week.
  • I've got a new job! — That's fantastic!
    Reacting with that.

Agreement traps

Learners often mix “this” and “these”, because the only difference is the vowel: short /ɪ/ in this, long /iː/ in these. A pronunciation mistake quickly becomes a meaning mistake.

Be careful with nouns that are always plural (lesson 12): these trousers, those glasses, these jeans. And with uncountable nouns, always singular: this information, that advice, this news.

  • These jeans are too small.
    jeans = plural.
  • This information is very useful.
    information = uncountable.

The formula

this + singular / these + plural (near) · that + singular / those + plural (far) · this one / that one

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “this” /ðɪs/ (short i, unvoiced s) and “these” /ðiːz/ (long i, voiced z): make the difference big at first so people understand you.
  • “that” /ðæt/: open your mouth wide. “those” /ðəʊz/ rhymes with “nose”.
  • All four words start with /ð/: tongue between the teeth, voice on.
  • Before a noun, these words are often stressed to point or contrast: I want THIS one, not THAT one.
  • “That's” /ðæts/ is one syllable.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

At a market in Yaoundé, a customer buys fruit and fabric.

Customer

Good morning! How much are these mangoes?
these: plural, near.

Seller

These ones are 500 francs a kilo. Those over there are sweeter, but more expensive.
these ones / those: choosing.

Customer

I'll take a kilo of these, please. And what's that?
what's that: a thing further away.

Seller

That's a soursop. It's delicious in juice.
Answer with it.

Customer

Great. And this fabric? Is this cotton?
this: singular, near.

Seller

Yes, it is. This one is from Ghana, and that one is from Cameroon.
this one / that one.

Customer

I love that one! The colours are beautiful.
colours: British spelling.

Seller

That's a good choice. It's our best fabric this year.
That's… to react; this year.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. This, that, these or those?

Complete as shown (near / far).

  1. ___ book (near) is very interesting.
  2. ___ houses (far) are very old.
  3. ___ glasses (near) are mine.
  4. Look at ___ mountain (far)!
  5. ___ cookies (near) are delicious.
  6. Is ___ man (far) your teacher?
Show the answers
  1. This
  2. Those
  3. These
  4. that
  5. These
  6. that

2. Singular → plural

Write the sentence in the plural.

  1. This is my friend.
  2. That car is red.
  3. Is this your key?
  4. That child is noisy.
Show the answers
  1. These are my friends.
  2. Those cars are red.
  3. Are these your keys?
  4. Those children are noisy.

3. Answer with it or they

Answer the question with it's or they're and the words in brackets.

  1. What's that? (a museum)
  2. What are those? (my new boots)
  3. What's this? (a present for you)
Show the answers
  1. It's a museum.
  2. They're my new boots.
  3. It's a present for you.

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Correct it.

  1. This shoes are too small.
  2. These is my sister, Awa.
  3. Those trousers is expensive.
  4. My sister is getting married! — This is great!
  5. These informations are wrong.
Show the answers
  1. These shoes are too small.
  2. This is my sister, Awa.
  3. Those trousers are expensive.
  4. That's great!
  5. This information is wrong.

5. What do you say?

Write a sentence with this, that, these, those or this one / that one for each situation.

  1. In a shop, you choose the shirt in your hand.
  2. You ask about some people on the other side of the street.
  3. You answer the phone and give your name (Pierre).
  4. You say you have a lot of work in the present week.
Show the answers
  1. I prefer this one. / I'll take this one.
  2. Who are those people over there?
  3. Hello, this is Pierre. / Hello, it's Pierre.
  4. I'm working a lot this week. / I'm very busy this week.

6. Your turn

Imagine you are showing a friend around your home or office. Write four sentences with this, that, these and those.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. This is my office. These are my books about history. That door is the bathroom. Those photos on the wall are from my trip to Canada.
Key points
  • this / these = near; that / those = far.
  • this, that + singular (is); these, those + plural (are).
  • this one / that one to choose without repeating the noun.
  • Phone: This is Karim. Reaction: That's great! Time: this week (now), that day (past).
  • Careful: these jeans (always plural), this information (uncountable).
