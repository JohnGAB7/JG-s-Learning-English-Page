Comparatives

We use comparatives to compare two people, things or situations: “bigger than”, “more expensive than”. The form depends on the length of the adjective: -er for short words, more for long words.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Form comparatives of short adjectives (-er) and long adjectives (more).
  • Use the spelling rules and know the irregular forms (better, worse, further).
  • Use than and less … than, and modify comparatives with much, a lot, a bit, far.
  • Avoid typical mistakes: “more bigger”, “more good”, “that” instead of “than”.

Short adjectives: -er + than

For a one-syllable adjective, add -er, then “than” before the thing you compare: cheap → cheaper than, old → older than, fast → faster than.

Two-syllable adjectives ending in -y follow the same rule, but y changes to i: easy → easier, happy → happier, busy → busier.

RuleAdjectiveComparative
1 syllable: + -ersmall, cheap, tallsmaller, cheaper, taller
ends in -e: + -rnice, large, safenicer, larger, safer
1 vowel + 1 consonant: double itbig, hot, thin, wetbigger, hotter, thinner, wetter
consonant + -y: y → iereasy, heavy, funny, earlyeasier, heavier, funnier, earlier
ends in -w, -x, or -y after a vowel: no doublingnew, slow, greynewer, slower, greyer
  • Lagos is bigger than Accra.
    big → bigger (double g).
  • This bag is cheaper than that one.
    cheap → cheaper.

Long adjectives: more + adjective + than

For two-syllable adjectives that do not end in -y, and for all adjectives with three or more syllables, put “more” before the adjective. The adjective does not change.

A few two-syllable adjectives can take both forms: quiet (quieter / more quiet), simple, narrow, clever, polite. With these words, “more” is rarely wrong, but the -er form often sounds more natural for quiet, simple, narrow and clever.

  • English is more useful than Latin for my job.
    useful: 2 syllables, no -y → more.
  • The train is more comfortable than the bus.
    comfortable: long adjective.
  • This exercise is more difficult than the first one.
    difficult: 3 syllables.

Irregular comparatives

Some very common adjectives (and adverbs) are irregular. Learn them by heart: you will use them all the time.

“Further” and “farther” both mean “more far” for distance; “further” is more common in British English, and only “further” also means “more, extra” (further information).

Adjective / adverbComparativeExample
good / wellbetterYour English is better than mine.
bad / badlyworseThe traffic is worse today.
farfurther / fartherKribi is further than Limbé from Douala.
much / manymoreI have more work this week.
little (amount)lessI want less sugar, please.

Than, less and pronouns

“Than” introduces the second part of the comparison. Do not confuse it with “that” or with “then” (after that).

After than, we usually use an object pronoun: “She's taller than me”. “Taller than I am” is more careful; “taller than I” alone sounds very formal.

For the opposite idea, use “less + adjective + than”, especially with long adjectives: less expensive, less important. With short adjectives, “not as … as” (lesson 66) is more natural: “not as old as” rather than “less old than”.

  • My brother is older than me.
    than + object pronoun.
  • This hotel is less expensive than the one in Brussels.
    less + long adjective.
  • You work harder than I do.
    than + subject + helper verb.

Making a comparison stronger or weaker

For a big difference, put much, a lot or far before the comparative. For a small difference, use a bit, a little or slightly. Never say “very bigger”.

To show a change over time, repeat the comparative with and: “bigger and bigger”, “more and more expensive”.

  • Montreal is much colder than Paris in winter.
    much = a big difference.
  • The new phone is a bit more expensive.
    a bit = a small difference.
  • Rent is getting more and more expensive.
    A continuing change.

The formula

short adjective + -er + than · more + long adjective + than · (much / a bit) + comparative

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • The -er ending is /ə/ in British English: bigger /ˈbɪɡə/, cheaper /ˈtʃiːpə/; in American English the final r is pronounced /ər/.
  • “than” is almost always weak: /ðən/: taller than me /ˈtɔːlə ðən miː/.
  • The th in “than” is voiced /ð/, with your tongue between your teeth: not “zan” or “dan”.
  • “better” is /ˈbetə/ (UK); in American English the tt sounds like a quick d /ˈbedər/.
  • To emphasise, stress “much”: “It's MUCH better”.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ She is more tall than me.✓ She is taller than me.One-syllable adjective: -er, not more.
✗ This is more better.✓ This is better.Never more + -er: use only one.
✗ My car is more good than yours.✓ My car is better than yours.good → better (irregular).
✗ He is older that me.✓ He is older than me.In comparisons, use than, not that.
✗ The weather is badder today.✓ The weather is worse today.bad → worse.
✗ It's very cheaper here.✓ It's much cheaper here.Before a comparative: much / a lot / far, not very.
✗ This road is more busy.✓ This road is busier.Two syllables ending in -y: -ier.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Nadia can't choose between two flats in Montreal. She talks to her friend Thomas.

Thomas

So, which flat do you prefer?
A question about preference.

Nadia

The one on Rue Saint-Denis is bigger and brighter than the other one.
Two short comparatives.

Thomas

Is it more expensive?
Long adjective: more.

Nadia

Yes, a bit. It's 100 dollars more a month.
a bit = a small difference.

Thomas

And the other one?
A short question.

Nadia

It's cheaper and closer to my office, but it's much smaller and noisier.
much + comparative; noisy → noisier.

Thomas

Hmm. A quieter flat is better for your sleep.
quiet → quieter; good → better.

Nadia

You're right. Sleep is more important than money!
more + long adjective + than.

Rewrite the sentence with the word in brackets in the right place.

  1. Paris is bigger than Lyon. (much)

Show the answers

  2. This test was a bit easier.

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

Show the answers
  1. My sister is younger than me.
  2. This film is better than the book.
  3. He's taller than his father.
  4. The traffic is much worse today.
  5. Your phone is cheaper.

5. Write the comparison

Use the words to write a full sentence.

Show the answers

  2. Is French more difficult than English?
  3. This hotel is less expensive than the other one.

6. Your turn

Compare two cities you know in five sentences (size, prices, weather, atmosphere…).

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. Douala is bigger and busier than Buea. Buea is much cooler because it's near Mount Cameroon. Life is a bit cheaper in Buea. Douala is noisier, but the nightlife is more exciting. For me, Buea is more relaxing.
Key points
  • Short adjective: -er + than (cheaper than); long adjective: more + adjective + than.
  • Spelling: big → bigger, easy → easier, nice → nicer.
  • Irregular: good → better, bad → worse, far → further / farther.
  • Never “more better”, never “very + comparative”: much / a lot / a bit + comparative.
  • Use than (not that); after than: me, him, her… or subject + helper verb (than I do).
