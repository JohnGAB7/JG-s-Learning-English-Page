The third conditional

The third conditional (If I had known, I would have come) imagines a different past: what would have happened if things had gone differently. We use it to express regret, criticism and relief, and to analyse mistakes.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Build the third conditional with if + past perfect and would have + past participle.
  • Recognise and use the contracted forms ('d = had / 'd = would, would've) in speech.
  • Express regret, criticism and relief about the past.
  • Add meaning with could have and might have, and tell the three conditionals apart.

The structure

Condition: if + past perfect (had + participle). Result: would have + past participle. Both parts are about the past, and about something that did not happen.

The classic mistake is to put would in the if-clause: “If I would have known” is wrong in standard English, even if you sometimes hear it in casual American speech.

Condition (if + past perfect)Result (would have + participle)Reality
If I had known,I would have come.I didn't know, so I didn't come.
If she had studied,she would have passed.She didn't study; she failed.
If we hadn't missed the bus,we wouldn't have been late.We missed the bus; we were late.
If it hadn't rained,the match would have taken place.It rained; the match was cancelled.

Contractions: the 'd puzzle

In speech, we contract almost everything: “If I'd known, I'd have come.” The first 'd = had (followed by a participle); the second 'd = would (followed by have).

Would have sounds like /ˈwʊdəv/ and is sometimes written would've. Never write “would of”: it's a common mistake, even among native speakers, because 've and of sound the same /əv/.

  • If you'd told me, I'd have helped you.
    = If you had told me, I would have helped you.
  • She would've loved this place.
    Would've = would have (informal writing).
  • If they'd left earlier, they wouldn't have missed the flight.
    Negative: wouldn't have.

Regret, criticism, relief

Regret (about yourself): “If I had studied harder, I would have got a better job.” Criticism (of someone else): “If you had listened to me, this wouldn't have happened!” Relief (luckily…): “If the doctor hadn't come so quickly, he would have died.”

The third conditional also helps to analyse causes, for example at work: “If we had tested the software, the client wouldn't have complained.”

  • If I'd taken the job in Toronto, I would have earned more.
    Regret.
  • If you had checked the address, we wouldn't have got lost.
    Criticism.
  • If she hadn't worn a helmet, she would have been badly hurt.
    Relief.

Could have, might have

In the result: could have + participle = it would have been possible; might have + participle = perhaps it would have happened. “If you had asked me, I could have helped you” / “If we had left earlier, we might have caught the train.”

In the condition, could have is not possible: use had been able to. “If I had been able to come, I would have come.”

ResultExampleMeaning
would haveIf he'd trained, he would have won.certain
could haveIf he'd trained, he could have won.he would have been able to
might haveIf he'd trained, he might have won.perhaps
wouldn't haveIf he'd trained, he wouldn't have lost.certain (negative)
  • If I had been able to come, I would have come.
    In the condition: had been able to (not could have).
  • You could have told me earlier!
    Criticism with could have, without if.

The three conditionals side by side

First: possible future (if + present, will). Second: imaginary present or future (if + past, would). Third: imaginary past (if + past perfect, would have). Each time, we move one tense back.

We can mix the second and third when a past cause has a present result: “If I had taken the medicine, I would feel better now.” This mixed conditional is studied at B2 (lesson 158).

TypeExampleMeaning
1stIf I see Ali, I'll tell him.possible: I may see him
2ndIf I saw Ali, I'd tell him.unlikely: he's abroad
3rdIf I had seen Ali, I'd have told him.past: I didn't see him
  • If I had taken the medicine, I would feel better now.
    Past cause, present result (mixed conditional).
  • If I had seen Ali, I'd have told him the news.
    Third conditional: I didn't see him.

The formula

If + had + participle, would / could / might have + participle (If I had known, I would have come.)

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • would have reduces to /ˈwʊdəv/ or even /ˈwʊdə/ in fast speech: “I'd have come” /aɪd əv ˈkʌm/.
  • wouldn't have: /ˈwʊdntəv/, three syllables joined together.
  • had is weak and often contracted: “If I'd known” /ɪf aɪd ˈnəʊn/. The 'd is light but necessary.
  • In criticism, the key word gets strong stress: “If you had LISTENED to me…”
  • could have /ˈkʊdəv/ and should have /ˈʃʊdəv/ follow the same pattern (never write “could of”).

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ If I would have known, I would have come.✓ If I had known, I would have come.Past perfect in the if-clause, never would have.
✗ If I had known, I would come.✓ If I had known, I would have come.For a past result: would have + participle.
✗ If she had studied, she would have pass.✓ If she had studied, she would have passed.After would have, use the past participle.
✗ I would of helped you.✓ I would have helped you. / I'd have helped you.Write have (or 've), never of.
✗ If I knew, I would have told you.✓ If I had known, I would have told you.Past situation: had known, not knew.
✗ If we had left earlier, we could caught the train.✓ If we had left earlier, we could have caught the train.Could have + participle for a past possibility.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Brussels, after a bad job interview. Inès talks on the phone with her sister Mariam, in Dakar.

Mariam

So, how did the interview go?
Past simple question.

Inès

Badly. If I'd known they wanted a presentation, I would have prepared one.
Regret: third conditional.

Mariam

Didn't they tell you in the email?
Negative question = surprise.

Inès

They did, but I didn't read it carefully. If I had read it, I wouldn't have been so surprised.
Reality, then the imaginary past.

Mariam

Well, if you'd called me, I could have helped you prepare.
Gentle criticism with could have.

Inès

I know. And if the train hadn't been late, I might have had time to calm down.
Might have = perhaps.

Mariam

Don't worry. You've learnt something. Next time you'll be ready.
Back to the present and future.

Inès

You're right. If I get another interview, I'll read everything twice!
First conditional: a real possibility.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. What really happened?

Say what really happened.

  1. If I had known about the party, I would have gone.
  2. If she hadn't missed the bus, she wouldn't have been late.
  3. If they had saved money, they could have bought a house.
  4. If it hadn't snowed, the flight wouldn't have been cancelled.
Show the answers
  1. I didn't know about the party, so I didn't go.
  2. She missed the bus, so she was late.
  3. They didn't save money, so they couldn't buy a house.
  4. It snowed, so the flight was cancelled.

2. Put the verbs in the right form

Put the verbs in the third conditional.

  1. If you ___ (ask) me, I ___ (help) you.
  2. We ___ (not / get) lost if we ___ (take) a map.
  3. If he ___ (not / eat) that fish, he ___ (not / be) ill.
  4. If I ___ (have) your number, I ___ (call) you.
  5. She ___ (might / win) if she ___ (train) more.
Show the answers
  1. had asked … would have helped
  2. wouldn't have got … had taken
  3. hadn't eaten … wouldn't have been
  4. had had … would have called
  5. might have won … had trained

3. Transform

Rewrite the sentence in the third conditional.

  1. I didn't set my alarm, so I overslept.
  2. You didn't tell me, so I didn't come.
  3. The shop was closed, so we didn't buy any bread.
  4. He forgot his passport, so he missed the flight.
Show the answers
  1. If I had set my alarm, I wouldn't have overslept.
  2. If you had told me, I would have come.
  3. If the shop hadn't been closed, we would have bought some bread.
  4. If he hadn't forgotten his passport, he wouldn't have missed the flight.

4. Correct the mistakes

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. If I would have seen you, I would have said hello.
  2. If they had come, they would enjoy the concert.
  3. I would of called you, but my phone was dead.
  4. If she had went to the doctor, she would have felt better.
Show the answers
  1. If I had seen you, I would have said hello.
  2. If they had come, they would have enjoyed the concert.
  3. I would have called you, but my phone was dead.
  4. If she had gone to the doctor, she would have felt better.

5. React to the situation

Write a third conditional sentence for each situation.

  1. You didn't know about the party, so you didn't come. Explain.
  2. Your friend didn't listen to you, and there was a problem. Criticise him.
  3. You left late and missed the beginning of the film. Say what was possible.
  4. Your friend didn't ask her. Maybe she would have said yes.
Show the answers
  1. If I had known, I would have come.
  2. If you had listened to me, this wouldn't have happened.
  3. If we had left earlier, we could have seen the beginning of the film.
  4. She might have accepted if you had asked her.

6. Your turn

Think of an important decision in your life. Write 4 third conditional sentences to imagine what would have happened otherwise (would have, could have, might have).

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. If I hadn't moved to Montreal, I wouldn't have met my wife. If I had stayed in Douala, I might have opened a business with my brother. I could have become an engineer if I had chosen maths at university. But if I hadn't made those choices, I wouldn't have this life, and I love it.
Key points
  • If + past perfect, would have + participle: an imaginary past that didn't happen.
  • Never would have in the if-clause.
  • 'd = had before a participle; 'd = would before have.
  • could have = it was possible; might have = perhaps.
  • Write would have / would've, never “would of”.
