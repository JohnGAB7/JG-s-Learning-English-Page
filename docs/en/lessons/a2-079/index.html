At the restaurant

Booking, ordering, asking for things during the meal, mentioning a problem and paying: in a restaurant, everything is said with a few polite phrases. This lesson builds on “would like” (lesson 34) and adds the customs of English-speaking countries (tips, cooking a steak, allergies).

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Book a table on the phone and say who you are when you arrive.
  • Order a full meal politely (I'll have…, Could I have…?) and mention how you want food cooked or an allergy.
  • Ask for things during the meal and mention a problem without being rude.
  • Ask for the bill, pay and understand tipping customs.

Booking and arriving

To book: “I'd like to book a table for four for Saturday at eight, please.” The restaurant asks: “What name is it, please?” / “Can I have a name for the booking?” Answer: “It's under the name Diallo.”

When you arrive: “Hello, we have a reservation for eight o'clock. The name's Diallo.” or, with no booking: “Have you got a table for two?” / “A table for two, please.”

Careful: don't say “We are four”. Say “There are four of us” or simply “A table for four, please”.

  • I'd like to book a table for six on Friday evening.
    Booking by phone.
  • Good evening. Have you got a table for three? — Inside or on the terrace?
    Arriving with no booking.
  • There are five of us. Can we sit by the window?
    There are + number + of us.

Ordering

The waiter asks: “Are you ready to order?”, “What would you like to drink?”, “What can I get you?”. If you are not ready: “Could we have a few more minutes, please?”

To order, the natural phrases are “I'll have…”, “Could I have…?” and “I'd like…”. Avoid “I take…” and “I want…”.

The meal: “a starter” (UK; US “appetizer”) = the first, small dish; “a main course” (US “entrée”!) = the main dish; “a dessert”; “a side dish” = extra food with the main dish; “the dish of the day” = the special dish today.

WaiterCustomer
Are you ready to order?Yes. For my starter, I'll have the soup.
And for your main course?Could I have the grilled chicken, please?
How would you like your steak?Medium, please. (rare = red inside, medium = pink inside, well done = cooked all through)
Would you like any side dishes?Yes, some chips and a salad.
Anything to drink?A bottle of still water, please. (still = without bubbles, sparkling = with bubbles)
Would you like a dessert?No, thank you. Just a coffee.
  • What do you recommend? — The fish is excellent today.
    Asking for advice.
  • I'm allergic to nuts. Is there any peanut in this sauce?
    An allergy.
  • I'm vegetarian. Have you got any dishes without meat?
    A diet.

During the meal

To call the waiter politely: “Excuse me!” with a small hand sign (never click your fingers). To ask for things: “Could we have some more bread, please?”, “Could I have another glass of water?”, “Could you bring me a spoon?”

The waiter may say: “Enjoy your meal!” and come back to ask: “Is everything OK?” / “How is everything?” Answer: “It's delicious, thank you.”

To mention a problem politely: “Excuse me, I think there's a mistake. I ordered the fish, not the chicken.”, “I'm sorry, but my soup is cold.”

  • Excuse me, could we have some more napkins, please?
    Asking for something.
  • How is everything? — Lovely, thanks. The sauce is delicious.
    The waiter checks.
  • Sorry, but this isn't what I ordered. I asked for the vegetarian pasta.
    A polite complaint.
On the table: a knife, a fork, a spoon, a teaspoon, a napkin (UK also serviette), a plate, a glass.

Paying and tipping

For the bill: “Could we have the bill, please?” (UK) / “Could we have the check, please?” (US). Useful questions: “Can we pay separately?”, “Can I pay by card?”, “Is service included?”.

Tipping (“a tip”) is different in each country: in the UK, about 10 to 12.5% if service is not included; in the USA and Canada, people leave 15 to 20% (it is almost expected); in France, Belgium and Cameroon, it is optional.

“Keep the change” is an easy way to leave a tip in cash.

  • Could we have the bill, please? — Of course. Together or separately?
    Asking for the bill.
  • Is service included? — No, it isn't.
    Checking before you tip.
  • Here's 50 euros. Keep the change.
    A tip in cash.

The formula

I'd like to book a table for… · A table for two, please. · I'll have… / Could I have…? · Could we have the bill, please?

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “I'll have” is /aɪl hæv/; in fast speech the h is often weak: “I'll 'ave the fish”.
  • “menu” /ˈmenjuː/: stress on the first syllable, “MEN-yoo”.
  • “dessert” /dɪˈzɜːt/ (stress at the end, with /z/) ≠ “desert” /ˈdezət/ (a dry, sandy place).
  • “steak” /steɪk/ rhymes with “make”, not with “week”.
  • “Could we have…”: /kʊd wi hæv/; let your voice go up a little at the end to sound polite: “…the BILL, please? ↗”.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In a restaurant in Brussels, Grace (from Nairobi) and her colleague Thomas have a business lunch.

Waiter

Good afternoon. Are you ready to order?
The usual question.

Grace

Almost. What do you recommend?
Asking for advice.

Waiter

The dish of the day is mussels with chips. It's very popular.
The special dish today.

Thomas

Perfect, I'll have the mussels, please.
Ordering with I'll have.

Grace

I'm allergic to shellfish. Could I have the vegetable lasagne?
An allergy + a polite order.

Waiter

Of course. And to drink?
Asking about drinks.

Thomas

A bottle of sparkling water, please.
sparkling = with bubbles.

Waiter

Here you are. Enjoy your meal!
Serving the food.

Grace

Excuse me, could we have the bill, please? Can I pay by card?
Asking for the bill.

Waiter

Yes, of course. Service is included.
No need for a big tip.

Key points
  • Book: I'd like to book a table for four at eight. Arrive: A table for two, please. / We have a reservation.
  • Order: I'll have… / Could I have…?; starter (UK) = first dish, main course (US entrée!).
  • Be exact: rare / medium / well done; I'm allergic to…; I'm vegetarian.
  • During the meal: Excuse me, could we have…?; Enjoy your meal!; complain politely: I'm sorry, but…
  • Pay: Could we have the bill (US check), please? Is service included? Tip 15–20% in the USA and Canada.
