Like / love / hate + -ing

Saying what you like or hate doing is important when you introduce yourself, talk about hobbies or meet new people. After like, love, hate and enjoy, English usually uses the -ing form of the verb: “I love dancing”.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Show your feelings from very positive to very negative (love, like, don't mind, hate, can't stand).
  • Use like / love / hate + -ing verb or + noun.
  • Spell the -ing form correctly (dancing, swimming).
  • Ask and answer questions about free time (Do you like…? Yes, I do).

From love to hate

Here are the most common verbs and expressions, from the most positive to the most negative. All of them can take a noun (I love music) or an -ing verb (I love dancing).

“I don't mind” means “it's OK for me, it's not a problem”: it is neutral. In conversation, “I can't stand” is stronger than “I hate”.

ExpressionFeelingExample
lovevery positiveI love cooking.
really like / enjoypositiveI really like reading. I enjoy walking.
likepositiveI like swimming.
quite likea little positiveI quite like jazz.
don't mindneutralI don't mind waiting.
don't likenegativeI don't like running.
hatevery negativeI hate ironing.
can't standvery, very negativeI can't stand queuing.
“I like Paul” means Paul is nice and you enjoy his company. “I love Paul” shows love (or very strong affection).

The structure: verb of feeling + -ing or + noun

After like, love, hate, enjoy, don't mind, can't stand, use a noun or an -ing verb. Here the -ing verb works like a noun: “swimming” = the activity of swimming.

Use the present simple: remember the s with he / she / it (“She likes dancing”) and “do / does” for questions and negatives (“Does he like cooking?”, “He doesn't like cooking”).

With a noun in a general meaning, don't use an article: “I like dogs” (all dogs), “She loves music”.

  • My brother loves playing football.
    love + -ing.
  • Does your sister like travelling? — Yes, she does.
    Question and short answer.
  • I don't mind working at the weekend.
    don't mind + -ing.
  • We hate getting up early.
    hate + -ing.
  • She can't stand cold weather.
    can't stand + noun.

Spelling the -ing form

These are the same rules as for the present continuous (lesson 8). Usually you just add -ing.

If the verb ends in a silent e, drop the e. If a short verb ends in consonant + vowel + consonant, double the last consonant. If the verb ends in -ie, write -ying.

RuleExamples
+ ingread → reading, play → playing, cook → cooking
silent e: drop itdance → dancing, write → writing, ride → riding
consonant + vowel + consonant: doubleswim → swimming, run → running, shop → shopping
-ie → -yinglie → lying, die → dying
-l after a vowel (UK: double)travel → travelling (US: traveling)

-ing or to?

With like, love and hate, you can also use “to” + verb, especially in American English: “I like to swim”. For things you do for pleasure, British English prefers -ing: “I like swimming”. Both are correct.

But enjoy, don't mind and can't stand ALWAYS take -ing: “I enjoy cooking”, never “I enjoy to cook”.

Big trap: “would like” (= want, politely) takes “to”: “I'd like to go to the cinema” (lesson 34). Compare: “I like going to the cinema” (in general, I enjoy it).

  • I like swimming. / I like to swim.
    Both are correct.
  • I enjoy cooking for my friends.
    enjoy + -ing only.
  • I like dancing, but tonight I'd like to stay at home.
    like + -ing (general) vs would like + to (now).

Talking about free time

The usual question is “What do you like doing in your free time?” or “What are your hobbies?”.

To react: “Me too!” after a positive sentence, “Me neither” after a negative sentence. Lesson 70 presents “So do I” and “Neither do I”.

“I like… very much” or “I really like…”: “very much” goes after the object, never between the verb and the object.

  • What do you like doing at the weekend? — I love going to the market.
    Question and answer about free time.
  • I don't like shopping. — Me neither!
    Me neither = I don't like it either.
  • I like reading very much.
    very much at the end.

The formula

love / like / don't mind / hate / enjoy / can't stand + verb-ing (or + noun): I love dancing.

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • The ending -ing is /ɪŋ/: a nasal sound at the back of the mouth, with no hard g at the end. “singing” = /ˈsɪŋɪŋ/.
  • “likes” /laɪks/ ends with /s/; “loves” /lʌvz/ and “enjoys” /ɪnˈdʒɔɪz/ end with /z/.
  • “love” is /lʌv/: the vowel of “but”, not an o.
  • “I don't mind”: the t often disappears and the n becomes m: /aɪ dəʊm ˈmaɪnd/.
  • Stress the feeling and the activity: “I LOVE DANCING”, “I CAN'T STAND QUEUING”.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ I like swim.✓ I like swimming. / I like to swim.After like: -ing (or to + verb).
✗ I enjoy to cook.✓ I enjoy cooking.“Enjoy” always takes -ing.
✗ I like the dogs.✓ I like dogs.General meaning: no article.
✗ She like dancing.✓ She likes dancing.Present simple: s with he / she / it.
✗ Do you like reading? — Yes, I like.✓ Do you like reading? — Yes, I do.Short answer with “do”.
✗ I like very much football.✓ I like football very much.“Very much” goes after the object.
✗ I hate runing.✓ I hate running.run → running: double the n.
✗ I would like going to Canada.✓ I would like to go to Canada.“Would like” takes “to”.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Ottawa, two students, Samira and Jake, are going to share a flat. They get to know each other.

Jake

So, what do you like doing in your free time?
The classic question.

Samira

I love cooking, especially Moroccan food. And I really enjoy hiking.
love + -ing; enjoy + -ing.

Jake

Great! I can't cook at all, but I love eating!
can't = not able; love + -ing.

Samira

Ha! And you? What are your hobbies?
hobbies = free-time activities.

Jake

I like playing the guitar and watching hockey.
like + two -ing verbs.

Samira

I don't mind music, but not at night, please!
don't mind + noun.

Jake

Don't worry. Do you like cleaning? I hate cleaning.
hate + -ing.

Samira

Me too! Let's make a cleaning plan.
Me too = I hate it too.

Key points
  • love > like > don't mind > don't like > hate > can't stand, + -ing verb or + noun.
  • Present simple: She likes dancing. Does he like cooking? — Yes, he does.
  • Spelling: dance → dancing, swim → swimming, lie → lying, travel → travelling (US traveling).
  • enjoy, don't mind, can't stand: always + -ing; like / love / hate: -ing or to.
  • Careful: I like going (in general) ≠ I'd like to go (I want to, now).
