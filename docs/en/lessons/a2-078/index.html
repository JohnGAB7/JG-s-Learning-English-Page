Phoning and leaving a message

Phoning in English can be scary: you can't see the other person and you can't use gestures. Luckily, phone calls follow very fixed phrases. This lesson gives you the phrases to call, answer, take or leave a message and deal with a bad line.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Say who you are and ask to speak to someone (This is…, Can I speak to…?).
  • Answer the phone and ask the caller to wait (Hold on, I'll put you through).
  • Leave a clear message on voicemail or with a person, and say a phone number clearly.
  • Deal with problems: a bad line, asking someone to repeat or spell, a wrong number.

Calling and saying who you are

On the phone, don't say “I am Paul”: say “This is Paul” or “It's Paul” (informal). Use “Paul speaking” when you answer.

To ask for someone: “Can I speak to Ms Ndiaye, please?” (neutral), “Could I speak to…?” (more polite), “Is Paul there?” (informal). Use “speak to” in this phrase, not “talk with”.

Then say why you are calling: “I'm calling about…” (+ noun) or “I'm calling to…” (+ verb).

SituationPhraseStyle
Saying who you areHello, this is Karim Benali.neutral
Asking for someoneCould I speak to Mrs Owusu, please?polite
Saying why (+ noun)I'm calling about the job advert.neutral
Saying why (+ verb)I'm calling to confirm my appointment.neutral
  • Hi, it's Sophie. Is Marc there?
    Informal call to a friend.
  • Good morning. This is Dr Eto's office. I'm calling to change your appointment.
    Formal call.

Answering and asking the caller to wait

At work, answer with the company name and your first name: “Good morning, Lumina Bank, Sara speaking. How can I help you?” At home, just “Hello?”.

To find out who is calling: “Who's calling, please?” / “May I ask who's calling?” (formal). To ask the caller to wait: “Hold on, please.”, “Just a moment.”, “One moment, please.” To connect the caller to another person: “I'll put you through.”

If the person is not free: “I'm sorry, she's not available at the moment.”, “He's in a meeting.”, “She's out of the office today.”, “The line is busy.” (US; UK: “The line's engaged.”).

  • Who's calling, please? — It's Ama Mensah from Ghana Air.
    Asking who is calling.
  • Hold on, I'll put you through.
    Connecting the caller.
  • I'm sorry, Mr Diallo is in a meeting. Can I take a message?
    The person is not free.

Leaving a message

With a person: “Could you tell her that I called?”, “Could you ask him to call me back?”, “Can I leave a message?”. The person writes it down: “Can I have your name and number?”

On voicemail, follow a short plan: who you are, the time or day, why you are calling, what you want, your number (twice), a word to finish.

To say a number: read the numbers one by one; 0 is “oh” (or “zero”, especially in the US); two of the same number: “double” (UK): 077 = “oh double seven”. Make groups with pauses.

StepExample
1. WhoHi, this is Lina Haddad from the Liège office.
2. WhenIt's Tuesday, about ten o'clock.
3. WhyI'm calling about the meeting on Friday.
4. What you wantCould you call me back before five?
5. Your numberMy number is oh four nine five, two two three, eight one six. That's 0495 223 816.
6. FinishThanks a lot. Bye!
With a pronoun, “call back” separates: “call me back”, “I'll call you back”. Don't say “recall”: it means “remember” or “ask people to return a faulty product”.

When communication is difficult

Don't be afraid to ask someone to repeat: it is normal and polite. “Sorry, could you say that again?”, “Could you speak a bit more slowly, please?”, “Could you speak up, please?” (louder).

For a name or an address: “Could you spell that, please?”. For a bad line: “Sorry, the line is bad.”, “You're breaking up.” (I can only hear parts), “I can't hear you very well.”

For a mistake: “Sorry, I think you've got the wrong number.” To finish: “Thanks for calling.”, “Speak to you soon.”, “Bye!”

  • Sorry, you're breaking up. Can I call you back?
    A bad line.
  • Could you spell your surname, please? — Yes, it's O-W-U-S-U.
    Spelling a name.
  • Sorry, wrong number. — Oh, sorry!
    A wrong number.

Phone phrasal verbs

Phone calls use many phrasal verbs (lesson 72). Learn them as blocks.

VerbMeaningExample
call (someone) backphone someone againI'll call you back in ten minutes.
hold onwait, don't end the callHold on, I'll check.
put (someone) throughconnect to another personI'll put you through to sales.
pick upanswer the phoneNobody picked up.
hang upend the callDon't hang up!
get throughmanage to speak to someoneI couldn't get through to the bank.
  • I called three times, but nobody picked up.
    pick up = answer.
  • She was angry and she hung up.
    hang up → hung up.

The formula

Hello, this is… · Could I speak to…? · I'm calling about… · Can I leave a message? · Could you ask him to call me back? · My number is…

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • Say numbers in groups of 3 or 4, with a pause and a rising voice except on the last group: “oh four nine five ↗ | two two three ↗ | eight one six ↘”.
  • “Double” in numbers: 0044 = “oh oh double four” or “double oh double four” (UK).
  • “I'll” is short: /aɪl/; “I'll call you back” = /aɪl ˈkɔːl ju ˈbæk/. The l in “call” is dark, at the back of the mouth.
  • Spell with the English alphabet (lesson 19): careful with A /eɪ/, E /iː/, I /aɪ/, G /dʒiː/, J /dʒeɪ/, R /ɑː/, W /ˈdʌbljuː/.
  • On the phone, speak a little more slowly and say the ends of words clearly: people can't read your lips.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Which phrase?

Write the phrase for the situation.

  1. You want to know who is calling.
  2. You want the caller to wait.
  3. The person isn't there; you offer to write a note.
  4. You can't hear well.
  5. You don't know how to write a name.
Show the answers
  1. Who's calling, please?
  2. Hold on, please. / Just a moment, please.
  3. Can I take a message?
  4. Sorry, the line is bad. Could you speak up, please?
  5. Could you spell that, please?

2. Complete

Complete with one word.

  1. Hello, ___ is Fatou Sow.
  2. Could I ___ to the manager, please?
  3. I'm calling ___ my order.
  4. Could you ask her to call me ___?
  5. Hold on, I'll put you ___.
  6. I'm afraid he isn't ___ at the moment.
Show the answers
  1. this
  2. speak
  3. about
  4. back
  5. through
  6. available

3. Say the number

Write how you say these numbers in British English.

  1. 0477 21 58
  2. 020 3344
  3. 999
Show the answers
  1. oh four double seven, two one, five eight
  2. oh two oh, double three double four
  3. nine nine nine (the UK emergency number)

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. Hello, I am Julie.
  2. Hold on, I pass you to him.
  3. Could you say him that I called?
  4. I recall you in five minutes.
  5. Sorry, you have a false number.
Show the answers
  1. Hello, this is Julie. / Hello, Julie speaking.
  2. Hold on, I'll put you through.
  3. Could you tell him that I called?
  4. I'll call you back in five minutes.
  5. Sorry, I think you've got the wrong number.

5. Your turn

Write the message you leave on a garage's voicemail: your car has a problem and you would like an appointment this week. Follow the six steps in section 3.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. Hi, this is Paul Mbarga. It's Monday morning, about nine o'clock. I'm calling about my car, a grey Toyota. It makes a strange noise when I brake. I'd like an appointment this week, if possible on Wednesday or Thursday. Could you call me back on oh six nine nine, four five two, one one eight? That's 0699 452 118. Thanks a lot. Bye!
Key points
  • Say who you are: This is… / It's… (never “I am…”); when you answer: Sara speaking.
  • Ask: Could I speak to…, please? — Who's calling? — Hold on, I'll put you through.
  • Messages: Can I take / leave a message? Could you ask him to call me back? My number is…
  • Numbers: one by one, 0 = oh, 77 = double seven, in groups.
  • Problems: Could you say that again / speak up / spell that? You're breaking up. Wrong number.
