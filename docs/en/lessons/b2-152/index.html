The passive with reporting verbs

“It is said that…”, “He is believed to have…”: English reports opinions, rumours and unconfirmed news with verbs like say, believe and expect in the passive. This is the language of newspapers, reports and careful conversation.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Use both patterns: “It is said that…” and “He is said to…”.
  • Choose the right infinitive (to do, to be doing, to have done, to have been done) for the time of the action.
  • Use “There is said to be…” and report past opinions (“It was thought that…”).
  • Choose between the passive pattern and adverbs like reportedly, allegedly and apparently.

The verbs and what they do

Reporting verbs tell us what people say or think: say, think, believe, expect, report, consider, know, understand, estimate, claim, allege, suppose, rumour.

In the passive, they let you present information without saying exactly who gave it, and without saying that you yourself are sure. Journalists, researchers and careful colleagues use them to show that the information comes from somewhere else or is not confirmed.

“Rumour” is almost only used in the passive: “It is rumoured that…”, “She is rumoured to be leaving.” “Allege” (to say something is true without proof) is typical of legal and crime news.

Pattern 1: It is said that + clause

The simplest pattern is: It + be + past participle + that + a full clause. “It” is an empty subject.

The verb in the “that” clause keeps its normal tense: “It is believed that the fire started in the kitchen.”

This pattern is a little heavier and more formal. It is useful when the subject of the clause is long, or when you want to avoid a complicated infinitive.

  • It is said that the castle is haunted.
    A general belief.
  • It is believed that the thieves escaped through the roof.
    The clause keeps the past simple.
  • It is expected that interest rates will rise.
    A prediction.
  • It has been reported that several flights were delayed.
    The reporting verb can be in other tenses.

Pattern 2: subject + be said + to-infinitive

You can also make the person or thing you are talking about the subject: subject + be + past participle + to-infinitive. This is the more elegant pattern and the most common one in the press.

The form of the infinitive shows the time relation between the reporting (“people say…”) and the action that is reported.

Note: with “say”, this pattern exists only in the passive. “They say him to be rich” is impossible; say “He is said to be rich”.

Time of the reported actionInfinitiveExample
same time (state, habit)to + base formHe is said to live in Lagos.
in progress nowto be + -ingThe two leaders are reported to be meeting right now.
earlier (finished action)to have + past participleThe suspect is believed to have left the country.
earlier, with durationto have been + -ingShe is thought to have been working there for years.
future (mainly with expect)to + base formThe minister is expected to resign.
passive, same timeto be + past participleThe painting is thought to be kept in a private collection.
passive, earlierto have been + past participleTwo people are known to have been injured.

Past reports and “There is said to be”

If the opinion itself is in the past, the reporting verb is in the past simple: “It was thought that the Earth was flat” → “The Earth was thought to be flat.” The infinitive follows the same logic: an earlier action needs to have + past participle.

With “there is / there are”, keep “there” as the subject: “There are thought to be about 300 species of birds in the park.” “There is said to be a hidden room in the house.”

Transform like this: It is said that there is… → There is said to be…; It is believed that there were… → There are believed to have been…

  • The ship was believed to have sunk near the coast.
    A past opinion about an even earlier event.
  • At the time, the drug was considered to be safe.
    People considered it safe then.
  • There are estimated to be 2,000 people in the stadium.
    An approximate number.
  • There is rumoured to be a new album on the way.
    Just a rumour.

Other ways to report: adverbs and informal phrases

Some languages use a conditional form for unconfirmed news. English does not use “would” for this. Use the passive with a reporting verb, or an adverb.

The adverbs reportedly (according to the media), allegedly (according to an accusation), apparently (from what I have heard) and supposedly (people say so, but I doubt it) do the same job with a normal verb.

In everyday speech, people also say “They say (that)…”, “People think…” or “Apparently…”. The passive with reporting verbs is mainly written or formal.

Trap: “be supposed to” can also mean an obligation or an expectation: “You're supposed to wear a helmet” (it's the rule). The context tells you which meaning is intended.

  • The president is said to be ill. / The president is reportedly ill.
    Two ways to give unconfirmed news.
  • The suspect is believed to have left the country.
    The police think so, but it is not certain.
  • The CEO allegedly hid the losses from investors.
    An accusation, not a proven fact.
  • Apparently, the restaurant is closing next month.
    Informal: I have heard this.

The formula

It is said that + clause ↔ Subject + is said + to do / to be doing / to have done

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “said” is /sed/, never /seɪd/ like “say”.
  • “thought” is /θɔːt/: voiceless “th” with the tongue between the teeth, then a long vowel.
  • “to have” is reduced in “to have left”: /tə həv ˈleft/, or /təv ˈleft/ in fast speech. The stress is on the past participle.
  • “alleged” is /əˈledʒd/ (two syllables) but “allegedly” is /əˈledʒɪdli/ (four syllables): the -edly ending is pronounced /ɪd/. The same goes for “reportedly” /rɪˈpɔːtɪdli/ and “supposedly” /səˈpəʊzɪdli/.
  • In “It is believed that…”, “that” is weak: /ðət/.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ He is said that he is very rich.✓ He is said to be very rich. / It is said that he is very rich.Don't mix the two patterns.
✗ It is said him to be very rich.✓ He is said to be very rich.With the infinitive, the person becomes the subject.
✗ The suspect is believed to leave the country last week.✓ The suspect is believed to have left the country last week.An earlier action needs to have + past participle.
✗ The minister is expected resigning today.✓ The minister is expected to resign today.Reporting verbs in the passive are followed by a to-infinitive.
✗ It says that the mayor is corrupt.✓ It is said that the mayor is corrupt.“It says” refers to a text (the article says…); for a general report, use the passive.
✗ The president would be ill.✓ The president is said (reported) to be ill.English does not use “would” for unconfirmed news.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

The newsroom of an online newspaper in Brussels. Lucie, a journalist, checks an article with her editor, Pieter.

Pieter

Your headline says the coach has resigned. Is that confirmed?
He wants a confirmed fact.

Lucie

Not officially. But he's widely believed to have handed in his letter yesterday.
to have + past participle: an earlier action.

Pieter

Then we can't write it as a fact. Say he is reported to have resigned.
Reporting verb to avoid stating it as true.

Lucie

Fine. And the club is expected to make a statement this afternoon.
expect + to-infinitive: future.

Pieter

Good. What about the players? Do we know how they feel?
A normal question.

Lucie

Some of them are said to be furious. There are also rumoured to be problems with the owner.
“There are rumoured to be”: there stays as the subject.

Pieter

Rumoured by whom? Be careful with that one.
He asks for the source.

Lucie

Two sources. But I'll write that it is alleged, not that it is known.
Different verbs show different levels of certainty.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Choose the right form

Choose the correct option.

  1. The director is said (to be / being) very demanding.
  2. It is believed (that / to) the plane landed safely.
  3. The thief is thought (to enter / to have entered) through a window last night.
  4. There (is said / are said) to be thousands of species in the forest.
Show the answers
  1. to be
  2. that
  3. to have entered
  4. are said

2. Complete with the infinitive

Complete with the right form of the infinitive of the verb in brackets.

  1. The singer is thought ___ (live) in Lagos now.
  2. The fire is believed ___ (start) in the kitchen.
  3. The two leaders are reported ___ (discuss) the crisis at this moment.
  4. Over 3,000 people are estimated ___ (attend) last night's concert.
  5. The new stadium is expected ___ (complete) by 2028.
  6. The documents are known ___ (steal) in 2019.
Show the answers
  1. to live
  2. to have started
  3. to be discussing
  4. to have attended
  5. to be completed
  6. to have been stolen

3. Transform

Rewrite each sentence starting with the subject in square brackets.

  1. It is said that [the president] is ill.
  2. It is believed that [the thieves] escaped through the roof.
  3. It is expected that [the company] will announce job cuts.
  4. It is reported that [the minister] is negotiating with the unions.
  5. It was thought that [the painting] had been destroyed.
Show the answers
  1. The president is said to be ill.
  2. The thieves are believed to have escaped through the roof.
  3. The company is expected to announce job cuts.
  4. The minister is reported to be negotiating with the unions.
  5. The painting was thought to have been destroyed.

4. Correct the mistakes

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. He is said that he speaks seven languages.
  2. The suspect is believed to leave the country yesterday.
  3. It is said him to be very generous.
  4. The prime minister is expected resigning today.
Show the answers
  1. He is said to speak seven languages. (or: It is said that he speaks seven languages.)
  2. The suspect is believed to have left the country yesterday.
  3. He is said to be very generous.
  4. The prime minister is expected to resign today.

5. From conversation to news report

Rewrite these informal sentences with a reporting verb in the passive (pattern 2 if possible).

  1. People say she's the best lawyer in Douala.
  2. Everyone thinks the fire started in the kitchen.
  3. We expect the price of petrol to rise.
  4. Apparently there are more than 200 species of birds in this park.
Show the answers
  1. She is said to be the best lawyer in Douala.
  2. The fire is thought (believed) to have started in the kitchen.
  3. The price of petrol is expected to rise.
  4. There are said (thought) to be more than 200 species of birds in this park.

6. Your turn

Write a short news item (4 sentences) about unconfirmed information (sport, politics, a celebrity, a company). Use at least three reporting verbs in the passive with different infinitives.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. A famous Canadian tech company is reported to be planning a move to Brussels. Its founder is said to have visited several offices in the city last month. The move is expected to create about 500 jobs. However, the company is believed to be negotiating with other European cities as well.
Key points
  • Two patterns: It is said that + clause / Subject + is said + to-infinitive.
  • The infinitive shows the time: to do (same time or future), to be doing (in progress), to have done (earlier), to have been done (passive, earlier).
  • There is / are said to be…; past opinion: was thought to…
  • For unconfirmed news, use this passive or reportedly / allegedly, never “would”.
  • “They say him to be” is impossible: with say, the infinitive pattern exists only in the passive.
