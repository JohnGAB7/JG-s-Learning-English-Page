The verb “to be”

The most useful verb in English. Use it to say who you are, how you feel, where you are, how old you are and what the weather is like.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Use “to be” in the present with every subject, in the full form and the short form.
  • Know when English uses “to be” where other languages use “have” (age, hunger, cold, fear).
  • Make simple sentences about yourself: name, job, feelings, place.

The present forms

“To be” is irregular. It has three forms in the present: am, is and are. Learn them by heart: you need them in almost every sentence.

We use the short form (contraction) when we speak and in messages. We use the full form in careful writing or to make something stronger.

SubjectFull formShort formExample
II amI'mI'm Sara.
youyou areyou'reYou're late.
hehe ishe'sHe's my brother.
sheshe isshe'sShe's a doctor.
itit isit'sIt's cold.
wewe arewe'reWe're friends.
theythey arethey'reThey're at work.
“You” is for one person and for many people. The verb is always “are”: you are.

What we use “to be” for

We use it to say who we are (name, nationality, job), how we feel, where we are, and to describe people and things.

  • I'm Marie. I'm French.
    Name and nationality.
  • He's a nurse.
    Job: use a / an before a job.
  • We're tired but happy.
    Feelings.
  • They're in Yaoundé.
    Place.
  • The room is small.
    Description.

“To be”, not “have”

Many languages say “I have 30 years” or “I have hunger”. English uses “to be” + an adjective or a number. This is the most common mistake for beginners.

TopicCorrect English
AgeI'm 30. / I'm 30 years old.
Hunger and thirstI'm hungry. I'm thirsty.
Cold and heatI'm cold. I'm hot.
FearI'm afraid. / I'm scared.
Right and wrongI'm right. You're wrong.
WeatherIt's sunny. It's cold.
TimeI'm late. I'm early.

Always use a subject

An English sentence always has a subject. When there is no person, use “it”: for time, weather, distance and situations.

Never say “Is late” or “Is raining”. Say “It's late”, “It's raining”.

  • It's nine o'clock.
    Time.
  • It's far from here.
    Distance.
  • It's important.
    A situation.

The formula

I am · you are · he / she / it is · we are · they are

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “I'm” sounds like /aɪm/: the word “time” without the t.
  • “you're” and “your” sound almost the same. “they're”, “their” and “there” all sound like /ðeə/.
  • In “he's” and “she's”, the s sounds like /z/. In “it's”, it sounds like /s/.
  • For “th” in “they”, put your tongue between your teeth and use your voice: /ð/. It is not a z or a d.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ I have 25 years.✓ I'm 25.Age uses “to be”, never “have”.
✗ I have hungry.✓ I'm hungry.Hunger, thirst, cold, fear: “to be” + adjective.
✗ Is cold today.✓ It's cold today.The subject “it” is necessary.
✗ She is teacher.✓ She's a teacher.Use a / an before a job in the singular.
✗ They is happy.✓ They're happy.With they, we and you: always “are”.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

First day on a course. Two people meet.

Anna

Hi! I'm Anna. Are you new here?
Anna says her name and asks a question.

Karim

Yes, I am. I'm Karim. I'm from Douala.
Short answer: “Yes, I am.”

Anna

Nice to meet you, Karim. Are you a student?
A polite phrase when you meet someone.

Karim

No, I'm not. I'm a nurse. And you?
“I'm a nurse”: a before the job.

Anna

I'm a teacher. Oh, it's nine o'clock. We're late!
“It's” for the time; “we're late”.

Karim

Oh no! Let's go. I'm ready.
“Let's go” = we go now, together.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Choose the right form

Complete with am, is or are.

  1. I ___ happy.
  2. They ___ in Brussels.
  3. My brother ___ a developer.
  4. You ___ very kind.
  5. It ___ cold today.
  6. We ___ at the office.
Show the answers
  1. am
  2. are
  3. is
  4. are
  5. is
  6. are

2. Short forms

Write the short form.

  1. we are
  2. she is
  3. I am
  4. they are
  5. it is
Show the answers
  1. we're
  2. she's
  3. I'm
  4. they're
  5. it's

3. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. I have 28 years.
  2. We have cold.
  3. Is hot today.
  4. She is doctor.
  5. You is right.
Show the answers
  1. I'm 28.
  2. We're cold.
  3. It's hot today.
  4. She's a doctor.
  5. You're right.

5. Put the words in order

Put the words in the right order to make a sentence.

  1. a / is / she / nurse
  2. at / we / home / are
  3. is / it / o'clock / ten
  4. from / I / Canada / am
Show the answers
  1. She is a nurse.
  2. We are at home.
  3. It is ten o'clock.
  4. I am from Canada.

4. Your turn

Write four true sentences about you with “to be”: your name, your age, your job or studies, how you feel today.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. I'm John. I'm 34. I'm an accountant. Today I'm a bit tired but happy.
Key points
  • I am · you are · he / she / it is · we are · they are. When you speak: I'm, you're, he's, she's, it's, we're, they're.
  • Age, hunger, thirst, cold, heat, fear, right, wrong: use “to be”.
  • Always a subject: “It's late”, “It's raining”.
  • Before a job: a / an (She's a nurse. He's an engineer.).
