Prepositions of place (in, on, under…)

In, on, at, under, next to, between, opposite… Prepositions of place say where a thing or a person is. Some are fixed expressions you need to learn: “on the bus”, “in the photo”, “at home”.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Choose between in, on and at by the type of place (space, surface, point).
  • Use prepositions of position: under, next to, between, behind, in front of, opposite, near, above.
  • Know the fixed expressions: at home, at work, on the bus, in the photo, on the left, on the first floor.
  • Understand the difference between “in front of” and “opposite”.

In, on, at: space, surface, point

Use “in” for a closed space or an area: a room, a box, a city, a country. Use “on” for a surface: a table, a wall, the floor, a page. Use “at” for an exact point, or a place where you do something: the bus stop, the station, an address, an event.

The choice often depends on how you see the place. “In the station” = inside the building; “at the station” = at the station as a meeting point.

PrepositionIdeaExamples
ininside, in an areain the kitchen, in a box, in Montréal, in Belgium, in the garden, in the car
onon a surface or a lineon the table, on the wall, on the floor, on page 12, on the river
ata point, a place of activityat the door, at the bus stop, at the station, at school, at a party, at 15 Rue Royale
  • Your keys are in your bag.
    Inside.
  • There's a picture on the wall.
    Surface.
  • I'm at the bus stop. Where are you?
    A point.
  • She lives in Liège, at 8 Rue Léopold.
    City → in; address → at.

Prepositions of position

These prepositions show where one thing is compared to another. They are followed by a noun or an object pronoun (next to me, behind them).

Be careful: “in front of” means on the same side, just before something. “Opposite” means on the other side, facing it (for example, across the street). In American English, people often say “across from” for “opposite”.

PrepositionMeaningExample
underlower than, covered byThe cat is under the bed.
next to / besideat the side ofSit next to me.
betweenin the middle of two thingsThe bank is between the café and the pharmacy.
behindat the back ofThe garden is behind the house.
in front ofjust before, on the same sideThere's a tree in front of my window.
opposite (US: across from)on the other side, facingThe school is opposite the church.
nearnot far fromI live near the university.
above / overhigher thanThere's a light above the table.
belowlower thanThe temperature is below zero.
inside / outsidein / out of a building or spaceWait outside, please.
“Near” has no “of”: near the station, never “near of the station”. But we say “close to”: close to the station.

Transport and fixed expressions

For public transport and big vehicles where you can stand up, use “on”: on the bus, on the train, on the plane, on the metro, on a boat. For a car or a taxi, use “in”: in the car, in a taxi. For a bike or a motorbike, use “on”: on my bike.

Some expressions have no article: at home, at work, at school, at university, in bed, in hospital (British; US: in the hospital), in prison.

  • I'm on the train. I'm arriving at six.
    Train → on.
  • She's in a taxi.
    Taxi → in.
  • The children are at school.
    No article.
  • Dad is still in bed.
    No article.

Pictures, streets, floors, sides

Say “in the photo / in the picture”, “in the newspaper”, but “on the internet”, “on TV”, “on the radio”, “on the screen”, “on page 5”.

For streets: in the street (in general, British), on Main Street (with the street name, mostly American; British people also say “in Oxford Street”). For floors: on the ground floor, on the first floor (UK: one level up; US: ground level).

For sides and corners: on the left / on the right, in the corner (of a room), on the corner (of a street), in the middle, at the top / at the bottom.

  • Who's the man in the photo?
    in the photo.
  • My office is on the third floor.
    on + floor.
  • The toilets are on the left.
    on the left.
  • There's a café on the corner.
    Corner of a street: on.

Cities and countries: in, never at or to

To say where you are or where you live, use “in” with cities, regions and countries: I live in Paris, She works in Cameroon. Don't say “at Paris”.

“To” shows movement towards a place (I'm going to Paris), not position (lesson 71). Don't say “I live to Paris”.

  • My cousin lives in Toronto.
    City → in.
  • We're in Belgium this week.
    Country → in.

The formula

in (inside, city, country) · on (surface, bus, train) · at (point, address, at home / work / school) · under · next to · between · behind · in front of · opposite · near

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • Short prepositions are weak in the sentence: at /ət/, from /frəm/, to /tə/. “in” and “on” stay quite clear: /ɪn/, /ɒn/.
  • “between” /bɪˈtwiːn/ and “behind” /bɪˈhaɪnd/: stress on the second syllable.
  • “opposite” /ˈɒpəzɪt/: stress on the first syllable; the end is /zɪt/, not “-zite”.
  • “next to” often sounds like /ˈneks tə/: the t of next disappears before to.
  • “in front of”: stress FRONT, /ɪn ˈfrʌnt əv/; “of” is weak.

Don’t saySayWhy

In a conversation

Listening Studio

A flatmate in Douala looks for his things before he goes to work.

Eric

Where's my phone? It isn't on the table.
on the table: surface.

Sandra

Look on the sofa, next to the cushions.
next to.

Eric

No, it isn't there. Oh, it's under the sofa! And my keys?
under.

Sandra

They're in the kitchen, in the drawer near the fridge.
in, near.

Eric

Thanks. Where's my bag?
A Wh- question.

Sandra

It's behind the door, between your shoes and the umbrella.
behind, between.

Eric

Great. I'm meeting Paul at the bus stop opposite the bank.
at the bus stop; opposite.

Sandra

Hurry up! The bus is in front of the building. I can see it from the window!
in front of.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. In, on or at?

Complete with in, on or at.

  1. The milk is ___ the fridge.
  2. There's a clock ___ the wall.
  3. I'm waiting ___ the bus stop.
  4. They live ___ Canada.
  5. She's ___ work until six.
  6. We're ___ the train to Lille.
Show the answers
  1. in
  2. on
  3. at
  4. in
  5. at
  6. on

2. Describe the position

Complete with: under, next to, between, behind, in front of, opposite.

  1. The cat is sleeping ___ the table (lower, covered).
  2. The bank is ___ the bakery and the post office (in the middle).
  3. The car park is ___ the supermarket (at the back).
  4. Stand ___ me in the photo (at my side).
  5. The museum is ___ the park, on the other side of the street.
  6. There's a fountain ___ the town hall (at the front).
Show the answers
  1. under
  2. between
  3. behind
  4. next to
  5. opposite
  6. in front of

3. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Correct it.

  1. My sister lives at Brussels.
  2. I'm in the plane now.
  3. Who is the girl on this photo?
  4. The school is near of my house.
  5. He's at the bed. He's ill.
Show the answers
  1. My sister lives in Brussels.
  2. I'm on the plane now.
  3. Who is the girl in this photo?
  4. The school is near my house.
  5. He's in bed. He's ill.

4. Write the sentence

Write one sentence for each situation, with the right preposition.

  1. You are in your house now.
  2. The office is one level up, on your right side.
  3. The pharmacy faces the station, across the street.
  4. Your keys are inside your bag, below your wallet.
Show the answers
  1. I'm at home.
  2. The office is on the first floor, on the right.
  3. There's a pharmacy opposite the station. / The pharmacy is opposite the station.
  4. My keys are in my bag, under my wallet.

5. Your turn

Describe your bedroom or your office in five sentences, with at least five different prepositions of place.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. My bed is next to the window. There's a small table between the bed and the wardrobe. My books are on a shelf above my desk. My shoes are under the bed. There's a big plant in the corner.
Key points
  • in = inside, city, country; on = surface; at = exact point, address, place of activity.
  • on the bus / train / plane, but in a car / taxi.
  • No article: at home, at work, at school, in bed.
  • in the photo, on the wall, on the left, on the first floor, on the corner.
  • in front of = just before, same side; opposite = facing, other side; near with no of.
