Both, either, neither

Both, either and neither are for talking about two things or two people: “the two”, “one or the other”, “not one and not the other”. There are two big traps: we never say “the both”, and neither is already negative, so we do not use it with not.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Use both, either and neither with a singular or plural noun, or with of + the/my/them.
  • Build both … and, either … or and neither … nor to join two things.
  • Put both in the right place in the sentence (We both like… / They are both…).
  • Avoid double negatives with neither, and choose “Neither of them…” instead of “Both of them don't…”.

Three words for two things

Both = the two (this one AND that one). Either = one or the other, it doesn't matter which. Neither = not this one and not that one.

These three words are only for TWO things or people. For three or more, use all, any and none (“none of my friends”).

Be careful with the noun: both takes a plural noun (both hands), but either and neither take a singular noun (either hand, neither hand).

WordMeaning+ nounExample

  • Both my parents are teachers.
    My father AND my mother.
  • You can pay by card or in cash. Either way is fine.
    The two options are OK.
  • Neither bus goes to the airport.
    Not one of the two buses; singular noun.

With of: both of, either of, neither of

Before a determiner (the, my, these…) or a pronoun (us, you, them), use of: both of the films, either of my sisters, neither of them. The noun after of is always plural.

With both, of is optional before a noun: both my brothers = both of my brothers. But it is necessary before a pronoun: both of them (never “both them”).

After neither of / either of, the verb is singular in careful English (Neither of them is ready); the plural (Neither of them are ready) is very common in speech.

  • Both of us were tired after the trip.
    of is necessary before us.
  • Do either of you speak Spanish?
    One of you two, any one.
  • Neither of my brothers lives in Douala.
    Singular verb (careful English).

Where to put both

Both can also go after the subject, like an adverb: We both like jazz. It goes before the main verb, but after be and after an auxiliary: They are both students. We can both come.

At the end of a sentence, after an object pronoun: I love them both (= I love both of them).

We never say “the both”. Many languages use an article here, but English does not: both cities, not “the both cities”.

  • My sister and I both work in a bank.
    Before the main verb.
  • The flats are both very expensive.
    After be.
  • Here are two T-shirts. You can have them both.
    After the object pronoun.

Both … and, either … or, neither … nor

To join two things: both A and B (A and B together), either A or B (A or B, one of the two), neither A nor B (not A and not B).

Neither … nor is already negative, so the verb stays positive: Neither Paul nor Ana eats meat (not “doesn't eat”). With a negative verb, use either … or: He doesn't eat either meat or fish (= He eats neither meat nor fish).

The verb usually agrees with the nearest noun: Either my brother or my parents are coming.

  • She speaks both French and English.
    The two languages.
  • We can meet either on Saturday or on Sunday.
    One of the two days.
  • Neither Marc nor Sophie was at the meeting.
    Not Marc and not Sophie; positive verb.

Negative sentences and short answers

To say “not one of the two”, use neither: Neither of them came. “Both of them didn't come” sounds strange and is not clear. In the same way, after not, use either: I don't like either of them (= I like neither of them).

In short answers, neither / either can stand alone: Tea or coffee? — Either, thanks. (any one) / Neither, thanks. (not one of them).

To agree with a negative sentence, say “Me neither” in informal speech. The full structure “Neither do I” comes in lesson 70.

  • Which one do you want, the red or the blue? — I don't mind. Either.
    Any one of the two.
  • I don't like horror films. — Me neither.
    I don't like them either.

The formula

both + plural noun · either / neither + singular noun · both / either / neither of + the/my/them · both A and B · either A or B · neither A nor B (positive verb)

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • both /bəʊθ/: the final “th” is voiceless /θ/, as in “think”. Don't say “bot”.
  • either: /ˈaɪðə/ (common in British English) or /ˈiːðə/ (common in American English). Both are correct.
  • neither: /ˈnaɪðə/ or /ˈniːðə/, with the voiced /ð/ of “the”.
  • nor /nɔː/ sounds like “more” with an n; no final r in British English.
  • In “neither … nor”, stress the two names: NEIther MARC nor soPHIE.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In a travel agency in Liège, Chloé and her husband cannot choose between two trips.

Agent

So, you can choose either Lisbon or Prague. Both trips are five days long.
either … or; both + plural noun.

Chloé

We both love old cities. What's the difference?
both before the main verb.

Agent

Lisbon is by the sea, and Prague has beautiful bridges. Both of them are lovely in May.
both of + pronoun.

Hugo

Are the hotels in the centre?
A question.

Agent

Yes, both hotels are in the old town, and neither hotel is expensive.
neither + singular noun, positive verb.

Chloé

I don't speak Portuguese or Czech.
not … or.

Hugo

Me neither! But neither language is a problem. People speak English in both cities.
Me neither = I don't either.

Agent

That's right. Either city is a good choice.
either = any one of the two.

Chloé

OK… Let's go to Lisbon this year and Prague next year!
A happy solution.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Both, either or neither?

Complete with both, either or neither.

  1. ___ my parents are from Bafoussam. (my father and my mother)
  2. You can come on Friday or Saturday. ___ day is OK for me.
  3. ___ shop was open, so we went home. (not one of the two)
  4. I have two brothers. ___ of them are doctors. (the two)
  5. The red bag or the black one? — ___. I don't like them. (not one of the two)
  6. There are two buses to the station. You can take ___ of them.
Show the answers
  1. Both
  2. Either
  3. Neither
  4. Both
  5. Neither
  6. either

2. With or without of?

Complete with of or Ø (nothing). Sometimes both are possible.

  1. Both ___ them like football.
  2. Neither ___ bike is new.
  3. Either ___ my friends can help you.
  4. Both ___ my cousins work in Lyon.
Show the answers
  1. of
  2. Ø
  3. of
  4. of or Ø (Both my cousins / Both of my cousins)

3. Join two ideas

Rewrite with both … and, either … or or neither … nor.

  1. Léa plays the guitar. Léa plays the piano.
  2. Marc isn't at home. Sophie isn't at home.
  3. We can take the train, or we can take the car.
  4. The hotel is cheap. The hotel is comfortable.
  5. He doesn't eat meat. He doesn't eat fish.
Show the answers
  1. Léa plays both the guitar and the piano.
  2. Neither Marc nor Sophie is at home.
  3. We can take either the train or the car.
  4. The hotel is both cheap and comfortable.
  5. He eats neither meat nor fish.

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Correct it.

  1. The both girls are tall.
  2. Neither of us didn't understand.
  3. They like both rice.
  4. Neither Paul nor Ana doesn't smoke.
  5. Either answers is correct.
Show the answers
  1. Both girls are tall. / Both of the girls are tall.
  2. Neither of us understood.
  3. They both like rice.
  4. Neither Paul nor Ana smokes.
  5. Either answer is correct.

5. What do you say?

Write one sentence for each situation.

  1. You and your friend are tired. (both)
  2. You have two brothers. Not one of them speaks German.
  3. Someone asks: Tea or coffee? It's the same for you.
  4. Your colleague speaks English and she speaks Spanish too.
Show the answers
  1. We are both tired. / Both of us are tired.
  2. Neither of my brothers speaks German.
  3. I don't mind. Either.
  4. She speaks both English and Spanish.

6. Your turn

Think of two people in your family or two friends. Write four sentences: one with both, one with neither, one with either, and one with neither … nor.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. My two best friends are Awa and Kevin. They both live in Brussels. Neither of them has a car. I can call either of them when I need help. Neither Awa nor Kevin likes cold weather!
Key points
  • Both, either, neither: only for two things (otherwise all, any, none).
  • Both + plural noun; either / neither + singular noun; with of + the/my/them, plural noun.
  • Never “the both”; both of + pronoun (both of them); position: We both like… / They are both…
  • Neither is negative: no not with it. After not, use either.
  • Both A and B · either A or B · neither A nor B · Me neither = I don't either.
