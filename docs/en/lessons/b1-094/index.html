B1 · Intermediate · lesson 94

“Will” or “be going to”?

English has several ways to talk about the future, and the choice depends on meaning: a decision made now, a plan made earlier, a prediction from evidence, or a fixed arrangement. Choosing well makes your English sound natural.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Tell the difference between a decision made now (will) and an intention decided earlier (be going to).
  • Tell the difference between a prediction based on opinion (will) and one based on present evidence (be going to).
  • Use will for offers, promises, refusals and requests, and the present continuous for fixed arrangements.
  • Avoid using will after when, if and as soon as.

The forms: a quick review

Will + base verb, the same for every subject: I will go, she will go. Contraction 'll (I'll, she'll); negative won't /wəʊnt/ (= will not).

Be going to + base verb: be changes with the subject (I'm going to, he's going to, they're going to). Negative: I'm not going to, she isn't going to. Question: Are you going to…?

In casual speech, going to often sounds like “gonna” /ˈɡɒnə/. Don't write it in careful texts.

willbe going to
AffirmativeI'll call you tonight.I'm going to call the bank.
NegativeShe won't come.She isn't going to come.
QuestionWill you help me?Are you going to help him?
Short answerYes, I will. / No, I won't.Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.

Deciding now or decided before

Will = the decision is made at the moment of speaking, often as a reaction. Be going to = the intention existed before; we are announcing a plan.

A simple test: “Had I thought about this before this conversation?” If yes → going to. If no → will.

  • The phone's ringing. – I'll get it!
    Instant decision.
  • I'm going to paint my bedroom this weekend. I've bought the paint.
    Plan decided earlier (the paint is bought).
  • It's cold in here. – OK, I'll close the window.
    Immediate reaction.
  • We're going to have a baby in May!
    Intention, announced as news.

Two kinds of prediction

Will = a prediction based on what you think, believe or know, often with I think, I'm sure, probably, I expect, I don't think. Be going to = a prediction based on present evidence: you can see it coming.

Watch the position of probably: after will (She'll probably win) but before won't (She probably won't win). And we prefer “I don't think he'll come” to “I think he won't come”.

  • I think Cameroon will win the match.
    Personal opinion.
  • Look at those clouds! It's going to rain.
    Visible evidence: the clouds.
  • Careful! You're going to fall!
    We can see the fall is about to happen.
  • Prices will probably rise next year.
    General forecast; probably after will.

Other uses of will

We also use will for actions we do with words: offering help, promising, threatening, refusing, asking for a favour. Going to is not possible here.

Shall I / Shall we…? (mainly British English) makes offers and suggestions or asks for advice: “Shall I open the window?”, “Shall we go?”

Won't can also show refusal, even for things: “The car won't start.”

FunctionExampleMeaning
OfferI'll carry your bag.I am offering to do it
PromiseI won't tell anyone, I promise.you can trust me
RequestWill you lend me your charger?please do this for me
RefusalHe won't listen to me.he refuses to listen
ThreatStop that or I'll call the police!a warning
SuggestionShall we take a taxi?what about taking a taxi?

Arrangements: the present continuous

For a plan that is organised, with a date, a time, other people or a ticket, we prefer the present continuous (lesson 50): “I'm meeting Sophie at six.” Going to is possible but says less about the organisation.

Official timetables (trains, classes, films) use the present simple: “The train leaves at 7.15.”

  • I'm flying to Montreal on Friday.
    Ticket booked: fixed arrangement.
  • The conference starts at nine tomorrow.
    Official schedule: present simple.

After when, if, as soon as: no will

In time and condition clauses (when, as soon as, before, after, until, if), we use the present simple even for the future: “I'll call you when I arrive”, not “when I will arrive”.

This is one of the most common mistakes for learners. The first conditional (next lesson) uses the same rule.

  • I'll text you as soon as I get home.
    Not “as soon as I will get”.
  • When she finishes her degree, she's going to work in Abidjan.
    Present simple after when.

The formula

decision now / opinion → will + verb · plan already decided / visible evidence → be going to + verb

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • 'll is a light, “dark” /l/: I'll /aɪl/, you'll /jʊəl/ or /jɔːl/, we'll /wiːl/, it'll /ˈɪtl/. Don't drop it: “I go” ≠ “I'll go”.
  • won't /wəʊnt/ (the vowel in “no”) is different from want /wɒnt/. “I won't go” (I refuse / it isn't going to happen) ≠ “I want to go”.
  • Before a verb, going to often reduces to /ˈɡəʊɪŋ tə/ or “gonna” /ˈɡɒnə/ in casual speech. But not when going means movement: “I'm going to Paris” never becomes “gonna Paris”.
  • Shall is weak in “Shall we go?”: /ʃəl wi ˈɡəʊ/.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Ottawa, a Friday evening. Sarah and her flatmate Jean-Paul, from Bafoussam, organise their Saturday.

Sarah

What are you doing tomorrow?
Present continuous to ask about arrangements.

Jean-Paul

I'm meeting my cousin at the market at ten. Then I'm going to cook ndolé for everyone.
Arrangement, then a plan.

Sarah

Sounds great! I'll help you if you like.
An offer: will.

Jean-Paul

Thanks. Oh, look at the sky. It's going to snow tonight.
Prediction from evidence.

Sarah

Then the roads will probably be terrible tomorrow.
Prediction from opinion; probably after will.

Jean-Paul

Hmm. I'll call my cousin and ask him to come here instead.
A decision made now.

Sarah

Good idea. Shall I buy the vegetables at the corner shop?
Shall I…? = an offer.

Jean-Paul

Yes, please. I'll send you a list as soon as I finish this call.
As soon as + present simple.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Which use?

Write the use: instant decision (D), plan (P), prediction from evidence (E), opinion (O) or promise (Pr).

  1. I'm going to study law next year.
  2. I'll pay you back on Monday, I promise.
  3. Watch out! That glass is going to fall.
  4. I'm hungry. – I'll make you a sandwich.
  5. I think the economy will grow next year.
Show the answers
  1. P
  2. Pr
  3. E
  4. D
  5. O

2. Will or be going to?

Complete with will ('ll) or the correct form of be going to.

  1. I've bought a ticket. I ___ see the match on Saturday.
  2. It's dark in here. – I ___ turn on the light.
  3. She's very pale. I think she ___ faint.
  4. Don't worry, I ___ tell anyone.
  5. We ___ get married next June. We've booked the hall.
Show the answers
  1. am going to ('m going to)
  2. 'll (will)
  3. is going to
  4. won't
  5. are going to

3. Future form or present?

Choose the correct form.

  1. I'll call you when I (will get / get) home.
  2. (I'm having / I'll have) dinner with my boss tonight; we booked a table.
  3. The film (starts / will be starting) at 8.30 according to the programme.
  4. (Shall / Will) I open the window?
Show the answers
  1. get
  2. I'm having
  3. starts
  4. Shall

4. Correct the mistakes

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. I think it won't rain tomorrow.
  2. As soon as I will know, I'll tell you.
  3. She will to visit us next week.
  4. Wait, I'm going to help you with that box!
Show the answers
  1. I don't think it'll rain tomorrow.
  2. As soon as I know, I'll tell you.
  3. She will visit us next week. / She's going to visit us next week.
  4. Wait, I'll help you with that box!

5. What do you say?

Write what you say in each situation, choosing the right future form.

  1. Someone knocks at the door. You decide to open it.
  2. You see dark clouds and tell your friend.
  3. You tell a friend about your decision to learn Spanish.
  4. Promise to phone your mother after your arrival in Brussels.
Show the answers
  1. I'll go! / I'll get it!
  2. Look at those clouds: it's going to rain.
  3. I've decided: I'm going to learn Spanish.
  4. I'll phone you when I arrive in Brussels. / I'll call you when I get to Brussels.

6. Your turn

Write 4–5 sentences: two plans you have already decided (going to or present continuous), one prediction about your country's future (will), and an offer or a promise to a friend (will).

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. Next month I'm going to start a new course in project management. On Saturday I'm meeting my old classmates in Dakar. I think more people will work from home in the future. Don't worry about your move: I'll help you carry the boxes, I promise.
Key points
  • Decision made now, offer, promise, request, refusal → will.
  • Plan already decided → be going to; fixed arrangement → present continuous.
  • Prediction: opinion → will (I think…, probably); visible evidence → going to.
  • After when, if, as soon as, before, until: present simple, never will.
  • Ask yourself: was this decided before I started speaking?
