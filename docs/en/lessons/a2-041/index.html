The past simple: regular verbs

We use the past simple to talk about finished actions: yesterday, last week, in 2015. For regular verbs, you just add -ed, with every subject.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Form the past simple of regular verbs with the correct -ed spelling.
  • Pronounce the -ed ending correctly: /t/, /d/ or /ɪd/.
  • Make negatives and questions with did / didn't + base verb.
  • Talk about finished actions with the right time words (yesterday, last week, ago, in 2020).

When do we use the past simple?

The past simple is for an action that is finished, at a time in the past that we know or say. It is the main tense for telling stories and talking about your day, your weekend or your life.

We often use a time word: yesterday, last night / last week / last year, two days ago, in 2019, on Monday, when I was a child.

Be careful: “ago” comes after the length of time (three years ago), not before it.

  • I worked in Douala last year.
    Finished period: last year.
  • She called me yesterday evening.
    A finished moment: yesterday evening.
  • We visited Bruges two weeks ago.
    “Ago” comes after the time period.
  • They moved to Montreal in 2021.
    A year in the past: in 2021.
The past simple has the same form with every subject: I worked, you worked, he worked, we worked, they worked. There is no -s for he / she / it!

Positive form and -ed spelling

General rule: base verb + -ed (work → worked, play → played, open → opened).

Some spelling rules depend on the end of the verb. The same rules work for other endings (-er, -est), so they are useful later too.

End of the verbRuleExamples
most verbs+ -edwork → worked, watch → watched, clean → cleaned
silent -e+ -d onlylive → lived, like → liked, dance → danced
consonant + yy → i + -edstudy → studied, try → tried, carry → carried
vowel + y+ -ed (keep the y)play → played, enjoy → enjoyed, stay → stayed
one vowel + one consonant, stressed syllabledouble the consonantstop → stopped, plan → planned, prefer → preferred
last syllable not stresseddo not doublevisit → visited, open → opened, listen → listened
final -w, -x, -ynever doubledshow → showed, fix → fixed, snow → snowed
British and American English: travel → travelled (UK) / traveled (US), cancel → cancelled (UK) / canceled (US). In British English, a final -l is doubled even when the syllable is not stressed.

Negative: didn't + base verb

In the negative, use the helper verb did + not (short form didn't) and then the base verb, without -ed. “Did” already shows the past, so the main verb goes back to its base form.

The full form “did not” is for careful writing or for emphasis.

  • I didn't watch the match.
    Not “didn't watched”.
  • He didn't answer my email.
    didn't + base verb.
  • We did not receive the parcel.
    Full form: formal writing.

Questions and short answers

To ask a question: Did + subject + base verb? Answer with a short answer: “Yes, I did.” or “No, I didn't.” Do not repeat the main verb.

With a question word, put it first: What did you cook? Where did they stay? You will practise past questions more in lesson 67.

    Short answer with did.
  • Where did you stay in Brussels?
    Question word + did + subject + base.

Past simple or present perfect? A first guide

Many learners say “I have worked yesterday”. This is wrong: when we say or know a finished past time (yesterday, in 2020, last week), English needs the past simple.

The present perfect (lesson 51) links the past to now, with no finished time. For now, remember: finished past time word = past simple.

  • I finished the report yesterday.
    Not “I have finished … yesterday”.
  • When did you arrive?
    A question about the time → past simple.

The formula

subject + verb-ed · subject + didn't + base · Did + subject + base …?

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • After voiceless sounds /p/, /k/, /f/, /s/, /ʃ/, /tʃ/, -ed sounds like /t/: worked /wɜːkt/, stopped /stɒpt/, watched /wɒtʃt/.
  • After voiced sounds (vowels, /b/, /g/, /v/, /z/, /m/, /n/, /l/…), -ed sounds like /d/: played /pleɪd/, lived /lɪvd/, opened /ˈəʊpənd/.
  • Only after /t/ or /d/, -ed adds a syllable /ɪd/: wanted /ˈwɒntɪd/, needed /ˈniːdɪd/, visited /ˈvɪzɪtɪd/.
  • A common mistake is to say “worked” with two syllables (“work-ed”). Only verbs ending in a t or d sound get an extra syllable.
  • “didn't” is /ˈdɪdnt/; the final t is very light, sometimes not heard before a consonant (I didn't know).

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Monday morning at an office in Brussels. Two colleagues talk about their weekend.

Sophie

Hi Daniel! Did you enjoy your weekend?
A Did question.

Daniel

Yes, I did, thanks. My cousin visited us from Yaoundé.
Short answer, then visited /ɪd/.

Sophie

Oh, nice! What did you do?
Wh- question: What did you do?

Daniel

On Saturday we walked around the Grand-Place and we tried some Belgian waffles.
walked /t/, tried: y → ied.

Sophie

Did he like them?
Did + base verb.

Daniel

He loved them! But it rained on Sunday, so we stayed at home and watched a film.
loved /d/, rained /d/, stayed /d/, watched /t/.

Sophie

I didn't do much. I just cleaned my flat and called my parents.
Negative: didn't + do.

Daniel

That sounds relaxing!
A natural reaction.

Key points
  • Regular past simple = base + -ed, the same for every subject (no -s).
  • Spelling: live → lived, study → studied, play → played, stop → stopped.
  • Negatives and questions: didn't / Did + base verb (never didn't worked).
  • -ed sounds: /t/, /d/, and /ɪd/ only after a t or d sound.
  • Finished past time (yesterday, last…, … ago, in 2020) = past simple, not present perfect.
