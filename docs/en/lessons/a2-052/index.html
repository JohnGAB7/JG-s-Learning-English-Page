Ever / never

“Ever” (at any time in your life) and “never” (at no time) are very often used with the present perfect to talk about experiences: “Have you ever been to Canada?”, “I've never eaten snails”. They are the key words for talking about what you have done in your life.

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Ask about experiences with “Have you ever + past participle?”.
  • Answer with never, without a double negative.
  • Put ever and never in the right place in the sentence.
  • Use “the first time”, “before” and “superlative + I've ever” to give richer answers.

Have you ever…?: the experience question

“Ever” means “at any time in your life until now”. Put it between have / has and the past participle: Have you ever + past participle?

Do not use “already” for this kind of question. “Already” has a different meaning (lesson 53). For life experience, use “ever”.

  • Have you ever been to Canada?
    At any time in your life.
  • Has she ever worked abroad?
    has + she + ever + participle.
  • Have your parents ever seen snow?
    A question about experience.

Answers

Answer with a short answer (Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.), or with “No, never.” If the answer is yes, we often add details in the past simple (lesson 51).

In a full negative answer, “never” replaces “not”: I've never + past participle. Never use never and not together (no double negative).

QuestionPositive answerNegative answer
Have you ever eaten ndolé?Yes, I have. I ate it in Douala last year.No, I haven't. / No, never.
Has he ever ridden a horse?Yes, he has. Twice!No, he's never ridden a horse.
Have they ever met your boss?Yes, they have.No, they've never met her.

Never: position and meaning

“Never” goes between have / has and the past participle: “I have never seen”, “She's never been”. It is already negative, so the verb stays positive.

“I haven't ever…” exists, but it is much less common; prefer “I've never…”. To stress that an experience is new, add “before” at the end: “I've never done this before”.

  • I've never been to Belgium.
    never = not at any time.
  • My grandmother has never used a computer.
    has + never + participle.
  • We've never tried Ethiopian food before.
    before at the end = until now.

Ever with a superlative and “the first time”

“Superlative + noun + I've ever + past participle” describes an exceptional experience: “the best … I've ever …”.

“It's the first time + I've + past participle” says something is happening for the first time. English uses the present perfect here, not the present simple.

  • It's the most beautiful place I've ever seen.
    Superlative + I've ever.
  • This is the best coffee I've ever had.
    the best … I've ever …
  • It's the first time I've flown.
    Not “the first time I fly”.

Ever and never with other tenses

“Never” is also used with the present simple for habits (I never drink coffee, lesson 7) and with the past simple for a finished period: “My father never learned to swim” (the period of his life we are talking about is over).

In American English, people often say “Did you ever…?” where British English says “Have you ever…?”. For now, use “Have you ever…?” for life experience.

  • I never eat meat on Fridays.
    Habit: present simple.
  • I never met my grandfather. He died before I was born.
    Finished period: past simple.

The formula

Have / Has + subject + ever + past participle? · subject + have / has + never + past participle

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “ever” is /ˈevə/ (UK) / /ˈevər/ (US); “never” is /ˈnevə/: a short e as in “pet”.
  • In “Have you ever…?”, the words link: /həv juː ˈevə/; “you ever” sounds almost like /juˈwevə/.
  • In negative sentences, stress “never”: I've NEVER been there.
  • “Have you ever…?” goes up at the end (yes / no question); the answer “No, never” goes down.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

During a lunch break in Montreal, some colleagues play “Have you ever…?”.

Chloé

OK, my turn. Have you ever eaten poutine, Kwame?
The experience question.

Kwame

Yes, I have! I had it on my first day here. It's the best snack I've ever eaten.
Detail in the past simple; superlative + I've ever.

Chloé

Ha! And have you ever been skiing?
been + -ing activity: been skiing.

Kwame

No, never. I've never even seen a ski slope!
No, never + I've never.

Marie

Have you ever tried, Chloé?
Another ever question.

Chloé

Yes, lots of times. I went to Mont-Tremblant last winter.
Finished time → past simple.

Kwame

Can you teach me? It'll be the first time I've tried a winter sport!
the first time + present perfect.

Marie

Great idea. Let's all go next Saturday!
A suggestion.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Ever or never?

Complete with ever or never.

  1. Have you ___ been to Ghana?
  2. I've ___ eaten frogs' legs.
  3. Has your brother ___ worked in a restaurant?
  4. She's ___ flown in a plane. She's a bit nervous.
  5. It's the best film I've ___ seen.
Show the answers
  1. ever
  2. never
  3. ever
  4. never
  5. ever

2. Ask the question

Write the question with Have / Has … ever …?

  1. you / climb / a mountain
  2. she / lose / her passport
  3. they / see / a lion
  4. he / write / a poem
Show the answers
  1. Have you ever climbed a mountain?
  2. Has she ever lost her passport?
  3. Have they ever seen a lion?
  4. Has he ever written a poem?

3. Answer

Answer the question (✓ = yes, with a detail in the past simple; ✗ = no, with never).

  1. Have you ever been to Brussels? (✓ last year)
  2. Have you ever played golf? (✗)
  3. Has Ana ever driven a truck? (✗)
  4. Have you ever met a celebrity? (✓ in 2020)
Show the answers
  1. Yes, I have. I went there last year.
  2. No, I've never played golf.
  3. No, she's never driven a truck.
  4. Yes, I have. I met one in 2020.

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. I haven't never been to Spain.
  2. Have you ever saw a ghost?
  3. It's the first time I eat cassava.
  4. I've ever visited Rome.
Show the answers
  1. I've never been to Spain.
  2. Have you ever seen a ghost?
  3. It's the first time I've eaten cassava.
  4. I've visited Rome.

5. Rewrite

Rewrite the sentence with the word or pattern in brackets.

  1. Is snow something you have seen in your life? (ever)
  2. We haven't been to South Africa at any time. (never)
  3. I have received many presents. This one is the best. (the best … ever)
Show the answers
  1. Have you ever seen snow?
  2. We've never been to South Africa.
  3. It's the best present I've ever received.

6. Your turn

Write three “Have you ever…?” questions for a friend, then answer them for yourself (yes with a detail in the past simple, or no with never).

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. Have you ever slept in a tent? — Yes, I have. I slept in a tent near Kribi in 2019. Have you ever run a marathon? — No, I've never run a marathon. Have you ever cooked for a big party? — Yes, I have. I cooked for my sister's wedding last year.
Key points
  • Have you ever + past participle? = life experience (ever = at any time in your life).
  • I've never + past participle: never is already negative, no not.
  • ever and never go between have / has and the participle.
  • If yes, give the details (when, where) in the past simple.
  • the best … I've ever …; It's the first time I've + past participle.
