“To be”: negatives and questions

After the positive forms of “to be” (lesson 1), learn how to say “not” and how to ask questions. You need these sentences when you meet people, check information or correct a mistake.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Make the negative of “to be” with not, in the full form and the short forms.
  • Ask a yes / no question by changing the order of the subject and the verb (Are you…? Is she…?).
  • Give a correct short answer (Yes, I am. / No, she isn't.).
  • Ask questions with a question word + be (Where are you? What is it?).

The negative

To make “to be” negative, just add “not” after the verb: I am not, she is not, they are not. You don't need “do” with “to be”.

When we speak, we use short forms. For is and are there are two short forms: join the subject and the verb (she's not, they're not) or join the verb and not (she isn't, they aren't). Both are correct and common. “'s not / 're not” makes the “not” a little stronger.

With “I”, there is only one short form: I'm not. “Amn't” is not standard English.

II am notI'm not—
youyou are notyou're notyou aren't
he / she / ithe is nothe's nothe isn't

  • I'm not tired.
    The only short form with I.
  • She isn't at home. / She's not at home.
    Two correct short forms.
  • We aren't from Paris. We're from Lille.
    Negative, then the correct information.
  • It's not expensive.
    A description in the negative.

Yes / no questions: change the order

To ask a question with the answer yes or no, put the verb before the subject: You are French → Are you French? You don't need “do”.

“You are ready?” with a rising voice is possible only to show surprise. The normal question is “Are you ready?”.

StatementQuestionNote
I am late.Am I late?am before I
You are a student.Are you a student?are before you
He is at work.Is he at work?is before he
It is far.Is it far?is before it
They are ready.Are they ready?are before they
  • Is your sister a doctor?
    The subject can be a longer group of words.
  • Are the shops open on Sunday?
    Plural subject: are.
Negative questions (Aren't you hungry? Isn't it beautiful?) show surprise or expect “yes”. With I, say “Aren't I?”: Aren't I right?

Short answers

In English, only “Yes” or “No” can sound unfriendly. Add a short answer: Yes / No + pronoun + verb. Use the same verb as in the question.

Important rule: never use a short form in a positive short answer. Say “Yes, I am”, “Yes, she is”, never “Yes, I'm” or “Yes, she's”. In negative answers, short forms are normal: No, I'm not. No, he isn't.

QuestionYesNo
Are you tired?Yes, I am.No, I'm not.
Is he Belgian?Yes, he is.No, he isn't. / No, he's not.
Is it cold?Yes, it is.No, it isn't. / No, it's not.
Are we late?Yes, we are. / Yes, you are.No, we aren't. / No, you aren't.
Are they ready?Yes, they are.No, they aren't. / No, they're not.
In the answer, use a pronoun, not the noun: Is your brother at home? — Yes, he is (not “Yes, my brother is”).

Questions with a question word

For an open question, put the question word first, then the verb, then the subject: question word + am / is / are + subject? The main question words: what, where, who, how, how old, when, why.

When we speak, “is” is often short after the question word: What's, Where's, Who's, How's. In writing, don't shorten “are” in these questions (write “Where are you?”). You will study Wh- questions in more detail in lesson 29.

  • Where are you? — I'm at the station.
    Place.
  • What's your address?
    What + is.
  • Who is that man? — He's my boss.
    Person.
  • How are your parents?
    Health and news.
  • Why are you sad?
    Reason.

Traps: “to be” or “do”?

Never mix “to be” and “do”: “Do you are…?” and “I don't be…” are wrong. With “to be”, change the order or add not. That's all.

Some ideas use a verb in English, not “be” + adjective. The most common one: “agree” is a verb, so say “I agree” and “Do you agree?”, never “I am agree” or “Are you agree?”. But “afraid” and “right” are adjectives, so they use be: Are you afraid? Are you right?

  • Are you agree? ✗ → Do you agree? ✓
    “Agree” is a verb: it needs do in questions (lesson 6).
  • Is she right? — No, she isn't.
    “Right” is an adjective: use be.

The formula

Negative: subject + am / is / are + not · Question: (Wh-) + am / is / are + subject? · Answer: Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “isn't” /ˈɪznt/ and “aren't” /ɑːnt/: the final t is light, sometimes almost silent before a consonant (She isn't here).
  • Yes / no question (Are you ready?): your voice goes up at the end. Wh- question (Where are you?): your voice goes down at the end.
  • In questions, “are” is weak: Are you /ə juː/ or /ɑː jə/ in fast speech. In a short answer (Yes, I am), the verb is strong and stressed: /æm/.
  • In negatives, stress “not” or “-n't” so people hear it: I'm NOT tired. She ISn't here.
  • “What's” /wɒts/, “Where's” /weəz/, “Who's” /huːz/: the s joins the next word (Where's_it?).

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Two colleagues talk on the phone before a meeting in Paris.

Nadia

Hi, Pierre. Where are you? Are you at the office?
A Wh- question, then a yes / no question.

Pierre

No, I'm not. I'm on the train. Am I late?
Short answer; question with I.

Nadia

No, you aren't. The meeting is at ten. Is Lisa with you?
Negative short answer.

Pierre

Yes, she is. But she isn't very well today.
“Yes, she is”: no short form.

Nadia

Oh no. What's the problem? Is she ill?
What's = What is.

Pierre

No, she isn't ill. She's just tired.
Negative + correct information.

Nadia

OK. Are the documents ready?
Plural subject: are.

Pierre

Yes, they are. They're in my bag. See you soon!
Pronoun “they” in the answer.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Make it negative

Write the sentence in the negative (short form).

  1. I am hungry.
  2. She is at work.
  3. They are Canadian.
  4. It is cold today.
  5. We are late.
Show the answers
  1. I'm not hungry.
  2. She isn't at work. / She's not at work.
  3. They aren't Canadian. / They're not Canadian.
  4. It isn't cold today. / It's not cold today.
  5. We aren't late. / We're not late.

2. Make a question

Change the sentence into a question.

  1. You are tired.
  2. Paul is a teacher.
  3. The shop is open.
  4. Your parents are in Douala.
Show the answers
  1. Are you tired?
  2. Is Paul a teacher?
  3. Is the shop open?
  4. Are your parents in Douala?

3. Short answers

Give a short answer (yes or no, as shown).

  1. Are you French? (yes)
  2. Is it far? (no)
  3. Is your mother a nurse? (yes)
  4. Are the children at school? (no)
  5. Am I late? (no)
Show the answers
  1. Yes, I am.
  2. No, it isn't. / No, it's not.
  3. Yes, she is.
  4. No, they aren't. / No, they're not.
  5. No, you aren't. / No, you're not.

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Correct it.

  1. Yes, she's.
  2. Where you are from?
  3. Do you are a student?
  4. I amn't sure.
  5. Are you agree with me?
Show the answers
  1. Yes, she is.
  2. Where are you from?
  3. Are you a student?
  4. I'm not sure.
  5. Do you agree with me?

5. Write the question

Write a question with “to be” for each answer.

  1. Yes, I'm free tonight.
  2. No, it isn't expensive.
  3. The station? It's on the left.
  4. No, they aren't Belgian. They're Swiss.
Show the answers
  1. Are you free tonight?
  2. Is it expensive?
  3. Where's the station? / Where is the station?
  4. Are they Belgian?

6. Your turn

Write three questions with “to be” for a new colleague. Then answer them for you, with short answers and one full sentence.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. Are you from Brussels? — No, I'm not. I'm from Liège. Is your office on this floor? — Yes, it is. Are you busy today? — No, I'm not. I'm free this afternoon.
Key points
  • Negative: am / is / are + not → I'm not, she isn't (she's not), they aren't (they're not).
  • Yes / no question: change the order → Are you ready? Is it far? Never “do” with “to be”.
  • Short answer: Yes, I am / No, I'm not. No short form after Yes.
  • Open question: Wh- + be + subject → Where are you? What's your name?
  • “Agree” is a verb: I agree, Do you agree?
