Days, months and dates

Days, months and dates help you make appointments, talk about birthdays and fill in forms. In English they start with a capital letter, and we say dates with ordinal numbers (first, second, third…).

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Name the days of the week, the months and the seasons.
  • Use the right preposition: on Monday, in July, at the weekend.
  • Say and write a date in British and American English (the 14th of November / November 14th).
  • Ask and give the date: What's the date today? When's your birthday?

Days of the week

In English, days always start with a capital letter: Monday, Tuesday… In the UK the week starts on Monday; many American and Canadian calendars start on Sunday.

Use “on” before a day: “on Monday” (one Monday). For a habit, use the plural: “on Mondays” (every Monday), or say “every Monday”.

DayShort formExample
MondayMonI start work on Monday.
TuesdayTueThe gym is closed on Tuesdays.
WednesdayWedSee you on Wednesday.
ThursdayThuWe have a meeting every Thursday.
FridayFriFriday is my favourite day.
SaturdaySatI go to the market on Saturdays.
SundaySunOn Sunday I relax.
  • I have an English class on Wednesday.
    One Wednesday: this week.
  • The market is open on Saturdays.
    Every Saturday: a habit.
  • What do you do at the weekend?
    British English; American English says “on the weekend”.

Months and seasons

Months also start with a capital letter. Use “in” before a month, a season or a year: “in May”, “in summer”, “in 2026”.

Seasons use small letters. British English says “autumn”; American and Canadian English say “fall”.

  • My birthday is in June.
    in + month.
  • It's very cold in Montreal in winter.
    in + season.
  • The leaves are red in autumn.
    American English: in the fall.
Seasons: spring, summer, autumn / fall, winter. In tropical countries people also talk about “the rainy season” and “the dry season”.

Ordinal numbers for dates

For dates, English uses ordinal numbers: “the fourteenth”, not “the fourteen”. We often write them short: 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th…

Most ordinals end in -th: fourth, sixth, tenth. Learn the irregular ones: first, second, third, fifth, eighth, ninth, twelfth, and the tens in -ieth: twentieth, thirtieth.

After 20, only the last number changes: twenty-first (21st), twenty-second (22nd), thirty-first (31st). Lesson 89 looks at ordinals in more detail.

Writing and saying a date

In British English, write the day and then the month: 14 November 2026 or 14/11/2026. Say: “the fourteenth of November” or “November the fourteenth”.

In American English, the month comes first: November 14, 2026 or 11/14/2026. Say: “November fourteenth”. Careful: 03/04 is 3 April in the UK but March 4 in the USA. In an international email, write the month in letters.

Say years in pairs: 1998 = “nineteen ninety-eight”. From 2000: 2005 = “two thousand and five”; 2026 = “twenty twenty-six” or “two thousand and twenty-six”.

1 Maythe first of MayMay first
  • I was born on the 3rd of May 1998.
    Say: the third of May, nineteen ninety-eight.
  • The course starts on 2 February.
    Say: on the second of February.

Asking the day and the date

“What day is it today?” asks for the day of the week: “It's Friday.” “What's the date today?” asks for the date: “It's the fourteenth of November.” Like the time, the answer starts with “It's”.

Use “on” before an exact date: “on the 25th of December”, “on my birthday”.

Use no preposition before today, tomorrow, yesterday, this, next, last, every: “See you next Monday”, “I was in Lyon last month”.

  • What day is it today? — It's Tuesday.
    The day of the week.
  • What's the date today? — It's the first of March.
    The date.
  • When's your birthday? — It's on the 9th of October.
    on + exact date.
  • The shop is closed next Monday.
    No “on” before next.

The formula

on + day / date (on Monday, on 5 May) · in + month / season / year (in May, in 2026) · at the weekend

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “Wednesday” is /ˈwenzdeɪ/: don't say the first d (“wenz-day”).
  • Don't mix up “Tuesday” /ˈtjuːzdeɪ/ (with a “tyoo” sound) and “Thursday” /ˈθɜːzdeɪ/ (tongue between the teeth).
  • The -th of ordinals is /θ/: fourth, fifth, sixth. In “fifth” and “twelfth” you hear /fθ/: push the air out and keep the th.
  • In “the fourteenth of November”, “the” and “of” are weak: /ðə ˌfɔːˈtiːnθ əv nəʊˈvembə/.
  • Stress: JANuary, FEBruary, AUgust /ˈɔːɡəst/ (stress at the start), but sepTEMber, ocTOber, noVEMber, deCEMber (stress on the second syllable).

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Yaoundé, Ngono and Paul are planning a birthday party.

Ngono

When's your sister's birthday?
“When's” = when is.

Paul

It's on the twenty-second of December.
on + date, said with an ordinal.

Ngono

What day is that?
Which day of the week?

Paul

Let me check the calendar… It's a Tuesday.
“a Tuesday” = one Tuesday.

Ngono

A Tuesday? People work on Tuesdays. Let's have the party at the weekend.
“on Tuesdays” = every Tuesday.

Paul

Good idea. Saturday the nineteenth?
A short way to give a day and a date.

Ngono

Perfect. And it's the rainy season, so let's book a room.
Season in a tropical country.

Paul

OK, so I need to call the restaurant tomorrow.
No preposition before “tomorrow”.

Key points
  • Days and months: capital letter (Monday, July); seasons: small letter (summer, autumn / fall).
  • on Monday, on Mondays, on 5 May · in July, in summer, in 2026 · at the weekend (US: on the weekend).
  • Say dates with ordinals: the fourteenth of November (UK), November fourteenth (US).
  • UK: day / month; USA: month / day. If in doubt, write the month in letters.
  • What day is it? — It's Friday. What's the date? — It's the 1st of March. No preposition before next, last, this, every.
