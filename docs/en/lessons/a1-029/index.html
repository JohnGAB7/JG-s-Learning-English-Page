A1 · Beginner · lesson 29

Wh- questions (what, where, who…)

Question words (what, where, when, who, why, how…) make open questions: questions that ask for information. The secret is word order: question word + auxiliary + subject + verb.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Know the question words and their meaning: what, where, when, who, why, how, which, whose.
  • Make questions in the right order with be, do / does, can and the present continuous.
  • Use How + adjective (how old, how often, how far) and What + noun (what time, what colour).
  • Put the preposition at the end of the question (Where are you from?).

Question words

Most of them start with “wh-”, so we call them Wh- words; “how” is in the same family. The question word always comes at the start of the question.

WordIt asks about…Example
whata thing, an ideaWhat's your name?
wherea placeWhere do you live?
whena timeWhen is your birthday?
whoa personWho is that man?
whya reasonWhy are you sad?
howa way, a stateHow do you go to work?
whicha choice from a small groupWhich bus goes to the centre?
whosethe ownerWhose phone is this?
We usually answer “Why?” with “because”: “Why are you tired? — Because I work at night.”

Word order: Wh- + auxiliary + subject + verb

In English, you can't keep the order of a statement in a question (“Where you live?” is wrong). You need an auxiliary before the subject.

With “be”, “can” or the present continuous, the auxiliary is already there: just put it before the subject. With the present simple, add “do” or “does”; the main verb is then in the base form (no s).

Remember QASI: Question word, Auxiliary, Subject, Infinitive (base verb).

  • Where does your sister work? — In a bank.
    does + work (no s).
  • What are you reading? — A novel.
    Present continuous.
  • Why is the shop closed?
    be: is before the subject.

How + adjective, What / Which + noun

“How” goes with an adjective or adverb to ask about a measure: how old (age), how tall (height), how far (distance), how long (time), how often (frequency). “How much / how many” are in lesson 30.

“What” and “which” go with a noun: what time, what colour, what kind of music. Use “which” when the choice is small and known (Which one, the red or the blue?) and “what” when the choice is open.

“Whose” + noun asks about the owner: “Whose car is this? — It's Marc's.” Don't confuse it with “Who's” (= who is), which sounds the same.

  • How old is your daughter? — She's six.
    Age: How old + be.
  • How often do you go to the gym? — Twice a week.
    Frequency.
  • How far is the airport? — About ten kilometres.
    Distance.
  • What kind of music do you like?
    Open choice.
  • Which colour do you want, blue or green?
    Small, known choice.

The preposition at the end

When a verb has a preposition (be from, talk to, look at, wait for), the preposition stays at the end of the question in normal English: “Where are you from?”, not “From where are you?”.

This is the normal form in speaking and in writing.

  • Where are you from? — I'm from Cameroon.
    be from.
  • Who are you waiting for?
    wait for.
  • What are you looking at?
    look at.

When “who” or “what” is the subject

When “who” or “what” is the subject of the verb (you ask WHO does the action), don't use “do / does”: “Who lives here?”. Compare: “Who do you love?” (“you” is the subject).

At this level, learn the common questions: “Who wants coffee?”, “What happens next?”. Lesson 68 looks at this in more detail.

  • Who knows the answer?
    who = subject: no does.
  • Who does Marie know here?
    Marie = subject: does is necessary.

The formula

Question word + auxiliary (is / are / do / does / can) + subject + verb…? — Where do you live?

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • In what, where, when, why, which, “wh” sounds like /w/: /wɒt/, /weə/, /wen/, /waɪ/. But who, whose and whom start with /h/: /huː/, /huːz/.
  • In a Wh- question, the voice goes down at the end (like a statement): “Where do you LIVE? ↘”.
  • In fast speech, “do you” gets short: “Where do you live?” ≈ /ˈweə dʒə ˈlɪv/; “What do you” ≈ /ˈwɒdʒə/.
  • Common short forms: what's /wɒts/, where's /weəz/, who's /huːz/, how's /haʊz/.
  • “Whose” and “who's” sound exactly the same: /huːz/. The context shows the difference.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ Where you live?✓ Where do you live?You need the auxiliary “do” before the subject.
✗ Where does she lives?✓ Where does she live?After “does”, use the base verb, no s.
✗ What age have you?✓ How old are you?Ask about age with “How old + be”.
✗ From where are you?✓ Where are you from?The preposition stays at the end.
✗ How is called your brother?✓ What's your brother called? / What's your brother's name?Ask about names with What's … called? / What's … name?
✗ Why you are late?✓ Why are you late?With “be”, put the verb before the subject.
✗ Who is this bag?✓ Whose bag is this?Ask about the owner with “whose”.
✗ Who does live here?✓ Who lives here?When “who” is the subject, no “do / does”.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

At a conversation café in Montreal, Julie meets Emmanuel, who is new from Cameroon.

Julie

Hi! What's your name?
what + be.

Emmanuel

I'm Emmanuel. Nice to meet you.
A polite phrase.

Julie

Where are you from, Emmanuel?
Preposition at the end.

Emmanuel

I'm from Bamenda, in Cameroon.
Answer with “from”.

Julie

Cool! Why are you in Montreal?
why + be.

Emmanuel

Because I'm a student at the university. I study engineering.
Answer to why: because.

Julie

And how often do you come to this café?
how often = frequency.

Emmanuel

Every Thursday. What about you? What do you do?
“What do you do?” = What's your job?

Julie

I'm a nurse. Which language do you want to practise tonight, English or French?
which = a choice of two.

Emmanuel

English, please!
A short answer.

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. The right question word

Complete with what, where, when, who, why, how or whose.

  1. ___ do you live? — In Lille.
  2. ___ is your birthday? — In May.
  3. ___ is that woman? — She's my aunt.
  4. ___ are you sad? — Because my cat is ill.
  5. ___ do you go to work? — By bus.
  6. ___ umbrella is this? — It's mine.
Show the answers
  1. Where
  2. When
  3. Who
  4. Why
  5. How
  6. Whose

2. Put the words in order

Put the words in the right order to make a question.

  1. live / where / you / do
  2. does / what time / start / the film
  3. you / are / what / doing
  4. from / is / where / he
Show the answers
  1. Where do you live?
  2. What time does the film start?
  3. What are you doing?
  4. Where is he from?

3. Ask the question

Write the question for the part in CAPITAL LETTERS.

  1. I live IN DOUALA.
  2. She's TWENTY-FIVE.
  3. The film starts AT EIGHT.
  4. It's ANNA'S bag.
  5. I go to the gym TWICE A WEEK.
Show the answers
  1. Where do you live?
  2. How old is she?
  3. What time does the film start?
  4. Whose bag is it?
  5. How often do you go to the gym?

4. Correct the mistake

Each question has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. From where are you?
  2. What age has your brother?
  3. Where she works?
  4. How is called your sister?
  5. Who does live here?
Show the answers
  1. Where are you from?
  2. How old is your brother?
  3. Where does she work?
  4. What's your sister's name? / What's your sister called?
  5. Who lives here?

  1. Model answer
Show the answers

Key points
  • Order: question word + auxiliary + subject + verb (QASI): Where do you live? What are you doing?
  • Present simple: do / does + base verb (Where does she work?).
  • How old / how often / how far / how long; What time / what colour / what kind of; which = small choice; whose = owner.
  • The preposition stays at the end: Where are you from? Who are you waiting for?
  • The voice goes down at the end of a Wh- question.
