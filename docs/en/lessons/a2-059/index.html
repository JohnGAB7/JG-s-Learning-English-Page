Uncountable nouns

Some English nouns can't be counted: water, money, advice, information, furniture… They have no plural and no “a / an”. In many other languages these words are countable, so they cause very common mistakes.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Recognise common uncountable nouns, especially the ones that are countable in other languages.
  • Use them correctly: no a / an, no -s, singular verb, with some / any / much / a little.
  • Count an uncountable noun with a piece of, a cup of, a slice of, a bottle of…
  • Know nouns with two uses (coffee / a coffee, time / a time) and countable alternatives (advice → a tip, work → a job).

The grammar rules

An uncountable noun is a substance, a mass or an abstract idea that we don't count: water, rice, music, happiness. In English, it follows four rules.

1. No article a / an: “advice”, never “an advice”. 2. No plural: “information”, never “informations”. 3. Singular verb and pronoun: The news is good. Your luggage is here — it's heavy. 4. It goes with some, any, much, a little, a lot of, little, no, but not with many, a few, several, or a number.

PossibleNot possible
some information, any informationan information, two informations
much advice, a lot of advice, a little advicemany advices, a few advices
The furniture is new.The furnitures are new.
How much luggage have you got?How many luggages have you got?

The trap nouns

These nouns are uncountable in English, but countable in many other languages. Learn them one by one: no logical rule explains them.

Uncountable nounMeaningExample
informationfacts about somethingThanks for the information.
advicean opinion about what someone should doCan you give me some advice?
newsnew information about eventsThe news is on at eight.
furniturechairs, tables, beds…We need some new furniture.
luggage / baggagebags and suitcases for a tripHow much luggage do you have?
homeworkschool work you do at homeI've got a lot of homework.
workthe activity of working (but a job = a position)I've got too much work.
traveltravelling in generalTravel is expensive.
accommodationa place to live or stayWe're looking for accommodation.
equipmentthe things you need for an activityThe equipment is new.
progressimprovementYou're making good progress.
weathersun, rain, wind, temperatureWhat lovely weather!
hairwhat grows on your headHer hair is long.
bread, toastfood made from flourTwo slices of toast, please.
Because “weather” is uncountable, don't say “a beautiful weather” or “What a beautiful weather!”. Say “beautiful weather” and “What beautiful weather!”. In the same way: “Good news!” (no a).

Counting an uncountable noun

To talk about one unit or an exact quantity, add a countable noun + of: a piece of advice, two pieces of furniture, a piece of information.

For food and drink, use the container or the shape: a cup of tea, a glass of water, a bottle of milk, a slice of bread, a loaf of bread (a whole one), a bar of chocolate, a kilo of rice, a bowl of soup, a can of cola (US) / a tin of tomatoes (UK).

  • Let me give you a piece of advice.
    One unit of advice.
  • Can I have a glass of water, please?
    Container + of.
  • We bought two loaves of bread.
    loaf → loaves.
  • That's a useful piece of information.
    One fact.

Nouns with two uses

Some nouns are uncountable with one meaning and countable with another. The meaning changes, sometimes a lot.

In a café, people also say “a coffee, two teas, three beers” for cups or glasses.

UncountableCountable
coffee (the drink)a coffee (a cup of coffee)
chicken (the meat)a chicken (the whole animal)
glass (the material)a glass (for drinking); glasses (for your eyes)
paper (the material)a paper (a newspaper; a scientific article)
time (minutes, hours)a time (an occasion): three times
room (space)a room (part of a building)
hair (all of it)a hair (one single hair)
experience (knowledge from doing things)an experience (something that happened to you)
  • Is there any room in your car?
    room = space.
  • I've been to Kinshasa three times.
    times = occasions.
  • She's got five years' experience.
    experience = knowledge.

Countable alternatives

Often English has another, countable word for one unit. This is the most natural solution when you want to count.

work → a job, a task; travel → a trip, a journey; luggage → a suitcase, a bag; furniture → a chair, a table; advice → a tip, a suggestion; money → a coin, a note (US: a bill).

  • I'm looking for a job. ✓ / I'm looking for a work. ✗
    job is countable.
  • Have a good trip! ✓ / Have a good travel! ✗
    trip is countable.
  • I've only got two suitcases.
    suitcase is countable.

The formula

uncountable: no a / an, no -s, singular verb · some / much / a little + noun · a piece of / a cup of / a slice of + noun

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “advice” /ədˈvaɪs/ (noun, unvoiced s) and “advise” /ədˈvaɪz/ (verb, voiced z): the last sound changes the word.
  • “furniture” /ˈfɜːnɪtʃə/: stress on the first syllable; the t sounds like /tʃ/.
  • “luggage” /ˈlʌɡɪdʒ/: the end sounds like /ɪdʒ/ (as in “village”).
  • “a piece of” joins: /ə ˈpiːs əv/; “of” is very weak.
  • “news” /njuːz/ (UK) or /nuːz/ (US) ends in /z/, but it is singular.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ Thanks for the informations.✓ Thanks for the information.Information is uncountable: no -s.
✗ Can you give me an advice?✓ Can you give me some advice? / a piece of advice?Advice is uncountable: no an.
✗ The news are bad.✓ The news is bad.News takes a singular verb.
✗ We bought new furnitures.✓ We bought some new furniture.Furniture has no plural.
✗ What a nice weather!✓ What nice weather!Weather is uncountable: no a.
✗ I'm looking for a work.✓ I'm looking for a job. / I'm looking for work.A position = a job; work is uncountable.
✗ How many luggages do you have?✓ How much luggage do you have? / How many bags do you have?Luggage is uncountable.
✗ Have a good travel!✓ Have a good trip!One journey = a trip.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

A Cameroonian student arrives at Brussels Airport and speaks to someone at the information desk.

Grace

Excuse me, can you give me some information about buses to the city centre?
some information: no -s.

Employee

Of course. The train is faster, but the bus is cheaper. How much luggage have you got?
How much + luggage.

Grace

Two big suitcases and a backpack.
Countable: suitcases.

Employee

Then my advice is to take the train. There isn't much room on the bus.
room = space (uncountable).

Grace

Thanks for the advice. Is there any accommodation for students near the university?
accommodation: uncountable.

Employee

Yes, there's a student residence. Here's a leaflet with all the information.
all the information.

Grace

Great news! And the weather? Is it always this cold?
No a before news or weather.

Employee

Not always. But buy a warm coat. Welcome to Belgium, and good luck with your studies!
A friendly ending.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Countable or uncountable?

Write C (countable) or U (uncountable).

  1. advice
  2. suitcase
  3. furniture
  4. job
  5. news
  6. trip
Show the answers
  1. U
  2. C
  3. U
  4. C
  5. U
  6. C

2. Choose the right form

Choose the correct form.

  1. The news (is / are) on TV at eight.
  2. I need (an / some) advice.
  3. How (much / many) furniture is there?
  4. Her hair (is / are) very long.
  5. We had (a terrible weather / terrible weather).
Show the answers
  1. is
  2. some
  3. much
  4. is
  5. terrible weather

3. Counting

Complete with piece(s), cup, slice(s), glass or loaf.

  1. a ___ of tea
  2. two ___ of bread (cut thin)
  3. a ___ of water
  4. three ___ of furniture
  5. a ___ of bread (a whole one)
Show the answers
  1. cup
  2. slices
  3. glass
  4. pieces
  5. loaf

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Correct it.

  1. She gave me a good advice.
  2. I've got two homeworks for tomorrow.
  3. Are these informations correct?
  4. My brother is looking for a work in Lyon.
Show the answers
  1. She gave me some good advice. / She gave me a good piece of advice.
  2. I've got two pieces of homework for tomorrow. / I've got some homework for tomorrow.
  3. Is this information correct?
  4. My brother is looking for a job in Lyon. / My brother is looking for work in Lyon.

5. What do you say?

Write one sentence for each situation, with an uncountable noun.

  1. You want to tell a friend something good has happened.
  2. Your student's English is getting better and better.
  3. Your old chairs and tables are broken.
  4. Your friend is leaving for a holiday abroad.
Show the answers
  1. I've got some good news! / I have good news!
  2. You're making a lot of progress.
  3. We need some new furniture.
  4. Have a good trip! / Have a nice trip!

6. Your turn

Write four sentences with trap uncountable nouns: some advice for a traveller, a useful piece of information about your town, and a sentence about your luggage or your furniture.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. My advice is to visit Yaoundé in the dry season. A useful piece of information: taxis are cheap. Don't bring too much luggage. My flat is small, so I haven't got much furniture.
Key points
  • Uncountable: no a / an, no -s, singular verb (The news is…).
  • Traps: information, advice, news, furniture, luggage, homework, work, travel, accommodation, progress, weather, hair.
  • To count: a piece of advice, a cup of tea, a slice of bread, a loaf of bread.
  • Two uses: coffee / a coffee, time / three times, room / a room.
  • Countable alternatives: a job, a trip, a suitcase, a tip.
