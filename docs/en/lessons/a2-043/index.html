The past continuous

The past continuous describes an action in progress at a moment in the past: “I was having dinner when you called.” We use it to set the scene in a story and to show that one action interrupted another.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Form the past continuous with was / were + -ing, in all forms.
  • Say what you were doing at a particular time in the past.
  • Combine the past continuous and the past simple with when and while.
  • Know when not to use the past continuous (states and past habits).

The form

The past continuous uses the past of “to be” (was / were, lesson 39) + verb-ing. It is the present continuous (lesson 8) moved into the past: I am working → I was working.

Negative: wasn't / weren't + -ing. Question: Was / Were + subject + -ing? Short answers: Yes, I was. / No, they weren't.

SubjectPositiveNegativeQuestion
II was workingI wasn't workingWas I working?
youyou were workingyou weren't workingWere you working?
he / she / itshe was workingshe wasn't workingWas she working?
wewe were workingwe weren't workingWere we working?
theythey were workingthey weren't workingWere they working?
-ing spelling (the same as the present continuous): make → making, sit → sitting, lie → lying, study → studying.

Use 1: an action in progress at a past time

Use it to say what was happening at a particular time in the past. The action started before that moment and continued after it.

Common time phrases: at 8 o'clock yesterday, at this time last week, all day / all evening, at midnight.

  • At 9 p.m. yesterday, I was watching the news.
    In progress at 9 p.m.
  • What were you doing at this time last year?
    Question: were + you + doing.
  • It was raining all morning in Liège.
    A long, continuing action.

Use 2: an interrupted action (when + past simple)

We use the two tenses together: the past continuous for the long action (the background), the past simple for the short action that happens in the middle of it.

“When” usually comes before the short action in the past simple. Use a comma when the when-clause comes first.

Long action (past continuous)Short action (past simple)
I was having a showerwhen the phone rang.
We were walking homewhen it started to rain.
She was driving to Ottawawhen she saw the accident.
  • When you called, I was cooking.
    I started cooking before you called.
  • When you called, I cooked.
    Different meaning: you called, THEN I cooked (one after the other).

Use 3: two actions at the same time (while) and the background

“While” links two long actions that happen at the same time: both are in the past continuous. While can also introduce the long action before a past simple.

At the start of a story, the past continuous describes the scene: what was happening around, before the main event.

  • While I was studying, my brother was playing video games.
    Two actions at the same time.
  • While we were waiting for the bus, we met an old friend.
    Long action + short action.
  • The sun was shining and the birds were singing.
    Background of a story.

When not to use it

Do not use the past continuous for past habits: use the past simple or “used to” (lesson 44). Do not use it for states (be, have for possession, know, like…): use the past simple.

State verbs (know, like, want, need, believe, belong, understand) do not normally take -ing, in the past or in the present.

SituationCorrect EnglishWhy
action in progress, interruptedI was reading when he arrived.past continuous + past simple
past habitOn Saturdays I played / used to play football.repeated action, not in progress
knowledgeI knew the answer.state verb
possessionShe had two cats.state verb

The formula

was / were + verb-ing · long action (was / were + -ing) + when + short action (past simple)

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • In positive sentences, “was” and “were” are weak: was /wəz/, were /wə/ (UK): I was working /aɪ wəz ˈwɜːkɪŋ/.
  • In short answers and at the end of a sentence, they are strong: Yes, I was /wɒz/. Yes, we were /wɜː/.
  • “wasn't” /ˈwɒznt/ and “weren't” /wɜːnt/ are never weak.
  • The -ing ending is /ɪŋ/: do not pronounce a hard g at the end.
  • In “When the phone rang, I was sleeping”, stress the important words: PHONE, RANG, SLEEPing.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

At a police station in Namur, an officer asks a witness about a small bike accident.

Officer

Can you tell me what happened?
A polite request.

Léa

Yes. I was waiting at the bus stop on Rue de Fer.
Background: past continuous.

Officer

What was the weather like?
“What was … like?” asks for a description.

Léa

It was raining a little, and a lot of people were crossing the street.
Two background actions.

Officer

And the cyclist?
A short question.

Léa

He was riding very fast. He was looking at his phone while he was riding.
while + past continuous.

Officer

Was he wearing a helmet?
Question: Was + he + -ing.

Léa

No, he wasn't. When the car stopped suddenly, he hit it and fell.
Short answer, then the main events in the past simple.

Officer

Thank you. That's very helpful.
Polite thanks.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Was or were?

Complete with was or were.

  1. I ___ reading a book.
  2. They ___ having lunch.
  3. ___ you working at 10?
  4. The baby ___ sleeping.
  5. We ___ not listening.
Show the answers
  1. was
  2. were
  3. Were
  4. was
  5. were

2. What were they doing?

Put the verb in the past continuous.

  1. At 7 a.m., my parents ___ (have) breakfast.
  2. At midnight, I ___ (not / sleep).
  3. What ___ you ___ (do) at 6 p.m.?
  4. It ___ (snow) when we left Montreal.
  5. Kofi ___ (study) in the library all afternoon.
Show the answers
  1. were having
  2. wasn't sleeping
  3. were … doing
  4. was snowing
  5. was studying

3. Past continuous or past simple?

Choose the correct form of each verb.

  1. I ___ (walk) home when I ___ (see) Paul.
  2. When the teacher ___ (come) in, the students ___ (talk).
  3. While she ___ (cook), she ___ (cut) her finger.
  4. We ___ (watch) a film when the electricity ___ (go) off.
  5. While I ___ (wash) the dishes, my sister ___ (dry) them.
Show the answers
  1. was walking … saw
  2. came … were talking
  3. was cooking … cut
  4. were watching … went
  5. was washing … was drying

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. They was waiting outside.
  2. I was wanting a coffee.
  3. When he was calling, I was in the shower.
  4. She was drive to work when it happened.
Show the answers
  1. They were waiting outside.
  2. I wanted a coffee.
  3. When he called, I was in the shower.
  4. She was driving to work when it happened.

5. Join the sentences

Make one sentence with the word in brackets. Choose the right tenses.

  1. I slept. You phoned. (when)
  2. It rained. It was cold. (and – describe the scene)
  3. She drove home. She heard the news on the radio. (while)
  4. They played. We prepared the meal. (while – same time)
Show the answers
  1. I was sleeping when you phoned.
  2. It was raining and it was cold.
  3. While she was driving home, she heard the news on the radio.
  4. While they were playing, we were preparing the meal.

6. Your turn

Describe a moment when something unexpected happened. First describe the scene (past continuous), then the event (past simple).

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. Last Friday evening I was sitting on my balcony in Abidjan. The sun was going down and my neighbours were playing music. Suddenly, I heard a loud noise. A big branch fell from the tree onto my neighbour's car! Luckily, nobody was standing near it.
Key points
  • Past continuous = was / were + verb-ing.
  • It describes an action in progress at a past time, or the background of a story.
  • A long action (past continuous) is interrupted by a short one (past simple): I was cooking when you called.
  • While + two long actions at the same time.
  • No past continuous for state verbs or past habits (use the past simple or used to).
