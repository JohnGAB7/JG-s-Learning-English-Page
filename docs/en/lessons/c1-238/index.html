Nominalisation in academic writing

“Because prices rose sharply, people bought less” becomes “The sharp rise in prices led to a fall in consumption”. Nominalisation turns actions into nouns: it condenses information, makes a text more objective and helps link ideas. Used well, it is the signature of academic English; overused, it makes a text heavy and opaque.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level C1

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Form nouns from verbs and adjectives (increase, implementation, reliability) and choose the right preposition (a rise in, the failure to, the impact of… on).
  • Turn a verbal sentence into a noun phrase: adverb → adjective, subject → of / possessive, conjunction → verb of relation (lead to, result in).
  • Use summary nouns (this trend, these findings, such an approach) for cohesion.
  • Avoid over-nominalisation and nouns that don't exist in English (augmentation, realisation, formation).

Why nominalise?

Academic writing prefers to present phenomena rather than people's actions. “Researchers analysed the data and found that…” becomes “Analysis of the data revealed that…”. The text gains density (more information per sentence), objectivity (fewer actors) and cohesion (a noun can be picked up and qualified).

Nominalisation also lets you put a complex idea in subject position, then link it to another with a verb of relation: “The closure of the factory resulted in the loss of 300 jobs.”

Verbal style (spoken, neutral)Nominal style (academic)
Prices rose sharply, so people bought less.The sharp rise in prices led to a fall in consumption.
The government decided to close the mine, and many people protested.The government's decision to close the mine provoked widespread protests.
If we invest more in schools, children will learn better.Greater investment in schools would improve learning outcomes.
  • The introduction of the new tax caused a sharp decline in sales.
    Two nominalisations linked by “caused”.

Forming nouns: suffixes and conversion

Many verbs have a suffixed noun: -tion / -sion (reduce → reduction, decide → decision), -ment (develop → development), -ance / -ence (perform → performance, depend → dependence), -al (approve → approval, refuse → refusal), -ure (fail → failure), -sis (analyse → analysis).

Others have the same form as verb and noun (conversion): increase, decrease, rise, fall, decline, change, growth (grow), loss (lose). Adjectives give nouns in -ity (reliable → reliability), -ness (aware → awareness), -ance / -ence (important → importance), -cy (efficient → efficiency).

Watch the stress, which sometimes moves: to inCREASE (verb) / an INcrease (noun); to reCORD / a REcord.

BaseNounExample
implement (v.)implementationthe implementation of the policy
fail (v.)failurethe failure to meet targets
grow (v.)growthrapid growth in demand
refuse (v.)refusaltheir refusal to negotiate
available (adj.)availabilitythe availability of clean water
aware (adj.)awarenesspublic awareness of the risks
efficient (adj.)efficiencyimprovements in energy efficiency
  • The availability of cheap credit encouraged borrowing.
    Adjective “available” → noun “availability”.

Transforming a sentence: the method

1) The verb becomes a noun (rise → a rise). 2) The adverb becomes an adjective (sharply → sharp). 3) The subject becomes an of-phrase or a possessive (the government decided → the government's decision / the decision of the government). 4) The object becomes an of-phrase (they built the bridge → the construction of the bridge). 5) The conjunction (because, so, if) becomes a verb or preposition of relation.

Useful verbs of relation: lead to, result in, cause, contribute to, give rise to (cause → effect); result from, be due to, stem from, be attributable to (effect ← cause); be associated with, be linked to, reflect, account for (link or explanation). Prepositions: because of, due to, following, despite, as a result of.

  • Because the population is ageing rapidly, health costs are rising.
    The verbal starting sentence.
  • The rapid ageing of the population is driving up health costs.
    ageing (noun) + rapid (adjective) + of the population + verb of relation.
  • The rise in unemployment stems largely from the closure of two factories.
    “stem from”: effect ← cause.

Summary nouns and cohesion

An abstract noun can summarise the idea of the previous sentence and link it to the next: “Unemployment fell by 2 %. This decline was mainly due to…”. These “shell nouns” are essential for cohesion: this trend, this increase, this approach, these findings, such a policy, the problem, the issue, this view.

Choose a precise noun rather than a bare “this”: “This shows…” is vague; “This decline shows…” is clear.

  • Several regions reported a drop in tourism. This trend is likely to continue.
    “This trend” summarises the whole previous sentence.
  • Some authors favour stricter rules. Such an approach, however, may discourage investment.
    “Such an approach”: summary + evaluation.

Excesses to avoid

Too many nouns in a row make a text unreadable (“zombie nouns”): “The implementation of the evaluation of the reduction of costs…”. Keep at least one strong active verb per sentence and limit chains of “of”.

Don't nominalise everything: a precise verb is often better than a noun + empty verb (“make a decision” → “decide” if the sentence gets heavy; “carry out an investigation of” → “investigate”).

Writing guides (Plain English, scientific journals) recommend a balance: nominalise to summarise and link; keep verbs for the main actions.

Over-nominalisedBalanced
The undertaking of an evaluation of the effectiveness of the programme took place.We evaluated how effective the programme was. / The programme's effectiveness was evaluated.
There was a reduction in the number of accidents as a result of the introduction of speed cameras.The introduction of speed cameras reduced the number of accidents.
  • The committee investigated the complaints and rejected most of them.
    Clearer than “The committee carried out an investigation of the complaints and made a rejection of most of them”.
  • The closure of the line has made commuting much harder.
    Balanced: one noun (closure) + one strong verb (has made).

False nouns and common traps

Learners whose language also nominalises a lot (such as French) often use nouns that do not exist in English or have another meaning. Always check the noun and its preposition.

WrongCorrect EnglishExample
the augmentation of pricesthe increase / rise in pricesa 5 % increase in prices
the diminution of coststhe reduction in / of costs, the decrease in costsa sharp reduction in costs
the realisation of the projectthe completion / implementation of the projectthe completion of the bridge
a formationtraining (uncountable), a training coursestaff training
the putting in place of a systemthe introduction / implementation of a systemthe introduction of a new system
the evolution of pricesthe change / trend in prices, price trendsrecent trends in prices

The formula

Verb → noun, adverb → adjective, subject / object → of or possessive, conjunction → verb of relation (lead to, result from); then a summary noun (this trend)

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • Nouns in -tion are stressed on the syllable before the suffix: implemenTAtion, reDUCtion, deCIsion /dɪˈsɪʒn/.
  • In -ity, the stress falls on the syllable just before -ity: reliaBIlity /rɪˌlaɪəˈbɪləti/, availaBIlity.
  • Pairs with moving stress: to inCREASE / an INcrease; to deCREASE / a DEcrease; to reCORD / a REcord.
  • “analysis” is /əˈnæləsɪs/; the plural “analyses” is /əˈnæləsiːz/.
  • “growth” is /ɡrəʊθ/: pronounce the final th with the tongue between the teeth.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ The augmentation of salaries was small.✓ The increase in salaries was small.“Augmentation” is not the usual noun; use increase / rise + in.
✗ The realisation of the new hospital took three years.✓ The construction / completion of the new hospital took three years.“Realisation” = becoming aware of something.
✗ All employees will follow a formation.✓ All employees will receive training / attend a training course.“Formation” does not mean training.
✗ There was an increase of unemployment.✓ There was an increase in unemployment.“Increase in” + what increases.
✗ The government decision of closing the mine…✓ The government's decision to close the mine…“Decision to + infinitive”.
✗ This shows that the policy worked. (after a long paragraph)✓ This improvement shows that the policy worked.Make the summary noun precise.
✗ The making of a reduction of the costs was decided.✓ It was decided to reduce costs. / Management decided to cut costs.Over-nominalisation: an opaque sentence.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Yaoundé, a master's student and his supervisor rewrite a paragraph of his dissertation.

Mrs Etoundi

Your paragraph says “Because farmers used more fertiliser, they produced more maize.” Try to nominalise it.
A verbal sentence.

Brice

“The increased use of fertiliser led to higher maize production”?
Nominalisation + verb of relation.

Mrs Etoundi

Very good. Then you wrote “This is important.” What does “this” refer to?
A vague “this”.

Brice

The increase in production. So: “This rise in output is significant for food security.”
A precise summary noun.

Mrs Etoundi

Exactly. And here, “the augmentation of prices”?
A false noun.

Brice

Oh, “the rise in prices”. And “the realisation of the irrigation project” should be “the completion”.
Two corrections.

Mrs Etoundi

Right. But your last sentence has five nouns in a row. Keep a verb or two.
Avoid over-nominalisation.

Brice

OK: “The project improved access to water and reduced crop losses.”
Balanced: nouns and strong verbs.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Find the noun

Give the matching noun.

  1. reduce
  2. fail
  3. reliable
  4. approve
  5. grow
Show the answers
  1. reduction
  2. failure
  3. reliability
  4. approval
  5. growth

2. The right preposition

Complete with in, of, to or on.

  1. a sharp rise ___ house prices
  2. the government's decision ___ raise taxes
  3. the impact of the reform ___ small firms
  4. the construction ___ a new bridge
  5. the government's failure ___ act
Show the answers
  1. in
  2. to
  3. on
  4. of
  5. to

3. Nominalise

Rewrite the sentence in nominal style, using the verb of relation given.

  1. Prices rose sharply, so demand fell. (led to)
  2. The factory closed, and 400 people lost their jobs. (resulted in)
  3. Because the population is growing rapidly, there is more pressure on housing. (rapid growth… has increased)
  4. Crime has fallen because more police officers patrol the streets. (is due to)
Show the answers
  1. The sharp rise in prices led to a fall in demand.
  2. The closure of the factory resulted in the loss of 400 jobs.
  3. The rapid growth of the population has increased pressure on housing.
  4. The fall in crime is due to the increased presence of police officers on the streets.

4. Correct the false noun

Correct each sentence.

  1. The augmentation of taxes was unpopular.
  2. Staff will follow a formation in May.
  3. The realisation of the stadium cost 50 million.
Show the answers
  1. The increase / rise in taxes was unpopular.
  2. Staff will receive training / attend a training course in May.
  3. The construction of the stadium cost 50 million.

5. Lighten it

Rewrite the over-nominalised sentence with at least one strong verb.

  1. The carrying out of an investigation into the causes of the fire was undertaken by the police.
  2. There was a reduction in waiting times following the introduction of online booking.
Show the answers
  1. The police investigated the causes of the fire.
  2. The introduction of online booking reduced waiting times.

6. Your turn

Rewrite this passage in academic style (3 sentences), with at least three nominalisations and one summary noun: “Lots of young people moved to the cities because they couldn't find jobs in villages. So cities grew very fast. This was a problem because there weren't enough houses.”

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. The lack of employment in rural areas has led to the large-scale migration of young people to cities. This movement has resulted in rapid urban growth. Such growth has, in turn, created a serious shortage of affordable housing.
Key points
  • Nominalising = turning actions into nouns to condense, objectify and link (The rise in prices led to…).
  • Method: verb → noun, adverb → adjective, subject / object → of or possessive, because / so → verb of relation (lead to, result from, be due to).
  • Prepositions: a rise in, a decision to, the impact of X on Y, the failure to; precise summary nouns: this trend, these findings.
  • Balance: no chains of nouns; avoid false nouns such as augmentation, realisation, formation.
