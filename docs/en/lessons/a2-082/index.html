The weather

In Britain, talking about the weather is almost a national sport: it is the easiest way to start a conversation. In this lesson you learn to describe the weather (It’s sunny, It’s raining), talk about temperatures and seasons, understand a forecast and react naturally (Lovely day, isn’t it?).

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Describe the weather with “It’s + adjective” and “It’s + -ing verb”.
  • Tell the noun, the adjective and the verb apart: rain / rainy / it’s raining.
  • Talk about temperatures and seasons, and understand a weather forecast.
  • Use the weather to make small talk.

The subject “it”

For the weather, English always uses the subject “it”: “It’s cold.” “It’s raining.” “It was windy yesterday.”

To ask: “What’s the weather like?” or “What’s the weather like in Douala today?” Careful: here “like” doesn’t mean “enjoy”; it means “how is it / describe it”.

“weather” is uncountable: “nice weather”, “bad weather”, never “a nice weather”. Don’t confuse it with “time” (hours, minutes) or with “whether” (= if), which sounds the same.

  • What’s the weather like today? — It’s sunny and warm.
    The usual question and answer.
  • It was very cold in Montreal last week.
    Past: It was + adjective.
  • We had lovely weather on holiday.
    weather: no “a”.

Noun, adjective, verb

Many weather words have three forms: the noun (“rain”, “sun”, “wind”), the adjective with -y (“rainy”, “sunny”, “windy”) and sometimes a verb (“to rain”, “to snow”).

We say “It’s sunny / cloudy / windy / foggy” (adjective), but “It’s raining / snowing” (verb in the present continuous: now). “It rains a lot in Wales” = present simple for a habit.

Spelling: “sun” → “sunny”, “fog” → “foggy” (double consonant); “rain” → “rainy”, “cloud” → “cloudy”.

  • Take an umbrella, it’s raining.
    Now: It’s raining.
  • It’s very windy on the beach today.
    It’s + adjective.
  • It often snows in Quebec in January.
    Habit: present simple.

Temperatures and feelings

From coldest to hottest: “freezing”, “cold”, “cool”, “warm” (pleasantly hot), “hot”, “boiling” (informal: very hot).

Degrees: “It’s 25 degrees.” / “It’s minus five.” (−5). The UK and Canada use Celsius; the US uses Fahrenheit (25 °C ≈ 77 °F).

Careful: for a person, say “I’m cold” / “I’m hot”, not “I have cold”. And “humid” = hot and heavy, wet air; “wet” = covered in water, rainy.

  • It’s freezing this morning! It’s minus three.
    freezing = very, very cold.
  • It’s about thirty degrees in Yaoundé today.
    It’s + number + degrees.
  • Can you close the window? I’m cold.
    A person: I’m cold.

Seasons and forecasts

The seasons: “spring”, “summer”, “autumn” (US “fall”), “winter”; “in summer”, “in the winter”. In tropical countries: “the rainy season” and “the dry season”.

Forecasts (“the weather forecast”) use the future: “It’s going to rain this afternoon.” (you can see it coming, lesson 45); “Tomorrow will be sunny.”; “There will be showers in the north.” (short periods of rain).

To react: “I hope it’ll be sunny at the weekend.” “What’s the forecast for tomorrow?”

  • Look at those black clouds. It’s going to rain.
    going to: evidence now.
  • The forecast says it will be warm and sunny tomorrow.
    will in forecasts.
  • In Cameroon, the rainy season starts in March in the south.
    rainy season / dry season.

The weather for small talk

British people often start a conversation with the weather, using a question tag (lesson 69): “Lovely day, isn’t it?” “Cold today, isn’t it?” “Terrible weather, isn’t it?”

The expected answer is short and agrees: “Yes, beautiful!” / “Yes, it’s freezing!” / “I know, it hasn’t stopped raining all week.”

Useful expressions: “It’s pouring (down).” / “It’s raining cats and dogs.” (= it’s raining very hard; the second is a bit old-fashioned), “I’m soaked!” (= very wet), “What a lovely day!”

  • Lovely weather, isn’t it? — Yes, it’s beautiful!
    Small talk with a tag.
  • It’s pouring! I’m soaked.
    Heavy rain.
  • Cold today, isn’t it? — Yes, it’s freezing!
    Agreeing.

The formula

What’s the weather like? · It’s sunny / cloudy / windy / cold. · It’s raining / snowing. · It’s 20 degrees. · It’s going to rain. · Lovely day, isn’t it?

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “weather” /ˈweðə/ and “whether” /ˈweðə/ sound the same; the th is voiced /ð/, as in “the”.
  • “weather” ≠ “water” /ˈwɔːtə/: “WEH-thuh” vs “WAW-tuh”.
  • “cloudy” /ˈklaʊdi/: “KLOW-dee”, with the vowel of “now”.
  • “degrees” /dɪˈɡriːz/: stress on “GREEZ”, final s is /z/.
  • “autumn” /ˈɔːtəm/: the final n is silent.
  • “Lovely day, isn’t it?”: falling intonation ↘ on “isn’t it”: it’s not a real question; you expect agreement.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

At a bus stop in Edinburgh, Mrs Reid talks to Léa, a Belgian student who has just arrived.

Mrs Reid

Terrible weather, isn’t it?
Small talk with a question tag.

Léa

Yes, it’s freezing! Is it always like this in Scotland?
like this = the same as now.

Mrs Reid

Not always. It rains a lot, but we have some lovely days in summer.
Habit: present simple.

Léa

What’s the weather like in summer?
The key question.

Mrs Reid

It’s usually cool, about eighteen degrees. Where are you from?
cool = a bit cold.

Léa

I’m from Belgium. The weather is similar there, but it’s less windy.
Comparing.

Mrs Reid

Well, the forecast says it’s going to snow tonight.
A forecast.

Léa

Really? I hope it’ll be sunny at the weekend.
I hope + will.

Mrs Reid

Don’t count on it! Ah, here’s my bus. Nice talking to you.
Don’t count on it = it probably won’t happen.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Noun → adjective

Give the weather adjective.

  1. sun
  2. cloud
  3. wind
  4. fog
  5. storm
Show the answers
  1. sunny
  2. cloudy
  3. windy
  4. foggy
  5. stormy

2. Adjective or verb?

Choose the correct form.

  1. Take your umbrella. It’s (rainy / raining).
  2. It’s very (snowing / snowy) in the mountains in winter. It snows a lot.
  3. Look outside! It’s (snowing / snowy)!
  4. It’s (wind / windy) today. Hold your hat!
  5. It (rains / is raining) a lot in Ireland.
Show the answers
  1. raining
  2. snowy
  3. snowing
  4. windy
  5. rains

3. Complete

Complete with one word.

  1. What’s the weather ___ in Ottawa?
  2. It’s minus ten. It’s ___!
  3. Look at those clouds. It’s ___ to rain.
  4. Lovely day, ___ it?
  5. Have you seen the weather ___ for tomorrow?
Show the answers
  1. like
  2. freezing
  3. going
  4. isn’t
  5. forecast

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. It makes cold.
  2. I have hot.
  3. How is the weather like in Brussels?
  4. It’s a lovely weather.
  5. It’s pouring up.
  6. It will sunny tomorrow.
Show the answers
  1. It’s cold.
  2. I’m hot.
  3. What’s the weather like in Brussels?
  4. The weather is lovely. / It’s lovely weather.
  5. It’s pouring (down).
  6. It will be sunny tomorrow.

5. Your turn

In 5 or 6 sentences, describe today’s weather where you live, the usual weather in each season, and the forecast for tomorrow.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. Today in Douala it’s hot and humid. It’s about thirty-two degrees and it’s cloudy. It rains a lot here, especially in the rainy season, from March to November. In the dry season it’s sunny and very hot. The forecast says it’s going to rain tomorrow afternoon, so I’ll take my umbrella.
Key points
  • Always the subject “it”: It’s cold. It’s raining. What’s the weather like?
  • Adjective to describe (sunny, cloudy, windy, foggy); -ing verb for what is happening now (It’s raining / snowing).
  • “weather” is uncountable: nice weather (not “a nice weather”). A person: I’m cold.
  • Forecasts: It’s going to rain (evidence); It will be sunny tomorrow.
  • Small talk: Lovely day, isn’t it? — Yes, beautiful!
