Emphatic do / does / did

“I did lock the door!”, “I do like your dress”, “Do sit down”: in English, you can put do / does / did before an affirmative verb to insist, contradict or show contrast. Said with the right stress, this small word makes spoken English much more natural and expressive.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Form the emphatic affirmative: do / does / did + base form.
  • Use it to contradict, insist, show contrast or confirm an expectation.
  • Use the emphatic, polite imperative (Do sit down, Do be careful).
  • Know that with be, auxiliary have and modals, you stress the auxiliary instead of adding do.

Form

In an affirmative sentence in the present simple or past simple, add do, does or did before the base form, exactly as in a question or a negative, but without “not”.

The main verb loses its ending: “She likes” → “She does like”; “I went” → “I did go”. This is the most common mistake.

In speech, do / does / did is always stressed: the stress creates the emphasis. Without stress, the sentence sounds strange.

Neutral sentenceEmphatic sentenceEffect
I like your dress.I DO like your dress.I really like it
She speaks Lingala.She DOES speak Lingala.Yes, she really speaks it
We locked the door.We DID lock the door.I'm sure we locked it
Sit down.DO sit down.A warm invitation

Contradicting and correcting

The most common use: answering a negative statement or a doubt.

You can answer with the full sentence (“I did lock the door!”) or with a short answer with a stressed auxiliary (“I DID!”).

  • “You didn't lock the door.” – “I DID lock it! I checked twice.”
    Contradiction.
  • “You never listen to me.” – “I DO listen to you!”
    Contradiction.
  • “Ama doesn't eat meat.” – “She DOES eat meat. She just doesn't eat pork.”
    Correction.
  • “Did you really call him?” – “I DID!”
    Short answer.

Insisting and expressing strong feelings

Do / does / did strengthens a feeling, an opinion or a wish: “I do hope you can come”, “I do apologise for the delay” (a formal apology), “You do look tired.”

It also confirms that something expected really happened: “He said he'd come, and he did come.”

In argumentative writing, it highlights a concession: “This method does have some advantages, but…”.

  • I do hope the weather will be nice for the wedding.
    A strong wish.
  • We do apologise for any inconvenience.
    Formal apology (announcements, emails).
  • The plan did work in the end.
    It really worked.
  • Online learning does have advantages, but it can't replace a teacher.
    Concession in an essay.

Contrast: not… but do

Do / does / did highlights the positive part of a contrast: “I don't eat meat, but I do eat fish.”

Often we contrast what is not true with what is true, or what we don't know with what we do know: “I don't know her name, but I do know where she works.”

  • He doesn't speak Dutch, but he does understand it.
    Negative vs positive.
  • I didn't win, but I did enjoy the race.
    Past contrast.
  • We don't have much money, but we do have time.
    Money vs time.

The emphatic imperative

Before an imperative, “do” makes an invitation warmer or a request more urgent: “Do come in!”, “Do help yourself”, “Do be careful”. It sounds slightly formal or British, but very polite.

With “be”, the emphatic imperative also uses “do”: “Do be quiet!”, “Do be careful on the road.” This is the only case where “do” goes before “be”.

“Don't you dare!” (I forbid you to…) and “Do tell me!” (please tell me!) are common expressions.

  • Do come in and make yourself at home.
    Warm welcome.
  • Do help yourself to some more cake.
    Polite offer.
  • Do be careful, the roads are icy.
    Strong advice.

With be, have and modals: stress the auxiliary

When the sentence already contains “be”, an auxiliary (have / has, be + -ing) or a modal (can, will, must…), don't add “do”. Simply say the auxiliary in its full, stressed form, without contraction.

“I'm listening” → “I AM listening!”; “I've finished” → “I HAVE finished!”; “I can swim” → “I CAN swim!”

In informal messages, you can underline or use italics for the auxiliary; in careful writing, adverbs are often better (really, indeed, certainly).

SentenceEmphatic formNo “do” here
I'm listening.I AM listening!*I do am listening
She's finished.She HAS finished!*She does have finished
I can swim.I CAN swim!*I can do swim
He'll be there.He WILL be there!*He does will be there

The formula

DO / DOES / DID (stressed) + base form · Do + imperative · With be / have / modal: stressed auxiliary (I AM, I HAVE, I CAN)

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • The emphatic auxiliary is always in its full form and strongly stressed: do /duː/, does /dʌz/, did /dɪd/, with a higher, then falling voice.
  • The verb after it is less stressed: “I DID lock it” /aɪ ˈdɪd lɒk ɪt/.
  • With be and modals, no contraction: “I AM” /aɪ ˈæm/, “I CAN” /aɪ ˈkæn/ (strong form, although “can” is usually weak /kən/).
  • In “Do sit down”, the stress falls on “Do” and “sit”; the tone is warm.
  • Without stress, the emphatic sentence doesn't work: in speech, the intonation carries the meaning.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy

✗ I do am tired.✓ I AM tired.With be, stress “am”; no do.
✗ He do work hard.✓ He does work hard.Third person singular: does.
✗ I can do swim.✓ I CAN swim.With a modal, stress the modal.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Kigali, Eric and his sister Divine come home after a weekend away and find the house in a mess.

Divine

Eric! The kitchen window is open. You didn't close it before we left!
An accusation.

Eric

I did close it! I remember doing it.
Contradiction with did.

Divine

Then who opened it? And look, someone has eaten all the bananas.
Questions and a discovery.

Eric

I don't know who opened the window, but I do know who ate the bananas…
Contrast: don't know… but do know.

Divine

Who?
A short question.

Eric

Our cousin Patrick! He did say he would come and water the plants.
did say: he really said it.

Divine

Ah, that explains it. Well, he did water them. They look great.
Confirming an expectation.

Eric

You see? I am a responsible brother! Now do sit down, and I'll make some tea.
Stressed “am”; emphatic imperative.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Which use?

Say what do / does / did is used for: contradiction, contrast, polite invitation or strong feeling.

  1. “You didn't call me!” – “I did call you, twice!”
  2. I don't like sport, but I do enjoy watching football.
  3. Do come in and sit down.
  4. I do hope you'll be able to come.
Show the answers
  1. Contradiction.
  2. Contrast.
  3. Polite invitation.
  4. Strong feeling (hope).

2. Complete

Complete with do / does / did + the verb in brackets.

  1. “You don't love me.” – “I ___ (love) you!”
  2. She doesn't speak Dutch, but she ___ (understand) it.
  3. “Why didn't you tell me?” – “I ___ (tell) you! You weren't listening.”
  4. ___ (help) yourself to some cake.
  5. He ___ (work) hard, even if he's not very fast.
  6. I ___ (enjoy) the concert last night. It was amazing.
Show the answers
  1. do love
  2. does understand
  3. did tell
  4. Do help
  5. does work
  6. did enjoy

3. Contradict

Contradict each sentence with an emphatic form (watch out for be and modals).

  1. “You didn't lock the door.”
  2. “Ama doesn't like spicy food.”
  3. “You never help me.”
  4. “You aren't listening.”
  5. “You can't swim.”
Show the answers
  1. I did lock the door!
  2. She does like spicy food!
  3. I do help you!
  4. I AM listening! (no do with be)
  5. I CAN swim! (no do with a modal)

4. Correct the mistakes

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. She does likes chocolate.
  2. I did went to the meeting.
  3. I do am tired.
  4. Do sitting down, please.
Show the answers
  1. She does like chocolate.
  2. I did go to the meeting.
  3. I AM tired. (stress “am”)
  4. Do sit down, please.

5. Make it emphatic

Rewrite each sentence with do / does / did to add the emphasis shown in brackets.

  1. I closed the door. (contradicting someone)
  2. I like your dress. (insisting)
  3. He doesn't drink alcohol, but he drinks a lot of coffee. (contrast)
  4. Sit down, please. (warm invitation)
Show the answers
  1. I did close the door!
  2. I do like your dress.
  3. He doesn't drink alcohol, but he does drink a lot of coffee.
  4. Do sit down, please.

6. Your turn

Write a short exchange (4–6 lines) in which someone wrongly accuses you of forgetting something. Defend yourself with at least four different emphatic forms (do, does, did, stressed auxiliary, imperative).

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. A: You forgot to pay the electricity bill! B: I did pay it! I paid it online on Monday. A: Then why does the website say it's unpaid? B: I don't know, but I do have the receipt. Look, here it is. A: Oh… you're right. Sorry. B: No problem. I AM organised sometimes! Now do relax.
Key points
  • do / does / did (stressed) + base form: I do like it, she does speak it, we did lock it.
  • Uses: contradicting, insisting (I do hope), contrast (I don't…, but I do…), confirming (and he did come).
  • Warm or urgent imperative: Do sit down, Do help yourself, Do be careful.
  • With be, auxiliary have and modals: no do; stress the auxiliary (I AM, I HAVE, I CAN).
  • No ending after do / does / did: does like, did go.
