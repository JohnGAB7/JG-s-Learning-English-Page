Subject questions (Who called?)

In lesson 67, you learnt to make questions with did: Who did you call? But when the question word (who, what, which…) is the SUBJECT of the verb, we do not use do, does or did: Who called? This small difference changes the meaning of the question completely, and it is a classic difficulty for learners.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Tell the difference between a subject question (Who called you?) and an object question (Who did you call?).
  • Make subject questions with no auxiliary and the normal verb form (Who lives here? What happened?).
  • Use who, what, which and how many as subjects.
  • Answer subject questions correctly (Anna did. / My brother does.).

Subject or object?

Compare: Anna called Marc. If you don't know the person who called (the subject), you ask: Who called Marc? → Anna. If you don't know the person who received the call (the object), you ask: Who did Anna call? → Marc.

In a subject question, who simply takes the place of the subject: the word order is the same as in a statement. No change of order, no do / does / did.

Tip: ask yourself if the answer is the person who DOES the action (subject → no auxiliary) or the person the action happens TO (object → do / does / did).

SentenceSubject questionObject question
Anna called Marc.Who called Marc? → Anna.Who did Anna call? → Marc.
Tom loves Léa.Who loves Léa? → Tom.Who does Tom love? → Léa.
The dog ate my sandwich.What ate my sandwich? → The dog.What did the dog eat? → My sandwich.
  • Who called you last night?
    Someone called you: who = subject.
  • Who did you call last night?
    You called someone: who = object.

The form: Who / What + verb

In the present, the verb takes the -s of he / she / it, because who and what work like “he / she / it”: Who lives here? Who wants coffee? What makes you happy?

In the past, use the past simple form directly: Who broke the window? What happened? Who won the match?

With be and modal verbs, it's the same idea: Who is at the door? Who was absent? Who can help me? Who will come?

  • Who wants more rice?
    Present: wants with -s.
  • What happened at the station?
    Past: happened, not “did happen”.
  • Who broke my glasses?
    broke = past of break.
  • Who can drive?
    Modal + base form.

Which, whose, how many as subjects

Question words followed by a noun can also be subjects: Which team won? Whose phone is ringing? How many people came?

Compare: How many people came? (subject: people came) / How many people did you invite? (object: you invited people).

What + noun works in the same way: What music relaxes you? / What music do you listen to?

  • Which bus goes to the airport?
    Which bus = subject of goes.
  • Whose dog is barking?
    Whose dog = subject.
  • How many students passed the exam?
    Subject: no did.

Answering a subject question

The short answer uses the subject + the auxiliary for the tense: Who called? — Anna did. Who lives here? — My aunt does. Who is ready? — I am.

In speech, people often answer with just the name or an object pronoun: Who wants tea? — Me! (very common), or more carefully: I do.

Be careful: even if the answer is plural, the question stays singular: Who lives in that house? — The Mbarga family. / My cousins do.

  • Who made this cake? — My sister did.
    Answer: subject + did.
  • Who speaks Spanish here? — Carlos does.
    Answer: subject + does.
  • Who's hungry? — We are!
    Answer with be.

The most common traps

Trap 1: adding did from habit. “Who did call you?” is not the normal question (a stressed did is only possible for strong emphasis). The normal form is Who called you?

Trap 2: forgetting the -s in the present. Who knows the answer? (not “Who know”).

Trap 3: “what happen” without an ending. In the past: What happened? In the present, for something general: What happens when…?

  • What happened to your car?
    Not “What did happen”.
  • Who knows the answer?
    -s for he / she / it.

The formula

Subject question: Who / What / Which + noun / How many + noun + verb (no auxiliary) · Who called? · Object question: Who + did + subject + base form? · Who did you call?

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • In a subject question, the stress often falls on the verb and the main information: Who CALLED? What HAPPENED?
  • who /huː/: breathe out the h clearly; don't say “oo”.
  • happened /ˈhæpənd/: the -ed ending is /d/, not /ɪd/.
  • Who's = Who is or Who has: Who's there? /huːz/.
  • In the short answer, the auxiliary is stressed: ANna DID. My SIster DOES.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In a shared flat in Brussels, Yasmine comes home from work and finds the kitchen in a mess.

Yasmine

What happened here? Who used all the plates?
Two subject questions.

Lucas

Not me! Théo did. He cooked for his friends.
Short answer: Théo did.

Yasmine

How many people came?
How many people = subject.

Lucas

About ten. And who did he invite? Half of his football team!
Object question: who did he invite.

Yasmine

Great… And who's going to wash all this?
Subject question with be going to.

Lucas

Théo is. He promised.
Short answer with be.

Yasmine

Hmm. Who ate my yoghurt? It was in the fridge.
Subject question in the past.

Lucas

I don't know… Which yoghurt? The one with strawberries?
which + noun.

Yasmine

Yes! Lucas… what did you eat for breakfast?
Object question: what did you eat.

Lucas

Er… a strawberry yoghurt. Sorry!
The mystery is solved.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Subject or object?

Read the sentence, then ask two questions with who: one about the subject and one about the object.

  1. Paul helped Awa.
  2. My mother phoned the doctor.
  3. Sara loves Karim.
  4. The teacher saw Léo.
Show the answers
  1. Who helped Awa? (Paul) / Who did Paul help? (Awa)
  2. Who phoned the doctor? / Who did your mother phone?
  3. Who loves Karim? / Who does Sara love?
  4. Who saw Léo? / Who did the teacher see?

2. Subject questions

Ask the subject question for the part in CAPITALS.

  1. MY BROTHER lives in Toronto.
  2. SOMETHING STRANGE happened last night.
  3. CAMEROON won the match.
  4. TWENTY PEOPLE came to the meeting.
  5. AMÉLIE'S phone is ringing.
Show the answers
  1. Who lives in Toronto?
  2. What happened last night?
  3. Who won the match? / Which team won the match?
  4. How many people came to the meeting?
  5. Whose phone is ringing?

3. With or without did?

Choose the right question.

  1. You want to know the writer of this book. (Who wrote / Who did write) this book?
  2. You want to know the thing your friend bought. (What bought you / What did you buy)?
  3. You heard a noise. Something fell. (What fell / What did fall)?
  4. Marc met someone. You want to know the person. (Who met Marc / Who did Marc meet)?
Show the answers
  1. Who wrote this book?
  2. What did you buy?
  3. What fell?
  4. Who did Marc meet?

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Correct it.

  1. Who did take my pen?
  2. What did happen to you?
  3. Who want a coffee?
  4. Who you saw at the market?
  5. How many students did pass the test?
Show the answers
  1. Who took my pen?
  2. What happened to you?
  3. Who wants a coffee?
  4. Who did you see at the market?
  5. How many students passed the test?

5. What do you say?

Write one question for each situation.

  1. Someone told your friend a secret. You want to know the person.
  2. You see an accident in the street. You ask a person near you.
  3. Your friend saw someone yesterday. You want to know the person.
  4. You are making tea. You want to know the people who want some.
Show the answers
  1. Who told you that?
  2. What happened?
  3. Who did you see yesterday?
  4. Who wants (some) tea?

6. Your turn

Imagine a small mystery at work or at home (something disappeared or got broken). Write four subject questions (Who…? What…? How many…? Whose…?) and two object questions with did.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. Who opened the window? What happened to the plant? How many people came in this morning? Whose bag is this? What did the cleaner see? Who did the manager call?
Key points
  • Subject question: Who / What + verb, no do / does / did (Who called? What happened?).
  • Object question: Who / What + did + subject + base form (Who did you call?).
  • In the present: Who lives…? (singular verb with -s).
  • Which / Whose / How many + noun can be subjects: How many people came?
  • Short answers: Anna did. / My aunt does. / I am.
