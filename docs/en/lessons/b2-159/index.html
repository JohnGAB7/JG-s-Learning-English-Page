Wish + would / past perfect

“I wish I had come”, “If only he would stop complaining!”, “I wish I could swim”: “wish” and “if only” express regret, frustration and impossible wishes. At B2 you need all four structures (past, past perfect, would, could) and you must never confuse wish and hope.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Express regret about the past with wish / if only + past perfect.
  • Express annoyance, or a wish for someone else to change, with wish + would.
  • Choose between wish + past simple, past perfect, would and could according to the meaning.
  • Distinguish wish (unreal) from hope (possible), and know the other uses of “wish”.

Overview: one tense back

After “wish”, we describe a situation that is the opposite of reality. As in unreal conditionals, we move one tense back: the past simple for the present, the past perfect for the past.

The structure with “would” is different: it is not about an unreal state, but about a change of behaviour that we want to see in someone else or in a situation.

“If only” has the same structures, but it is stronger and more emotional: “If only I had listened to you!”

StructureTimeMeaningExample
wish + past simplepresentI want the situation to be differentI wish I had a bigger flat.
wish + past perfectpastI regret what happened (or didn't happen)I wish I had taken that job.
wish + wouldpresent / futureannoyance; I want someone to changeI wish you would stop interrupting me.
wish + couldpresent / futurean ability I don't haveI wish I could speak Lingala.

Wish + past perfect: regretting the past

To regret a past action, or something you didn't do, use “wish / if only + past perfect”.

Watch the reversed meaning: “I wish I had gone” = I didn't go and I regret it; “I wish I hadn't gone” = I went and I regret it.

“Wish” itself can be in the past: “She wished she had stayed at home.” The past perfect does not change.

  • I wish I had studied harder at school.
    I didn't study hard; I regret it.
  • I wish I hadn't eaten that third piece of cake.
    I ate it; I regret it.
  • If only we had left ten minutes earlier!
    Strong regret.
  • He wished he had told her the truth.
    Past regret reported in a story.

Wish + would: annoyance and wanting change

“Wish + subject + would + base form” says that we want a person (or a thing) to change their behaviour. The tone is often annoyed or impatient: “I wish you would listen to me!”

The subject after “wish” must be different from “I”: “I wish I would…” is impossible. For yourself, use “could” or the past simple: “I wish I could stop smoking.”

Do not use it with a state verb to describe a situation: “I wish my flat would be bigger” is wrong; say “I wish my flat were bigger”. But for an expected change (rain, a bus), “would” is natural: “I wish it would stop raining”, “I wish the bus would come”.

  • I wish my neighbours would turn their music down.
    Their behaviour annoys me.
  • I wish you wouldn't leave your clothes on the floor.
    Negative: wouldn't = please stop doing it.
  • I wish it would stop raining.
    A change we are waiting for.
  • If only he would call me back!
    Strong frustration.

Wish + past simple and wish + could: the unreal present

“Wish + past simple” describes a present state that we want to be different: “I wish I knew the answer”, “I wish it weren't so cold”. With “be”, you can use “were” for all persons (careful style) or “was” (everyday).

“Wish + could” expresses an ability or a possibility we don't have: “I wish I could come to your wedding, but I'll be in Ottawa.” It is also the form to use when the subject is “I” and you are thinking about the near future.

You saw these forms at B1 (lesson 98); now you must contrast them clearly with “would” and the past perfect.

  • I wish I lived closer to the office.
    I live far from it.
  • I wish I were (was) on a beach right now.
    I'm not on a beach.
  • I wish I could help you, but I'm really busy.
    I can't help.
  • I wish I didn't have to work this weekend.
    I have to work.

Wish or hope? And other uses of “wish”

Use “hope” for something possible or realistic, often in the future, with the present simple or “will”: “I hope you pass your exam.” Use “wish” for unreal situations. “I wish you will pass” is wrong.

“Wish” has other patterns too: wish + person + noun for good wishes (“We wish you a merry Christmas”, “I wish you good luck”); wish + to-infinitive, very formal, meaning “want” (“I wish to speak to the manager”).

Remember: if it can still happen, it's usually “hope”; if it is contrary to reality, it's “wish”.

SentenceMeaning
I hope it's sunny tomorrow.It's possible: I hope so.
I wish it were sunny today.It isn't sunny: present regret.
I wish you a safe journey.A good wish.
I wish to make a complaint.Formal: I want to make a complaint.

The formula

wish / if only + past simple (present) · + past perfect (past) · + would (change of behaviour) · + could (ability)

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • Linking: “wish I” is said as one block, /wɪʃ aɪ/ → /ˈwɪʃaɪ/. “I wish I'd known”: /aɪ ˈwɪʃaɪd ˈnəʊn/.
  • “I'd” = I had in “I wish I'd gone” (followed by a past participle); = I would elsewhere.
  • “would” is weak, /wəd/: “I wish you'd stop” /aɪ wɪʃ juːd ˈstɒp/.
  • “If only”: strong stress on “ON-”, often with a fall-rise intonation that shows emotion: “If ONly I'd known ↘↗”.
  • When annoyed, speakers often stress “would”: “I wish you WOULD listen!”

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ I wish I didn't go to the party.✓ I wish I hadn't gone to the party.Past regret: past perfect.
✗ I wish I would have more time.✓ I wish I had more time.Present situation: past simple; never “I wish I would”.
✗ I wish my flat would be bigger.✓ I wish my flat were (was) bigger.With a state, no “would”.
✗ I wish you will pass your exam.✓ I hope you pass (will pass) your exam.For a possible future: hope.
✗ I wish I would speak Chinese.✓ I wish I could speak Chinese.Ability: could.
✗ I wish I have studied harder.✓ I wish I had studied harder.After wish, move one tense back.
✗ I wish I would have known.✓ I wish I had known.“would have” after wish is not standard in writing.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Liège, two flatmates, Maxime and Yasmina, on a Sunday evening before an exam.

Yasmina

Can you hear that? I wish the neighbours would turn their music down.
wish + would: annoyance.

Maxime

Every Sunday! I wish we lived somewhere quieter.
wish + past simple: present situation.

Yasmina

And I wish I hadn't left all my revision until tonight. The exam is at nine.
wish + past perfect: regret.

Maxime

If only you had listened to me last week!
If only: stronger regret.

Yasmina

I know, I know. I wish you wouldn't remind me.
wish + wouldn't: please stop.

Maxime

Sorry. I wish I could help, but I don't know anything about statistics.
wish + could: ability.

Yasmina

It's fine. Just make me a coffee, please.
A simple request.

Maxime

Coming up. I hope you get a good mark tomorrow!
hope: a real possibility.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. What does it mean?

Explain the real situation behind each sentence.

  1. I wish I had a car.
  2. I wish I had bought that car.
  3. I wish you would stop smoking.
  4. I wish I could drive.
Show the answers
  1. I don't have a car now (present).
  2. I didn't buy it and I regret it (past).
  3. You smoke, it annoys me, and I want you to change.
  4. I can't drive (ability).

2. Complete

Put the verb in brackets into the correct form.

  1. I'm so tired. I wish I ___ (go) to bed earlier last night.
  2. It's freezing here. I wish we ___ (live) somewhere warmer.
  3. I wish my neighbours ___ (stop) making so much noise.
  4. I wish I ___ (can) play the guitar like you.
  5. If only I ___ (not / say) that to her!
  6. I wish it ___ (be) Friday today.
Show the answers
  1. had gone
  2. lived
  3. would stop
  4. could
  5. hadn't said
  6. were (or was)

3. Rewrite with “wish” or “if only”

Express the same idea with “I wish” (or “If only” when indicated).

  1. I'm sorry I didn't call you on your birthday.
  2. My brother always borrows my clothes without asking. It's annoying.
  3. I can't speak Lingala.
  4. I don't have enough money for the trip.
  5. We missed the last train. (If only)
Show the answers
  1. I wish I had called you on your birthday.
  2. I wish my brother wouldn't borrow my clothes without asking.
  3. I wish I could speak Lingala.
  4. I wish I had enough money for the trip.
  5. If only we hadn't missed the last train!

4. Correct the mistakes

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. I wish I didn't eat so much last night.
  2. I wish I would have more free time.
  3. I wish you will have a great trip.
  4. I wish I would speak Chinese.
Show the answers
  1. I wish I hadn't eaten so much last night.
  2. I wish I had more free time.
  3. I hope you have (will have) a great trip. (or: I wish you a great trip.)
  4. I wish I could speak Chinese.

5. Wish or hope?

Complete with the correct form of wish or hope.

  1. I ___ you pass your exam tomorrow.
  2. I ___ I had more time to read.
  3. We ___ the weather will be nice for the wedding.
  4. She ___ she hadn't sold her old car.
Show the answers
  1. hope
  2. wish
  3. hope
  4. wishes (or wished)

6. Your turn

Write 4 true sentences: one about your present situation (wish + past simple), one regret (wish + past perfect), one annoyance (wish + would) and one ability (wish + could).

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. I wish I had a garden, because my flat is very small. I wish I had started learning English when I was younger. I wish my colleagues wouldn't book meetings at lunchtime. I wish I could take a long holiday this year.
Key points
  • wish / if only + past simple = unreal present (I wish I knew).
  • wish / if only + past perfect = past regret (I wish I had known / hadn't said).
  • wish + would = you want another person or a situation to change; never “I wish I would”, not with a state.
  • wish + could = a missing ability (I wish I could swim).
  • Possible future: hope (I hope you pass), not wish.
