The imperative (instructions)

We use the imperative to give instructions, orders, advice or invitations: to follow a recipe, give directions or welcome a guest. In English it is very simple: the base verb, with no subject.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Make the positive imperative (Open the door) and the negative imperative (Don't open the door).
  • Use the imperative for instructions, advice, warnings and invitations.
  • Make an order softer with please, and know when a polite question is better.
  • Make a suggestion with Let's (Let's go!).

The form

The positive imperative is just the base verb, with no subject: “Open the window.” The form is the same for one person or many people.

The negative imperative is “Don't” + base verb: “Don't open the window.” In very formal writing, or to make it stronger, you see “Do not”.

With an adjective, use “be”: “Be careful!”, “Don't be late!”.

PositiveverbSit down.an instruction
NegativeDon't + verbDon't sit there.say not to do something
With an adjectiveBe + adjectiveBe quiet.about behaviour
Negative + adjectiveDon't be + adjectiveDon't be shy.about behaviour
SuggestionLet's + verbLet's go.for you and me
  • Close the door, please.
    Positive + please.
  • Don't forget your keys!
    Negative.

Uses

Instructions: recipes, exercise instructions, user guides. Order the steps with “first”, “then”, “next”, “finally”.

Directions: “Go straight on. Turn left at the bank.” (see lesson 37).

Advice and warnings: “Drink a lot of water.”, “Be careful!”, “Watch out!” (there's a danger), “Don't worry.”

Invitations and welcomes: “Come in!”, “Sit down.”, “Help yourself.” (take what you want), “Make yourself at home.” Here the imperative is warm and friendly, not bossy.

  • First, cut the onions. Then add the tomatoes.
    A recipe.
  • Watch out! The floor is wet.
    A warning.
  • Come in and sit down. Help yourself to some juice.
    A welcome.
  • Don't worry, it's not a problem.
    Advice.

Being polite

An imperative alone can sound rude, especially with a stranger or your boss. Add “please” at the start or at the end: “Please wait here.” / “Wait here, please.”

For a really polite request, English speakers often prefer a question: “Can you close the door?” (lesson 28), “Could you help me?” (lesson 57).

In speech, “Do” + imperative makes an invitation warmer: “Do sit down!”.

DirectMore politeVery polite
Close the door.Close the door, please.Could you close the door, please?
Wait here.Please wait here.Could you wait here, please?

Let's: making a suggestion

“Let's” (= let us) + base verb suggests doing something together: “Let's go to the beach!”.

Negative: “Let's not…”: “Let's not take the car.” To answer: “Yes, let's!”, “Good idea!”, “OK.” or “Sorry, I can't.”

Don't confuse it with “Let me…” (I will do it for you): “Let me help you.”

  • It's sunny. Let's have lunch outside! — Yes, let's!
    Suggestion and answer.
  • Let's not watch TV tonight. Let's play a game.
    Negative suggestion.
  • Let me carry your bag.
    An offer: I will carry it.

Signs and written instructions

On signs, you see the imperative (“Keep left”, “Push”, “Pull”, “Mind the gap”) and also “No + -ing” for things that are not allowed: “No smoking”, “No parking”.

Careful: “No smoking” is a sign. In a sentence, say “Don't smoke here”, never “Don't smoking”.

“Always” and “never” go before the imperative: “Always lock the door.”, “Never give your password to anyone.”

  • Never leave your bag alone at the airport.
    never + imperative.
  • Please don't park in front of the garage.
    A polite negative instruction.

The formula

Base verb (Open…) · Don't + verb (Don't open…) · Be / Don't be + adjective · Let's + verb

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • An order goes down at the end: “Sit DOWN. ↘” A warm invitation goes up a little and is longer: “Come IN!”.
  • “Don't” is /dəʊnt/; in fast speech the t often disappears before a consonant: “Don't worry” ≈ /dəʊn ˈwʌri/.
  • “Let's” /lets/ is one syllable; “Let's go” = /lets ˈɡəʊ/.
  • “Please” /pliːz/ ends with a /z/ sound, not /s/.
  • Stress the verb and the important word: “DON'T TOUCH it!”, “CLOSE the DOOR”.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

On a video call from Douala, Léa's grandmother teaches her to make fried plantain. Léa is in Paris.

Grandma

OK, Léa. First, take two ripe plantains. Yellow and black, not green!
ripe = ready to eat.

Léa

Got them. What next?
“Got them” = I have them.

Grandma

Peel them and cut them into small pieces. Be careful with the knife!
Two instructions and a warning.

Léa

Done. Do I add salt now?
“Done” = I finished.

Grandma

Yes, add a little salt. Then heat some oil in a pan.
then = the next step.

Léa

The oil is hot. Can I put the plantain in?
Asking permission.

Grandma

Yes, but don't put all the pieces in at the same time. And don't touch the oil!
Negative imperatives.

Léa

Mmm, they smell delicious!
smell = have a smell.

Grandma

Turn them after three minutes. Finally, put them on some paper. Enjoy!
finally = the last step.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Make it negative

Write the negative imperative.

  1. Open the window.
  2. Be late.
  3. Sit here.
  4. Let's take the bus.
  5. Touch the screen.
Show the answers
  1. Don't open the window.
  2. Don't be late.
  3. Don't sit here.
  4. Let's not take the bus.
  5. Don't touch the screen.

2. Complete

Complete with Be, Wash, Turn, Don't, Come or Do.

  1. ___ quiet! The baby is sleeping.
  2. ___ your hands before dinner.
  3. ___ left at the bank.
  4. ___ touch the plate. It's hot!
  5. ___ in and sit down.
  6. ___ your homework before you watch TV.
Show the answers
  1. Be
  2. Wash
  3. Turn
  4. Don't
  5. Come
  6. Do

3. Make a suggestion with Let's

Answer the situation with a suggestion that starts with Let's.

  1. We're hungry.
  2. It's very hot today.
  3. The film starts in ten minutes.
  4. We're tired.
Show the answers
  1. Let's eat something. / Let's have lunch.
  2. Let's go to the beach. / Let's go swimming.
  3. Let's go now. / Let's hurry.
  4. Let's have a break. / Let's go home.

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. No worry!
  2. You sit down, please.
  3. Not be late!
  4. Let's going to the cinema tonight!
  5. Be care!
Show the answers
  1. Don't worry!
  2. Sit down, please. / Please sit down.
  3. Don't be late!
  4. Let's go to the cinema tonight! (US: Let's go to the movies tonight!)
  5. Be careful! / Watch out!

5. Your turn

Write a recipe for a simple dish you know in five steps with the imperative. Use first, then, next and finally, and at least one negative sentence.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. First, boil some water in a big pan. Then add a little salt and put in the pasta. Next, cook it for ten minutes. Don't forget to stir it! Finally, add some tomato sauce and some cheese.
Key points
  • Imperative = base verb, no subject, no s: Open the door. The same for one or many people.
  • Negative: Don't + verb (Don't touch!); with an adjective: Be careful! Don't be late!
  • Add “please”; to be very polite, use a question: Could you…?
  • Let's + verb to suggest (Let's go!); Let's not…; Let me… = I will do it.
  • Signs: No smoking; in a sentence: Don't smoke.
