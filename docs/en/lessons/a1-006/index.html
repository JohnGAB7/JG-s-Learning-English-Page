The present simple

Use the present simple for habits, general facts and permanent situations: what you do every day, where you live, what you like. It is the tense you use most to talk about yourself.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Use a verb in the present simple, with the -s for he / she / it and its spelling rules.
  • Make negatives and questions with do / does, and give short answers.
  • Know when to use the present simple: habits, facts, permanent situations, likes and opinions.
  • Say the final -s correctly: /s/, /z/ or /ɪz/.

Positive sentences: remember the -s

In the present simple, the verb keeps its base form (the dictionary form, without “to”) with every subject except one: with he, she, it (and any singular subject like “my sister” or “the shop”), add -s.

This -s is the rule learners forget most. Remember: he / she / it → -s. “Have”, “do” and “go” are irregular with he / she / it: has, does, goes.

  • I work in a bank.
    I → base form.
  • My sister lives in Québec.
    Singular subject → -s.
  • The shop opens at nine.
    “The shop” = it → -s.

Spelling for he / she / it

Most verbs just add -s. But some endings change the spelling, like plural nouns (lesson 12).

Verb endingRuleExamples
most verbs+ swork → works, read → reads, play → plays
-s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x, -z+ esmiss → misses, wash → washes, watch → watches, fix → fixes
-o+ esgo → goes, do → does
consonant + yy → iesstudy → studies, try → tries, carry → carries
vowel + y+ splay → plays, buy → buys, say → says
haveirregularhave → has
“says” sounds like /sez/ and “does” like /dʌz/: the vowel changes when you speak.

Negatives and questions with do / does

“To be” and “have got” don't need help, but a normal verb needs an auxiliary in negatives and questions: do (or does with he / she / it). The main verb goes back to its base form, without -s: “does” already shows he / she / it.

Negative: subject + don't / doesn't + base verb. Question: Do / Does + subject + base verb? Short answer: Yes, I do. / No, she doesn't. (Don't repeat the main verb.)

I / you / we / theyhe / she / it
NegativeI don't (do not) eat meat.She doesn't (does not) eat meat.
QuestionDo you eat meat?Does she eat meat?
Short answer (yes)Yes, I do.Yes, she does.
Short answer (no)No, I don't.No, she doesn't.
Wh- questionWhere do you work?What does he do?
  • He doesn't like coffee.
    doesn't + like, no -s.
  • Do your children speak English? — Yes, they do.
    Question and short answer.
  • What time does the train leave?
    Wh- word + does + subject + base verb.
“Do” can be an auxiliary and a main verb in the same sentence: What do you do? (= What is your job?), She doesn't do her homework.

When to use the present simple

1. Habits and routines, often with a time or frequency word: every day, every week, on Mondays, at the weekend, always, usually, often, sometimes, never (adverbs of frequency: lesson 7).

2. Facts and general truths: things that are always true.

3. Permanent or long situations: where you live, where you work.

4. Likes, opinions and states: like, love, hate, want, know, understand, think (= have an opinion), believe. Use these state verbs in the present simple even for now (lesson 9).

5. Timetables (trains, classes, films): The film starts at eight.

  • I get up at six every day.
    Habit.
  • Water boils at 100 degrees.
    General truth.
  • They live in Douala.
    Permanent situation.
  • I don't understand this word.
    State verb: no -ing form.
  • The bus leaves at 7.15.
    Timetable.

Present simple or not?

Some languages use one present tense for “I work” (habit) and “I am working” (now). English has two: the present simple for habits and permanent things (I work in Brussels), and the present continuous for what is happening now (I'm working now, lesson 8). Saying “I work” about this moment is a common mistake.

Also, English doesn't use the present simple for a period that started in the past and continues now: don't say “I live here since three years”. You will learn the right form at A2 / B1. For now, just remember: “since” and “for” + a period don't go with the present simple.

The formula

I / you / we / they + verb · he / she / it + verb-s · don't / doesn't + verb · Do / Does + subject + verb?

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • The final -s sounds like /s/ after p, t, k, f sounds: works /wɜːks/, stops, laughs.
  • It sounds like /z/ after a vowel or a voiced sound: lives /lɪvz/, plays /pleɪz/, reads /riːdz/.
  • It sounds like /ɪz/ (one extra syllable) after s, z, sh, ch, j, x sounds: watches /ˈwɒtʃɪz/, misses, washes, changes.
  • “does” /dʌz/, “says” /sez/ and “goes” /ɡəʊz/ have special vowels: don't say them the way they are written.
  • In questions, “do” and “does” are weak: Do you…? /də juː/, Does she…? /dəz ʃiː/. They are strong in short answers: Yes, I DO /duː/.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

A journalist from a radio station in Yaoundé interviews a baker about her working day.

Journalist

Good morning, Mrs Ngo. What time do you start work?
Wh- question with do.

Mrs Ngo

I start at four in the morning. I make the bread with my son.
A daily routine.

Journalist

Does your son work here every day?
Does + subject + base verb.

Mrs Ngo

No, he doesn't. He studies at university, so he only comes at the weekend.
studies, comes: -s with he.

Journalist

And when does the shop open?
A timetable question.

Mrs Ngo

It opens at six and closes at seven in the evening.
opens, closes: -s with it.

Journalist

Do you like your job?
A question about likes.

Mrs Ngo

Yes, I do! I love the smell of fresh bread, and my customers know me well.
Short answer; state verbs love and know.

Key points
  • He / she / it + verb-s (works, watches, studies, has, goes, does).
  • Negative: don't / doesn't + base verb. Question: Do / Does + subject + base verb? The -s moves to does.
  • Short answer: Yes, I do. / No, she doesn't.
  • Uses: habits, general truths, permanent situations, likes and states, timetables.
  • For an action happening now: present continuous (lesson 8), not present simple.
