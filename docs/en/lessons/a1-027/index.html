The home and its rooms

We describe our home when we introduce ourselves, look for a flat, invite someone or explain where something is. This lesson gives you rooms, furniture and sentences to describe a home, with the traps of floors and “at home”.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Name types of home, rooms and common furniture.
  • Describe your home with there is / there are, have got and adjectives.
  • Use floors correctly (ground floor, first floor) and upstairs / downstairs.
  • Use “at home” and “go home” correctly.

Homes and floors

A home in a building is “a flat” in British English and “an apartment” in American and Canadian English. “A house” is a building for one family; “a studio” (UK also “studio flat”) is a flat with one main room.

Floors: in British English, the floor at street level is “the ground floor” and the floor above it is “the first floor”. In American English, the floor at street level is “the first floor”! Use the preposition “on”: “on the second floor”.

“Upstairs” = on a higher floor; “downstairs” = on a lower floor. Use them with no preposition: “The bedrooms are upstairs.”

Rooms

Many room names are compound words: bed + room = bedroom, bath + room = bathroom. Say “in the kitchen”, “in the living room”.

“Toilet” is the room with a toilet at home (UK informal: “the loo”). In a public place, say “the toilets” or “the ladies / the gents” (UK) and “the restroom” (US). “Bathroom” is the room with a bath or shower; Americans also use it for the toilet.

Careful: “a cave” is a big hole in a mountain or under the ground. The room under a house for wine or old things is “the cellar” (or “the basement”). For the room where you sleep, say “bedroom”, not “chamber”.

Furniture and equipment

“Furniture” is uncountable: “There's a lot of furniture.” For one thing, say “a piece of furniture”.

“A cupboard” has doors and shelves, often in the kitchen; “a wardrobe” is a tall cupboard for clothes (US: “closet”).

Describing your home

To describe a home, use tools you know: “there is / there are” (lesson 16), prepositions of place (lesson 17) and “have got” (lesson 5).

Useful adjectives: big / small, bright (with a lot of light), dark, quiet, noisy, modern, old, comfortable, clean, tidy (everything in its place), messy.

For renting: “rent” (the money you pay every month; to pay to live somewhere), “a landlord / a landlady” (the owner), “a neighbour” (US neighbor).

  • There are three bedrooms upstairs and there's a kitchen downstairs.
    there are + plural; there's + singular.
  • My flat is small but very bright.
    be + adjectives.
  • The rent is 750 euros a month.
    a month = every month.

“At home”, “go home”

“At home” = in your house or flat: “I'm at home.” Don't say “at the house” for your own home.

With a verb of movement, use “home” with no preposition: “go home”, “come home”, “get home” (arrive at home). Never “go to home”.

For someone else's home: “at Paul's” / “to Paul's”, or “at Paul's house / flat”.

  • Are you at home tonight?
    at home = in your home.
  • I go home at six o'clock.
    No “to” before home.
  • The party is at Julie's flat.
    Someone else's home.

The formula

There is / There are + room / furniture + place · on the first floor · upstairs / downstairs · at home, go home

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “cupboard” is /ˈkʌbəd/: don't say the p (“kub-ed”).
  • “comfortable” has three syllables: /ˈkʌmftəbl/ (“kumf-te-bl”), with the stress at the start.
  • “furniture” /ˈfɜːnɪtʃə/: stress on the first syllable, “ch” at the end.
  • In compound words, stress the first word: BEDroom, BATHroom, LIVing room.
  • “kitchen” /ˈkɪtʃɪn/: two short i sounds, with “ch” in the middle.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ I go to home.✓ I go home.No preposition between a verb of movement and “home”.
✗ I'm at the house tonight.✓ I'm at home tonight.Your own home = at home.
✗ There are many furnitures.✓ There's a lot of furniture.“Furniture” is uncountable.
✗ My flat is at the first floor.✓ My flat is on the first floor.Use “on” with floors.
✗ I sleep in my chamber.✓ I sleep in my bedroom.The room for sleeping = a bedroom.
✗ We keep the wine in the cave.✓ We keep the wine in the cellar.A cave is a hole in a mountain.
✗ There is two bedrooms.✓ There are two bedrooms.“There are” before a plural.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Brussels, Yasmine visits a flat to rent with the landlord, Mr Peeters.

Mr Peeters

Welcome! The flat is on the third floor. There's a lift.
on + floor.

Yasmine

Great. How many bedrooms are there?
Question with “are there”.

Mr Peeters

There are two bedrooms, and a bathroom with a shower.
There are + plural.

Yasmine

Oh, the living room is very bright! Is the furniture included?
furniture = singular verb “is”.

Mr Peeters

Yes: the sofa, the table, the beds and the wardrobes.
A list of furniture.

Yasmine

And the kitchen? Is there a fridge?
Question with “is there”.

Mr Peeters

Yes, there's a fridge and a new cooker. And there's a small balcony.
cooker = machine for cooking.

Yasmine

It's perfect. What's the rent?
rent = money every month.

Mr Peeters

Nine hundred euros a month.
a month = every month.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Which room?

In which room do you do these things?

  1. You sleep here.
  2. You cook here.
  3. You have a shower here.
  4. You watch TV with your family here.
  5. You park the car here.
  6. You grow flowers here.
Show the answers
  1. the bedroom
  2. the kitchen
  3. the bathroom
  4. the living room
  5. the garage
  6. the garden

2. Where is it?

In which room do you usually find this?

  1. a bed
  2. a fridge
  3. a sofa
  4. a shower
Show the answers
  1. the bedroom
  2. the kitchen
  3. the living room
  4. the bathroom

3. There is or there are?

Complete with There's or There are.

  1. ___ three bedrooms in the house.
  2. ___ a big kitchen.
  3. ___ a lot of furniture in the living room.
  4. ___ two bathrooms upstairs.
  5. ___ a small garden behind the house.
Show the answers
  1. There are
  2. There's
  3. There's
  4. There are
  5. There's

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. I'm at the home.
  2. My flat is in the ground floor.
  3. There are a lot of furnitures.
  4. I go to home at 6 p.m.
  5. The bedrooms are at upstairs.
Show the answers
  1. I'm at home.
  2. My flat is on the ground floor. (US: My apartment is on the first floor.)
  3. There's a lot of furniture.
  4. I go home at 6 p.m.
  5. The bedrooms are upstairs.

5. Your turn

Describe your home in four or five sentences: the type of home, the floor, the rooms, your favourite room and what is in it.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. I live in a flat in Yaoundé. It's on the second floor. There are two bedrooms, a living room, a kitchen and a bathroom. My favourite room is the living room: it's bright and there's a big comfortable sofa. There's also a small balcony with plants.
Key points
  • flat (UK) / apartment (US); ground floor (UK) = street level; first floor (UK) = the floor above; on the first floor.
  • Rooms: living room, kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, dining room, hall, garden.
  • “Furniture” is uncountable: a lot of furniture, a piece of furniture.
  • at home (in your home); go home, come home (no “to”).
  • To describe: There's a…, There are…, upstairs / downstairs, bright, quiet, comfortable.
