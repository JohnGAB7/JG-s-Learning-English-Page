Reflexive pronouns (myself…)

Myself, yourself, themselves… show that the subject does something to itself, or they add emphasis. English uses them much less than many other languages: “I get up, I remember, they meet” have no reflexive pronoun. This lesson shows when to use them… and when not to.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Know the eight reflexive pronouns and their spelling (-self in the singular, -selves in the plural).
  • Use them when the subject and the object are the same person (hurt yourself, enjoy yourselves).
  • Use myself for emphasis (I made it myself) and by myself for “alone”.
  • Avoid reflexives with verbs like get up, relax, remember, meet, and tell themselves and each other apart.

The forms

In the singular, add -self; in the plural, -selves. Be careful: “yourself” is for one person, “yourselves” for more than one.

Most forms use the possessive adjective (my, your, our), but him, it and them use the object pronoun: himself, itself, themselves. “Hisself” and “theirselves” are not standard English.

SubjectReflexiveExample

you (one person)yourselfHelp yourself!
hehimselfHe hurt himself.
sheherselfShe taught herself Spanish.
ititselfThe door closes by itself.
weourselvesWe enjoyed ourselves.
you (more than one)yourselvesLook after yourselves!
theythemselvesThey introduced themselves.

Use 1: the subject does the action to itself

Use a reflexive pronoun when the subject and the object are the same person: She looked at herself in the mirror. Without the reflexive, the meaning changes: She looked at her (another woman).

Very common expressions: hurt yourself, cut yourself, burn yourself, introduce yourself, enjoy yourself (have a good time), help yourself (take food or drink freely), look after yourself, teach yourself, behave yourself.

“Enjoy” needs an object: I enjoyed the party or I enjoyed myself, but never “I enjoyed” alone.

  • Be careful! Don't burn yourself.
    Subject = object.
  • Let me introduce myself: I'm Dr Ekotto.
    A formal introduction.
  • Did you enjoy yourselves at the wedding?
    enjoy + reflexive (plural).
  • Please help yourself to some cake.
    Take some cake freely.

Use 2: emphasis and by myself (alone)

A reflexive pronoun can also add emphasis: “I did it, not another person”. Put it after the object or just after the subject: I painted the room myself. The manager himself called me.

“By + reflexive” means “alone, without help or company”: She lives by herself. I did it by myself. Synonyms: on my own, alone.

  • Did you make this cake yourself? — Yes, I did!
    Emphasis: you, not a shop.
  • My grandmother still lives by herself.
    Alone.
  • The children went to school by themselves.
    Without an adult.

The big trap: no reflexive

Many verbs that are reflexive in other languages are NOT reflexive in English. Adding “myself” is a very common mistake.

For daily actions (wash, shave, dress), we normally add nothing: I wash and get dressed. The reflexive is possible only to show that it is difficult: The baby can dress himself now.

  • I usually get up at six and have a shower.
    No reflexive.
  • Relax! Don't worry.
    No reflexive.
  • I don't remember his name.
    No reflexive.

Themselves or each other?

“Themselves” means each person does the action to himself or herself. “Each other” (or “one another”) means the people do the action to the other person: it is reciprocal.

Compare: They're looking at themselves (each one in the mirror) / They're looking at each other (one looks at the other). We love each other.

  • Anna and Marc met in 2019. They call each other every day.
    Anna calls Marc and Marc calls Anna.
  • The players were proud of themselves.
    Each player was proud of his or her own work.

The formula

myself · yourself · himself · herself · itself · ourselves · yourselves · themselves · by myself = alone · each other = one another

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • Stress -SELF / -SELVES: mySELF /maɪˈself/, themSELVES /ðəmˈselvz/.
  • “-selves” ends in /lvz/: say the v clearly.
  • “ourselves” /aʊəˈselvz/ or /ɑːˈselvz/; “yourself” /jɔːˈself/.
  • For emphasis (I did it myself), the reflexive pronoun is strongly stressed at the end of the sentence.
  • “each other” joins: /iːtʃ ˈʌðə/, with the /ð/ sound of “the”.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ I get up myself at seven.✓ I get up at seven.“Get up” is not reflexive.
✗ I don't remember myself.✓ I don't remember.“Remember” takes no reflexive.
✗ Relax yourself!✓ Relax!“Relax” takes no reflexive.
✗ We enjoyed a lot.✓ We enjoyed ourselves a lot. / We had a great time.Enjoy needs an object.
✗ He hurt hisself.✓ He hurt himself.The correct form is himself.
✗ They love themselves. (meaning: one loves the other)✓ They love each other.Reciprocal meaning: each other.
✗ She lives by her own.✓ She lives by herself. / She lives on her own.By + reflexive, or on + possessive + own.
✗ You can help yourselves. (to one person)✓ You can help yourself.One person: yourself; more than one: yourselves.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

At a birthday party in a garden in Lyon, the host welcomes his guests.

Julien

Welcome, everyone! Help yourselves to drinks and food.
yourselves: many guests.

Amélie

Thanks, Julien. Wow, did you make all this yourself?
yourself: emphasis.

Julien

Most of it. My sister made the cake. She taught herself to bake last year.
taught herself = learnt alone.

Amélie

Impressive! Oh, I don't know these people. Who are they?
A question.

Julien

Colleagues of mine. Go and introduce yourself, they're very nice.
introduce yourself.

Amélie

OK. Careful with the barbecue, by the way. Don't burn yourself!
burn yourself.

Julien

Don't worry. Just relax and enjoy yourself!
No reflexive with worry and relax; enjoy yourself.

Amélie

I will. Look, the kids are playing by themselves in the garden. So cute!
by themselves = alone.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. The right pronoun

Complete with the reflexive pronoun.

  1. I cut ___ with a knife.
  2. She bought ___ a new dress.
  3. We really enjoyed ___ in Kribi.
  4. Children, look after ___!
  5. He lives by ___.
  6. The light turns off by ___.
Show the answers
  1. myself
  2. herself
  3. ourselves
  4. yourselves
  5. himself
  6. itself

2. Reflexive or nothing?

Complete with a reflexive pronoun or Ø (nothing).

  1. I get up ___ at seven every day.
  2. Be careful! Don't hurt ___.
  3. Sit down ___ and relax ___.
  4. Do you remember ___ her name?
  5. Let me introduce ___.
Show the answers
  1. Ø
  2. yourself
  3. Ø … Ø
  4. Ø
  5. myself

3. Themselves or each other?

Choose the right form.

  1. Marie and Paul write to (themselves / each other) every week.
  2. The children dressed (themselves / each other) without help.
  3. My brother and I don't see (ourselves / each other) very often.
  4. The team was proud of (itself / each other).
Show the answers
  1. each other
  2. themselves
  3. each other
  4. itself

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Correct it.

  1. I wake myself up at six.
  2. They hurt theirselves.
  3. We enjoyed at the concert.
  4. Don't worry yourself.
  5. We met ourselves in Brussels.
Show the answers
  1. I wake up at six.
  2. They hurt themselves.
  3. We enjoyed ourselves at the concert. / We enjoyed the concert.
  4. Don't worry.
  5. We met in Brussels.

5. What do you say?

Write one sentence for each situation.

  1. You tell your guests to take the food freely.
  2. Nobody helped you to make the cake.
  3. Your aunt has no husband or children at home.
  4. Tom loves Ana and Ana loves Tom.
Show the answers
  1. Help yourselves! / Help yourself!
  2. I did it myself. / I made it myself.
  3. She lives by herself. / She lives alone. / She lives on her own.
  4. They love each other very much.

6. Your turn

Write four sentences: something you did yourself, something you do alone, some advice for a friend (look after / enjoy yourself), and a sentence with each other.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. I painted my living room myself last summer. I usually go running by myself. Look after yourself and don't work too much! My best friend and I call each other every Sunday.
Key points
  • myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves.
  • Subject = object: hurt yourself, introduce myself, enjoy yourselves, help yourself.
  • Emphasis: I did it myself; alone: by myself / on my own.
  • No reflexive: get up, wake up, relax, remember, feel, hurry, worry, meet, sit down.
  • Reciprocal (one another): each other, not themselves.
