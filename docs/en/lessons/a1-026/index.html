Food and meals

Everyone talks about food: what we eat, when we eat and what we like. This lesson gives you the key words and the common traps: “have breakfast”, uncountable words like “bread”, and confusing words (raisins, chips).

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Name the meals and say what you eat with “have breakfast / lunch / dinner”.
  • Know the most common foods and drinks.
  • Count uncountable food with a glass of, a piece of, a cup of…
  • Talk about what you like and describe a dish (delicious, spicy, sweet).

The meals of the day

The three meals are “breakfast” (morning), “lunch” (midday) and “dinner” (evening). A little food between meals is “a snack”.

To say you eat a meal, use “have”, not “take”: “I have breakfast at seven.” Don't use an article before the meal: “have lunch”, not “have the lunch”.

To say what you eat at a meal, use “for”: “What do you have for breakfast? — I have bread and tea.”

  • I have breakfast at half past six.
    have + meal, no article.
  • What do you have for lunch? — Usually rice and fish.
    for + meal.
  • We have dinner with the family on Sundays.
    A habit: present simple.
In Britain, the evening meal is also called “tea” (in the north) or “supper” (a light, late meal). In the USA, “dinner” is always the evening meal.

Food and drink

Here are the most useful words in groups. Many foods (rice, bread, meat, cheese, water) are uncountable: they have no plural and don't take “a”.

GroupWordsCountable?
Fruitan apple · a banana · an orange · a mango · a pineapple · grapesyes (but “fruit” in general: no)
Vegetablesa tomato · a potato · an onion · a carrot · beans · saladmostly yes
Meat and fishchicken · beef · pork · fish · eggseggs yes; meat no
Basicsbread · rice · pasta · plantain · cassavano
Dairymilk · cheese · butter · yoghurtno
Drinkswater · tea · coffee · juice · soft drinksno (but “a coffee” in a café)
  • I eat a lot of fruit and vegetables.
    “fruit” is usually singular in general.
  • Do you want chicken or fish?
    No article: uncountable meat.

Counting things you can't count

Some foods are countable: an apple, two apples, an egg. Others are uncountable: bread, rice, water, milk, meat, cheese, sugar. Don't say “a bread” or “two waters” (but in a café, “two coffees” means two cups of coffee).

To talk in general, use the noun alone: “I like bread.” For an amount that is not exact, use “some” (lesson 31): “I'd like some water.”

To count, add a quantity word or a container: a glass of water, a piece of bread. Lessons 30 and 31 look at this in more detail.

Quantity wordExamplesNote
a glass ofa glass of water / milk / juicecold drinks
a cup ofa cup of tea / coffeehot drinks
a bottle ofa bottle of water / oila closed container
a piece ofa piece of bread / cake / cheesea part of something
a slice ofa slice of bread / pizzaa thin, flat piece
a bowl ofa bowl of rice / soupa deep round dish
a kilo ofa kilo of rice / tomatoes1,000 grams
  • Can I have a glass of water, please?
    A polite request.
  • I eat two slices of bread for breakfast.
    The quantity word takes the plural: slices.

Saying what you like and describing a dish

Your tastes: “I like fish”, “I don't like coffee”, “My favourite food is ndolé”. Your diet: “I'm vegetarian” (I don't eat meat), “I don't eat pork”.

To describe a dish: delicious, tasty (with a good taste), spicy / hot (with a lot of chilli), sweet (with sugar), salty (with salt), fresh (new, not old).

Careful: “cook” is a verb (to make food) and a noun (a person who cooks). “A cooker” is a machine in the kitchen (US: a stove), not a person!

  • This soup is delicious!
    A very good taste.
  • Be careful, the sauce is very spicy.
    A lot of chilli.
  • My father is a great cook.
    cook = a person.

Confusing food words

Some words look like words in other languages but have a different meaning, and some change between British and American English.

biscuits (UK)small, dry, sweet cakesUS: cookies

The formula

have breakfast / lunch / dinner · What do you have for breakfast? · a glass of water, a piece of bread

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “vegetable” has three syllables: /ˈvedʒtəbl/ (“vedj-te-bl”), not four.
  • “breakfast” /ˈbrekfəst/: “brek-fest”, not “break-fast”.
  • Don't mix up “chicken” /ˈtʃɪkɪn/ (the bird, the meat) and “kitchen” /ˈkɪtʃɪn/ (the room): the “ch” sound moves.
  • “dessert” /dɪˈzɜːt/ has the stress at the end; “desert” /ˈdezət/ has the stress at the start.
  • “bread” /bred/ and “fruit” /fruːt/: you hear only one vowel; the i in “fruit” is silent.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ I take my breakfast at 7.✓ I have breakfast at 7.For a meal: “have”, with no article.
✗ Can I have a bread?✓ Can I have some bread? / Can I have a piece of bread?“Bread” is uncountable.
✗ I eat a rice every day.✓ I eat rice every day.No “a” before an uncountable noun.
✗ I love raisins, especially fresh ones.✓ I love grapes.Fresh = grapes; dried = raisins.
✗ My mother is a good cooker.✓ My mother is a good cook.“Cooker” = the machine for cooking.
✗ We have the lunch at noon.✓ We have lunch at noon.No article before breakfast, lunch, dinner.
✗ I eat many fruits.✓ I eat a lot of fruit.In general, “fruit” is used as an uncountable noun.
✗ I'd like a cup of tea with a milk.✓ I'd like a cup of tea with milk.“Milk” is uncountable: no “a”.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Douala, two colleagues, Brice and Hannah (from Canada), choose a restaurant for lunch.

Brice

It's one o'clock. Let's have lunch!
have lunch: no article.

Hannah

Good idea. I'm hungry. What do people eat here for lunch?
for + meal.

Brice

Try ndolé. It's a dish with leaves, peanuts and meat or fish.
A dish from Cameroon.

Hannah

Is it spicy?
spicy = with chilli.

Brice

Not very. And you can have it with plantain or rice.
Uncountable: no “a”.

Hannah

Perfect. I love plantain. And to drink?
“to drink” = for a drink.

Brice

A bottle of water for me, and a fresh juice for you?
Quantity word: a bottle of.

Hannah

Yes, please. Mango juice! This is a delicious lunch.
“a delicious lunch”: article because of the adjective.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Which meal?

Give the name of the meal.

  1. The meal in the morning
  2. The meal in the middle of the day
  3. The main meal in the evening
  4. A little food between meals
Show the answers
  1. breakfast
  2. lunch
  3. dinner
  4. a snack

2. The right quantity word

Complete with glass, cup, piece, bowl, bottle or bar.

  1. a ___ of water (to drink now)
  2. a ___ of tea
  3. a ___ of bread
  4. a ___ of rice
  5. a ___ of oil
  6. a ___ of chocolate
Show the answers
  1. glass
  2. cup
  3. piece (or slice)
  4. bowl
  5. bottle
  6. bar

3. A, an or nothing?

Complete with a, an or — (nothing).

  1. I eat ___ apple every day.
  2. She drinks ___ water with her meals.
  3. We have ___ rice for lunch.
  4. Do you want ___ banana?
  5. He has ___ egg for breakfast.
Show the answers
  1. an
  2. — (nothing)
  3. — (nothing)
  4. a
  5. an

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. I take breakfast at eight o'clock.
  2. I'd like a water, please.
  3. I love fresh raisins.
  4. My brother is a very good cooker.
  5. What do you have at lunch?
Show the answers
  1. I have breakfast at eight o'clock.
  2. I'd like a glass of water, please. / I'd like some water, please.
  3. I love grapes.
  4. My brother is a very good cook.
  5. What do you have for lunch?

5. Your turn

Describe your meals on a normal day in four or five sentences: what you eat and drink, and when. Say what your favourite food is.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. I have breakfast at seven: two slices of bread with butter and a cup of coffee. For lunch I usually have rice and chicken. At four o'clock I have a snack, often a banana. I have dinner at eight with my family. My favourite food is grilled fish with plantain.
Key points
  • have breakfast / lunch / dinner (not take, no article); What do you have for lunch?
  • bread, rice, water, milk, meat, cheese: uncountable, no “a”, no plural.
  • To count: a glass of, a cup of, a bottle of, a piece of, a slice of, a bowl of.
  • Careful: grapes (fresh) vs raisins (dried); chips (UK) = fries (US); a cook = a person, a cooker = a machine.
  • To describe a dish: delicious, tasty, spicy, sweet, salty, fresh.
