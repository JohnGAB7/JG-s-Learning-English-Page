Numbers from 20 to 1,000

After the numbers from 0 to 20 (lesson 3), learn the tens, the hundreds and “a thousand”. You need them for prices, distances, addresses, years and quantities. English also has its own way to write big numbers and decimals (comma, point, “and”).

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Say and write the tens (twenty to ninety) and the compound numbers with a hyphen (twenty-one).
  • Say hundreds with “and” in British English (two hundred and fifty) and 1,000 (a thousand).
  • Hear and say the difference between -teen and -ty (thirteen / thirty) with stress.
  • Read a price, a decimal number and a simple year, and use the comma and the point correctly.

The tens

The tens end in -ty. Like the -teen numbers, some change: twenty (2), thirty (3), forty (4), fifty (5), eighty (8, only one t).

The most common spelling trap: “forty” has NO u, but “four” and “fourteen” have one.

DigitWordSoundTrap
20twenty/ˈtwenti/
30thirty/ˈθɜːti/thir-, like thirteen
40forty/ˈfɔːti/no u
50fifty/ˈfɪfti/fif-, like fifteen

70seventy/ˈsevnti/
80eighty/ˈeɪti/only one t
90ninety/ˈnaɪnti/keeps the e of nine
English tens are regular: 70 = seventy, 80 = eighty, 97 = ninety-seven. No complicated combinations.

Compound numbers: the hyphen

From 21 to 99, write the ten, a hyphen, then the unit: twenty-one, thirty-five, sixty-eight, ninety-nine. Never use “and” between the ten and the unit.

-teen or -ty? When you speak, -teen numbers are stressed at the end (fifTEEN), and -ty numbers at the start (FIFty). The t in -ty is also lighter, and in American English it often sounds like a quick d (/ˈfɪfdi/). To be sure, you can say the digits: “one-five or five-oh?”.

-teen (stress at the end)-ty (stress at the start)

14 fourTEEN /fɔːˈtiːn/40 FORty /ˈfɔːti/
15 fifTEEN /fɪfˈtiːn/50 FIFty /ˈfɪfti/
16 sixTEEN60 SIXty
18 eighTEEN80 EIGHty
  • My father is sixty-two.
    Age.
  • There are thirty-one days in May.
    A hyphen, no and.
  • It costs forty-five euros.
    forty: no u.

Hundreds and a thousand

100 is “a hundred” or “one hundred” (more exact). After a number, “hundred” never takes -s: two hundred, five hundred (never “five hundreds”). The -s appears only in “hundreds of” (a very large number of): hundreds of people.

In British English, put “and” between the hundreds and the rest: 250 = two hundred and fifty; 105 = a hundred and five. In American English, “and” is often left out: two hundred fifty.

1,000 is “a thousand” or “one thousand”, with no -s after a number: three thousand. The same rule works for “hundreds of / thousands of”.

DigitBritish EnglishAmerican English
100a / one hundreda / one hundred
101a hundred and oneone hundred (and) one
250two hundred and fiftytwo hundred fifty
999nine hundred and ninety-ninenine hundred ninety-nine
1,000a / one thousanda / one thousand
  • The hotel has three hundred rooms.
    No -s on hundred.
  • There are hundreds of birds on the lake.
    hundreds of = a very large number of.
  • It's more than a thousand kilometres from Paris to Rome.
    It's for a distance.

Comma and point

In English, a comma separates thousands: 1,000, 2,500. Some languages write 1 000 or 1.000. English uses a point for decimals: 2.5, said “two point five”. After the point, say each digit: 3.75 = three point seven five.

Prices: £4.50 is “four pounds fifty”; €19.99 is “nineteen euros ninety-nine”; $1.20 is “a dollar twenty”. The symbol is written before the number, but said after it.

  • The ticket is £12.50.
    Say: twelve pounds fifty.
  • The baby weighs 3.2 kilos.
    Say: three point two kilos.
  • About 1,500 people live in my village.
    Say: one thousand five hundred.

Years, as a bonus

Years up to 1999 are said in two pairs: 1985 = nineteen eighty-five; 1906 = nineteen oh six. Years 2000 to 2009 are “two thousand (and) + number”: 2005 = two thousand and five (UK) / two thousand five (US). From 2010, people mostly use pairs: 2026 = twenty twenty-six (you also hear “two thousand and twenty-six”).

For ages and quantities up to 1,000, use the rules above: She's ninety-one. Full dates with ordinal numbers are in lesson 22.

  • My mother was born in 1972.
    Say: nineteen seventy-two.
  • We're in 2026.
    Say: twenty twenty-six.

The formula

twenty, thirty, forty, fifty… · twenty-one (hyphen) · two hundred and fifty (UK) · a thousand · 1,000 (comma) · 2.5 = two point five

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • -ty is stressed at the start (FORty /ˈfɔːti/), -teen at the end (fourTEEN /fɔːˈtiːn/). This is how people hear the difference.
  • In “two hundred and fifty”, “and” is very weak: /ən/ → /tuː ˈhʌndrəd ən ˈfɪfti/.
  • “hundred” /ˈhʌndrəd/: two syllables, breathed h. “thousand” /ˈθaʊznd/ starts with /θ/ (tongue between the teeth, breathe out).
  • In American English, a t between two vowels often becomes a quick d: thirty /ˈθɝːdi/, forty /ˈfɔːrdi/; “twenty” often sounds like /ˈtweni/.
  • “eighty” /ˈeɪti/: the gh is silent, like in eight.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In an estate agent's office in Liège, a client asks about a flat.

Agent

This flat is on the fourth floor. It's eighty-five square metres.
eighty-five: hyphen.

Client

Sorry, eighty-five or ninety-five?
Checking a number.

Agent

Eighty-five: eight, five.
Saying the digits.

Client

OK. And how much is the rent?
A question about price.

Agent

It's seven hundred and fifty euros a month, plus sixty euros for charges.
British “and” after hundred.

Client

So, eight hundred and ten euros in total. Is the building old?
A sum.

Agent

Yes, it's from 1930: nineteen thirty. But the kitchen is new.
A year in two pairs.

Client

And how far is it from the station?
A question about distance.

Agent

About nine hundred metres. Fifteen minutes on foot, or five by bus.
about = approximately.

Key points
  • twenty, thirty, forty (no u), fifty, sixty, seventy, eighty, ninety.
  • 21–99: hyphen, no and (twenty-one); hundreds: two hundred and fifty (UK).
  • hundred and thousand have no -s after a number; but hundreds of / thousands of.
  • 1,000 (comma for thousands); 2.5 = two point five (point for decimals).
  • -ty is stressed at the start (FORty), -teen at the end (fourTEEN).
