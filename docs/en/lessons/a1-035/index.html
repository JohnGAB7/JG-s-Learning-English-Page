Adjectives: position and order

In English, adjectives never change and go before the noun: “a red car”, “red cars”. When you use several adjectives, they follow an order (opinion, size, age, colour, origin, material). This lesson helps you avoid the most common mistakes.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Put the adjective before the noun or after a verb like be, look, feel.
  • Use adjectives with no plural and no female form.
  • Put several adjectives in the right order (a beautiful old wooden table).
  • Use a noun like an adjective (a coffee cup, a bus stop).

Before the noun or after the verb

An adjective goes before the noun: “a big house”, “an interesting book”. Never after the noun: “a red car”, not “a car red”.

It can also go after some verbs: be, look, feel, seem, get / become, taste, smell: “The house is big.”, “You look tired.”, “This soup tastes good.”

“A” or “an” depends on the sound of the next word, so often on the adjective: “a car” but “an old car”; “an apple” but “a big apple”.

  • We live in a small flat.
    Before the noun.
  • The flat is small but comfortable.
    After be.
  • You look happy today!
    After look.
  • It's an expensive restaurant.
    an + vowel sound of “expensive”.

Adjectives never change

English adjectives have no plural and no female form: “a tall man, a tall woman, tall people.”

You can't use an adjective alone as a noun for one person or thing: add “one”, “man”, “woman”, “person”: “the new one”, “a rich man”. (“The rich” exists, but for a whole group.)

The order of several adjectives

When there are several adjectives before a noun, English follows a fairly fixed order. Opinion (what you think) comes first, then facts: size, age, shape, colour, origin, material, and finally purpose, just before the noun.

In practice, we rarely use more than two or three adjectives. Remember: opinion → size → age → colour → origin → material.

Join two colours with “and”: “a black and white cat”.

  • She's got a beautiful old wooden table.
    opinion – age – material.
  • We often eat in a lovely small Italian restaurant.

  • He's wearing black leather shoes.

To remember: OSASCOMP (Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Colour, Origin, Material, Purpose).

Adjectives that go only after the verb

Some adjectives that start with “a-” go only after the verb: afraid, alone, asleep, awake, alive. Say “The baby is asleep”, not “an asleep baby”: say “a sleeping baby”.

In British English, “ill” is also used mostly after the verb: “She's ill.” Before a noun, say “a sick child”.

  • Don't make noise, the baby is asleep.
    asleep after be.
  • I'm afraid of dogs. — He's a frightened child.
    afraid after the verb; frightened before the noun.

A noun used like an adjective

English often puts a noun before another noun to describe it. The first noun works like an adjective and stays singular: “a shoe shop”, “a bus stop”, “a coffee cup”.

The main word is the last one: “a coffee cup” is a cup; “a cup of coffee” is the coffee in the cup.

These groups can take an adjective too: “a new shoe shop”, “a big football match”.

  • There's a bus stop in front of the school.
    bus + stop.
  • My sister works in a shoe shop.
    “shoe” is singular.
  • Let's watch the football match tonight.
    football + match.

The formula

(a / an) + opinion + size + age + shape + colour + origin + material + NOUN · be / look / feel + adjective

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • In adjective + noun, the main stress is on the noun: “a big HOUSE”, “a red CAR”.
  • In noun + noun, the stress is on the first noun: “a COFFEE cup”, “a BUS stop”, “a SHOE shop”.
  • “an” links to the next word: “an old car” = /ən ˈəʊld kɑː/ ≈ “a-nold car”.
  • “interesting” has three syllables: /ˈɪntrəstɪŋ/ (“in-tres-ting”), stress at the start.
  • “comfortable”: /ˈkʌmftəbl/, three syllables; “beautiful”: /ˈbjuːtɪfl/, stress on “beau”.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

At Brussels airport, Moana can't find her bag. She describes it to the man at the lost property office.

Agent

Good morning. How can I help you?
A polite offer of help.

Moana

I can't find my bag. I think it's on the plane from Montreal.
can't = not able.

Agent

OK. What does it look like?
A question about appearance.

Moana

It's a small black leather bag.
size – colour – material.

Agent

Is it a backpack or a handbag?
Two kinds of bag.

Moana

A handbag. It's got a long thin strap and a little silver key ring.
Two adjectives before each noun.

Agent

Let me check… Is this your bag?
Let me = I will.

Moana

No, that's a big brown bag. Mine is black.
Adjective after be: is black.

Agent

And this one?
one = bag.

Moana

Yes! That's it! Thank you so much!
“That's it” = that is the right one.

Key points
  • Adjective before the noun (a red car) or after be / look / feel / seem (The car is red).
  • Adjectives never change: no s, no female form (red cars, a tall woman).
  • Order: opinion – size – age – shape – colour – origin – material – noun (a beautiful old wooden table).
  • afraid, alone, asleep, awake, alive: only after the verb.
  • Noun + noun: the first noun stays singular (a shoe shop, a bus stop).
