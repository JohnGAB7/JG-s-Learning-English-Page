The past simple: irregular verbs

The most common English verbs (go, have, make, see, take…) are irregular: their past simple does not end in -ed, so you must learn it. Good news: negatives and questions work exactly as with regular verbs.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Know the past simple of about forty very common irregular verbs.
  • Learn irregular verbs in groups with similar forms, not as a long list.
  • Use didn't and Did …? with the base verb, even for irregular verbs.
  • Tell the story of a trip or a day with regular and irregular verbs.

The idea

An irregular verb has a special past form: go → went, see → saw, buy → bought. As with regular verbs, the form is the same for every subject (I went, she went, they went).

Lists of irregular verbs have three columns: base form, past simple, past participle (go – went – gone). In this lesson, only the second column matters. The past participle is for the present perfect (lesson 51).

  • I went to Kribi last weekend.
    go → went
  • She bought a new phone yesterday.
    buy → bought
  • We had a great time.
    have → had; “have a great time” = enjoy yourself

Learn them in groups

There are about 150 common irregular verbs, but about fifty are enough for daily life. They are easier to remember in families with similar forms.

Tip: learn five verbs a day and say one true sentence with each (“Yesterday I drank three coffees”). Personal sentences help you remember the form.

Negatives and questions: back to the base

In negatives and questions, the irregular form disappears: did / didn't shows the past, and the main verb goes back to its base form. It is the same rule as for regular verbs (lesson 41).

Be careful with “to be”: it never uses did. We say “I wasn't”, “Were you…?” (lesson 39).

PositiveNegativeQuestion
I went.I didn't go.Did you go?
She saw it.She didn't see it.Did she see it?
They ate fish.They didn't eat fish.Did they eat fish?
He had time.He didn't have time.Did he have time?
I was tired.I wasn't tired.Were you tired?
  • We didn't take the bus; we took a taxi.
    didn't take (base) / took (past).
  • What did you buy at the market?
    Wh- + did + subject + base.
  • Did you hear the news? — Yes, I did.
    Short answer with did.

Did as a main verb

“Do” is both a helper verb and a main verb. In the past, the main verb “do” becomes “did”. So you can see did and do in the same sentence.

This is not a mistake: the first one is the helper, the second one is the main verb.

  • I did my homework.
    did = main verb (past of do).
  • I didn't do my homework.
    didn't = helper; do = main verb.
  • What did you do on Sunday?
    did = helper; do = main verb.

Traps: spelling and meaning

Some past forms look like other words, so be careful.

read → read: same spelling, but different sounds (/riːd/ now, /red/ in the past). The context and the time word tell you the tense.

leave → left (go away) but live → lived (have your home); feel → felt (have a feeling) but fall → fell (go down to the ground); lose → lost (not find) but loose is an adjective (not tight).

  • I read that book last year.
    read /red/: past.
  • She left Paris in 2018.
    leave → left.
  • He fell and felt a pain in his knee.
    fall → fell; feel → felt.

The formula

subject + irregular past (went, saw, took…) · didn't + base · Did + subject + base …?

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • All -ought / -aught forms sound /ɔːt/: bought /bɔːt/, thought /θɔːt/, taught /tɔːt/, caught /kɔːt/. The gh is silent.
  • “said” is /sed/, not /seɪd/; “says” is /sez/.
  • Past “read” is /red/, like the colour “red”.
  • “saw” /sɔː/ sounds like “sore” in British English; “ate” is /et/ (UK) or /eɪt/ (UK and US).
  • “went” /went/ has a short e as in “pet”; do not confuse it with “wanted” /ˈwɒntɪd/ (from want).

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Fatou is back from a trip to Canada. Her friend Claire calls her from Lyon.

Claire

Welcome back! How was Canada?
How was…? = a question with “to be”.

Fatou

Amazing! I flew to Montreal and then I took the train to Quebec City.
fly → flew; take → took.

Claire

Did you see the snow?
Did + base: see.

Fatou

Yes, I did! It was minus fifteen. I bought a big coat on the first day.
buy → bought.

Claire

Ha! What did you eat?
Wh- question with did.

Fatou

I ate poutine three times. And I met some very friendly people.
eat → ate; meet → met.

Claire

Did you speak French or English?
Did + base: speak.

Fatou

Both! But I didn't understand the Quebec accent at first.
didn't + base: understand.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Find the past form

Write the past simple of each irregular verb.

  1. go
  2. see
  3. take
  4. buy
  5. eat
  6. write
  7. think
  8. put
Show the answers
  1. went
  2. saw
  3. took
  4. bought
  5. ate
  6. wrote
  7. thought
  8. put

2. Complete the story

Put the verbs in the past simple.

  1. Last year I ___ (go) to Belgium for the first time.
  2. I ___ (leave) Douala on a Friday night.
  3. The flight ___ (be) long, so I ___ (sleep) a lot.
  4. In Brussels I ___ (meet) my cousin at the station.
  5. We ___ (have) dinner and ___ (drink) some hot chocolate.
Show the answers
  1. went
  2. left
  3. was … slept
  4. met
  5. had … drank

3. Negative and question

Change the sentence as shown in brackets.

  1. She found her keys. (negative)
  2. They came by car. (question)
  3. He told the truth. (negative)
  4. You did the shopping. (question)
  5. I was at home. (negative)
Show the answers
  1. She didn't find her keys.
  2. Did they come by car?
  3. He didn't tell the truth.
  4. Did you do the shopping?
  5. I wasn't at home.

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. I catched the bus at 8.
  2. Did you saw the film?
  3. We didn't went out.
  4. He teached maths in Yaoundé.
Show the answers
  1. I caught the bus at 8.
  2. Did you see the film?
  3. We didn't go out.
  4. He taught maths in Yaoundé.

5. Write the question

Write a question about the part in CAPITALS.

  1. I forgot MY PHONE at home.
  2. They came to the party AT NINE.
  3. She said “SEE YOU TOMORROW”.
Show the answers
  1. What did you forget at home?
  2. When did they come to the party?
  3. What did she say?

6. Your turn

Write five or six sentences about a trip or a day out, with at least five different irregular verbs.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. Last month I went to Ghent with two friends. We took the train from Brussels and got there at ten. We saw the old castle and ate waffles in a small café. I bought a postcard for my mother. We didn't spend much money, but we had a wonderful day.
Key points
  • The most common verbs are irregular: went, had, did, made, saw, took, got, said…
  • One past form for all subjects.
  • Negatives and questions: didn't / Did + base (didn't go, Did you see…?).
  • “to be” never uses did: wasn't, weren't, Was…? Were…?
  • Learn verbs in groups (bought / thought / taught; slept / felt / kept…).
