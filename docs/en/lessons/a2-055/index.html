Mustn’t or don’t have to?

In positive sentences, must and have to are very similar. But in the negative, they have opposite meanings: “mustn't” = it is not allowed; “don't have to” = it is not necessary, you can choose. Mixing them up can cause real misunderstandings.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Say that something is not allowed with mustn't (and can't / not allowed to).
  • Say that something is not necessary with don't / doesn't have to (and needn't).
  • Choose the right form for the meaning you want.
  • Talk about no obligation in the past with didn't have to.

Two opposite meanings

“Mustn't” (must not) means an action is not allowed: don't do it. It is a prohibition, often a rule or a warning.

“Don't have to” means an action is not necessary: you can do it if you want, but you don't need to. It is a freedom, not a prohibition.

FormMeaningExampleNote
mustn't + baseprohibitionYou mustn't smoke here.It is not allowed.
don't have to + baseno obligationYou don't have to come.You can choose.
doesn't have to + baseno obligation (he / she / it)She doesn't have to work today.It's her day off.
  • You mustn't be late for the exam.
    A warning: being late is not OK.
  • You don't have to be early. Ten o'clock is fine.
    Not necessary: you can come at ten.

The big trap

Many learners think “don't have to” means “it is not allowed”. It doesn't! It means “it is not necessary”.

If a sign says “You don't have to pay”, it means it is free — not that paying is forbidden!

  • You mustn't touch the paintings.
    Not allowed.
  • You don't have to touch the screen — it works with your voice.
    Not necessary.
  • We don't have to get up early tomorrow. It's Sunday!
    No obligation: we can sleep.

Other ways to say “not allowed”

“Can't” and “be not allowed to” also express prohibition, especially when the rule comes from outside (a law, official rules). “Mustn't” is common for a personal warning or very strong advice.

In conversation, “can't” is often more natural than “mustn't” for a rule: “You can't park here”.

  • You can't park here. It's for buses.
    A rule.
  • Children aren't allowed to swim without an adult.
    An official rule.
  • You mustn't tell anyone. It's a secret!
    A personal warning.

Needn't and the past

In British English, “needn't + base” means the same as “don't have to”: “You needn't bring anything”. “Don't need to” is also very common.

In the past, prohibition uses “couldn't” or “wasn't / weren't allowed to” (mustn't has no past form). No obligation uses “didn't have to”.

IdeaPresentPast
prohibitionYou mustn't / can't use your phone.We couldn't / weren't allowed to use our phones.
no obligationI don't have to wear a tie.I didn't have to wear a tie.
no obligation (UK)You needn't wait.You didn't need to wait.

The formula

mustn't + base = not allowed · don't / doesn't have to + base = not necessary

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “mustn't” is /ˈmʌsnt/: the first t is silent. Don't say “must-ent”.
  • “don't have to”: /dəʊnt ˈhæf tə/ (UK), with an f; “doesn't have to” /ˈdʌznt ˈhæf tə/.
  • “needn't” is /ˈniːdnt/, two syllables.
  • For a prohibition, stress “mustn't”: You MUSTN'T do that. For no obligation, the stress is often on “have”: You don't HAVE to.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ You mustn't pay. It's free.✓ You don't have to pay. It's free.Free = no need to pay, not a prohibition.
✗ You don't have to smoke in the hospital.✓ You mustn't smoke in the hospital.A prohibition: mustn't (or can't).
✗ She don't have to work.✓ She doesn't have to work.he / she / it: doesn't.
✗ You mustn't to run.✓ You mustn't run.No to after mustn't.
✗ Yesterday I mustn't go out.✓ Yesterday I couldn't go out. / I wasn't allowed to go out.mustn't has no past form.
✗ I haven't to go.✓ I don't have to go.Make the negative of have to with do.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In a museum in Ottawa, a guard talks to two visitors from Douala, Linda and her son Ryan.

Guard

Good morning. Just so you know, you mustn't take photos with a flash.
Prohibition.

Linda

OK. Do we have to leave our bags at the entrance?
Question about obligation.

Guard

Small bags are fine, so you don't have to leave yours. But you mustn't eat or drink in the galleries.
No obligation, then a prohibition.

Ryan

Do I have to pay? I'm twelve.
Question about obligation.

Guard

No, children under thirteen don't have to pay.
Free for children.

Ryan

Great! Can I touch the dinosaur?
Asking for permission.

Guard

Sorry, you mustn't touch it. But you can touch the stones in room 4.
Prohibition, then permission.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Not allowed or not necessary?

Does the sentence show a prohibition or no obligation?

  1. You mustn't cross the road here.
  2. You don't have to wear a suit on Fridays.
  3. He doesn't have to cook tonight.
  4. Passengers mustn't stand near the doors.
Show the answers
  1. prohibition
  2. no obligation
  3. no obligation
  4. prohibition

2. Mustn't or don't / doesn't have to?

Complete with the right form.

  1. It's a holiday tomorrow, so we ___ go to work.
  2. You ___ drive after drinking alcohol.
  3. The museum is free. You ___ buy a ticket.
  4. You ___ open this door. The alarm will ring.
  5. My brother ___ get up early. He works from home.
  6. You ___ forget your passport!
Show the answers
  1. don't have to
  2. mustn't
  3. don't have to
  4. mustn't
  5. doesn't have to
  6. mustn't

3. In the past

Put the sentence in the past.

  1. I don't have to wear a uniform.
  2. We can't use our phones in the exam.
  3. She doesn't have to pay.
Show the answers
  1. I didn't have to wear a uniform.
  2. We couldn't use our phones in the exam.
  3. She didn't have to pay.

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake in form or meaning. Write it correctly.

  1. Breakfast is included, so you mustn't pay for it.
  2. You don't have to swim here. It's dangerous!
  3. He don't have to come.
  4. I haven't to study tonight.
Show the answers
  1. Breakfast is included, so you don't have to pay for it.
  2. You mustn't swim here. It's dangerous!
  3. He doesn't have to come.
  4. I don't have to study tonight.

5. Say it another way

Rewrite with mustn't, don't have to or needn't.

  1. It isn't necessary for you to answer.
  2. Don't forget the keys — it's very important!
  3. It isn't necessary to bring a present.
Show the answers
  1. You don't have to answer.
  2. You mustn't forget the keys. / We mustn't forget the keys.
  3. You don't have to bring a present. / You needn't bring a present.

6. Your turn

Write the rules of a place you know (your job, your school, your home): three prohibitions with mustn't / can't and two freedoms with don't have to.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. At my gym in Brussels, you mustn't use your phone on the machines. You can't enter without a card, and you mustn't leave your towel on the floor. But you don't have to book a time, and you don't have to pay extra for the classes.
Key points
  • mustn't + base = not allowed.
  • don't / doesn't have to + base = not necessary (you can choose).
  • Prohibition also with can't or not allowed to; no obligation also with needn't / don't need to.
  • Past: couldn't / wasn't allowed to (prohibition); didn't have to (no obligation).
  • Free = You don't have to pay, never You mustn't pay.
