Future in the past (was going to, would)

“I was going to call you, but I forgot”, “I knew he would come”, “We were about to leave”: to talk about the future as seen from a moment in the past, English uses was going to, would, was about to, was + -ing and was to. You need these forms to tell stories, apologise for plans that didn't happen and report what people said.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B2

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Express a past intention or prediction with was / were going to, often one that did not happen.
  • Use would for the future seen from the past in stories and reported speech.
  • Use was about to, was + -ing, was due to and was to according to the meaning.
  • Avoid “would” after when / as soon as.

The principle: move the point of view into the past

Each way of talking about the future has a “past” equivalent: you simply move the auxiliary back. will → would; am going to → was going to; am meeting → was meeting; am about to → was about to.

The point of reference is a past moment; the action is in the future compared with that moment. It may or may not have happened: the context tells you.

Future (from now)Future in the pastExample
willwouldI knew she would win.
be going towas / were going toI was going to call you, but I forgot.
present continuous (arrangement)was / were + -ingWe were leaving the next day, so we packed.
be about towas / were about toThe train was about to leave.
be due towas / were due toThe meeting was due to start at nine.
be to (formal)was / were toHe was to become president ten years later.

Was / were going to: past intention or prediction

“Was going to” expresses an intention you had in the past. Very often it did not happen, and we add “but…”: “I was going to call you, but I forgot.”

It also expresses a prediction based on evidence in the past: “The sky was black. It was obviously going to rain.”

In speech, “was going to” often sounds like “was gonna”; only write it like that in very informal messages.

  • I was going to buy a new phone, but the price went up.
    A plan that changed.
  • We were going to visit Kribi, but it rained all week.
    A plan that didn't happen.
  • I could see she was going to cry.
    A prediction from evidence.
  • Sorry, I wasn't going to say anything, but…
    I didn't intend to speak.

Would: the future seen from the past

“Would” is “will” moved into the past. It appears in reported speech (“She said she would call”) and in stories, to announce what was going to happen: “In 1995, she met the man she would marry two years later.”

Careful: you cannot use “would” for a personal intention that didn't happen. “I would call you yesterday” is wrong; say “I was going to call you yesterday”.

  • I knew she would win.
    Future from a past point of view.
  • He promised he would help us the next day.
    Reported promise.
  • Nobody thought the project would succeed.
    A past opinion about the future.
  • Little by little, the village would become a town.
    Narrative: what was going to happen.

Was about to, was + -ing, was due to

“Was about to + base form”: an action that was going to happen immediately. We often add “when” for an interruption: “We were about to leave when the phone rang.” Add “just” for emphasis: “I was just about to call you!”

“Was + -ing”: an arrangement planned in the past, like the present continuous for the future: “I was flying to Montreal the next morning, so I went to bed early.”

“Was due to + base form”: an official time or scheduled event: “The flight was due to land at 6, but it was delayed.”

  • The film was about to start when she arrived.
    Immediate future in the past.
  • I was just about to call you!
    Common phrase.
  • She was meeting her parents that evening, so she couldn't stay.
    A past arrangement.
  • The new bridge was due to open in May.
    A scheduled event.

Was to: destiny and formal arrangements

“Was / were to + base form” is more literary. It announces something that really happened later (destiny): “He was to become one of the greatest writers of his generation.”

“Was to have + past participle” means that the planned arrangement did not happen: “The wedding was to have taken place in June.” This form is formal and rare in speech.

“Was supposed to” is the everyday equivalent for a plan or duty that was not carried out: “We were supposed to meet at eight, but he never came.”

  • Nelson Mandela was to spend 27 years in prison.
    A historical fact, seen from an earlier point.
  • The concert was to have taken place in Liège.
    It was planned, but it didn't happen.
  • We were supposed to meet at eight.
    Everyday English.

Time clauses and common errors

As with “will”, do not use “would” after when, as soon as, before, after or until. Use the past simple: “He said he would phone when he arrived” (not “when he would arrive”).

In a past narrative, don't switch to the present: “I thought it is going to rain” is wrong; say “I thought it was going to rain”.

After “going to” and “about to”, always use the base form: “I was going to go”, not “I was going to went”.

  • She told me she would call as soon as she got home.
    “got”, not “would get”.
  • I thought it was going to rain, so I took an umbrella.
    Keep the past.

The formula

was / were going to · would · was / were + -ing · was / were about to · was / were due to · was / were to + base form

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “was going to” is often reduced to /wəz ˈɡʌnə/ (“was gonna”) in fast speech.
  • “would” contracts to 'd: “I knew she'd win” /aɪ njuː ʃiːd ˈwɪn/.
  • “were about to”: /wər əˈbaʊt tə/, with a linking r in British English.
  • In “I was going to call you, but…”, the voice rises on “call” and then falls: the intonation tells the listener the plan didn't happen.
  • “due” is /djuː/ in British English and /duː/ in American English.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Nairobi, Wanjiru and her Belgian colleague Thomas are back at the office after a long weekend.

Wanjiru

So, did you go to the Maasai Mara in the end?
in the end = finally.

Thomas

No! We were going to leave on Friday morning, but the car broke down.
Plan that didn't happen.

Wanjiru

Oh no. I knew that old car would cause problems one day.
would: future seen from the past.

Thomas

We were just about to get on the road when the engine started smoking.
about to + when: interruption.

Wanjiru

And the mechanic?
A short question.

Thomas

He promised he would fix it by Saturday, but he didn't have the part.
Reported promise: would.

Wanjiru

Too bad. Our flight to Mombasa was due to leave on Sunday, so we had the same stress!
was due to: scheduled time.

Thomas

Well, I was going to call you to ask for a lift, but I didn't want to ruin your weekend.
Intention not carried out.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. What does it mean?

Explain the meaning of each sentence.

  1. I was going to call you, but I forgot.
  2. She knew she would never see him again.
  3. We were leaving the next morning, so we packed our bags.
  4. The train was about to leave when I arrived.
Show the answers
  1. A past intention that didn't happen.
  2. Future seen from the past (and it happened).
  3. An arrangement planned in the past.
  4. Immediate future in the past.

2. Complete

Complete with the right form (was going to, would, was about to, was due to).

  1. I ___ (go) to the gym yesterday, but it was closed.
  2. When I met Paul in 2015, I didn't know he ___ (become) my husband.
  3. We ___ (just / leave) when the phone rang. (immediately)
  4. He promised he ___ (help) us the next day.
  5. The meeting ___ (start) at nine, but it began at ten. (scheduled)
  6. Look at those clouds! I knew it ___ (rain).
Show the answers
  1. was going to go
  2. would become
  3. were just about to leave
  4. would help
  5. was due to start
  6. was going to rain (or would rain)

3. Rewrite

Rewrite each sentence with the form given.

  1. I planned to visit my aunt, but I didn't have time. (was going to)
  2. She said: “I will send the report tomorrow.” (reported speech)
  3. We had arranged to meet at eight. (was / were + -ing)
  4. The plane was going to take off in a few seconds. (about to)
  5. Later he became a famous writer. (was to)
Show the answers
  1. I was going to visit my aunt, but I didn't have time.
  2. She said (that) she would send the report the next day.
  3. We were meeting at eight.
  4. The plane was about to take off.
  5. He was (later) to become a famous writer.

4. Correct the mistakes

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. I knew she will be late.
  2. I would call you yesterday, but I forgot.
  3. He said he would phone when he would arrive.
  4. We were about leaving when it started to rain.
Show the answers
  1. I knew she would be late.
  2. I was going to call you yesterday, but I forgot.
  3. He said he would phone when he arrived.
  4. We were about to leave when it started to rain.

5. Choose

Choose the best option.

  1. I ___ call you, but I forgot. (was going to / would)
  2. She knew she ___ never forget that day. (would / was about to)
  3. We ___ leave when she arrived. (were about to / would)
  4. He didn't know yet that he ___ become president. (was to / was about to)
Show the answers
  1. was going to
  2. would
  3. were about to
  4. was to (or would)

6. Your turn

Tell the story of a plan that didn't go as expected (4–5 sentences), with at least four future-in-the-past forms.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. Last summer, I was going to spend two weeks in Canada with my cousin. My flight was due to leave Brussels on 2 July. I was just about to go to the airport when I realised my passport had expired. I had promised my cousin I would arrive on time, so I felt terrible. In the end, I flew a week later, and it turned out to be the best holiday of my life.
Key points
  • Future in the past = move the auxiliary back: will → would, am going to → was going to, am about to → was about to.
  • was going to = a past intention, often not carried out. Never “I would call you yesterday”.
  • would = future seen from the past in stories and reported speech (I knew he would come).
  • was about to (immediate), was + -ing (arrangement), was due to (schedule), was to (destiny, formal).
  • No “would” after when / as soon as: “when he arrived”.
