The possessive ’s (Paul’s car)

To say that a car belongs to Paul, English says “Paul's car”: first the owner, then 's, then the thing. You need this structure to talk about your family, your friends and the things that belong to them.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Build the possessive: owner + 's + thing, in the right order.
  • Put the apostrophe in the right place in the singular, in plurals with -s (my parents') and in irregular plurals (children's).
  • Use the possessive with no noun after it (at Marie's, at the doctor's).
  • Choose between “'s” (people, animals, time) and “of” (things).

The structure: owner first

In English, the owner comes first: owner + 's + thing → Paul's car. There is no article before the thing: don't say “the Paul's car”.

The owner can be a first name, a family name, or a noun with its determiner: my sister's room, the teacher's desk, our neighbour's dog.

the dog of our neighboursour neighbours' dogour neighbours + ' + dog
the toys of the childrenthe children's toysthe children + 's + toys
  • This is Karim's bike.
    The bike belongs to Karim.
  • My mother's name is Odile.
    The name of my mother.
  • Is that the manager's office?
    The office of the manager.

Where does the apostrophe go?

Singular: add 's → the boy's bag (one boy).

Regular plural with -s: add only the apostrophe after the s → the boys' bags (several boys), my parents' house. When you speak, boy's, boys and boys' sound the same: the situation helps.

Irregular plural with no -s (children, men, women, people): add 's → the children's room, women's rights, people's opinions.

Names ending in s: both forms exist: James's car or James' car. “James's” is more common in modern British English.

OwnerRuleExample
singular+ 'smy brother's flat (one brother)
plural with -s+ 'my brothers' flat (two or more brothers)
irregular plural+ 'sthe men's team, the children's toys
name ending in -s+ 's or 'Thomas's phone / Thomas' phone

Two owners, and no noun after 's

When two people own one thing together, put 's only after the second name: Paul and Marie's flat (they share it). If each person has their own, put 's after both: Paul's and Marie's cars.

You can leave out the noun after 's when it is clear: Whose bag is this? — It's Ana's. This is very common with places: at my aunt's (= at my aunt's house), at the doctor's, at the hairdresser's, the baker's (the bakery).

  • We're having dinner at Julie's tonight.
    at Julie's = at Julie's home.
  • I've got an appointment at the dentist's.
    at the dentist's = at the dentist's surgery.
  • Whose keys are these? — They're Omar's.
    No noun after Omar's.
  • Tom and Lisa's wedding is in June.
    One wedding, two people.
“Whose?” is the question for possession: Whose phone is this? Don't confuse it with “Who's” (= who is): they sound the same.

's or of?

Use 's mainly with people, animals, countries, organisations and time expressions: my friend's car, the cat's food, Canada's capital, the company's results, today's newspaper, a week's holiday.

With things, use “the … of …”: the end of the film, the name of the street, the door of the car. For some common pairs, you can also put a noun before a noun: the car door, the street name.

With a long owner (several words), “of” is also more natural: the house of the family next door (not “the family next door's house”).

OwnerBest formExample
person, animal'smy aunt's garden, the dog's bed
time'stoday's meeting, next week's exam
thingof / noun + nounthe top of the page, the kitchen window
long group of wordsofthe name of the woman in the red dress

The 's trap: is, has or possessive?

The letters 's can mean three things: “is” (She's French), “has” (She's got a car) or possession (Sophie's car). To know which, look at the next word: a noun → possession; an adjective, a noun with an article or an -ing verb → is; got → has.

Don't confuse possession with the plural: “my friends” (more than one friend, no apostrophe), “my friend's car” (the car of my friend).

  • Marc's tired. → Marc is tired.
    's + adjective = is.
  • Marc's car is new.
    's + noun = possession.
  • Marc's got a new car. → Marc has got a new car.
    's + got = has.

The formula

owner + 's + noun (Paul's car) · plural with -s + ' (my parents' house) · irregular plural + 's (children's toys)

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • 's sounds like the plural -s: /s/ after p, t, k, f (Pat's /pæts/), /z/ after a vowel or voiced sound (Paul's /pɔːlz/), /ɪz/ after s, z, sh, ch, j sounds (Alice's /ˈælɪsɪz/, George's /ˈdʒɔːdʒɪz/).
  • “parents'” sounds exactly like “parents”: /ˈpeərənts/. You can't hear the apostrophe.
  • “James's” is /ˈdʒeɪmzɪz/, with an extra syllable; “James'” is usually /dʒeɪmz/.
  • “Whose” and “who's” both sound like /huːz/.
  • In “at the doctor's”, stress DOC-: /ət ðə ˈdɒktəz/.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

After a party in a flat in Brussels, two friends tidy up and find things people forgot.

Lina

Whose jacket is this? It's on the sofa.
Whose? = who does it belong to?

Hugo

I think it's Yannick's. He's always got a black jacket.
No noun after Yannick's.

Lina

And these keys? Are they your brother's?
your brother's = your brother's keys.

Hugo

No, they're my parents'. They're for their car. My mum's going to call me, I'm sure!
parents': plural owner; mum's = mum is.

Lina

Ha ha. And look, the children's drawings are on the table.
children's: irregular plural.

Hugo

Those are my sister's kids. They're at my aunt's tonight.
at my aunt's = at my aunt's house.

Lina

OK. Is the last piece of cake for anyone?
the last piece of cake: of with things.

Hugo

Yes! It's for Mariam and Léo's little girl. It's her favourite.
Two parents, one girl: 's after the second name.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Make the possessive

Rewrite with 's.

  1. the car of my father
  2. the room of Léa
  3. the bikes of the children
  4. the house of my grandparents
  5. the office of the boss
  6. the name of the dog
Show the answers
  1. my father's car
  2. Léa's room
  3. the children's bikes
  4. my grandparents' house
  5. the boss's office
  6. the dog's name

2. Where does the apostrophe go?

Add the apostrophe in the right place.

  1. my sisters bag (one sister)
  2. my sisters bags (two sisters)
  3. the womens team
  4. the students books (many students)
Show the answers
  1. my sister's bag
  2. my sisters' bags
  3. the women's team
  4. the students' books

3. 's or of?

Choose the most natural form.

  1. (the end of the story / the story's end)
  2. (the phone of my brother / my brother's phone)
  3. (today's newspaper / the newspaper of today)
  4. (the top of the page / the page's top)
Show the answers
  1. the end of the story
  2. my brother's phone
  3. today's newspaper
  4. the top of the page

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Correct it.

  1. The Marie's flat is very nice.
  2. Who's coat is this?
  3. My parent's car is red. (both parents)
  4. The mens room is on the left.
  5. This is the computer of Anna.
Show the answers
  1. Marie's flat is very nice.
  2. Whose coat is this?
  3. My parents' car is red.
  4. The men's room is on the left.
  5. This is Anna's computer.

5. Write the sentence

Use the words to write a sentence with 's or '.

  1. she / be / the wife of my brother
  2. I / sleep / at the home of my cousin / tonight
  3. the toys of the children / be / in the garden
  4. whose bag / this? — it / belong to / Fatou
Show the answers
  1. She's my brother's wife.
  2. I'm sleeping at my cousin's tonight.
  3. The children's toys are in the garden.
  4. Whose bag is this? — It's Fatou's.

6. Your turn

Write four sentences about your family or friends with 's: someone's job or name, a thing that belongs to someone, a place (at my…'s), and a sentence with a plural (my parents').

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. My sister's name is Inès. My best friend's car is a small red Fiat. On Sundays we have lunch at my grandmother's. My parents' house is near the river.
Key points
  • Owner + 's + thing, no article: Paul's car, my sister's room.
  • Plural with -s → ' (my parents' house); irregular plural → 's (children's, women's).
  • No noun after: It's Ana's; at my aunt's, at the doctor's.
  • 's for people, animals, time; of for things (the end of the film).
  • Whose? = who does it belong to? ≠ who's (= who is).
