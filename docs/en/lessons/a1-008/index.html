The present continuous

The present continuous (be + verb-ing) describes what is happening now, or around now for a short time. Many languages use one present tense for this; English uses a different form, and this lesson shows you when.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Make the present continuous: am / is / are + verb-ing, in all forms.
  • Use the spelling rules for -ing (making, running, lying).
  • Use the present continuous for actions happening now, temporary situations and changes.
  • Know the signal words: now, at the moment, today, this week, Look!, Listen!

The form: be + verb-ing

Make the present continuous with “to be” in the present (am, is, are) + the verb with -ing. All the rules of “to be” from lessons 1 and 4 work here: short forms, negatives with not, questions with a change of order, short answers with be.

The -ing verb never changes with the subject: only “be” changes.

ExampleNote
PositiveI'm working. / She's working. / They're working.be + -ing
NegativeI'm not working. / He isn't working. / We aren't working.be + not + -ing
QuestionAre you working? / Is she working?be before the subject
Short answerYes, I am. / No, she isn't.be only
Wh- questionWhat are you doing? / Where is he going?Wh- + be + subject + -ing
  • I'm cooking dinner.
    An action now.
  • They aren't listening.
    Negative.
  • What are you reading? — A novel.
    Wh- question.

Spelling of -ing

Most verbs just add -ing. Three rules change the spelling.

Double the last letter in one-syllable verbs that end in consonant + vowel + consonant (run, sit, stop), and in two-syllable verbs stressed on the last syllable (beGIN → beginning). Don't double w, x or y: snow → snowing, fix → fixing, play → playing.

RuleExamples
most verbs: + ingwork → working, eat → eating, play → playing, study → studying
silent e at the end: remove itmake → making, write → writing, come → coming, dance → dancing
consonant + vowel + consonant: doublerun → running, sit → sitting, swim → swimming, stop → stopping, begin → beginning
-ie → -yinglie → lying, die → dying, tie → tying
-ee: keep everythingsee → seeing, agree → agreeing
British / American: travel → travelling (UK) / traveling (US). British English doubles a final l even when the stress is not on the last syllable.

Use 1: what is happening now

The first use is an action happening at the moment of speaking. Here the present continuous is necessary: “I eat” is wrong for this moment.

Signal words: now, right now, at the moment, at present. “Look!” and “Listen!” show an action that is happening.

  • Sorry, I can't talk. I'm driving.
    Happening now.
  • Look! It's raining.
    Look! + action now.
  • Listen! Someone is knocking at the door.
    Listen! + action now.
  • The children are sleeping right now.
    right now.

Use 2: a temporary situation around now

The present continuous also describes an activity that continues for some time around now, even if you are not doing it at this second. The idea is “in this period of my life, for a limited time”.

Signal words: today, this week, this month, this year, these days, currently.

Compare: “I live in Lyon” (present simple: my permanent situation) / “I'm living in Lyon this year” (present continuous: temporary).

  • I'm reading a very good book this week.
    Not necessarily at this moment.
  • She's working from home today.
    Usually she doesn't.
  • We're learning Spanish this year.
    A temporary project.

Use 3: a change in progress

Use the present continuous for a situation that is slowly changing, often with verbs like get (become), become, change, grow, increase, improve.

This tense can also talk about future plans that are already arranged (I'm meeting Paul tomorrow). You will study this use in lesson 50. For now, just know that it exists.

  • It's getting cold.
    The temperature is going down.
  • Your English is improving!
    It is getting better.
  • Prices are going up.
    A change.

Verbs that don't take -ing

Some verbs describe a state, not an action: like, love, hate, want, need, know, understand, believe, remember, own, belong. Use them in the present simple, even for now: I want a coffee now (not “I'm wanting”). Lesson 9 explains this and compares the two present tenses.

“Have” for possession follows the same rule (I have a car), but “have” for an action can take -ing: I'm having lunch.

  • I don't understand. ✓ / I'm not understanding. ✗
    State verb: present simple.
  • She's having a shower. ✓
    “have” = an action: -ing is possible.

The formula

subject + am / is / are (+ not) + verb-ing · Am / Is / Are + subject + verb-ing?

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • -ing is /ɪŋ/: a short i and a nasal sound at the back of the mouth, with no hard g at the end.
  • In informal speech you often hear /ɪn/ (“workin'”): understand it, but don't write it.
  • Short forms join the subject: I'm /aɪm/, she's /ʃiːz/, they're /ðeə/. In the sentence, stress the -ing verb: I'm WORking.
  • In “What are you doing?”, “are” is weak: /wɒt ə jə ˈduːɪŋ/. Informally it can sound like “Whatcha doing?”.
  • Doubled verbs keep a short sound: running /ˈrʌnɪŋ/ (short u). Compare writing /ˈraɪtɪŋ/ (long i).

In a conversation

Listening Studio

On a Saturday afternoon, Malik, in Brussels, calls his sister Inès in Toulouse.

Malik

Hi, Inès! What are you doing?
Wh- question, present continuous.

Inès

Hi! I'm sitting in a café with Zoé. We're having a hot chocolate.
sitting: double t; having = drinking (action).

Malik

Nice! It's raining here. Is it raining in Toulouse?
Weather now.

Inès

No, it isn't. The sun is shining. Are you working today?
Short answer with be.

Malik

No, I'm not. I'm painting my new flat this weekend.
A temporary activity: this weekend.

Inès

Wow! Is Sami helping you?
Question with is.

Malik

Yes, he is, but he's sleeping on the sofa right now!
right now.

Inès

Ha ha! Typical Sami. Send me a photo!
Imperative.

  6. stop

  2. swimming
  3. playing
  4. lying
  5. writing

2. Complete

Put the verb in the present continuous.

  1. Be quiet! The baby ___ (sleep).
  2. We ___ (not / watch) TV. You can turn it off.
  3. ___ you ___ (wait) for the bus?
  4. Look! Those men ___ (run) to the station.
  5. I ___ (study) for an exam this week.
Show the answers
  1. is sleeping
  2. aren't watching
  3. Are … waiting
  4. are running
  5. am studying / 'm studying

3. Questions and short answers

Write the question in the present continuous, then the short answer shown.

  1. you / work / today? (no)
  2. it / rain / in Brussels? (yes)
  3. your parents / travel / this month? (yes)
  4. Léo / listen? (no)
Show the answers
  1. Are you working today? — No, I'm not.
  2. Is it raining in Brussels? — Yes, it is.
  3. Are your parents travelling this month? — Yes, they are.
  4. Is Léo listening? — No, he isn't.

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Correct it.

  1. She cooking dinner now.
  2. Listen! Someone sings.
  3. Do you waiting for me?
  4. I'm knowing the answer.
  5. They are siting in the garden.
Show the answers
  1. She's cooking dinner now. / She is cooking dinner now.
  2. Listen! Someone is singing.
  3. Are you waiting for me?
  4. I know the answer.
  5. They are sitting in the garden.

5. Describe the situation

Write one sentence in the present continuous for each situation.

  1. You look out of the window. You see snow falling. (Look!)
  2. Your friend asks about your activity now. You have a book in your hands.
  3. This week your sister doesn't go to the office. (from home)
  4. The temperature is going up. (get / hot)
Show the answers
  1. Look! It's snowing.
  2. What are you doing? — I'm reading.
  3. She's working from home this week.
  4. It's getting hot.

6. Your turn

Write four sentences in the present continuous: what you are doing now, what a person near you is doing, a temporary project this week or month, and a change in progress.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. I'm sitting at my desk and I'm doing this exercise. My son is playing in the garden. This month I'm learning to drive. It's getting dark outside.
Key points
  • am / is / are + verb-ing. Negative with not; question with be first (never do).
  • Spelling: making (no e), running (double consonant), lying (-ie → -y).
  • Uses: action now (now, Look!), temporary situation (this week), change (It's getting cold).
  • State verbs (want, know, like, understand): present simple, no -ing.
  • “I eat” = habit; “I'm eating” = now. You must choose.
