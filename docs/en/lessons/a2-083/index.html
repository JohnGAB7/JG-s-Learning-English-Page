Work and jobs

“What do you do?” is one of the first questions people will ask you. In this lesson you learn to say your job (with the article “a”!), describe your work and your hours, talk about where you work and your conditions, and mention a change of job. You will use the present simple for habits and the present continuous for temporary situations.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Ask and say what someone does with “I’m a / an…” and “I work as…”.
  • Describe your tasks, your working hours and your workplace.
  • Use the right prepositions: work for, work in, work at, work on, work as.
  • Talk about looking for a job and working conditions (full-time, salary, apply for…).

Saying your job

The natural question is “What do you do?” or “What do you do for a living?”. “What’s your job?” exists but is more direct.

Answer: “I’m a nurse.” “She’s an engineer.” In English the article “a / an” is necessary before a job in the singular. In the plural: “They’re teachers.”

Other answers: “I work as a receptionist.”, “I’m self-employed.” (I work for myself), “I’m unemployed / I’m looking for a job.”, “I’m retired.” (I stopped working because of my age), “I’m a student.”

  • What do you do? — I’m an accountant.
    an before a vowel sound.
  • My sister works as a nurse in a big hospital.
    work as + job.
  • My father is retired. He was a taxi driver.
    Past job: was a…

Common jobs

Many job words end in -er, -or, -ist or -ian: “teach → teacher”, “act → actor”, “science → scientist”, “electric → electrician”.

Most job words are neutral in English: “a doctor”, “a teacher”, “a lawyer” are for men and women. Today people prefer “a police officer” (not policeman / policewoman), “a firefighter” (not fireman), “a flight attendant” (not steward / stewardess), and “an actor” for everyone.

Words that confuse learners: “a librarian” works in a library (a shop that sells books = a bookshop); “a chemist” (UK) = a pharmacist; “a physician” (mainly US) = a doctor, not a physicist.

    Job + what she does.

Work for, in, at, on, as

“work for” + company / boss: “I work for a bank.” “She works for Mr Diallo.”

“work in” + sector or general place: “I work in marketing / in tourism / in an office / in a hospital.” “work at” + a specific place: “He works at the Hilton / at Brussels Airport.”

“work as” + job: “I work as a translator.” “work on” + project: “We’re working on a new website.” “work from home”: do your job at home.

  • I work for a telecom company in Douala.
    work for + company.
  • She works in finance, at a big bank in Toronto.
    in + sector, at + place.
  • This month we’re working on a new project.
    work on + project (temporary: present continuous).
  • I work from home on Mondays and Fridays.
    work from home.

Describing your work and hours

For habits, use the present simple: “I start at eight and finish at five.” “I deal with customers.” (I help and talk to them), “I’m responsible for the accounts.”, “I travel a lot for work.”

For a temporary situation, use the present continuous (lesson 9): “I’m working in Brussels this month.” “I’m training a new colleague at the moment.”

Hours and contracts: “full-time”, “part-time”, “shifts” (fixed periods of work, e.g. “I work night shifts”), “overtime” (extra hours), “a day off”, “on holiday” (US “on vacation”).

  • I work full-time, from Monday to Friday.
    full-time = about 35–40 hours a week.
  • Nurses often work night shifts.
    shift = a period of work.
  • I’m responsible for ten people in my team.
    responsible for.

Looking for a job, changing jobs

Vocabulary: “a job” (countable), “work” (uncountable: never “a work” for a job), “a career”, “a colleague” (a person you work with), “a boss / a manager”.

The steps: “look for a job”, “apply for a job”, “send your CV” (US “résumé”), “have an interview”, “get the job”, “earn a salary” (money paid every year or month) or “wages” (money paid per hour or week).

Changes: “I’ve just changed jobs.” “She got a promotion.” (a better job in the same company); “He lost his job.”; “I resigned” or “I quit” (I left my job; “quit” is more informal).

  • I’m looking for a new job. I applied for three jobs last week.
    apply for.
  • She had an interview yesterday and she got the job!
    have an interview, get the job.
  • He earns a good salary, but he works long hours.
    earn = receive money for work.
Careful: “I have a lot of work” (uncountable), but “I have two jobs” (countable). And for the daily trip to work, say “commute”: “I commute to work by train.”

The formula

What do you do? — I’m a / an + job. · I work as a… · I work for + company / in + sector / at + place. · I’m responsible for… · I work full-time / part-time.

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “engineer” /ˌendʒɪˈnɪə/: stress on the last syllable, “en-ji-NEER”.
  • “accountant” /əˈkaʊntənt/: stress on “COUNT”.
  • “colleague” /ˈkɒliːɡ/: stress on the first syllable, “KOL-eeg”.
  • “career” /kəˈrɪə/: stress on the second syllable, “kuh-REER”; don’t confuse it with “Korea”.
  • “nurse” /nɜːs/: the vowel /ɜː/ as in “bird”; “work” /wɜːk/ too.
  • “What do you do?” is often very fast: /wɒdʒə ˈduː/, “wotcha DOO?”.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

At a wedding in Ottawa, Samuel (from Cameroon) meets Claire, a Canadian guest.

Claire

So, what do you do, Samuel?
The natural question.

Samuel

I’m an engineer. I work for a solar energy company.
an + job; work for + company.

Claire

That sounds interesting! What does your job involve?
involve = what tasks are part of it.

Samuel

I design solar panels for schools and hospitals. I travel a lot for work.
Present simple: habits.

Claire

Do you like it?
A simple follow-up question.

Samuel

Yes, I love it, but I work long hours. And you?
long hours = many hours.

Claire

I’m a nurse. I work at the General Hospital, but only part-time now.
at + specific place.

Samuel

Do you work night shifts?
shifts.

Claire

Sometimes. And at the moment I’m studying to become a midwife.
Temporary: present continuous. A midwife helps women give birth.

Samuel

Wow, good luck with that!
Encouraging.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Which job?

Read the description and give the job with “a” or “an”.

  1. This person repairs cars.
  2. This person looks after patients in a hospital.
  3. This person works in a library.
  4. This person installs and repairs electrical systems.
  5. This person grows food and keeps animals.
Show the answers
  1. a mechanic
  2. a nurse
  3. a librarian
  4. an electrician
  5. a farmer

2. The right preposition

Complete with for, in, at, as or on.

  1. I work ___ a big insurance company.
  2. She works ___ a waitress at the weekend.
  3. He works ___ tourism.
  4. We’re working ___ a new app this month.
  5. My mother works ___ Douala Airport.
  6. I’m responsible ___ the budget.
Show the answers
  1. for
  2. as
  3. in
  4. on
  5. at
  6. for

3. Job or work?

Complete with “job” or “work”.

  1. I’ve got a lot of ___ today.
  2. She’s got a new ___ in Montreal.
  3. I go to ___ by bus.
  4. He’s looking for a part-time ___.
Show the answers
  1. work
  2. job
  3. work
  4. job

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. I’m nurse.
  2. What do you do in the life?
  3. He works like a waiter.
  4. I applied to a job yesterday.
  5. She works part-times.
  6. I’m looking for a work.
Show the answers
  1. I’m a nurse.
  2. What do you do?
  3. He works as a waiter.
  4. I applied for a job yesterday.
  5. She works part-time.
  6. I’m looking for work. / I’m looking for a job.

5. Your turn

Present your job (or a relative’s job) in 5 or 6 sentences: job, employer or sector, tasks, hours, what you like or don’t like.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. I’m an accountant. I work for a small transport company in Yaoundé. I’m responsible for the accounts and I deal with our banks and customers. I work full-time, from eight to five, and I work from home on Fridays. I like my colleagues and my job is interesting, but sometimes I have to work overtime at the end of the month. Next year I’d like to apply for a manager’s job.
Key points
  • What do you do? — I’m a nurse / an engineer. Always use “a / an” before a job.
  • work for + company · in + sector / place · at + specific place · as + role · on + project.
  • “a job” is countable, “work” is uncountable: I’ve got a lot of work / a new job.
  • Habits: present simple (I start at eight); temporary situations: present continuous (I’m working in Brussels this month).
  • Vocabulary: full-time, part-time, shifts, apply for, interview, salary, retired, self-employed.
