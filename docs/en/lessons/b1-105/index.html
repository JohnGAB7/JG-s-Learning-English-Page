Reported questions

Reporting a question needs two more changes than reporting a statement: the word order becomes the order of a statement, and the auxiliary do / does / did disappears. “Where do you live?” becomes “She asked me where I lived.” We also report requests and orders with ask / tell + someone + to.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level B1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Report a wh- question with asked + question word + subject + verb (no inversion, no do).
  • Report a yes/no question with if or whether.
  • Apply the tense, pronoun and time-word changes from lesson 104.
  • Report a request or an order with ask / tell + person + (not) to + verb.

Wh- questions

Keep the question word (what, where, when, why, how, who, which, how much…), then use statement word order: subject + verb. There is no question mark.

The auxiliary do / does / did disappears, and the main verb takes the backshifted tense: “Where do you work?” → “He asked me where I worked.” “What did you buy?” → “She asked me what I had bought.”

The most common reporting verbs are ask, want to know and wonder. Ask can be followed by a person (asked me) or not (asked).

Direct questionReported question
“Where do you live?”She asked me where I lived.
“What are you doing?”He asked me what I was doing.
“Why did you leave?”They asked her why she had left.
“How much does it cost?”I asked how much it cost.
“When will you arrive?”She wanted to know when I would arrive.
“Who has taken my pen?”He asked who had taken his pen.

Yes/no questions: if / whether

For a question that has a yes or no answer, use if or whether, then statement word order. “Are you hungry?” → “She asked me if I was hungry.”

Whether is a little more formal and is used especially when there are two options: “He asked whether I wanted tea or coffee.” We also say “whether or not”.

  • “Do you speak French?” → She asked me if I spoke French.
    Do disappears; speak → spoke.
  • “Have you seen my keys?” → He asked if I had seen his keys.
    Present perfect → past perfect.
  • “Can you help me?” → She asked me whether I could help her.
    Can → could.
  • “Is it raining?” → He wanted to know if it was raining.
    Statement order: it was.

Pronouns, tenses and time words

All the rules from lesson 104 apply: tenses move back after a past reporting verb, and pronouns, possessives, and time and place words change (tomorrow → the next day, here → there).

If the reporting verb is in the present (He asks / wants to know), there is no backshift: “The customer wants to know when the parcel will arrive.”

  • “Will you come to my party tomorrow?” → She asked me if I would come to her party the next day.
    will → would; my → her; tomorrow → the next day.
  • “What time does this shop close?” → The tourist wants to know what time this shop closes.
    Present reporting verb: no backshift.

Requests and orders: ask / tell + to

To report a polite request (Could you…? Please…), use ask + person + to + verb. To report an order or instruction (imperative), use tell + person + to + verb.

In the negative, not goes before to: “Don't be late” → “She told me not to be late.” Not “She told me don't be late”. (“to not be late” is heard in speech, but avoid it in careful English.)

Direct speechReported speech
“Could you open the window, please?”She asked me to open the window.
“Sit down.”The teacher told us to sit down.
“Please don't smoke here.”He asked us not to smoke there.
“Don't touch that!”My mother told me not to touch it.
  • My boss asked me to send the report before Friday.
    Polite request → asked + to.
  • The police officer told the driver not to move.
    Negative order → told + not to.

Polite indirect questions

The same structure (question word + subject + verb) is used in polite indirect questions in the present: “Could you tell me where the station is?”, “Do you know what time it starts?”. This is the topic of lesson 114.

The trap is the same in both cases: keeping the inversion of the direct question. “Can you tell me where is the station?” is wrong.

  • Do you know where the post office is?
    Not “where is the post office”.

The formula

asked (+ person) + wh- / if / whether + subject + verb (tense moved back) · asked / told + person + (not) to + verb

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • asked /ɑːskt/ (US /æskt/): three consonants at the end, s-k-t. Don't say “ask”: the -ed /t/ must be heard.
  • A reported question doesn't have question intonation: the voice falls at the end, like a statement. “She asked me where I LIVED ↘.”
  • whether /ˈweðə/ sounds like weather.
  • In “told me not to”, the stress falls on not: “She told me NOT to wait.”

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ She asked me where did I live.✓ She asked me where I lived.No did and no inversion in a reported question.
✗ He asked me where was the station.✓ He asked me where the station was.Statement order: subject + verb.
✗ She asked me do I like coffee.✓ She asked me if I liked coffee.Yes/no question: if / whether.
✗ He asked me what time is it.✓ He asked me what time it was.Statement order and backshift.
✗ She asked if I was hungry?✓ She asked if I was hungry.No question mark.
✗ He told me don't call him.✓ He told me not to call him.Negative order: told + person + not to.
✗ She said me to wait.✓ She told me to wait. / She asked me to wait.Don't use say + person + to for orders: use tell or ask.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

Brussels, after a job interview. Olivier tells his flatmate Emmanuel, from Kinshasa, about the questions he was asked.

Emmanuel

So, what did they ask you?
A direct question.

Olivier

First, they asked me why I wanted to work for them.
Why + subject + verb.

Emmanuel

Classic. What else?
What else = what other things.

Olivier

The manager asked me if I had ever managed a team, and how many people I had worked with.
If + past perfect; how many + subject + verb.

Emmanuel

Did they ask whether you spoke Dutch?
Whether = if.

Olivier

Yes! And they wanted to know when I could start.
Can → could.

Emmanuel

That sounds positive. Anything else?
Anything else = any other thing.

Olivier

At the end, they asked me to send them two references and told me not to worry about the test.
Asked + to; told + not to.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Choose the right sentence

Choose the correct reported question (a or b).

  1. “Where are you going?” a) She asked me where was I going. b) She asked me where I was going.
  2. “Do you like jazz?” a) He asked if I liked jazz. b) He asked did I like jazz.
  3. “What time is it?” a) I asked what time it was. b) I asked what time was it.
  4. “Don't shout!” a) She told us to not shouting. b) She told us not to shout.
Show the answers
  1. b
  2. a
  3. a
  4. b

2. Wh- questions

Report the question, starting with “She asked me…”.

  1. “Where do you work?”
  2. “What are you reading?”
  3. “Why did you call?”
  4. “How long will you stay?”
  5. “Who is your teacher?”
Show the answers
  1. She asked me where I worked.
  2. She asked me what I was reading.
  3. She asked me why I had called.
  4. She asked me how long I would stay.
  5. She asked me who my teacher was.

3. Yes/no questions

Report the question with if or whether, starting with “He asked (me)…”.

  1. “Are you married?”
  2. “Can you drive?”
  3. “Have you finished the report?”
  4. “Do you want tea or coffee?”
Show the answers
  1. He asked me if (whether) I was married.
  2. He asked me if (whether) I could drive.
  3. He asked me if (whether) I had finished the report.
  4. He asked me whether I wanted tea or coffee.

4. Requests and orders

Report with asked / told + person + (not) to.

  1. The doctor to me: “Stay in bed.”
  2. My friend to me: “Could you lend me your car?”
  3. The guide to the tourists: “Don't take photos.”
  4. My neighbour to us: “Please don't make noise after ten.”
Show the answers
  1. The doctor told me to stay in bed.
  2. My friend asked me to lend him (her) my car.
  3. The guide told the tourists not to take photos.
  4. My neighbour asked us not to make noise after ten.

5. Correct the mistakes

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. She asked me where did I buy my shoes.
  2. They asked us were we tired.
  3. He told me don't be late.
  4. I asked her how old is she.
Show the answers
  1. She asked me where I had bought my shoes. / She asked me where I bought my shoes.
  2. They asked us if (whether) we were tired.
  3. He told me not to be late.
  4. I asked her how old she was.

6. Your turn

Think of an interview, a doctor's appointment or a first meeting. In 4–5 sentences, report the questions you were asked, and one request or order.

  1. Example answer
Show the answers
  1. Last week I went to the doctor's. She asked me how long I had had the cough. She wanted to know if I smoked and whether I was taking any medicine. Then she asked me to breathe deeply. At the end, she told me to drink a lot of water and not to go to work for two days.
Key points
  • Reported wh- question: asked + question word + subject + verb (no do, no inversion).
  • Yes/no question: asked + if / whether + subject + verb.
  • Backshift and pronoun changes as for statements.
  • No question mark and no rising intonation.
  • Request: asked + person + to; order: told + person + to; negative: not to.
