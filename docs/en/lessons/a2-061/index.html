Adverbs of manner (-ly)

Adverbs of manner say how you do something: slowly, well, carefully. Most of them are formed with -ly (slow → slowly). But some very common ones are irregular (good → well, fast, hard), and their position in the sentence follows strict rules.

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A2

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Form an -ly adverb from an adjective, with the spelling rules.
  • Know the irregular adverbs: well, fast, hard, late, early, and the false friends hardly and lately.
  • Put the adverb in the right place: after the verb and its object, never between them.
  • Choose between an adjective and an adverb, especially after look, feel, sound, smell, taste.

Adjective or adverb?

An adjective describes a noun (a slow car, she is careful). An adverb of manner describes a verb: the way someone does the action (the car moves slowly, she drives carefully).

Compare: He's a careful driver (adjective, before the noun driver) / He drives carefully (adverb, after the verb drives). Both sentences have the same meaning.

  • She's a quiet person. She speaks quietly.
    quiet → quietly.
  • The test was easy. I passed it easily.
    easy → easily.
  • He's a bad singer. He sings badly.
    bad → badly.

Forming the adverb: spelling rules

Usually, add -ly to the adjective: slow → slowly, careful → carefully (double l, because careful already ends in l), quick → quickly.

Some endings change the spelling.

Adjective endingRuleExamples
most adjectives+ lyquick → quickly, bad → badly, careful → carefully, polite → politely
consonant + yy → ilyeasy → easily, happy → happily, angry → angrily
-le (after a consonant)e → ygentle → gently, simple → simply, possible → possibly, terrible → terribly
-ic+ allyfantastic → fantastically, automatic → automatically (exception: public → publicly)
-ll+ yfull → fully, dull → dully
-ueremove the etrue → truly

Irregular adverbs and false friends

The adverb of “good” is “well”: She's a good cook. She cooks well. This is the most common irregular adverb, and the one learners forget most.

Some adverbs have the same form as the adjective: fast (a fast car / he drives fast), hard (hard work / she works hard), late (a late train / I arrived late), early (an early flight / we left early), straight (go straight on). Never say “fastly”.

Be careful: “hardly” and “lately” exist, but with a DIFFERENT meaning. Hardly = almost not (I can hardly hear you). Lately = recently (I've been tired lately).

AdjectiveAdverbTrap
goodwell“goodly” doesn't exist
fastfast“fastly” doesn't exist
hard (difficult, with effort)hard (with effort)hardly = almost not
late (not on time)late (not on time)lately = recently
earlyearlysame form
  • You speak English very well!
    good → well.
  • Don't drive so fast!
    fast = adverb too.
  • She works hard, but she hardly sleeps.
    hard ≠ hardly.
Adjectives that already end in -ly (friendly, lovely, lonely, silly, ugly) have no adverb. Say “in a friendly way”: She smiled in a friendly way.

Position of adverbs of manner

An adverb of manner goes after the verb, or after the object if there is one. Golden rule: NEVER put it between the verb and its object. Say: He speaks English well (not “He speaks well English”).

For emphasis, some adverbs can go before the verb: She quickly closed the door. But “well” and the adverbs with the same form as the adjective (fast, hard) always go after.

PatternExample
verb + adverbShe runs fast.
verb + object + adverbShe plays the piano beautifully.
adverb + verb (emphasis)He slowly opened the letter.
❌ verb + adverb + object❌ She plays beautifully the piano.
  • Please read the instructions carefully.
    Object, then adverb.
  • He answered the question correctly.
    Object, then adverb.

Look, feel, sound… + adjective

After be, look, feel, sound, smell, taste, seem, become and get, use an ADJECTIVE, not an adverb: you describe the subject, not an action. You look tired. This soup tastes good. The idea sounds great.

Compare: She looks happy (her appearance: adjective) / She looked at me happily (the action of looking: adverb).

Special case: “well” can be an adjective meaning “healthy”: I don't feel well (I feel ill). “I feel good” means I feel happy, in good form.

  • Your plan sounds interesting.
    sound + adjective.
  • The flowers smell lovely.
    smell + adjective.
  • Are you OK? You don't look well.
    well = healthy.

The formula

adjective + ly → adverb (slowly, carefully, easily, gently) · good → well · fast, hard, late, early = same form · verb + object + adverb

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • The -ly ending is weak: /li/. The stress stays on the same syllable as the adjective: CAREful → CAREfully /ˈkeəfəli/.
  • “-fully” sounds like /fəli/, not “full-y”: beautifully /ˈbjuːtɪfli/.
  • “-ically”: you often hear only /ɪkli/ at the end: fantastically /fænˈtæstɪkli/.
  • “well” /wel/: one vowel sound, as in “bell”, with a clear l at the end.
  • “hardly” /ˈhɑːdli/: don't confuse it with “hard” when you speak; -ly changes the whole meaning.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy
✗ He speaks well English.✓ He speaks English well.Don't separate the verb and its object.
✗ She sings very good.✓ She sings very well.The adverb of good is well.
✗ He drives fastly.✓ He drives fast.Fast is the same as an adjective and an adverb.
✗ I work hardly.✓ I work hard.Hard = with effort; hardly = almost not.
✗ You look tiredly.✓ You look tired.After look, feel, sound…: adjective.
✗ The soup tastes well.✓ The soup tastes good.After taste: adjective.
✗ She answered friendly.✓ She answered in a friendly way.Friendly is an adjective; it has no -ly adverb.
✗ He did it easyly.✓ He did it easily.Consonant + y → ily.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

After a driving lesson in Namur, an instructor talks to her student.

Instructor

Well done, Kevin. You drove very carefully today.
carefully: how he drove.

Kevin

Thanks! But I parked the car badly, didn't I?
verb + object + adverb.

Instructor

A little. You need to turn the wheel more slowly. And you sometimes brake too hard.
more slowly; hard = adverb.

Kevin

I know. I get nervous when cars drive fast behind me.
get + adjective; drive fast.

Instructor

That's normal. Just breathe deeply and stay calm.
stay + adjective (calm).

Kevin

Do you think I'm ready for the test?
A question.

Instructor

Almost. You learn quickly and you listen well. Two more lessons, and you'll pass easily.
quickly, well, easily.

Kevin

That sounds great! See you on Thursday.
sound + adjective.

Key points
  • Adjective + ly → adverb; easy → easily, gentle → gently, fantastic → fantastically.
  • good → well; fast, hard, late, early: same form. hardly = almost not, lately = recently.
  • Position: verb + object + adverb (She speaks English well).
  • After be, look, feel, sound, smell, taste, seem: adjective (You look tired).
  • friendly, lovely, lonely are adjectives: in a friendly way.
