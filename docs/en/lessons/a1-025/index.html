Colours and clothes

We use colours and clothes to describe people, to go shopping and to choose what to wear. Two points need care: the colour goes before the noun, and some clothes are always plural (trousers, jeans).

⏱ 30–45 min✎ Written by JG◎ Level A1

Prefer explanations in French? Version française de cette leçon →

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
  • Name colours and make them more exact (light blue, dark green, bright red).
  • Name common clothes and accessories, with British and American differences.
  • Use words that are always plural correctly: trousers, jeans, shorts, a pair of…
  • Choose the right verb: wear, carry, put on, take off, try on.

Colours

A colour adjective goes before the noun and never takes an s: “a red car”, “red cars”. After “be”, it is alone: “The car is red.”

To be more exact: “light” (pale), “dark” (deep), “bright” (strong and shining): “light blue”, “dark green”. In speaking you can add “-ish”: “greenish” (more or less green).

The question is “What colour is it?”. Spelling: “colour”, “grey” in British English; “color”, “gray” in American English.

    Question and answer.

“Maroon” is a very dark red. For the colour of chocolate or wood, say “brown”.

Clothes and accessories

The word “clothes” is always plural and has no singular. For one thing, say “a piece of clothing” or use its name. “Cloth” (no e, no s) means material, or a piece of material for cleaning.

GroupBritish EnglishNote
Topsa shirt · a T-shirt · a blouse · a jumper · a jacket · a coatUS: sweater for jumper

  • He's wearing a white shirt and a black tie.
    Colour before each noun.
  • Take a jumper. It's cold in the evening.
    jumper = sweater.
In British English, “pants” means underwear! For the clothes on your legs, say “trousers” in the UK and “pants” in the USA and Canada.

Clothes that are always plural

Clothes with two equal parts (two legs) are always plural in English: trousers, jeans, shorts, pyjamas (US pajamas), tights. The verb is plural: “My jeans are new.”

To count them, say “a pair of”: “a pair of jeans”, “two pairs of trousers”. Never say “a jean” or “a trouser”.

The same is true for “glasses” and “scissors”. We also count shoes and socks in pairs: “a pair of shoes”.

  • My new jeans are too long.
    Plural verb: are.
  • I need a pair of black trousers for work.
    a pair of + plural noun.
  • These shorts are very comfortable.
    these + plural.

Wear, carry, put on, take off, try on

“Wear” is for clothes, glasses or jewellery on your body. “Carry” is for things you hold and take with you (a bag, a child, a suitcase).

“Put on” = start to wear; “take off” = remove; “try on” = wear something in a shop to check it; “get dressed” = put on your clothes.

For what you are wearing today, use the present continuous; for a habit, use the present simple (lesson 9): “Today I'm wearing jeans, but I usually wear a suit.”

VerbMeaningExample
wearhave on your bodyShe always wears a hat.
carryhold and take with youHe's carrying a big suitcase.
put onstart to wearPut on your coat!

try onwear to check in a shopCan I try on this dress?
  • Today I'm wearing jeans, but I usually wear a suit.
    Now vs habit.
  • Put on your boots, it's raining.
    put on = start to wear.

The formula

colour + clothes (a red dress) · a pair of + jeans / trousers / shoes · wear (on your body) ≠ carry (in your hand)

Pronunciation

Pronunciation Lab

  • “colour” /ˈkʌlə/: the first o sounds like the u in “but”.
  • “clothes” /kləʊðz/: one syllable. Many English speakers just say /kləʊz/, like “close”.
  • “shirt” /ʃɜːt/ and “skirt” /skɜːt/ have the same long vowel /ɜː/, like in “bird”.
  • “suit” /suːt/ rhymes with “boot”; you don't say the i.
  • “purple” /ˈpɜːpl/ and “orange” /ˈɒrɪndʒ/: stress on the first syllable; the end of “orange” sounds like “-rinj”.

Common mistakes

Don’t saySayWhy

✗ My trouser is black.✓ My trousers are black.Trousers, jeans, shorts: always plural.
✗ I want a jean.✓ I want a pair of jeans. / I want some jeans.Count them with “a pair of”.
✗ I carry a suit to work.✓ I wear a suit to work.Clothes on your body: “wear”.
✗ Which colour is your car?✓ What colour is your car?The normal question is “What colour…?”.
✗ It's cold, wear your coat!✓ It's cold, put on your coat!The action of starting to wear: “put on”.
✗ I need new cloths.✓ I need new clothes.“Clothes” = things you wear; “cloth” = material.

In a conversation

Listening Studio

In Lyon, Chloé helps her friend Ibrahim choose clothes for a wedding.

Ibrahim

What do I wear to the wedding on Saturday?
wear + clothes.

Chloé

What about your dark blue suit and a white shirt?
“What about…?” = a suggestion.

Ibrahim

Good idea. And a tie? I've got a red one and a green one.
“one” = tie, so we don't repeat it.

Chloé

The red one. Red and dark blue look great together.
Colours as nouns: red, dark blue.

Ibrahim

And shoes? My black shoes are old.
Plural: shoes are.

Chloé

Let's go to the shops. You need a new pair.
a pair (of shoes).

Ibrahim

OK. Can I wear my trainers to the party after?
trainers = sports shoes (US sneakers).

Chloé

Ha! Maybe. But take off your tie first!
take off = remove.

Exercises

Writing Workshop

From easy to harder. Answer without looking at the lesson, then check each exercise.

1. Mixing colours

What colour do you get?

  1. blue + yellow
  2. red + white
  3. black + white
  4. red + yellow
  5. blue + red
Show the answers
  1. green
  2. pink
  3. grey (US gray)
  4. orange
  5. purple

2. Is or are?

Complete with is or are.

  1. My jeans ___ new.
  2. This dress ___ beautiful.
  3. Your shoes ___ dirty.
  4. Her trousers ___ black.
  5. My jacket ___ warm.
  6. These shorts ___ comfortable.
Show the answers
  1. are
  2. is
  3. are
  4. are
  5. is
  6. are

3. The right verb

Complete with put on, wears, carries or take off.

  1. It's cold. ___ your coat!
  2. He always ___ a suit at work.
  3. She ___ her bag in her hand.
  4. ___ your shoes, please. The floor is clean.
Show the answers
  1. Put on
  2. wears
  3. carries
  4. Take off

4. Correct the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

  1. She's wearing a dress red.
  2. My trousers is grey.
  3. I've got two jeans.
  4. Which colour is your bag?
  5. Can I try these shoes on me?
Show the answers
  1. She's wearing a red dress.
  2. My trousers are grey. (US: My pants are gray.)
  3. I've got two pairs of jeans.
  4. What colour is your bag?
  5. Can I try on these shoes? / Can I try these shoes on?

5. Your turn

In three or four sentences, describe what you are wearing today and what you usually wear for work or school. Give the colours.

  1. Model answer
Show the answers
  1. Today I'm wearing blue jeans, a grey jumper and white trainers. At work I usually wear black trousers, a shirt and a dark jacket. My favourite colour is green, so I often wear a green scarf.
Key points
  • The colour goes before the noun and never changes: a red car, red cars. Question: What colour is it?
  • trousers, jeans, shorts, pyjamas, glasses: always plural; to count them: a pair of.
  • wear = on your body; carry = in your hands; put on = start to wear; take off = remove; try on = check in a shop.
  • UK / US: trousers / pants, jumper / sweater, trainers / sneakers, colour / color, grey / gray.
  • clothes (things you wear) ≠ cloth (material).
